## A registered right, not a fence {#what-you-hold}

A servitude is registered against the title deed of the land it crosses. The land stays with its owner; the servitude holder has the rights the servitude registers, over the strip its diagram defines. That is why encroachment is so common: the strip runs through farms, townships and fast-growing metros whose owners and occupiers go on using the land around the line, and sometimes the strip itself.

Two documents decide what a survey should measure against:

- **The diagram.** Surveys for registrable diagrams and general plans are reserved to professional land surveyors ([Geomatics Profession Act 19 of 2013, s13(2)(b)](https://www.sagc.org.za/pdf/legislation/Geomatics%20Profession%20Act%20-%20Act%2019%20of%202013.pdf)), and a diagram must be approved by the Surveyor-General before registration ([Land Survey Act 8 of 1997](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a8-97.pdf)). Where your records hold the diagram as a GIS polygon, a register can report inside and outside it directly.
- **The conditions.** The registered conditions, and the rules for that kind of asset, say what may and may not happen in the strip. Tell your survey provider what they are, so the bands in the register match the widths that matter.

A **wayleave** is the other side of the same coin: permission to place a service in, or work in, another party's servitude or road reserve. Every wayleave application that crosses your strip is a moment when you need to know what already stands there.

## Five kinds of strip, one problem {#kinds}

|---|---|---|
| **Power-line servitudes** | Eskom has publicly urged people not to build structures under high-voltage lines ([Engineering News, 19 February 2024](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/eskom-urges-public-not-to-build-structures-below-high-voltage-powerlines-2024-02-19)). A February 2026 High Court judgment concerned a refused wayleave for a solar plant crossing an Eskom servitude ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/eskom-notes-the-high-court-judgment-in-a-matter-brought-by-sibanye-stillwater-and-others-for-a-wayleave-application/)). | [Power lines](/za/power-lines) |
| **Fuel and gas pipeline servitudes** | NERSA licenses pipelines under the Petroleum Pipelines Act 60 of 2003 and the Gas Act 48 of 2001. A Gas Bill introduced in March 2026 would repeal the Gas Act ([Parliament](https://www.parliament.gov.za/bill/2327140)). | [Pipelines](/za/pipelines) |
| **Bulk-water servitudes** | A 2026 paper by an author at a large bulk-water utility ranks encroachment second on the utility's strategic risk register, across about 3,660 km of pipelines, and describes a gap in encroachment data from 2017 to 2025 ([FIG 2026](https://fig.net/resources/proceedings/fig_proceedings/fig2026/papers/ts05h/TS05H_singh_13637.pdf)). It is the author's account, not an official statement. | [Water utilities](/za/water-utilities) |
| **Rail reserves** | In March 2024 the Housing Development Agency told Parliament that 3,941 households were living on the Central Line between Philippi and Khayelitsha ([GroundUp, 14 March 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/thousands-of-shack-dwellers-are-still-living-on-prasa-rail-lines-in-langa-philippi-and-khayelitsha/)). | [Rail and roads](/za/rail-and-roads) |
| **National road building restriction areas** | Not a servitude, but managed like one: any structure in the building restriction area needs SANRAL's written permission. Outside urban areas it covers land within 60 m of the road boundary, or within 500 m of a point of intersection ([SANRAL Act 7 of 1998, s48](https://www.sagc.org.za/pdf/legislation/S%20A%20National%20Roads%20Agency%20Act%207%20of%201998.pdf)). | [Rail and roads](/za/rail-and-roads#roads) |

The pattern is the same in each: a long, narrow strip, a patrol or inspection cycle measured in months or years, and land beside it that changes faster than the cycle can record.

## Why the first structure matters most {#early}

Once a structure is occupied, resolving an unlawful occupation goes through the courts under the Prevention of Illegal Eviction from and Unlawful Occupation of Land Act 19 of 1998 (PIE Act), with the Constitution's protection of access to housing (s26) and of property (s25) in the background. That takes time, and it should: it is a process about people's homes. The FIG paper cited above argues for finding encroachment early for exactly this reason.

Early knowledge changes the options a servitude holder has. A single new structure, or a fresh patch of cleared ground, can be raised with the landowner, handled through engagement or a notice programme, or dealt with through a wayleave, while those options are still open. A settled cluster years later is a different problem, for the servitude holder and for the families living there. The practical question for any programme is how quickly it learns that something new has appeared. See [land invasion on servitudes](/za/land-invasion-monitoring) for what imagery can see at each stage.

## Decide the purpose before the first survey {#popia}

Satellite and drone imagery of a servitude shows homes and yards, and sometimes people. Under the [Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/3706726-11act4of2013protectionofpersonalinforcorrect.pdf), imagery of identifiable people, homes or businesses can be personal information, and the servitude holder commissioning the survey is usually the responsible party. Four points belong in the plan from the start:

- **An operator agreement** (s21) and security safeguards (s19) with anyone processing the imagery for you, and a plan for breach notification (s22).
- **Transfers abroad** need a lawful basis (s72) if imagery or results are stored or processed outside South Africa.
- **Prior authorisation.** Section 57(1)(b) requires the Information Regulator's prior authorisation before a responsible party processes information on unlawful or objectionable conduct on behalf of third parties. Whether an encroachment programme falls within it depends on how the register will be used. Take legal advice before a register is used as evidence.
- **Security measures.** Some installations are national key points or critical infrastructure, and their security features stay out of the imagery you circulate and the deliverables you receive.

On South African work we scope registers for planning, maintenance and engagement, and deliver them only to the contacts you name. More in [POPIA and aerial imagery](/za/popia).

## New servitudes: count before you acquire {#new}

South Africa is adding transmission capacity fast. NTCSA's Transmission Development Plan 2024 plans about 14,494 km of lines over ten years and names delays in servitude acquisition among its risks; one 400 kV line "was delayed due to servitude acquisition challenges" ([TDP 2024](https://www.ntcsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/TDP-2024-Public-Report_Rev1.pdf)). Seven bidders were prequalified for the first Independent Transmission Projects in December 2025 ([DBSA](https://www.dbsa.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/2025-12/15%20Dec%202025%20ITP%20PQBs%20and%20REIPPPP%20BW7.pdf)).

For any new line or pipeline, the cheapest time to understand the land is before the route is fixed:

- **Compare the alignments** by the structures each would put inside its servitude width, on the same imagery and the same bands. See [route and servitude baselines](/za/transmission-route-baseline).
- **Fix a dated baseline** once the route is chosen, so that what was there before acquisition is on record, and re-survey the strip until construction.
- **Plan for lenders.** Projects financed by development finance institutions often apply IFC Performance Standard 5, where a dated record supports cut-off-date documentation. See [dated imagery for cut-off dates](/insights/dated-imagery-cut-off-dates).
- **Expropriation.** The Expropriation Act 13 of 2024 is not yet implemented; the Minister told Parliament on 19 December 2025 that it faces constitutional challenges and that the Expropriation Act 63 of 1975 remains applicable ([PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/35164/)).
- **Environmental authorisation.** Lines and substations that comply with the Power Line Standard inside the strategic transmission corridors are excluded from needing environmental authorisation ([GN 2313 of 2022](https://www.dffe.gov.za/sites/default/files/legislations/nema_powerlinessubstationsdevelopmet_g47095gon2313.pdf)), so the land baseline is needed earlier, without an EIA to carry it.

## Drones on the strip {#drones}

Commercial drone work in South Africa needs a UAS Operator Certificate and an Air Service Licence, and the landowner's permission for every flight. Flying within 50 m of a structure or a person needs specific approval, which matters on a servitude lined with homes. Many utilities, mines and metros already fly under their own certificates; where they do, analysing that imagery, with satellite imagery between flights, is often the quickest way to a current register. Drone surveys we propose are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. The full rules are in [hiring a drone survey in South Africa](/za/drone-regulations).

## A checklist for servitude holders {#checklist}

:::checklist
- The servitude line or polygon for each asset, with the registered width and what the line represents
- The conditions that say what may stand in the strip, and who in your organisation applies them
- A dated register of what stands in and beside the strip now, with each structure's distance to the line
- A re-survey schedule that matches how fast each stretch is changing and what imagery is available
- A written purpose for the register, settled with counsel under POPIA before the first survey
- An operator agreement, safeguards and a transfer basis with anyone who handles the imagery
- A way to look up what stands at any wayleave crossing before you reply to the applicant
- For new lines, route options compared by the structures they affect, and a baseline before acquisition
- A professional land surveyor for any boundary that has to be fixed on the ground
:::

faq:
  - q: Is the new Public Procurement Act in force?
    a: Not as of 26 September 2026. The Public Procurement Act 28 of 2024 was assented to on 18 July 2024 and comes into operation by proclamation. Draft General Public Procurement Regulations were published on 16 April 2026, with comments closing on 15 July 2026. Until it commences, the Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act and the 2022 regulations apply.
  - q: Can a small supplier use a B-BBEE affidavit?
    a: An exempted micro enterprise can use a sworn affidavit or a CIPC certificate. A qualifying small enterprise can use an affidavit only if it is at least 51 per cent black-owned; otherwise it needs verification by a SANAS-accredited agency, as large companies do. The B-BBEE Commission's FAQ sets out the detail.
  - q: Does a satellite mapping contract raise the drone-licence questions?
    a: No. The UASOC and Air Service Licence rules apply only where an aircraft is flown. Where a scope includes drone work, the proposal separates it, names the company that will fly it and gives its licence details, so your evaluation can treat each part on its own terms.
  - q: Our organisation already licenses satellite imagery through SANSA. Can you use it?
    a: Tell us which imagery and on what terms. SANSA announced a single-licence, multi-user model for government imagery procurement in February 2026. Whether that licence allows a contractor to process the imagery for you is a question for its terms; where it does, we work on the imagery you supply rather than buying new scenes.
  - q: What do you need from us to prepare a proposal?
    a: The route, network or boundary file, the province, the servitude widths or zones to report, the date the record must reflect, the deliverables and the deadline. With a tender, send the documents, the portal and the reference number your process uses.

::::section{id="registration" tone="alt" eyebrow="Supplier registration" title="The Central Supplier Database, and where the main buyers advertise"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Organs of state, including national and provincial departments, municipalities and state-owned companies, buy from suppliers registered on the [Central Supplier Database](https://secure.csd.gov.za/) (CSD), and publish tenders on [eTenders](https://www.etenders.gov.za/). Transnet, for example, states that it does not keep its own database of prospective suppliers and uses the CSD ([Transnet tenders](https://www.transnet.net/TransnetTenders)).

Municipalities buy through their supply-chain management policies under the Municipal Finance Management Act. Private asset owners, mining houses and independent power producers run their own vendor registration.

After award, a vendor-master process usually asks for the CSD report, company registration documents from CIPC, a SARS tax compliance status and VAT registration where it applies, a bank confirmation letter, identity documents for directors, and a B-BBEE certificate or affidavit. [Transnet Engineering's vendor registration](https://www.transnet.net/SubsiteRender.aspx?id=6107034) lists that set.

::::section{id="preference" eyebrow="Preferential procurement" title="Preference points and specific goals"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Tenders by organs of state are evaluated under the Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act and the [Preferential Procurement Regulations, 2022](https://www.treasury.gov.za/legislation/regulations/PREFERENTIAL%20PROCUREMENT%20REGULATIONS,%202022.pdf) (GG 47452, in effect from 16 January 2023). The regulations use an 80/20 preference point system for tenders up to the threshold they set and 90/10 above it: 80 or 90 of the 100 points are awarded on the financial offer, and the rest on the "specific goals" the organ of state publishes in the tender, such as B-BBEE status or ownership by designated groups (PPPFA s2(1)(d)).

The [Public Procurement Act 28 of 2024](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2024/2024081301%20Media%20Statement%20-%20Commencement%20of%20Public%20Procurement%20Act.pdf) was assented to on 18 July 2024 but is not yet in force; it commences by proclamation. National Treasury published draft General Public Procurement Regulations and Tribunal Regulations on 16 April 2026, and extended comments to 15 July 2026 ([draft regulations](https://www.treasury.gov.za/public%20comments/ProcReg/default.aspx)). Check the position before each tender.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="B-BBEE evidence"}
- **Exempted micro enterprises** use a sworn affidavit or a CIPC certificate and are at least Level 4; Level 2 if at least 51 per cent black-owned, Level 1 if wholly black-owned.
- **Qualifying small enterprises** use an affidavit only if at least 51 per cent black-owned; otherwise a verification certificate.
- **Large companies** need verification by a SANAS-accredited agency.

Source: [B-BBEE Commission FAQ](https://www.bbbeecommission.co.za/frequently-asked-questions/category/general-principles/).
:::

::::section{id="drones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Drone work" title="Who may fly, and what to ask for"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Commercial drone work needs a SACAA UAS Operator Certificate with current Operations Specifications and an Air Service Licence. Unless the Minister directs an exemption, the licence goes only to a South African resident, or to a company incorporated in South Africa with at least 75 per cent of its voting rights held by South African residents and actively controlled by them, flying South African aircraft, and those conditions apply at all times ([Air Services Licensing Act, ss16(4) and 19](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201503/act-115-1990.pdf)). A foreign supplier therefore flies through a South African operator that meets the test.

For evaluation, split any scope into its satellite or desk-based part, which needs no aviation approval, and its drone part, which needs all of them. Ask for the flying company's UASOC and OpSpec expiry dates and approvals, its Air Service Licence number and gazette notice, and its insurance.

Drone surveys are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. Afridrone is working towards the operator approvals South Africa requires, and every AfriScan proposal with a drone element names the company that will fly it.

::::section{id="data" eyebrow="Data and imagery" title="POPIA terms, imagery licences and state imagery"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="POPIA operator terms" icon="lock"}
Aerial imagery of homes and businesses can contain personal information. The contract should carry operator terms (s21) and safeguards (s19), breach notification (s22), the basis for any transfer abroad (s72) and a retention period. If the register may be used as evidence about unlawful conduct, check the prior-authorisation rule in s57 first. [POPIA and aerial imagery](/za/popia).
:::
:::card{title="Imagery licences and credits" icon="satellite"}
Each proposal names the imagery the work will use and its capture dates and licence terms. Open data is credited as its licence requires: Google Open Buildings (CC BY 4.0), Microsoft Building Footprints (CDLA-Permissive-2.0), © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) and Copernicus Sentinel. Map-service basemaps are never delivered as survey imagery.
:::
:::card{title="Imagery the state already holds" icon="layers"}
SANSA announced a single-licence, multi-user model for government satellite imagery procurement on 11 February 2026, naming Eskom and several national departments among the entities it serves ([SANSA](https://www.sansa.org.za/2026/02/sansa-moves-to-centralise-satellite-imagery-procurement-in-cost-saving-drive/)). Before a new purchase, check what your organisation can already use.

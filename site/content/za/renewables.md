::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="The land question arrives before the first panel"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="“What was on this land before we optioned it?”" icon="history"}
Clearing, cultivation, informal structures and earlier works all matter to the environmental assessment and the lender's review. The imagery archive shows roughly when each happened.
:::
:::card{title="“Our connection line crosses a dozen farms.”" icon="route"}
A grid connection needs a route and a servitude across land that may be farmed, settled or already crossed by other services. Comparing options by what they cross saves negotiation later.
:::
:::card{title="“The lender wants a land baseline.”" icon="clipboard"}
Projects financed by Equator Principles banks and DFIs apply the IFC Performance Standards on top of national law, and ask for a dated record of the land and any structures affected.
:::
:::card{title="“We need to see progress between site visits.”" icon="calendar"}
Earthworks, laydown areas and access roads change weekly during construction. Dated drone surveys compared side by side show what changed, where and when.
:::

::::section{id="programme" tone="alt" eyebrow="The South African picture" title="New generation needs new lines, and new servitudes"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The three announcements for Bid Window 7 of the Renewable Energy Independent Power Producer Procurement Programme named preferred bidders for 1,760 MW on 23 December 2024, 1,290 MW on 21 July 2025 and 890 MW on 15 December 2025, 3,940 MW in all ([DBSA, 15 December 2025](https://www.dbsa.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/2025-12/15%20Dec%202025%20ITP%20PQBs%20and%20REIPPPP%20BW7.pdf)). Each project needs a grid connection, and a connection line needs a route, a servitude and, often, a wayleave across another party's servitude. Projects that sell power privately raise the same land questions.

The grid itself is expanding: the national [Transmission Development Plan](https://www.ntcsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/TDP-2024-Public-Report_Rev1.pdf) plans about 14,494 km of new lines, and the first Independent Transmission Projects prequalified seven bidders in December 2025. Lines that comply with the [power-line standard (GN 2313)](https://www.dffe.gov.za/sites/default/files/legislations/nema_powerlinessubstationsdevelopmet_g47095gon2313.pdf) inside the strategic transmission corridors are excluded from environmental authorisation, so the land baseline for them has to come from somewhere else.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Where projects are procured"}
The IPP Office publishes the procurement programmes and their documents at [ipp-projects.co.za](https://www.ipp-projects.co.za/). Environmental authorisation for generation facilities runs under NEMA and the EIA Regulations 2014, as amended, with a registered environmental assessment practitioner.
:::

::::section{id="site-selection" eyebrow="Site selection and due diligence" title="A dated land history for each candidate site"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
For each candidate site we assemble the documented history of the land from archived satellite imagery and open datasets: what was built, cleared or cultivated, and roughly when. A land-use and land-cover baseline maps built-up land, cropland, bare ground, water and tree cover across the site and a buffer around it, combined with the structure register.

Candidate sites and connection routes are compared side by side, by the structures they affect and by the terrain: steep and erosion-prone ground and drainage crossings from elevation data, and historically flooded or low-lying land from past satellite records. It is screening that informs the choice; the geotechnical and hydrological studies stay with your engineers.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="connection-lines" tone="alt" eyebrow="Grid connection lines" title="What the connection route would cross"}
Along each connection route option, we list the structures within the servitude width and a wider band, rate each 500 m stretch for encroachment density, and map tracks, cleared ground and other services' corridors the line would cross. Once the route is fixed, a dated register of the structures on it supports landowner engagement and, where structures are affected and lenders apply IFC Performance Standard 5, the cut-off-date records. The servitude diagram itself is a professional land surveyor's work.

::::section{id="construction" eyebrow="Construction and earthworks" title="Dated progress between site visits"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Earthworks and progress" icon="layers"}
Dated drone orthophotos and elevation models compared side by side, with maps of where ground has been cut or filled between visits. Not a certified survey or a quantity surveyor's valuation.
:::
:::card{title="Plant and equipment" icon="grid"}
Heavy equipment visible at laydown yards and work fronts, recorded on each survey date: a snapshot of activity on the land, never tracking.
:::
:::card{title="Volumes" icon="ruler"}
Stockpile, spoil and borrow-pit volumes from drone elevation models, to support your surveyors. Not a statutory survey.
:::
:::

Drone surveys are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. In South Africa that means an operator with a UASOC and an Air Service Licence, the landowner's permission for every flight, and approvals for flights within 50 m of structures, people or roads ([drone law](/za/drone-regulations)).
::::

::::section{id="fire" tone="alt" eyebrow="Fire near the site" title="Fire-hotspot notices and burnt-area maps"}
CSIR's AFIS already gives South Africa free satellite fire information. For the sites and connection lines you name, we add notices of satellite-detected hotspots from NASA FIRMS filtered to the buffers around them, checked by a reviewer, and maps of the burnt area afterwards. It is not an emergency or early-warning service, and small, short-lived or cloud-covered fires can be missed.

::::section{id="why" eyebrow="Why the land question comes first" title="Servitude acquisition is a named risk in the national plan"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="The national plan" icon="power" eyebrow="TDP 2024"}
About 14,494 km of new lines and 210 transformers for 2025 to 2034. The plan lists "delays in obtaining environmental authorisations, servitude acquisitions, and other statutory approvals" among its risks, and records a 400 kV line delayed by servitude acquisition ([TDP 2024](https://www.ntcsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/TDP-2024-Public-Report_Rev1.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="Independent transmission" icon="route" eyebrow="ITP Phase 1"}
The request for qualification launched on 31 July 2025 and drew 17 responses; seven bidders were prequalified on 15 December 2025. The draft request for proposals goes to them, and the final one is expected in the 2026/27 financial year ([DBSA](https://www.dbsa.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/2025-12/15%20Dec%202025%20ITP%20PQBs%20and%20REIPPPP%20BW7.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="Corridors without authorisation" icon="map" eyebrow="GN 2313"}
Compliant lines inside the strategic transmission corridors are excluded from environmental authorisation under the [power-line standard](https://www.dffe.gov.za/sites/default/files/legislations/nema_powerlinessubstationsdevelopmet_g47095gon2313.pdf) (GN 2313, 27 July 2022). The land baseline has to come from somewhere else, and earlier.
:::
:::

Renewable projects add their own lines: the three REIPPPP Bid Window 7 announcements between December 2024 and December 2025 named preferred bidders for 3,940 MW, and each project needs its grid connection and the servitude for it. See [renewables and grid connections](/za/renewables).

::::section{id="options" tone="alt" eyebrow="Compare alignments" title="Every option, measured the same way"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer each alternative alignment at the servitude width you expect and at a wider screening band, and count the structures inside each, stretch by stretch. The same rules apply to every option, so the numbers are comparable: which alignment crosses the fewest structures, where each one runs through dense settlement, and which stretches would need the most negotiation.

Alongside the counts, each option is screened for what makes a route expensive to build and maintain: steep and erosion-prone stretches and drainage crossings from elevation data, stretches in historically flooded or low-lying ground, and the land cover along the way. Where a parcel is contested, the imagery history shows roughly when it was built on or cleared.

The comparison is an input to route selection, not the decision. It does not promise that any option avoids resettlement, and the engineering, environmental and social assessments stay with your teams and consultants.

::::section{id="baseline" eyebrow="Once the route is fixed" title="A dated register before servitude negotiations start"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
When the alignment is chosen, we produce a dated register of the structures on it: each with an ID, coordinates, distance to the centreline, band and, where scoped, a reviewer's category such as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction. Categories describe what the imagery shows; they do not establish use, ownership or value, and compensation needs ground verification.

Where you supply the cadastral parcels, the register is also cut by property, so each landowner conversation starts from the same dated list. During negotiations, the route is re-surveyed on a schedule you set, and new structures are flagged and confirmed by a reviewer.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="For lender-financed lines"}
Where DFIs or Equator Principles banks finance a line or an IPP, IFC Performance Standard 5 applies. Our dated register supports your census and asset inventory and the IFC Performance Standard 5 cut-off-date records; it does not replace the census, and the cut-off date comes from your resettlement process.

::::section{id="law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The law around acquisition" title="Servitudes, surveys and expropriation"}
:::cards{cols="3" style="plain"}
:::card{title="Servitude diagrams" icon="ruler"}
Surveys for registrable diagrams are reserved to professional land surveyors ([Geomatics Profession Act, s13(2)(b)](https://www.sagc.org.za/pdf/legislation/Geomatics%20Profession%20Act%20-%20Act%2019%20of%202013.pdf)), and a diagram needs Surveyor-General approval ([Land Survey Act 8 of 1997](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a8-97.pdf)). Our registers show the surveyor what stands on the strip; they do not replace the survey.
:::
:::card{title="Expropriation" icon="scale"}
The Expropriation Act 13 of 2024 is not yet implemented: the Minister told Parliament on 19 December 2025 that it faces constitutional challenges and that the Expropriation Act 63 of 1975 remains applicable ([PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/35164/)).
:::
:::card{title="Environmental authorisation" icon="leaf"}
Where a line still needs authorisation, it runs under NEMA and the EIA Regulations 2014, as amended, with a registered environmental assessment practitioner. We supply counts and layers for the practitioner's own maps and reports.
:::

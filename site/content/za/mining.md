::::section{id="tailings" tone="alt" eyebrow="Tailings, blast and hazard zones" title="A current list of the structures inside your zones"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
You supply the zones: the inundation area from a dam-break study, a blast exclusion radius, a flood line or a safety setback. We list each structure the review confirms inside them, with its location, its distance to the source and its band, and show how settlement inside each zone changes between surveys. Where villages are dense or roofs are hard to read, reviewers mark structures by hand on high-resolution imagery.

On request, we add estimated households derived from the structure counts, with the assumptions stated, for preparedness planning and engagement, and screen the zones for historically flooded and low-lying ground. Zones are re-surveyed on the schedule you agree with us, with a notice after each survey saying what changed.

The Department of Water and Sanitation has called on mines to register their tailings dams on its dam-safety database ([DWS](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/water-and-sanitation-calls-mines-register-tailing-dams-its-database-ensure)). A current list of the structures inside the downstream zone belongs with that record: it describes the zone as it is today, not as it was when the study was done.

It is a list of structures in the zones you define, with the imagery date on every record. It is not a dam-break model, a dam-safety assessment or a statement of conformance with any tailings standard, and it counts structures, not people at risk.

::::section{id="concessions" tone="alt" eyebrow="Mining-right boundaries" title="What stands inside your right, and in a ring around it"}
Mining rights are granted under the Mineral and Petroleum Resources Development Act, and their edges are where land pressure shows first. We list the structures inside your boundary and in a ring around it, map new structures, cleared ground and tracks at the boundary between dates, and produce a structure layer across the whole right for your land team. On drone imagery, a reviewer checks fence lines and gates; fences are too thin to find automatically on satellite imagery.

We work from the boundary you give us and do not verify title, tenure or surface rights. A boundary that needs surveying or beaconing is a professional land surveyor's work.

::::section{id="surface-excavation" eyebrow="Surface excavation" title="Pits, spoil and disturbed ground on and around your right"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
New pits, spoil heaps, water-filled excavations and disturbed ground inside and around your right are mapped from dated imagery, with new sites flagged between dates and confirmed by a reviewer. In the rainy season, radar comparisons show larger ground changes even under cloud, and flagged areas are followed up with optical imagery or a drone check.

What we map is the ground. We do not identify, count or follow people, and underground workings and covered shafts are not visible from above. Alerts are indications for your teams to check, not proof of anything, and whether and how to act on them is your decision.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="resettlement" tone="alt" eyebrow="Expansions and resettlement" title="Record the footprint before the census"}
When a pit, dump or tailings facility expands, we produce a dated record of the structures inside the new footprint, with reviewer categories such as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction, and on request estimated households. South Africa has no single national resettlement regulation, so the process follows the project's own resettlement plan and, where DFIs or Equator Principles banks are involved, IFC Performance Standard 5. Our dated record supports the census and asset inventory and the cut-off-date records; it does not replace them, and categories do not establish use, ownership or value.

[Resettlement cut-off-date baselines](/solutions/resettlement-cut-off-baselines)

Vegetation cover on rehabilitated land compared between dates, from Copernicus Sentinel data and, where flown, drone elevation models. It shows the trend; it is not an ecological assessment or a closure sign-off.

Cut and fill between drone visits, and stockpile and void volumes from drone elevation models, to support your mine surveyors. Not a statutory survey.

:::card{title="A pre-disturbance baseline" icon="history"}
A land-use and land-cover baseline for new ground before it is disturbed, and the imagery history of what was there before.

Financial provision for rehabilitation and closure is made under the NEMA Financial Provisioning Regulations, 2015, and the compliance date for holders of mining rights has been extended pending amendments ([statement](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-barbara-creecy-grants-further-extension-holders-mineral-and)).

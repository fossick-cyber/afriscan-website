::::section{id="law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The law and the regulator" title="Licences, the Mining Commission and what occupation does not give" lead="A summary of the public rules as of 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice."}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The [Mining Act, Cap. 123](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2010/14/eng@2019-11-30), gives the Mining Commission (*Tume ya Madini*) the function to "regulate and monitor the mining industry" (s.22). It sets exclusive areas for primary licences (s.16) and the grant of primary mining licences (PMLs, *leseni ndogo za uchimbaji madini*) (s.55), and the local-content duties in Part VIII. Licence information is published on the [Mining Cadastre Portal](https://portal.madini.go.tz/).

The courts have been clear that occupation does not create mineral rights. The Court of Appeal held in 2018 that occupation or administrative promises do not ([2018] TZCA 225), and in 2024 it affirmed that "a party cannot claim lawful ownership of mining property without a valid licence under the Mining Act, regardless of occupation or official correspondence" ([2024] TZCA 580).
::::
::::col
### Small-scale licence areas {#small-scale}

The Mining Commission oversees small-scale miners (*wachimbaji wadogo*), including licence holders who work under technical-support agreements under the *Kanuni za Msaada wa Kiufundi kwa Wamiliki wa Leseni Ndogo za Uchimbaji Madini, 2025*. In August 2026 it inspected 128 such projects, found 42 inactive and 86 with shortcomings, and gave them 30 days to correct them; it may revoke technical-support approvals ([Mining Commission, 26 August 2026](https://www.tumemadini.go.tz/pages/news/b32732ea-93aa-4937-bcc3-41ae201b8a6d/)).

Whether ground is being worked in a licence area, and what changed between two dates, is exactly what dated imagery shows. For the Commission and for technical-support partners, we map pits, disturbed ground, spoil and new tracks across small-scale licence areas and flag what is new between surveys, so inspections go where the ground has changed.

::::section{id="boundary" eyebrow="What we map around a licence" title="The boundary, the ring around it, and the zones inside"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Boundary and perimeter change.** We map new structures, cleared ground and new tracks inside the licence boundary and in a ring around it, and compare between dated surveys. New and removed features are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views. Fence lines and gates are too thin to detect on satellite imagery, so a reviewer checks them on drone imagery where it is flown.

**Excavations.** Pits, disturbed ground, spoil heaps and water-filled excavations inside and around the licence area are mapped with their distance to the boundary, and new sites are flagged between dates. We describe the sites and the ground, never who is working them.

**Hazard and buffer zones.** Structures inside the tailings, blast or buffer zones your engineers define are listed with their location and distance to the source, so your team knows which structures stand inside a zone it is responsible for.

**Rehabilitation.** Vegetation cover on rehabilitated land is compared between dates from Copernicus Sentinel imagery and drone orthophotos, so the closure team can see the trend.

::::section{id="resettlement" tone="alt" eyebrow="New projects and expansions" title="A dated baseline before the valuation"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
A new pit, plant or tailings facility often needs land that villages use. Tanzanian compensation follows the valuation: the Valuation and Valuers Registration Act treats payment as prompt only within six months after the valuation is approved (s.52), and the High Court has held that developments made after the valuation and notice are not compensable. On a lender-financed project, IFC Performance Standard 5 adds a publicly established cut-off date.

A dated structure register of the project area before the valuation, and another on the cut-off date, supports the census, the asset inventory and the valuation. It does not replace them, and it does not decide eligibility.
::::
::::col
### Imagery history {#history}

Where archived satellite imagery exists, the documented history of a licence area and its surroundings shows what was built, cleared or dug, and roughly when. How much archive exists varies by place and year. It is not a title, tenure or valuation check.

::::section{id="local-content" eyebrow="Local content" title="How we work on Tanzanian mining contracts"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The Mining (Local Content) Regulations, 2018 (GN 3 of 2018), as amended, set the rules for anyone supplying a mining licence holder or its contractors:

- an **indigenous Tanzanian company** has at least 20 per cent of its equity owned by Tanzanian citizens, with Tanzanians in at least 80 per cent of executive and senior management posts and all other posts;
- a **non-indigenous company** supplying "a contractor, a subcontractor, licensee, the Corporation or other allied entity" must "incorporate a joint venture company with an indigenous Tanzanian company and afford that indigenous Tanzanian company an equity participation of at least twenty percentum" (reg 8(6));
- sole-sourced contracts, and competitive contracts above a set value, are notified to the Commission (reg 16);
- since 2022, "contractor" and "mining activities" cover activities "within and outside Tanzania".

In mining, AfriScan therefore works as a technology supplier: a qualified Tanzanian company leads the contract, and AfriScan supplies the imagery analysis, the review and the deliverables to it. Each proposal names the lead company and sets out its local-content position. The regulations have been amended several times, so check the current text with the Mining Commission or your counsel before a contract is signed.

::::section{id="drones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Drone surveys at mines" title="Orthophotos, volumes and fence checks, with the licence holder's authority"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Where satellite imagery is not detailed enough, drone surveys capture orthophotos and elevation models of pits, dumps, stockpiles and rehabilitation areas, with stockpile and earthworks volumes calculated from them, subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires. Volumes from drone models are not a statutory survey; where a registered surveyor is required, the data supports their work.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Flying over a licence area"}
Fly only for the licence holder or with its written authority, with the launch and recovery consents of GN 268 of 2026, reg 86, and within the 50 m rule for people and structures not under the operator's control unless TCAA approves otherwise. Blasting is regulated under the mining laws. Afridrone is working towards Tanzanian drone approvals, and every drone proposal names the company that will fly.

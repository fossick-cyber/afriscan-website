faq:
  - q: Is the register a census or a valuation?
    a: No. The census, the asset inventory and the valuation stay with the RAP team and the valuers, and consultation with the village council, the village assembly and the people affected stays with the project. The register gives them a dated, located count of the structures that existed on each date, so the field inventory can be planned, checked and defended.
  - q: Which dates should we record?
    a: At least three. Before any notice goes out, such as the 90-day notice for a village land transfer or the notices for a wayleave; on the valuation date; and on the cut-off date for eligibility. A survey after the cut-off date then shows what appeared since, flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer.
  - q: Can you put a value on the structures?
    a: No. Values are for valuers registered under the Valuation and Valuers Registration Act, Cap. 138. Reviewer categories describe what the imagery shows, such as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction; they do not establish a building's use, who lives there, ownership or value, and compensation always needs ground verification.
  - q: What imagery does a baseline use?
    a: Dated imagery only, meaning a very-high-resolution satellite scene with a known capture date, a new satellite capture, or a drone survey, subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires. Map-service basemaps are never used as the dated record. The report states the capture date of every scene it relies on.
  - q: Can the register show structures built after the valuation?
    a: Yes. We compare the valuation-date register with a later dated survey and list the structures that are new, each confirmed by a reviewer with before-and-after views. Whether any of them is compensable is for the valuers, the project's resettlement framework and, in a dispute, the courts.
  - q: Can you map graves?
    a: Not reliably. Graves are often small, unmarked or under vegetation, so they may not be visible from above. Where graves have to be moved under the Graves (Removal) Act, the field team's inventory stays essential; the register can show the structures and cleared plots around them.

::::section{id="dates" eyebrow="Why the date decides" title="Compensation in Tanzania follows the valuation" lead="We found no stand-alone Tanzanian resettlement statute. Compensation (*fidia*) and valuation (*tathmini*) run through the Land Act, the Land Acquisition Act, the Village Land Act and the valuation law, and on lender-financed projects through IFC Performance Standard 5. Each of them turns on dates."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Prompt payment within six months" icon="calendar" eyebrow="Valuation Act, Cap. 138"}
Section 52 of the [Valuation and Valuers Registration Act](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2016/7/eng@2023-12-31) defines "prompt payment of compensation" as "payment of compensation within six months after" the valuation is approved.
:::
:::card{title="After the valuation, not compensable" icon="scale" eyebrow="High Court"}
TanzLII's summaries of a land case decided in 2016 and 2020 ([2016] TZHC 2292; [2020] TZHC 1972) record that the "valuation date governs applicable rates", that "post-valuation developments" are "not compensable if made after valuation and notice", and that 6 per cent statutory interest is due for delayed payment under the Land Acquisition Act, s.15.
:::
:::card{title="A public cut-off date" icon="file-check" eyebrow="IFC Performance Standard 5"}
A census, and "a cut-off date for eligibility" that is "well documented and disseminated" (para 12), with no duty to compensate those who encroach after a cut-off date that was clearly established and made public (para 23) ([PS5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf)).
:::
:::

**Village land.** Under the Village Land Act, s.4, a transfer of village land starts with a gazetted notice at least 90 days ahead, and "no village transfer land shall be transferred" until the type, amount, method and timing of compensation are agreed, or the High Court approves an interim payment (s.4(8)). **Wayleaves.** Under the Land Act, s.166, compensation is payable for the use of land and, for a wayleave, for damage to trees, crops and buildings, including damage from preliminary survey work. **Railways.** Tanzania Railways Corporation summarises it in its land-acquisition news: under the 1999 land law and the 2016 valuation law, areas a project crosses "yatafanyiwa tathmini, kutwaliwa na wamiliki wa maeneo hayo watalipwa" (will be valued and acquired, and their owners paid).

::::section{id="two-dates" eyebrow="How a baseline programme runs" title="Before the notice, on the valuation date, on the cut-off date"}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Before any notice"}
A register from the most recent dated imagery before notices go out, and an imagery history of the area, so the project knows what existed before anyone expected acquisition.
:::
:::step{title="On the valuation date"}
A register from imagery captured as close to the valuation date as satellites and weather allow, or a drone survey, to size the valuation and plan the field inventory. The valuers and the RAP team walk it; the register shows them where.
:::
:::step{title="On the cut-off date"}
A register on the publicly established cut-off date, the date PS5 eligibility turns on, set against the valuation-date register so any structures that appeared in between are visible and reviewed.
:::
:::step{title="After the cut-off date"}
Scheduled re-surveys show new structures and cleared plots in the project area, confirmed by a reviewer, for the land team and the lenders' monitors. Whether any of them is compensable is decided under the project's framework, not by the map.
:::

::::section{id="data" eyebrow="Personal data" title="Structures in the register, names with the RAP team"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Lists of project-affected persons (PAPs), household data and drone frames that show people are personal data under the [Personal Data Protection Act, Cap. 44](https://pdpc.go.tz/documents/3/PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_ACT_2022.pdf). The Act requires registration with the Personal Data Protection Commission before personal data are collected or processed (s.14(1)) and a legal basis for transfers outside Tanzania (ss.31–32). The Commission began compliance inspections on 28 August 2026.

Our registers describe structures and carry no names. The PAP list, the census and the valuation roll stay with the RAP team and the valuers, and each contract states who processes what, where it is stored and how any transfer is covered.

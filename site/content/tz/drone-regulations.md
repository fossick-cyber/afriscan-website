faq:
  - q: Are drone permits issued under the 2018 rules still valid?
    a: GN 268 of 2026 revokes the 2018 regulations, GN 758 of 2018 (reg 121), and contains no transitional or savings clause. The text does not say how registrations, pilot licences or permits issued under the 2018 rules carry over, so ask TCAA in writing before a job relies on one.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly commercial drone surveys in Tanzania?
    a: Not without a Tanzanian base. TCAA issues an RPAS Operator Certificate only to an applicant whose principal place of business is registered in Tanzania (reg 47(a)), and only a Tanzanian citizen, a resident, a company registered in Tanzania or the Government may own a drone there (reg 7). Reg 51 says TCAA shall recognise a certificate issued by another contracting state where the requirements behind it are not less favourable; how TCAA applies that in practice is for TCAA to confirm. The realistic routes are a Tanzanian company with its own ROC, or a Tanzanian operator that has one.
  - q: Does a satellite survey need TCAA approval?
    a: No aircraft is flown, so GN 268 does not apply to the satellite work itself, and we found no Tanzanian licensing law for satellite analytics. The Personal Data Protection Act still applies to any personal data, and protected places are left out of every deliverable.
  - q: Do off-the-shelf survey drones meet the 2026 C2 frequency rules?
    a: The text does not answer it. GN 263 of 2026 confines terrestrial C2 links to bands allocated to the Aeronautical Mobile (Route) Service, such as 960–1164 MHz and 5030–5091 MHz, and GN 259 of 2026 allows only bands allocated and protected by the ITU Radio Regulations. On their face, neither lists the 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz bands most off-the-shelf survey drones use. How TCAA applies this to small drones flown within sight is not settled, so get TCAA's written answer before any equipment is bought or imported.
  - q: Whose consent is needed for launch and recovery sites along our wayleave?
    a: Reg 86(1) requires "the consent of the relevant Authority" to launch or recover a drone from any public or private property, and reg 86(2) requires permission from, or notice to, the appropriate authorities and the people around the area before the operation starts. The regulations do not define "relevant Authority". The landowner's or asset owner's written consent is sensible practice as well, because reg 87 protects private property; agree in the contract who obtains each consent.
  - q: Can a drone fly beyond the pilot's sight along a pipeline or power line?
    a: Only with TCAA's special authorisation and a detect-and-avoid system (reg 30(4)). Without them, a corridor is flown in legs within visual line of sight, in visual meteorological conditions, at or below 400 ft above ground level, with the 50 m rule for people, vessels, vehicles and structures not under the operator's control.
  - q: Which security approvals does a drone survey need?
    a: A ROC needs a security clearance issued by the appropriate authority, meaning the authority with jurisdiction over the area of operation, and an Approved Aircraft Operator Security Programme (reg 47(d)–(e)). Pilot licence and drone registration applications go to the competent security agencies for vetting (reg 84), and a commercial operator that wants to fly with a camera declares its camera use for security review (reg 80(4)(d)). Ask TCAA which other authorities must clear your particular site.

### The numbers that shape a drone job {#numbers}

:::facts{cols="4"}
- ROC application: at least 60 days before the operation (reg 46(5))
- ROC validity: 12 months from issue or renewal; an expired ROC needs a fresh application (reg 48)
- Category B risk assessment: at least 14 days ahead, for each operation (reg 6)
- Height: at or below 400 ft above ground level (reg 30)
- Separation: 50 m from people and structures not under the operator's control (reg 30)
- Aerodromes: 10 km (code C–F) or 7 km (code A–B) need written permissions (reg 42)
- Remote pilots: 18 or older, with a Class 3 medical (regs 55–57)
- Background checks: every 24 months for everyone handling drones (reg 84)
:::

**The regulator's own pages lag the text.** On 27 September 2026, TCAA's [RPAS Regulations page](https://www.tcaa.go.tz/publications/rpas-regulations) still listed only the 2018 instruments, and its [drone permit procedures](https://www.tcaa.go.tz/pages/drones) and [FAQ](https://www.tcaa.go.tz/faqs) still described the 2018-era process. Where this guide cites them, it says so. The new regulations are nine days old at the date of this review, so expect practice to settle over the coming months.

### Three mass classes {#classes}

Reg 4(2) sorts drones by mass, payload included:

- **Class 1:** not more than 5 kg;
- **Class 2:** more than 5 kg and less than 25 kg;
- **Class 3:** 25 kg and above.

### Three operation categories {#operation-categories}

- **Category A** is low-risk flying within set limits (reg 5): a registered drone under 5 kg maximum take-off mass, within visual line of sight, not over assemblies of people, not above 400 ft (120 m) above ground level, outside controlled, restricted and prohibited airspace, and **only** as "a club activity" or "recreational operation". Survey work for a client falls outside it.
- **Category B**, "specific", is where commercial survey work sits (reg 6).
- **Category C** operations are treated like manned aircraft.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Category B, in four requirements"}
- An operational authorisation or a ROC (the text calls it a "Remote Operation Certificate")
- An operations manual approved by TCAA
- Proof of pilot competency: a training certificate or licence
- For **each operation**, a safety risk assessment and mitigation plan submitted to TCAA for approval "at least fourteen days prior to the intended operations", except for known and justified emergency operations (reg 6(6)–(8))
:::

The 14 days are a floor for every Category B job, not a one-off: plan a separate risk assessment for each site or stretch you want flown.

::::section{id="operator" tone="alt" eyebrow="The operator" title="The RPAS Operator Certificate (ROC)" lead="Part IV of GN 268. No commercial operation may take place without a ROC, which authorises operations under the operations specifications attached to it (reg 45)."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Applying** (reg 46). The application goes on TCAA's prescribed form with a minimum equipment list. An application for a new ROC or a renewal is made "at least sixty days before the date of the intended operation" (46(5)). The certificate states "the authorised areas of operation" (46(3)(h)), so check that your site falls inside them.

**What TCAA looks for** (reg 47). TCAA "may issue" a ROC if the applicant:

- "(a) has principal place of business and the place is registered in the United Republic of Tanzania";
- (c) is properly qualified, staffed and equipped;
- (d) has a security clearance issued by the appropriate authority;
- "(e) has an Approved Aircraft Operator Security Programme".

A safety management system is required as well (45(4)), with a Flight Data Analysis Programme as part of it (reg 112), an accountable manager acceptable to TCAA and an operations manual following the Second Schedule (regs 52–53).

**How long it lasts** (reg 48). A ROC is "valid for 12 months from the date of issue or renewal" (48(1)), unless TCAA sets a shorter period or the ROC is suspended, revoked or surrendered before then. A ROC that has expired needs a fresh application (48(3)). TCAA inspects and tests the holders of a ROC during that time (reg 50).

**Each job** (reg 54). Every commercial operation also needs its own authorisation from TCAA. The request (54(6)) covers the registration, airworthiness, ROC and pilot licence; the purpose, route, dates and performance; the C2 links and their coverage; navigation and detect-and-avoid equipment; emergency procedures; remote pilot stations and handovers; the payload; and "(u) proof of adequate insurance coverage". Documents in other languages need an English translation (54(7)), and a flight across a border needs the other State's authorisation too (54(2)–(3)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="For foreign and international buyers"}
- A ROC goes only to an applicant with a **principal place of business registered in Tanzania** (reg 47(a)).
- Only a Tanzanian citizen, a resident, a **company registered in Tanzania** or the Government may **own** a drone (reg 7).
- No drone or component may be **imported** without a TCAA permit (reg 8).
- Reg 51 says TCAA "shall recognise as valid, a ROC issued by a contracting state, provided that the requirements under which the certificate was issued are not less favourable". How that works in practice is for TCAA to confirm.

When a proposal includes drone work in Tanzania, ask which company will fly, where its principal place of business is registered, and for its ROC and operations specifications.

:::card{title="Ownership and import" icon="building"}
Owners must be Tanzanian citizens, residents, Tanzania-registered companies or the Government (reg 7). Importing a drone "or any component thereof" needs a TCAA permit (reg 8); TCAA's procedures page warns that without one "the drone will be confiscated at the entry point upon arrival". Exporting needs a deregistration certificate.
:::
:::card{title="Registration and remote ID" icon="clipboard"}
TCAA registers each drone before it may operate (reg 9(1)), with a 5H- mark (regs 9–12); certificates cannot be transferred. The drone must broadcast its identity, transmit its location and altitude, and identify its operator (reg 13), and changes of particulars are notified to TCAA in writing (reg 15).
:::
:::card{title="Geo-fencing and the transponder wording" icon="map"}
Geo-fencing must keep the drone out of prohibited and restricted areas and "protected airspace declared by the government" (reg 27). Reg 30(6) reads: "All RPA shall be equipped with an appropriate mode transponder". Ask TCAA how that applies to small survey drones before a flight is planned.
:::
:::card{title="C2 link and radio" icon="layers"}
A service-level agreement with any C2 communication service provider, approved by TCAA (reg 22(6)); contingency procedures for loss of the link (reg 37); C2 radio equipment with national type approval (reg 20(3); Cap. 306, s.83); and an RPA radio station licence carried on operations (reg 110(2)(c)).
:::
:::card{title="Sale and maintenance" icon="shield"}
Selling, reselling or distributing drones needs TCAA's approval (reg 14). Maintenance is done by an organisation approved by TCAA or another contracting state, or under procedures TCAA authorises: "A person shall not conduct RPAS maintenance without approval from the Authority" (regs 18–19).
:::
:::card{title="Documents to keep" icon="file-check"}
At the operator's base: the ROC and operations specifications, registration and airworthiness certificates, manuals, the insurance certificate and an emergency response plan. At the remote pilot station: a copy of the ROC and operations specifications, crew licences, the radio station licence and logs (regs 109–111).

### The C2 frequency question {#c2-frequencies}

GN 263 of 2026 adds a Sixth Schedule to the spectrum regulations. Satellite C2 links use AMS(R)S bands (1610–1626.5 MHz; 5000–5150 MHz), mobile-satellite bands where AMS(R)S has priority (1545–1555 MHz; 1646.5–1656.5 MHz), or fixed-satellite bands under ITU Resolution 155. Terrestrial C2 links "shall operate in bands allocated to the Aeronautical Mobile (Route) Service", including 960–1164 MHz and 5030–5091 MHz. The C2 Link Regulations add that only bands "appropriately allocated and protected by the ITU Radio Regulations" may be used (GN 259, reg 15).

On their face, these rules do not list the 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz bands that most off-the-shelf survey drones use. Whether TCAA applies them to small drones flown within sight is not settled. **Get TCAA's written answer before buying or importing equipment for Tanzanian work.**

::::section{id="pilots" tone="alt" eyebrow="Each pilot" title="The remote pilot licence"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
A remote pilot licence is needed to fly commercially (reg 55). The applicant must (55(2)):

- be at least 18;
- have a current medical certificate, which must be Class 3 to act as remote pilot (57(1)(d));
- be proficient in English;
- have a radiotelephony licence;
- have completed an approved course;
- have passed the knowledge and skills tests.

Ratings are added as the work needs them: type, instrument, night and instructor (55(3)).
::::
::::col
**Keeping it current.** A pilot needs an initial or recurrent knowledge test within the previous 24 calendar months (reg 58), and the licence's validity follows the medical certificate (reg 61).

**Security vetting.** Every remote pilot licence application goes to the competent security agencies for vetting before issue (reg 84).

**Foreign pilots.** TCAA's FAQ says foreign pilots must convert their licences and comply with the immigration and work-permit rules.

*Rubani wa droni* is the term TCAA's Swahili procedures page uses for a drone pilot.

### The limits in reg 30 {#limits}

| Rule | What it takes to go beyond it |
|---|---|
| At or below **400 ft** above ground level | TCAA may approve private and commercial operations at "higher heights and lateral distances" (30(2)) |
| **50 m** from any person, vessel, vehicle or structure not under the control of the person in charge of the drone (30(1)(b)) | The same approval under 30(2) |
| Visual meteorological conditions and **visual line of sight** | Beyond or extended visual line of sight only with a "special authorization" and a detect-and-avoid system (30(4)) |
| Daylight | Night flights with TCAA's authorisation, lights and landing lights (reg 31) |
| One drone at a time | Approval to fly more than one (reg 33) |
| Not over open-air assemblies or crowds | Approval (reg 35) |

Reg 30(1)(b) itself says a person shall operate RPAS "within the radius of 50 meters from any person, vessel, vehicle or structure which is not under the control of the person in charge of the RPAS". We read it as a separation distance; confirm with TCAA how it applies to your operation. TCAA may also impose additional security restrictions (30(9)).

### The 50 m rule along a wayleave {#fifty-metres}

A pipeline or power-line wayleave that runs past homes, shops and farm buildings puts structures within 50 m of almost every flight path. Unless TCAA approves greater distances for the operation, the plan has to keep 50 m from each structure and person not under the operator's control. That is one reason drone work along a lived-in corridor needs more time to approve than a flight over open bush, and why satellite screening comes first.

### Airspace and aerodromes {#airspace}

- **Prohibited areas:** no flights. **Restricted and danger areas:** TCAA's written approval (reg 25).
- **Controlled airspace:** prior authorisation from the air traffic control unit with jurisdiction (reg 26).
- **Aerodromes** (reg 42): written permission from the aerodrome owner or operator and the air navigation service provider, plus TCAA's approval, to fly within **10 km** of the aerodrome reference point of a code C–F aerodrome or **7 km** of a code A–B aerodrome, on approach and take-off paths, near navigation aids, or in an aerodrome traffic zone or holding pattern. TCAA's FAQ adds that operations within a 10 km radius need ATC clearance.
- **Notified areas:** keep away from notified prohibited, restricted, controlled and danger areas unless expressly authorised (reg 117(2)). Check the whole route against them before a drone plan is costed.

::::section{id="security" tone="alt" eyebrow="Security and privacy" title="Vetting, camera review and consent" lead="Part VII of GN 268. Security runs through the whole approval chain, from the operator's clearance to each pilot's background checks."}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Operator security procedures** (reg 80). No operation without procedures developed under the Civil Aviation (Security) Regulations, covering secured premises and ground stations and protected IT systems. "(d) Commercial Operators requesting to operate with a camera shall be required to include details of the camera usage in the application for Security review and approval", and "(f) … recurrent background checks" apply.

**Vetting** (reg 84). Applications for remote pilot licences and drone registrations go to "competent security agencies for security vetting prior to issuance". Certificates go only to those who pass "a security threat assessment"; a risk found later can lead to amendment, suspension or revocation. Everyone who deploys, handles or stores drones undergoes "background and criminal record checks every 24 months".

**Storage and reporting** (regs 81–82, 85, 115). Drones are stored securely, a security coordinator is designated, drones no longer in use are disabled or destroyed, and acts of unlawful interference, unauthorised drone activity, loss of control and security breaches are reported to TCAA.

**Information sharing** (reg 118). TCAA "may, for safety and security purposes, share RPAS registration and operational data with (a) law enforcement agencies; (b) immigration authorities; and (c) security agencies."
::::
::::col
**Consent for the site** (reg 86):

> "(1) A remotely piloted aircraft shall not be launched or recovered from any public or private property without the consent of the relevant Authority."
>
> "(2) The RPAS pilot or the owner shall seek permission or notify the appropriate authorities, as well as people around the area before starting the operations."

**Privacy** (reg 87). Cameras must be operated "in a responsible way to respect the privacy of others". Reg 87 also bars using a drone to keep watch on a person without that person's consent, or on private real property without its owner's consent, and bars photographing or filming an individual without consent in order to publish or publicly disseminate the images, except for newsgathering or events open to the public.

Whether a corridor mapping flight over private plots falls within reg 87's rule on private real property is a question for counsel. Until it is settled, plan on the owner's written consent for each plot flown.

**The 2018 security regulations.** GN 261 of 2026 deletes subregulation (6) of regulation 14 of the [Civil Aviation (Security) Regulations, 2018](https://www.tcaa.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1789477709-GP%20GN%20756%20of%202018%20CIVIL%20AVIATION%20(SECURITY)%20REGULATIONS,%202018.pdf), the regulation on aircraft operator security programmes. Read the amendment against the consolidated 2018 text and confirm its effect with TCAA.

::::section{id="sensitive-sites" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="Protected places, installations and parks"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Military sites, State House, prisons" icon="shield"}
TCAA's FAQ says drones are not allowed near military installations, State House or prison areas, and that airports and airfields, government facilities and offices, national parks and crowded public areas need special permission. The [National Security Act, Cap. 47](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/1970/3/eng@2023-12-31), s.3, makes it an offence, for "any purpose prejudicial to the safety or interests of the United Republic", to approach, inspect, pass over or enter a protected place, or to make "any sketch, plan, model or note" of one. Protected places and areas are declared under the [Protected Places and Areas Act, Cap. 74](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/1969/38/eng@2002-07-31). We treat them as no-fly areas unless cleared, and leave them out of every image and report.
:::
:::card{title="Pipelines, stations and terminals" icon="pipeline"}
Fly near a pipeline, pump station or terminal only with the operator's written permission and through its site security. Altering, removing or connecting to a pipeline without the licensee's consent is an offence under the Petroleum Act, s.240. The Petroleum Act's s.203 safety zones sit in the Part on upstream operations, so they cover upstream facilities such as wells and upstream processing, not midstream pipelines or pump stations.
:::
:::card{title="Power lines and substations" icon="power"}
Lines, towers and substations are structures, so the 50 m rule in reg 30 applies unless TCAA approves closer work. GN 268 sets no live-line distance: the utility's own safety rules decide how close a drone may go.
:::
:::card{title="Mines and national parks" icon="tree"}
Fly over a mining licence area only for the licence holder or with its written authority, and with the reg 86 consents; blasting is regulated under the mining laws. National parks need a permit from the national park authorities as well as TCAA's approval (TCAA FAQ).
:::
:::

**Third-party insurance** (reg 113). No operation "unless there is in force an adequate insurance policy in respect of third-party risks". TCAA notifies the minimum sum (113(2)), the certificate is kept at operational management (113(3)), and TCAA "may dispense with requirement depending on the class and category of the RPAS" (113(4)). Each operation's authorisation request carries proof of the cover (54(6)(u)). TCAA's FAQ calls insurance mandatory.

**Exemptions** (Part IX). An exemption from a requirement is applied for at least 60 days ahead (reg 105(2)).

**Data management** (reg 116) covers electronic navigation data products only; it does not regulate survey imagery.

::::section{id="penalties" tone="alt" eyebrow="Enforcement" title="Penalties"}
Reg 119 makes it an offence to endanger airspace, aircraft, persons or property, to operate without authorisation, to fly without displaying the unique identifier or registration, or to operate an unregistered drone. Each carries a fine and, on conviction, up to six months' imprisonment, or both (119(1)–(4)); an operator failing its obligations faces the same (119(6)), and failing to comply with a TCAA direction carries a fine (119(5)). Licences, certificates, approvals and authorisations may be suspended or revoked (119(7)). Reg 120 adds a general penalty, and the drone may be confiscated. Under the C2 Link Regulations, each day of a continuing contravention counts as a separate offence (GN 259, reg 26).

TCAA's FAQ lists "substantial financial fines; confiscation of the drone; criminal prosecution; and in serious cases, imprisonment", and says TCAA and the security authorities can seize drones. In short: fines, confiscation and up to six months' imprisonment. This guide quotes no amounts.

:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="The Personal Data Protection Act" icon="lock"}
The [PDPA, Cap. 44](https://pdpc.go.tz/documents/3/PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_ACT_2022.pdf), requires registration with the Personal Data Protection Commission before personal data are collected or processed (s.14(1)), security measures (s.27) and a legal basis for transfers outside Tanzania (ss.31–32), for which the PDPC issues [cross-border transfer permits](https://pdpc.go.tz/services/cross-border-data-transfer-permit/). Drone frames that show identifiable people, and lists of project-affected persons, are personal data. The PDPC began compliance inspections on 28 August 2026.
:::
:::card{title="No data-deposit rule found" icon="file-text"}
We found no Tanzanian law that makes survey imagery State property or requires it to be deposited with an agency, unlike Mozambique's aerial-survey law. That is our reading of the texts, for your counsel to confirm. Camera use still goes through the security review (reg 80(4)(d)), and imagery of protected places is never made or published (Cap. 47, s.3).
:::
:::card{title="Satellite work" icon="satellite"}
No aircraft is flown, so GN 268 does not apply to satellite screening, and we found no Tanzanian licensing law for satellite analytics. Imagery licences are commercial. Our registers describe structures and ground, not people.
:::

::::section{id="checklist" tone="alt" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Tanzania" lead="Ask any operator, including one we propose, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="A Tanzanian base, and local-content status"}
A principal place of business registered in Tanzania, with its BRELA certificate and TIN. For oil, gas and mining work, the operator's local-content status as well: the Petroleum Act, s.219, and the Mining (Local Content) Regulations set their own tests.
:::
:::step{title="A valid ROC that covers your site"}
Issued or renewed within the last 12 months, with operations specifications and "authorised areas of operation" that include your wayleave or site.
:::
:::step{title="TCAA's authorisation for this operation"}
Under reg 54, for this job. For Category B, the safety risk assessment submitted for approval at least 14 days ahead (reg 6(8)). TCAA says permits are usually location-specific.
:::
:::step{title="Each drone registered and equipped"}
5H- registration, remote ID and geo-fencing, imported under a TCAA permit. Ask how each drone meets reg 30(6) (the transponder wording) and the GN 263 frequency rules.
:::
:::step{title="Each pilot with a Tanzanian licence"}
A Tanzanian remote pilot licence with the type rating, a Class 3 medical and a radiotelephony licence. Foreign pilots: converted licences and work permits.
:::
:::step{title="Security in place"}
The ROC's security clearance, the Approved Aircraft Operator Security Programme, security-vetted staff, and camera use approved in the security review (reg 80(4)(d)).
:::
:::step{title="Insurance"}
A third-party insurance certificate for the operation (reg 113; reg 54(6)(u)), and the indemnity your contract requires.
:::
:::step{title="Site permissions"}
The "relevant Authority" consent for launch and recovery (reg 86(1)); permission from, or notice to, the appropriate authorities and people around the area (86(2)); the landowner's or asset owner's consent; the local authority for public areas (reg 117); the aerodrome operator, air navigation service provider and TCAA within 10 km or 7 km of an aerodrome (reg 42); a park permit; and your own security manager at stations, substations, terminals and mines.
:::
:::step{title="A flight plan inside the limits"}
Visual line of sight, 400 ft or below and 50 m from uninvolved people and structures, or the specific approvals: special authorisation and detect-and-avoid beyond visual line of sight, night, crowds, more than one drone.
:::
:::step{title="Data clauses"}
Retention; who may publish images; how people in frame are handled (reg 87); the processor's PDPC registration; a PDPC transfer permit if data leave Tanzania; no imagery of protected places; and awareness that TCAA may share operational data with security agencies (reg 118).
:::
:::step{title="The operator's manuals and plans"}
A safety management system, an operations manual, an emergency response plan and C2 contingency procedures (regs 45(4), 53, 109 and 37).
:::

faq:
  - q: Which widths do you measure?
    a: Yours. Road and railway reserves are set in Tanzanian law, but the wayleave of a pipeline or power line comes from the licensee's own acquisition, agreement or standard, and we found no single statutory width for either. Send the widths with the route, or the wayleave polygons themselves, and we report each structure's distance to the line and the band it falls in, for example 0–15 m, 15–30 m and 30–60 m, with up to six widths in one survey.
  - q: How recent is the imagery?
    a: As recent as the dated imagery for your area allows. We start from dated very-high-resolution archive scenes or the imagery you hold, and request a new satellite capture where the archive is too old. Every report names its imagery and, where the source gives it, the capture date. New captures depend on satellite availability and weather, so no capture date is ever guaranteed.
  - q: Do you need drones?
    a: Not usually. Satellite mapping needs no drone permit, no launch sites and no one on site, and it covers a whole corridor. Drone surveys add detail where a stretch needs it, subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires. See [drone law in Tanzania](/tz/drone-regulations).
  - q: Can you tell whether a structure is lawful?
    a: No. We map what stands where, and when it first appears in the imagery. Whether it is inside a wayleave lawfully, predates the wayleave or valuation, or is compensable is for your land team, the valuers, the Commissioner for Lands and the courts to decide. The register gives them a dated, located list to work from.
  - q: How is personal data handled?
    a: Our registers describe structures and ground, not people, and carry no names. Lists of project-affected persons and drone frames that show people are personal data under the Personal Data Protection Act, so each contract says who processes them, where they are stored and how any transfer outside Tanzania is covered (ss.31–32).
  - q: Is the register a cadastral survey?
    a: No. It is a mapping and monitoring record built from imagery. Title boundaries, beacons and survey plans stay with land surveyors; the register shows them, your wayleave officers and your valuers where to look.

::::section{id="wayleaves" eyebrow="Why a dated record matters" title="Wayleaves bind everyone on the land" lead="Tanzanian law protects pipeline, power-line, road and railway corridors in several statutes. They share one practical need: a dated, measured record of what stands inside the corridor."}
:::cards{cols="4"}
:::card{title="A wayleave runs with the land" icon="scale" eyebrow="Land Act, Cap. 113"}
A wayleave created for the Government, a local authority, a public authority or a corporate body binds every occupier, under a right of occupancy, customary law or "as a trespasser". The Commissioner for Lands can order an obstruction or encroachment on a public right of way removed, and failing to comply is an offence (ss.186–187).
:::
:::card{title="Pipelines and gas installations" icon="pipeline" eyebrow="Petroleum Act, Cap. 392"}
A licensee acquires wayleaves around existing and future gas infrastructure and petroleum installations, and owners on or bordering a wayleave "shall not interfere" with the licensee's rights (s.186).
:::
:::card{title="Power lines" icon="power" eyebrow="Electricity Act, Cap. 131"}
A licensee acquires a wayleave for its electricity facilities, owners on or bordering it must not interfere (s.37), and the licensee must remove or trim high vegetation that threatens safe operation (s.36(3)).
:::
:::card{title="Roads and railways" icon="rail" eyebrow="Roads Act · Railways Act"}
The road reserve is "exclusive for the use of road development and expansion" (Roads Act, 2007, s.29), and the railway reserve runs 30 m either side of the track centre line (Railways Act, s.3).
:::
:::

Dates also decide compensation. The High Court has held that developments made after the valuation and notice are not compensable, and IFC Performance Standard 5 sets a cut-off date for lender-financed projects. When a structure first appeared can therefore matter as much as where it stands. [Wayleave law in Tanzania, explained](/tz/wayleave-law)

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Satellite first, drones where needed" title="Two routes to the picture, and the rules for each"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Satellite and your own imagery.** A survey starts from your route file and dated satellite imagery, open building datasets or the orthophotos you already hold. Satellite mapping needs no drone permit and can start straight away, however long the corridor.

**Drone detail.** Where you need more detail than satellite imagery shows, we plan drone surveys under Tanzania's 2026 drone regulations, subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires. Those regulations require an RPAS Operator Certificate from a company with a principal place of business registered in Tanzania, a security clearance and security programme, security-vetted pilots and TCAA's authorisation for each operation, with a risk assessment submitted at least 14 days ahead.

Afridrone is working towards Tanzanian drone approvals. Every drone proposal names the company that will fly and the approvals its flights rely on, so your team can check them.

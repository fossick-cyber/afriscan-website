::::section{id="public-procurement" tone="alt" eyebrow="Public tenders" title="The Public Procurement Act, 2023" lead="Act No. 10 of 2023, English version GN 813 of 20 September 2024. Public procuring entities, state utilities among them, run their procurement through NeST, the National e-Procurement System of Tanzania."}
| Section | What it says |
|---|---|
| s.53(3) | A foreign tenderer selected as lowest evaluated or best ranked must register with the appropriate professional statutory body and prove registration as an approved supplier, contractor or consultant |
| s.56 | A margin of preference for Tanzanian goods, contractors and consultants: Tanzania-incorporated, with the majority of paid-up share capital owned by the Government or citizens |
| s.57 | Where a Tanzanian public body funds the procurement, contracts below regulatory thresholds are reserved for local persons or firms |
| s.58 | Foreign firms in non-emergency consultancies must include local firms and experts: up to 15 per cent of the score for local firms, and up to 10 per cent for national experts |
| s.61 | Procuring entities set aside part of their annual volume for local individuals and firms; in a capacity-building joint venture with a foreign firm, "the local firm shall be the leading firm" |
| s.62 | Above a threshold, foreign tenderers for works and non-consultancy services form joint ventures with, or subcontract, local firms at a prescribed percentage, and attach the agreement to the tender |
| s.63 | Local firms under ss.58 and 61 must have share capital "wholly owned by citizens" |
| s.64 | Set-asides for special groups: women, youth, the elderly and persons with special needs |
| s.66 | Tender documents are in Kiswahili or English |
| s.73 | Procurement is "implemented and reported through the electronic public procurement system", which PPRA runs as [NeST](https://nest.go.tz/) |

Source: the [Public Procurement Act, 2023](https://www.ppra.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1736861173-THE%20PUBLIC%20PROCUREMENT%20ACT.pdf), on the PPRA website. Whether mapping from imagery is procured as a consultancy or a non-consultancy service, and which thresholds apply, is for your procurement team to decide under the Act and its regulations.

::::section{id="local-content" eyebrow="Oil, gas and mining" title="The local-content rules for this kind of service"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Petroleum {#petroleum}

Under the [Petroleum Act, Cap. 392](https://www.ewura.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1786364531-The%20Petroleum%20Act.pdf), s.219, licence holders, contractors and subcontractors "shall give preference to goods which are produced or available in Tanzania and services which are rendered by Tanzanian citizens or local companies". Goods and services not available locally are supplied by a company in a joint venture with a local company holding at least 25 per cent, or as the regulations provide. A "local company" is wholly owned by Tanzanian citizens, or is in a joint venture or partnership with citizens holding at least 15 per cent (s.219(9)). Licence holders file a procurement plan of at least five years with PURA (s.219(4)), and PURA runs a common qualification system for upstream suppliers.

### Mining {#mining}

Under the Mining (Local Content) Regulations, 2018, as amended, an indigenous Tanzanian company has at least 20 per cent citizen equity and Tanzanians in at least 80 per cent of executive and senior management posts and all other posts. A non-indigenous company supplying a contractor, a subcontractor or a licensee must form a joint venture giving an indigenous Tanzanian company at least 20 per cent of the equity (reg 8(6)), and larger contracts are notified to the Mining Commission (reg 16). Each mining proposal sets out how it meets reg 8(6): the indigenous Tanzanian company and its equity share. Check the current text with the [Mining Commission](https://www.tumemadini.go.tz/publications/regulations/).

::::section{id="data" tone="alt" eyebrow="Personal data" title="The Personal Data Protection Act, Cap. 44" lead="English version GN 395B of 13 June 2023. Personal data are *taarifa binafsi*, and the regulator is the Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC, *Tume ya Ulinzi wa Taarifa Binafsi*). It applies in Mainland Tanzania and Tanzania Zanzibar, except for non-Union matters in Zanzibar."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
- **Registration first.** "A person shall not collect or process personal data without being registered as a data controller or a data processor under this Act" (s.14(1)). Private organisations register online with a BRELA certificate, a TIN certificate and an audited financial report, and introduce their Data Protection Officer by letter ([PDPC registration](https://pdpc.go.tz/services/registration/)).
- **Security.** Controllers and processors keep personal data secure (s.27).
- **Transfers outside Tanzania.** To states with adequate protection (s.31), or otherwise under s.32; the PDPC issues [cross-border transfer permits](https://pdpc.go.tz/services/cross-border-data-transfer-permit/).
- **Enforcement.** Enforcement and penalty notices (ss.45–46) and administrative fines (s.47), with offences for unlawful disclosure (s.60) and for unlawful destruction or alteration of personal data (s.61).
- **Inspections.** On 28 August 2026 the PDPC began compliance inspections of data controllers and processors, covering registration, the Data Protection Officer's duties, how data are collected and stored, transfers under ss.31–32 and security under s.27(1).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" icon="lock" title="What counts as personal data here"}
Building footprints on their own are generally not personal data, in our reading. Drone frames that show identifiable people, lists of project-affected persons and household data are. Our registers describe structures and carry no names, so the PAP list and the census stay with your team, and each contract says who processes what and where.

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Drone approvals" title="Who flies, and what they need"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Under the Civil Aviation (Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems) Regulations, 2026 (GN 268 of 2026), commercial drone work needs an RPAS Operator Certificate, which TCAA issues only to an applicant with a principal place of business registered in Tanzania, a security clearance and an approved security programme. Each operation needs TCAA's authorisation, and a Category B risk assessment goes in at least 14 days ahead.

Drone surveys are subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires. Afridrone is working towards Tanzanian drone approvals, and every drone proposal names the company that will fly, with its ROC, operations specifications and the approvals its flights rely on.

::::section{id="scope" tone="alt" eyebrow="Where we work" title="Satellite everywhere, field and drone work where it is safe and approved"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Satellite mapping covers the whole of Tanzania with no one on site. Field and drone work is planned job by job, against the approvals each job needs and current government travel advice.

Where current travel advice is against travel to an area, our work there is satellite-only, and we check that advice again when each job is planned. Military sites, State House, prisons and other protected places are left out of every deliverable.

::::section{id="checklist" eyebrow="For procurement teams" title="What to ask any supplier, including us" lead="Ask for these in the proposal. Where a supplier cannot show one, the proposal should say so and how it will be met."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="The contracting company"}
Its name, where it is registered and its principal place of business; for work inside Tanzania, the BRELA certificate and TIN of the Tanzanian company involved.
:::
:::step{title="Local-content position"}
For petroleum work, how the contract meets the Petroleum Act, s.219; for mining, the indigenous Tanzanian company and its equity share under reg 8(6); for public tenders, the local inclusion the Public Procurement Act, 2023 scores.
:::
:::step{title="Registrations for your tender"}
NeST registration for public tenders, and registration with the professional statutory body after award where s.53(3) applies.
:::
:::step{title="Personal data"}
The PDPC registration of whoever processes personal data, its Data Protection Officer, where the data are stored, the retention period and the basis for any transfer outside Tanzania (ss.31–32).
:::
:::step{title="Drone approvals"}
The company that will fly, its ROC and operations specifications, security clearance, registered drones, pilots with Tanzanian remote pilot licences and insurance, as the drone-law checklist sets out.
:::
:::step{title="Imagery"}
Which imagery the deliverables rest on and its capture dates; map-service basemaps are never the dated record.
:::
:::step{title="Review and limits"}
Who reviews results before delivery, and what the survey cannot show: people, underground works, leaks or anything under optical cloud.

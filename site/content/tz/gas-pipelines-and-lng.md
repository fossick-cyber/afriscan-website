faq:
  - q: Do the Petroleum Act's safety zones apply to our gas transmission line?
    a: No. Section 203 sits in Part VI(a) of the Petroleum Act, on upstream operations, so its safety zones surround upstream facilities such as wells and upstream processing. A transmission pipeline is protected by the wayleave the licensee acquires under s.186 and by the s.240 offences. We register structures against whichever applies, using the zone or wayleave widths you give us.
  - q: Can you keep our own facilities out of the encroachment register?
    a: Yes. Well pads, field pipelines, processing plants, valve stations, camps and yards are part of the asset. We record them as the operator's installations, never as encroachment, and list them separately only if you ask.
  - q: Can you find leaks from a gas line?
    a: No. Imagery cannot show a leak or the condition of a pipe. We map what is visible at the surface around the line, such as new structures, fresh digging, spoil and new tracks, so your field teams know where to check.
  - q: What can imagery tell us about the LNG site since it was compensated?
    a: Where archived satellite imagery exists, a history of the site and its surroundings shows what was built, cleared or changed, and roughly when, from the years around compensation to today. How much archive exists varies by place and year, and the history is not a title, tenure or valuation check.
  - q: Do you work offshore?
    a: No. Our registers cover onshore land, meaning the gas fields' onshore facilities, landfall sites, pipelines and plants. Offshore wells, platforms and the sea surface are outside what we map.
  - q: Can you survey gas distribution routes in the city?
    a: Yes, from satellite imagery. Urban routes need the finest imagery and the most review, because structures are dense and works are small. Drone flights in public areas need the local authority's permission as well as TCAA's approvals (GN 268 of 2026, reg 117), so satellite comes first.

::::section{id="chain" eyebrow="The gas chain, on the ground" title="Three kinds of land, three sets of rules" lead="Natural gas in Tanzania moves from upstream fields through processing plants and high-pressure lines to users, and each stage sits on land protected in a different way."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Wells and upstream processing" icon="target" eyebrow="Upstream · PURA"}
The Petroleum Act puts "a safety zone surrounding every facility used for carrying out petroleum activities, unless otherwise determined by PURA", and "a person shall not carry unauthorised activity in the safety zones" (s.203(5)). The zones sit in the Part on upstream operations, so they surround upstream facilities such as wells and upstream processing.
:::
:::card{title="High-pressure transmission lines" icon="pipeline" eyebrow="Midstream · EWURA"}
A licensee secures rights of way for gas pipelines, subject to the Gas Utilisation Master Plan and in consultation with EWURA (s.185), and acquires wayleaves around existing and future gas infrastructure that owners on or bordering them must not interfere with (s.186).
:::
:::card{title="The LNG host area" icon="building" eyebrow="Land and lenders"}
The site for a planned LNG plant on the southern coast was selected in 2013, and its land title holder has completed the compensation process. The project is awaiting a final investment decision; the land around it has kept changing.
:::
:::

Two regulators divide the work. PURA regulates and monitors the petroleum upstream subsector for Mainland Tanzania (Petroleum Act, s.11), and EWURA has "regulatory powers in respect to midstream and downstream petroleum and natural gas activities" (s.29(1)). Altering, removing or connecting to a pipeline or gas installation without the licensee's consent is an offence at every stage (s.240).

::::section{id="upstream" tone="alt" eyebrow="Upstream" title="Structures inside the safety zones around wells and upstream plants"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
PURA determines the extent of each safety zone, and the Act forbids unauthorised activity inside it. The operator's land and HSE teams then need to know what stands in the zone and when it appeared. We draw the zone from the facility footprints and the distances in PURA's determination or your own licence conditions, and list each structure inside it with its distance to the facility, then compare between dated surveys.

A new field brings new wells, field pipelines, a processing plant and access roads. A dated baseline before construction, from archive imagery and a new survey, records the structures, cleared ground and tracks around each site, so later questions about what was there first have an answer.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="lng" tone="alt" eyebrow="The LNG host area" title="Compensated, and waiting: what has changed since" lead="When a site is compensated years before construction, the gap between the compensation date and the investment decision is where land questions build up."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**An imagery history since selection.** From archived satellite imagery and open datasets, we document what was built, cleared or changed on and around the site, and roughly when, from the years around its selection and compensation to today. How much archive exists varies by place and year; the report says which dates it rests on.

**Settlement change around the site.** Year-by-year trends in built-up land around the site boundary, access routes and nearby villages show where settlement pressure is growing. A structure register on a current dated scene gives the count.

**A fresh baseline for an investment decision.** If an investment decision brings a lenders' environmental and social review, one of its questions is one the imagery can answer: what stands on and around the site now, compared with the compensation record. A dated register on the day it is needed supports that review; it does not replace the census or the valuation.

**Corridors to the site.** If the project goes ahead, access roads and pipeline corridors to the site will need route options. Structure counts along each alternative let the land impact be weighed before a route is fixed.
::::
::::col
### Coastal land {#coastal-land}

Under the Land Act, land within 60 m of a beach or coast, or of a river bank or inland lake shore, is among the categories the Minister can declare hazardous land by Gazette notice (s.7). Along a coastline, check whether a declaration applies before a baseline is scoped.

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Drone detail near gas facilities" title="Satellite first near plants and wells"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Around wells, processing plants and valve stations, satellite screening covers the zone without anyone on site. Where a drone survey adds detail, it is subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires: an operator with a Tanzanian principal place of business and an RPAS Operator Certificate, TCAA's authorisation for the operation and, for Category B work, a risk assessment submitted at least 14 days ahead. Plants and wells are structures, so the 50 m rule of GN 268 reg 30 applies unless TCAA approves closer work.
::::
::::col

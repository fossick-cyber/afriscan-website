faq:
  - q: How wide is a road reserve in Tanzania?
    a: For trunk and regional roads, the Roads Management Regulations, 2009 (GN 21 of 2009), as quoted by the High Court in 2022, set 60 m, "consisting of thirty metres from either side of the centre of the road". A Roads Management (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 (GN 528 of 2025) is listed on TanzLII, so confirm the current width for your road class with the road authority. For other classes, or a wider reserve on a particular road, send us the width and we measure against it.
  - q: How wide is the railway reserve?
    a: The Railways Act defines the railway reserve as the land on both sides of the track "measuring thirty metres in width from the centre line of the track reserved for safety purpose" (s.3). Its Second Schedule adds a 30 m sidings reserve and bridge reserves of 60 m from the bank edge on either side and 200 m from the centre of the structure.
  - q: Does a title deed or building permit override the road reserve?
    a: In a 2022 case summarised on TanzLII, the High Court held a demolition within the statutory road reserve lawful and held that title documents and permits did not displace the reserve ([2022] TZHCLandD 282). For railways, the leading cases are still before the Court of Appeal, so take advice on your own facts.
  - q: Can you show whether a plot was inside the reserve when it was granted?
    a: We can show what the imagery shows on each available date and roughly when a structure first appeared, measured against the reserve line. When the plot was granted, and on what terms, comes from the land registry and the grant itself; the courts decide what follows.
  - q: Can you detect cable theft on a fibre route?
    a: No. Imagery cannot see a cable or whether it has been cut. It shows excavation, trenches, spoil and construction along the route between dates, which tells your patrols where to look first.
  - q: Can a drone survey fly along a public road?
    a: Only with planning. GN 268 of 2026 keeps drones 50 m from people, vehicles and structures not under the operator's control unless TCAA approves otherwise (reg 30), and flying in a public area needs the local authority's permission first (reg 117). Drone surveys are subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires, so satellite comes first along roads.

|---|---|---|
| Road reserve (*hifadhi ya barabara*) | "Exclusive for the use of road development and expansion"; other uses must not hinder future road use | [Roads Act, 2007, Cap. 167](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2007/13/eng@2021-07-14), s.29(1) |

### Measured from the centre line {#centre-line}

The 2009 Regulations, as quoted by the High Court in 2022, set the trunk and regional road reserve at 30 m either side of the centre line, and the Railways Act, s.3, sets the railway reserve at 30 m either side of the track centre line. The register measures each structure's distance from the centre line you supply and reports whether it falls inside the reserve or in an outer band you set.

**Check the road width before you rely on it.** A Roads Management (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 (GN 528 of 2025) is listed on TanzLII. We measure against the width you confirm for your road class, and against any wider reserve set for a particular road.

::::section{id="dates" eyebrow="Why dates decide reserve cases" title="What the courts have looked at"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Roads.** In a 2022 case, as summarised on TanzLII, the High Court held a demolition within the statutory road reserve lawful, and held that title documents and permits did not displace the reserve ([2022] TZHCLandD 282). On that summary, the statutory reserve prevailed over the paperwork.

**Railways.** A 2023 High Court decision that occupants within the railway reserve were not entitled to compensation is under appeal (Civil Appeal No. 61 of 2024). In 2021 the Court of Appeal held that the land-allocating authority must be joined where the validity of a title and its inclusion in the railway reserve are in issue ([2021] TZCA 198). Neither settles the compensation question.
::::
::::col
**What survives both.** Whether a plot was inside the reserve when it was granted, and when a structure went up, are evidential questions. Archive satellite imagery, where it exists, shows roughly when a structure first appears; a register on a dated scene shows exactly what stood inside the reserve bands on that date.

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Supports, never decides"}
The register shows what the imagery shows: where each structure stands, its distance to the centre line and the dates it is visible. Titles, grants, permits and compensation are for the land registry, the road or railway authority and the courts.
:::

::::section{id="register" tone="alt" eyebrow="What we map along the reserve" title="Structures and works in the reserve bands, then what changed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We take the centre line of the road or track, or the reserve polygon, buffer it in its UTM zone and list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the centre line and its band: inside the reserve, and in any outer band you set. Each 500 m stretch is rated for encroachment density, high where more than five structures stand inside the widest band, medium for one to five, low for none, so your reserve officers know which stretches to visit first.

After the baseline, the reserve is re-surveyed on a schedule agreed with you. New and removed structures between dated surveys are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views. Cleared plots, fresh excavations and new tracks across the reserve are flagged the same way.

Busy road frontages, where structures are dense and small, get the finest imagery available and the closest review.

::::section{id="new-alignments" eyebrow="New alignments and land acquisition" title="Before the valuation, and on the cut-off date"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
A standard-gauge railway lot is acquiring land, with valuation, compensation and grave relocation along the route (*utwaaji wa ardhi*, *uhamishaji wa makaburi*), and the World Bank approved a second intermodal and rail development project for Tanzania in March 2024. Each new alignment or widening starts with a count of what it would affect.

We count the structures along each alternative alignment within the same bands, so route engineers and the land team can compare options on the land they would affect. Once the alignment is fixed, a dated baseline before the valuation, and another on the cut-off date, show what stood in the corridor and what appeared in between. The comparison is one input to route selection, not a full alternatives assessment.

::::section{id="fibre" tone="alt" eyebrow="Fibre and buried cables" title="Excavation and construction along buried routes"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Fibre-optic cables are buried along many linear routes, pipeline rights of way among them, and the national ICT broadband backbone is being extended by 183 km towards the Democratic Republic of the Congo. A buried cable is most at risk from works nobody told its owner about.

Between dated surveys we flag fresh digging, trenches, spoil heaps and earthworks along the route, and new construction beside it, so your patrols know where to go first. Imagery does not detect cable theft or cuts: it shows excavation and construction at the surface, to guide the people who check.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What imagery can and cannot see"}
- Fresh excavation, trenches and spoil along the route
- New structures and tracks beside it
- Not the cable itself, a cut or a theft
- Not works hidden under tree cover or roofs

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Drone detail along roads and railways" title="Satellite first along busy corridors"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
A road carries vehicles and people who are not under the drone operator's control, so GN 268 of 2026 keeps flights 50 m from them unless TCAA approves greater heights and distances for the operation (reg 30), and flying in a public area needs the local authority's permission first (reg 117). A railway adds its own operator's safety rules. Drone surveys are subject to the TCAA approvals and security clearances each job requires; Afridrone is working towards Tanzanian drone approvals, and every drone proposal names the company that will fly.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Plan the permissions early"}
Along a road or railway, the launch and recovery consents of reg 86, the local authority's permission for public areas and the corridor owner's safety rules are part of the plan. Satellite screening needs none of them.

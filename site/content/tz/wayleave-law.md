faq:
  - q: Does a wayleave bind people who settled on the land after it was created?
    a: Yes. Under the Land Act, a wayleave created for the Government, a local authority, a public authority or a corporate body attaches to and runs with the servient land and binds all occupiers, whether under a right of occupancy, customary law, as a successor in title, "or as a trespasser" (s.161 of the consolidated Act; s.151(3) of the 1999 text). Whether a particular structure is lawful, and whether anything is payable for it, is decided under the Act and the valuation rules, not by the map.
  - q: What can the Commissioner for Lands do about a structure on a public right of way?
    a: Where the Commissioner is satisfied that a public right of way has been wrongfully obstructed or encroached on, he may order the person responsible to remove the obstruction or encroachment within a time of not less than fourteen days, and may take the steps necessary if the order is not complied with (s.186). There are rights of reconsideration and appeal. Not removing the obstruction or ceasing the encroachment in the time specified is an offence (s.187).
  - q: Is there a statutory wayleave width for pipelines or power lines?
    a: We found none in the texts we read. The Petroleum Act (s.186) and the Electricity Act (s.37) require the licensee to acquire a wayleave, so its width comes from the licensee's own acquisition, agreement or standard. Road and railway reserves are different, because their widths are set in the Roads Management Regulations, 2009 and the Railways Act. Send us the widths from your own licence or agreement, and we measure against them.
  - q: Do the Petroleum Act's safety zones cover pipelines?
    a: No. The s.203 safety zones, whose extent PURA determines, sit in Part VI(a), "Health and Safety in Petroleum Upstream Operations", so they surround upstream facilities such as wells and upstream processing. For midstream pipelines and pump stations, the hooks are the s.186 wayleave duty and the s.240 offences.
  - q: Can a landowner ask for a power line to be moved?
    a: Yes. Under the Electricity Act, s.36(4), EWURA may, on the landowner's application, order the licensee to alter the position of a line or remove it. The licensee in turn must compensate the property owner for any wayleave granted, with disputes settled as if the wayleave had been obtained under the Land Acquisition Act (s.37(3)).
  - q: Why does the date of a structure matter so much?
    a: Because compensation follows the valuation. The Valuation and Valuers Registration Act treats compensation as paid promptly if paid within six months after the valuation is approved (s.52), and High Court decisions summarised on TanzLII hold that developments made after the valuation and notice are not compensable. On lender-financed projects, IFC Performance Standard 5 adds a publicly established cut-off date for eligibility. A dated record of what stood where, and when, is what these rules are applied to.

## Where the law applies {#scope}

The Land Act, Cap. 113, and the Village Land Act, Cap. 114, govern land in Mainland Tanzania. Land is not a Union matter, so Zanzibar has its own land laws, which this guide does not cover. The Land Act's sections are numbered here as in the TanzLII consolidation of 30 June 2025, with the 1999 numbering alongside where it differs, because older judgments and textbooks use it.

Two categories of land matter along corridors:

- **Reserved land** (s.6(1) of the 1999 text) includes land reserved under the forests, national parks, wildlife, marine parks, town planning, highways and land acquisition laws, "(c) land reserved for public utilities", and land the Minister declares hazardous by order.
- **Hazardous land** (s.7) includes mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands and offshore islands, and "(d) land within sixty metres of a river bank, shoreline of an inland lake, beach or coast". A declaration is made by Gazette notice with a date at least 60 days after publication. Coastal sites and river crossings are where this applies to pipelines and power lines.

## The Land Act: public rights of way and wayleaves {#land-act}

Part XI of the Land Act lets the Minister create public rights of way. One created for the benefit of the Government, a local authority, a public authority or any corporate body is "a wayleave". It "shall attach to and run with the servient land … and shall be binding on all occupiers … whether under a right of occupancy or a derivative right thereof, or under customary law or as a successor in title … or as a trespasser", and it authorises entry to build, maintain and "pass and repass".

| Step | Section (2025 · 1999) | What the Act says |
|---|---|---|
| Creation | s.161 · s.151 | The Minister creates the right of way; for a public or corporate beneficiary it is a wayleave that runs with the land and binds every occupier |
| Application | s.162 · s.152 | Applications go to the Commissioner. Notice goes to occupiers under a right of occupancy (including customary pastoral rights), local government authorities, people in actual occupation in urban and peri-urban areas and other interested persons, with publicity "along the route" |
| Determination | s.164 · s.154 | Considered no sooner than 90 days after the notices are served, by inquiry, referral to the local authority or negotiation. The Minister's order must "delineate the route", is gazetted and publicised, and comes "into force thirty days after it has been published in the Gazette". Appeal to the High Court on a point of law within six weeks |
| Registration | s.165 · s.155 | The route is recorded on certificates of occupancy |
| Compensation | s.166 · s.156 | Payable "for the use of land … and, with respect to a wayleave, in addition … for any damage suffered in respect of trees, crops and buildings", including damage from preliminary survey work, and paid "promptly" |
| Unlawful occupation | s.185 · s.175 | Occupying, building, clearing, digging, cultivating, grazing or cutting timber without right; a notice gives the occupier 60 days to show cause. The factors weighed include whether the occupation "is preventing some necessary or desirable development or public works" |
| Obstruction | s.186 · s.176 | Where the Commissioner is satisfied that there has been "any wrongful obstruction of or encroachment on any public right of way", he may order its removal within a time "not less than fourteen days", and may take "any steps which may be necessary" if the order is not complied with; reconsideration and appeal rights apply |
| Offence | s.187 | Obstructing or encroaching on a public right of way and not removing the obstruction or ceasing the encroachment within the time specified is an offence |

**What this means for a record.** A removal order names a person, an obstruction and a place. The Commissioner has to be satisfied that it is there; a show-cause notice gives an occupier 60 days, and a removal order at least 14. A dated, located list of the structures and works inside the route, measured to the delineated line, is the kind of record those steps are worked from. It does not replace the notices, the inquiry or the hearing.

## Village land {#village-land}

Where a corridor crosses village land, the Village Land Act, Cap. 114, applies. The President may transfer village land to general or reserved land "for public interest", which "shall include investments, of national interest" (s.4):

- the Minister gazettes a notice with a date at least 90 days away, and the village council informs the villagers affected;
- representations may be made;
- for land under 250 ha, the village assembly approves or refuses; over 250 ha, the Minister decides after the recommendations of the village assembly, the village council and the district council;
- "No village transfer land shall be transferred" until the type, amount, method and timing of compensation are agreed, or the High Court approves an interim payment (s.4(8)).

The 90-day notice and the compensation agreement give a transfer two natural dates for a structure baseline: before the notice, and when compensation is agreed.

## Power lines: the Electricity Act {#electricity}

The [Electricity Act, Cap. 131 R.E. 2023](https://www.ewura.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1786363737-The%20Electricity%20Act.pdf), sets the wayleave duties behind every transmission and distribution line (*njia ya kusafirisha umeme*; the wayleave itself is *haki ya njia ya umeme*):

- **s.36(1):** compulsory access to private property on written notice to the landowner and EWURA;
- **s.36(3):** "A licensee shall remove or trim trees or other high vegetation which may pose threat to the safe and reliable operation of his facilities";
- **s.36(4):** on the landowner's application, EWURA may order the licensee to alter the position of a line or remove it;
- **s.37:** "(1) The licensee shall acquire a wayleave for electricity facilities. (2) Owners of property on or bordering a wayleave shall not interfere with a licensee's rights related thereto. (3) A licensee shall compensate a property owner for any wayleave granted", with disputes settled as if the wayleave had been obtained under the Land Acquisition Act;
- **s.38:** compulsory acquisition through the Minister for lands, with EWURA's consent.

The electricity activities regulations of 2024 (GN 679 of 2024) ask a licence applicant for a wayleave, or proof that the process of obtaining one has started (*haki ya njia au uthibitisho wa kuanza mchakato*). We found no wayleave width by voltage in the Act or the regulations we read, so the licensee's own standard and acquisitions decide it.

Power-line wayleave disputes reach the High Court. In 2014 it granted an interim injunction against demolishing a house pending trial of a "triable issue as to encroachment and entitlement to compensation" ([2014] TZHC 2736), and in 2025 it upheld a supplementary valuation as the basis for compensation, given "fraud suspicions in the initial assessment" ([2025] TZHC 3298; both as summarised on TanzLII).

## Pipelines and petroleum installations: the Petroleum Act {#petroleum}

The [Petroleum Act, Cap. 392 R.E. 2023](https://www.ewura.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1786364531-The%20Petroleum%20Act.pdf), puts the wayleave duty on the licensee:

- **s.185:** subject to the Gas Utilisation Master Plan and in consultation with EWURA, a licensee takes "all necessary measures to secure acquisition of rights of way for laying, operation and repair of gas pipelines";
- **s.186:** "(1) A licensee shall, in consultation with the relevant authorities, acquire wayleave around existing and future gas infrastructure and petroleum installations. (2) Owners of property on or bordering a wayleave shall not interfere with a licensee's acquired rights." Compensation disputes go under the Land Acquisition Act (s.186(3));
- **s.240:** offences for altering, removing or connecting to "any pipeline or gas installations without consent of the licensee", including attempts and assistance. Under s.240(4), altering, removing or connecting to a pipeline without consent, sabotage, or endangering the system is "an offence of economic and organised crime" under the Economic and Organised Crime Control Act, Cap. 200.

**Safety zones are upstream only.** Section 203 provides that "there shall be a safety zone surrounding every facility used for carrying out petroleum activities, unless otherwise determined by PURA", and that "(5) A person shall not carry unauthorised activity in the safety zones". It sits in Part VI(a), "Health and Safety in Petroleum Upstream Operations" (ss.199–206); downstream health and safety is Part VI(b). So s.203 surrounds wells and upstream processing, not midstream pipelines or pump stations.

**Pipelines with their own Act.** One cross-border products pipeline has its own statute, the [Tanzania-Zambia Pipeline Act, Cap. 40](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/1966/65/eng@2023-12-31), which gives the power to "construct, place, maintain, alter or remove pipeline in, on, over, under, along or across, any land, building, road, railway" (s.5). We found no strip width in the Tanzanian texts we read.

## Road reserves: the Roads Act, 2007 {#roads}

The road reserve, *hifadhi ya barabara*, rests on the [Roads Act, 2007, Cap. 167](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2007/13/eng@2021-07-14):

- **s.29(1):** "The road reserve is exclusive for the use of road development and expansion"; other uses must not hinder future road use;
- **s.23:** the road authority may give written notice to "any person obstructing or encroaching on any public road to remove or abate such obstruction";
- the Minister prescribes road widths by Gazette notice.

**The width.** The Roads Management Regulations, 2009 (GN 21 of 2009), as quoted by the High Court in 2022, set: "(b) Trunk roads and regional roads sixty metres consisting of thirty metres from either side of the centre of the road". A Roads Management (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 (GN 528 of 2025) is listed on TanzLII; confirm the current width for your road class with the road authority before relying on 60 m. In that 2022 case, as summarised on TanzLII, the High Court held a demolition within the statutory road reserve lawful, and held that title documents and permits did not displace the reserve ([2022] TZHCLandD 282).

## Railway reserves: the Railways Act {#railways}

The [Railways Act, Cap. 170](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2017/10/eng@2025-07-01) (consolidation of 1 July 2025) sets the reserve in the Act itself:

- **s.3** defines the "railway reserve" as the land on both sides of the track "measuring thirty metres in width from the centre line of the track reserved for safety purpose";
- **s.27** forbids anyone to "farm, erect any building, structure or execute any works on a railway strip" without authority;
- **the Second Schedule** (under s.26) defines a mainline right-of-way reserve of 30 m either side, a 30 m railway sidings reserve, and bridge reserves of 60 m from the bank edge on either side and 200 m from the centre of the structure.

A separate Act, the Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act, Cap. 143, governs the cross-border line it names.

**Cases still moving.** A 2023 High Court decision that occupants within the railway reserve were trespassers, not entitled to compensation, is under appeal (Civil Appeal No. 61 of 2024; the Court of Appeal allowed an amended notice of appeal on 30 June 2026). In 2021 the Court of Appeal held that the land-allocating authority must be joined where the validity of a title and its inclusion in the railway reserve are in issue ([2021] TZCA 198). Neither settles the law on compensation. What survives both is practical: whether a plot was inside the reserve when it was granted, and when a structure went up, are live evidential questions.

## Compensation, valuation and resettlement {#compensation}

We found no stand-alone Tanzanian resettlement statute. The framework is:

- the Land Act, s.166 (compensation for wayleaves);
- the [Land Acquisition Act, Cap. 118](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/1967/47/eng@2019-11-30);
- the Village Land Act, s.4, including s.4(8);
- the [Valuation and Valuers Registration Act, Cap. 138](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2016/7/eng@2023-12-31), whose s.52, "Validity of valuation", defines "prompt payment of compensation" as "payment of compensation within six months after" approval of the valuation;
- the [Graves (Removal) Act, Cap. 72](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/1969/9/eng@2002-07-31), where graves have to be moved (*uhamishaji wa makaburi*).

**Dates decide compensation.** TanzLII's summaries of a High Court land case decided in 2016 and 2020 ([2016] TZHC 2292; [2020] TZHC 1972) record that the "valuation date governs applicable rates", that "post-valuation developments" are "not compensable if made after valuation and notice", and that 6 per cent statutory interest is due for delayed payment under the Land Acquisition Act, s.15. The judgments' full text was not available to us; read them before relying on them.

Tanzania Railways Corporation (TRC) puts the process plainly in a news release on railway land acquisition: "Kwa mujibu wa sheria ya ardhi ya mwaka 1999 na sheria ya tathmini ya mwaka 2016, maeneo yote yanayopitiwa na mradi … yatafanyiwa tathmini, kutwaliwa na wamiliki wa maeneo hayo watalipwa" (under the 1999 land law and the 2016 valuation law, all areas the project crosses will be valued and acquired, and their owners paid).

On lender-financed projects, [IFC Performance Standard 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf) adds its own rule: a census, and a cut-off date for eligibility that is "well documented and disseminated" (para 12), with no duty to compensate "those who encroach on the project area after the cut-off date for eligibility, provided the cut-off date has been clearly established and made public" (para 23). See [valuation and cut-off baselines in Tanzania](/tz/valuation-cut-off-baselines).

::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="Under the line, between inspections" lead="Transmission wayleaves cross farmland, bush and the edges of towns, and the land under a line changes between inspections. What changes decides where a maintenance crew, a wayleave officer or a valuer goes next."}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="“There are houses under the line.”" icon="houses"}
Houses, shops and outbuildings can go up inside the wayleave, especially where a line passes a village or a trading centre. Section 37(2) says owners on or bordering a wayleave shall not interfere with the licensee's rights; a dated list shows where each structure stands and since when.
:::
:::card{title="“The trees are growing into the wayleave.”" icon="tree"}
Section 36(3) puts the duty on the licensee: "remove or trim trees or other high vegetation which may pose threat to the safe and reliable operation" of its facilities. Knowing where tall vegetation stands lets crews go there first.
:::
:::card{title="“A landowner wants the line moved.”" icon="scale"}
On a landowner's application, EWURA may order the licensee to alter the position of a line or remove it (s.36(4)). The first questions are what stood where, and when it appeared.
:::
:::card{title="“The valuation is disputed.”" icon="file-check"}
Wayleave disputes reach the High Court: an interim injunction against demolishing a house pending trial of the encroachment and compensation issues ([2014] TZHC 2736), and a supplementary valuation upheld where the first assessment was in doubt ([2025] TZHC 3298, as summarised on TanzLII).
:::
:::

In July 2026 the Deputy Minister for Energy told utility managers in Mara region to identify every place where power lines pass over homes that are not yet connected, so that those households can be connected. A structure register of what stands under each line is where that list starts.

::::section{id="law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The law in Tanzania" title="The wayleave duties behind every line" lead="A summary of the public rules as of 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice; our wayleave-law guide has the detail and the sources."}
| Instrument | What it sets |
|---|---|
| [Electricity Act, Cap. 131 R.E. 2023](https://www.ewura.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1786363737-The%20Electricity%20Act.pdf), s.36(1) | Compulsory access to private property on written notice to the landowner and EWURA |
| Electricity Act, s.36(3) | The licensee "shall remove or trim trees or other high vegetation which may pose threat to the safe and reliable operation of his facilities" |
| Electricity Act, s.36(4) | On the landowner's application, EWURA may order the licensee to alter the position of a line or remove it |
| Electricity Act, s.37 | "The licensee shall acquire a wayleave for electricity facilities"; owners on or bordering it "shall not interfere"; the licensee compensates the property owner, with disputes settled as if the wayleave had been obtained under the Land Acquisition Act |
| Electricity Act, s.38 | Compulsory acquisition through the Minister for lands, with EWURA's consent |
| Electricity activities regulations, 2024 (GN 679 of 2024) | A licence applicant shows a wayleave, or proof that the process of obtaining one has started (*haki ya njia au uthibitisho wa kuanza mchakato*) |
| Land Act, Cap. 113, ss.161, 166, 186–187 | A wayleave runs with the land; compensation for trees, crops and buildings; removal orders for obstructions and encroachments on a public right of way |

The wayleave itself is *haki ya njia ya umeme*, and a transmission line *njia ya kusafirisha umeme*. We found no statutory wayleave width by voltage, so each survey is measured against your own standard. [Wayleave law in Tanzania](/tz/wayleave-law#electricity)

::::section{id="structures" eyebrow="Structures under the line" title="A register of what stands in the wayleave, band by band"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the line in its UTM zone, list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the line and its band, and cut the route into 500 m stretches. Each stretch is rated for **encroachment density**: high where more than five structures stand inside the widest band, medium for one to five, low for none. It is a count rule that tells your wayleave officers where to go first, not a safety rating.

Towers and substations are the utility's own assets and stay out of the register. After the baseline, the line is re-surveyed on a schedule agreed with you: new and removed structures are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views, and your team receives a notice after each survey saying what changed and where. Fresh digging and new tracks near towers and underground cable routes are flagged the same way.

Each report names the imagery and its capture date, so the register can be read against the date the wayleave was acquired or a valuation was approved.

::::section{id="vegetation" tone="alt" eyebrow="Vegetation in the wayleave" title="Where tall vegetation stands, and where it came back"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Section 36(3) makes vegetation a standing duty, and the question every maintenance plan starts from is where the tall vegetation is. We map it inside each wayleave band from drone elevation models where they are flown and from open canopy-height data elsewhere, and compare vegetation cover between dates from Copernicus Sentinel imagery to show where the wayleave was cleared and where it has regrown.

In the dry season, bush fires can run through a wayleave. We send notices of satellite-detected fire hotspots near your lines and substations and map the burnt area afterwards, so crews can check towers and conductors that were near a fire.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="The limits"}
- Larger patches of vegetation, not individual trees
- Not a measured clearance to conductors, and not a species survey
- Open canopy-height data is older in places; drone surveys give the current picture
- Fires that are small, short-lived or under cloud can be missed; it is not an early-warning service
- Copernicus Sentinel and NASA FIRMS data are credited as their licences require
:::

A 345 km 400 kV line was reported 99 per cent complete in August 2026. A World Bank-financed programme approved in 2018 covers about 620 km of 400 kV double-circuit line in the south-west, towards the Zambian border, and a 128 km 220 kV line with a new substation is in preparation in the south. At regional level, the Eastern Africa Power Pool's Council of Ministers resolved in July 2026 to speed up the common power market.

Every new line starts with route options. We count the structures along each alternative alignment within the wayleave bands, so the route and land teams can compare options on the land they would affect, alongside engineering and environmental criteria. Once the route is chosen, a dated baseline before the valuation, and another on the cut-off date, show what stood in the wayleave and what appeared in between.

::::section{id="substations" tone="alt" eyebrow="Substations and switching stations" title="Around the fence line"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Around substations, the question is what is being built against the fence and on the access road. We map new structures, cleared ground and new tracks in a ring around each site between dates. Fence lines and gates are too thin to detect on satellite imagery, so a reviewer checks them on drone imagery where it is flown.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Drone work near lines and substations"}
Lines, towers and substations are structures under GN 268 of 2026, so flights keep 50 m from them and from people not under the operator's control unless TCAA approves closer work (reg 30). GN 268 sets no live-line distance: your own safety rules decide it. Afridrone is working towards Tanzanian drone approvals, and every drone proposal names the company that will fly.

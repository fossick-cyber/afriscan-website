faq:
  - q: Does a satellite-based right-of-way survey need an RCAA permit?
    a: No aircraft flies in satellite-based work, so the Part 27 approvals do not come into it. One question is still open. [Law n° 22/2021](https://rwandalii.org/akn/rw/act/law/2021/22/eng@2021-04-30) gives the Rwanda Space Agency power to issue the "permits, authorizations and licenses that are required in space activities" (art. 8(2°)), and whether buying or analysing commercial satellite imagery counts as a space activity has not been settled. Your counsel should confirm it for your project.
  - q: Is a drone under 1 kg enough for a commercial survey?
    a: No. The Basic class (1 kg or less) "will not be considered for any commercial UAS flights" and is limited to private property with the owner's authorisation (27.020, 27.135(a)). Commercial work is in the Specific class, up to 25 kg, with registration, an activity permit, a pilot licence and an operator certificate listed for it (Table 27.020). Above 25 kg, a drone flies only under an approved person or organisation (27.135(c)).
  - q: Can a company from outside Rwanda fly drone surveys there?
    a: "Only Rwandan citizens and residents aged 18 or over, companies registered in Rwanda and Government institutions may own and register a drone (App. 1 §1.1.1, §1.3.3). A drone registered abroad may fly with proof of that registration and RCAA's authorisation (§1.3.11–1.3.12), a foreign pilot licence counts towards eligibility if it is equivalent and the holder meets Rwanda's security requirements (27.165(c)(3)), and both the activity permit and the UOC need an address for service in Rwanda (27.205(d)(1), 27.220(b)(2)). In practice that means a Rwanda-registered operator, or flying under one."
  - q: Can a drone fly over the homes along our right of way?
    a: Not under the standard conditions, which exclude flight over a populated area and within 30 m of anyone not directly involved (27.050). Outside segregated airspace, a drone also stays 100 m horizontally from anyone who has not consented, and does not fly above property without the prior consent of its occupiers or owner, or of the appropriate authority acting for the public (27.095(a)(1)). Stretches lined with homes need consent planning before any flight, or satellite imagery instead.
  - q: Does a flight along a power line need extra permission?
    a: Yes. Flying at "high tension cables and communication masts" or at strategic installations without RCAA's permission, or outside the conditions of the restriction, is an offence under art. 100 of Law n° 20/2018 as restated in 2024, and RCAA's advisory circular lists power plants among the strategic installations. Ask for an RCAA permission that names the line or site.
  - q: Can a drone fly near Kigali?
    a: Within 10 NM (about 18.5 km) of the centre of an international controlled aerodrome, and 5 NM of a domestic one, a drone flies only under an agreement with the aerodrome operator and with an observer at an uncontrolled aerodrome, or with ATC authorisation at a controlled one, and only as or under a qualified pilot (27.050, 27.090). Kigali International is one of the international airports Rwanda Airports Company lists, so much of Kigali needs ATC coordination.
  - q: How long do the approvals last?
    a: A remote pilot licence and a UOC are each valid for 12 months at most (27.187, 27.230), and RCAA's advisory circular asks for UOC renewal one month before expiry. A BVLOS area approval lasts up to a year, with renewal filed at least 30 days before it expires (27.455). An activity permit covers the locations and dates in the application.

:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="A registered drone" icon="drone" eyebrow="RCAA · App. 1"}
A registration certificate and a **9XR-** mark for each drone, held by an eligible owner in Rwanda, with the marks and a fire-resistant identification plate fitted.
:::
:::card{title="A pilot licence for every pilot" icon="user-check" eyebrow="RCAA · 27.160–27.187"}
A **remote pilot licence or certificate** for each pilot, valid for 12 months at most, issued after training, an exam or a flight test, and security vetting.
:::
:::card{title="An activity permit" icon="file-check" eyebrow="RCAA · 27.205"}
An **activity permit** for the locations and dates of the job, applied for through the RCAA Drone Portal with the mission, the pilot in command and an address for service in Rwanda.
:::
:::card{title="An operator certificate" icon="building" eyebrow="RCAA · 27.205–27.230"}
A **UAS Operator Certificate (UOC)** where RCAA asks for one. Table 27.020 lists it for commercial operations, and it names the approved geographical areas, so plan on needing one.
:::
:::card{title="Security review" icon="shield" eyebrow="27.375–27.395"}
Vetting of pilots, owners and operators, operator security procedures accepted by RCAA, and **camera use submitted "for Security review and approval"**.
:::
:::card{title="Site permissions and consent" icon="boundary" eyebrow="Art. 100 · 27.095"}
RCAA permission for restricted sites such as high-tension cables and masts, ATC coordination near aerodromes, and the consent of people and property owners under the flight path.

| **Air traffic control** and the aerodrome operator, Rwanda Airports Company | ATC authorisation in controlled airspace and near aerodromes; NOTAMs for flights above 120 m | 27.065, 27.090, 27.095(c) |
| **The competent security agencies** (not named in the text) | Security vetting of pilots, owners and operators, and review of camera use | 27.375, 27.380(e)(4); App. 1 §1.3.9 |
| **National Civil Aviation Security Committee** (its chair) | Consulted before highly automated operations are approved; sets security conditions | 27.110(d)–(e) |
| **Rwanda National Police**, at the point of entry | Declaration of drones on arrival; drones held on a seizure form until registered or authorised | App. 1 §1.2; [RCAA drones page](https://www.caa.gov.rw/drones) |
| **Local police and local authorities** | Shown the activity permit before the flight | [RCAA FAQs](https://www.caa.gov.rw/faqs) |

Part 27 covers "basic and specific" operations and, "unless otherwise exempted", does not apply to international operations of drones (27.001(c)). Part 27 has been re-issued twice as special regulations of the Director General: RSR/01/2024 of 15 November 2024, and the current RSR/01/2026 of 15 February 2026, which added Subpart I on BVLOS area approvals. RCAA's service pages still point to its [advisory circular RCAA-AC-UAS-21-001](https://www.caa.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/RCAA/Laws___Regulations/Advisory_Circulars/unmanned_aircraft_system__uas__advisory_circular_in_compliance_with_part_27.pdf), which predates both and adds document lists this guide cites where they apply. Rwandan laws are published in Kinyarwanda, English and French; in Kinyarwanda an unmanned aircraft is an *indege itarimo umupilote*.

::::section{id="categories" class="compare" eyebrow="The categories" title="Basic or Specific: where a survey drone fits"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 27 sorts operations into two classes (27.020 and Table 27.020):

| | Class 1, Basic | Class 2, Specific |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | "will not be considered for any commercial UAS flights" | "fit UAS operations that are for commercial UAS operations" |
| Mass | 1 kg or less (27.135(a)) | 25 kg or less (27.135(b)) |
| Where | Private property only, with the owner's authorisation | As the activity permit and operator certificate allow |
| Approval | No prior authorisation, but notification to RCAA, club registration, a location or area approval and a basic risk assessment | Registration, an activity permit, a pilot licence and UAS Operator Certification; approval on a risk assessment and mitigation plan, or an operator declaration for standard scenarios |

Beyond the Specific class, an operational risk assessment applies as the Authority describes (27.020(c)), and drones over 25 kg fly only under an approved person or organisation (27.135(c)). Type certification may be required.

A corridor or site survey for a client is commercial, so it is a Specific operation. Plan for all four approvals in Table 27.020: registration, an activity permit, a pilot licence and an operator certificate.

::::section{id="approvals" tone="alt" eyebrow="The approvals" title="What a commercial survey needs, in order"}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="An eligible owner and a registered drone"}
Rwandan citizens or residents aged 18 or over, companies registered in Rwanda and Government institutions may own and register a drone (App. 1 §1.1.1, §1.3.3); RCAA's FAQ lists "a foreigner who is legally residing in Rwanda" and "a company in Rwanda" among them. Registration starts with a formal letter and the applicant form, then an account on the [Drone Portal](https://systems.caa.gov.rw/uasportal/) ([RCAA registration](https://www.caa.gov.rw/service-details/uas-registration)). The advisory circular also asks for the security process, a copy of the national ID or company registration certificate, the manuals, a copy of the insurance policy, the serial number and proof of payment. RCAA assigns a 9XR- mark and a certificate that cannot be transferred (§1.3.5, §1.3.8). Changes of ownership are notified seven days ahead (§1.1.2), and drones with military specifications are banned (§1.1.3).
:::
:::step{title="A remote pilot licence for each pilot"}
The pilot is 18 or over and passes a knowledge exam or equivalent, with a training course, a manufacturer's course or an RCAA flight test (27.165). The advisory circular adds security vetting, a Class 3 medical certificate, a criminal record certificate, a copy of the national ID and the ability to read, write, speak and understand English ([RCAA pilot certificate](https://www.caa.gov.rw/service-details/uas-pilot-certificate)). The licence lasts 12 months at most (27.187). Without a radio or other listed qualification, a pilot stays below 120 m and outside 10 NM of an international aerodrome (27.175(b)), flies within visual line of sight unless qualified for BVLOS under a UOC (27.175(c)), and flies one drone at a time (27.175(d)).
:::
:::step{title="An activity permit for the locations and dates"}
Applied for "using a platform specified by the Authority", with "the name and address for service in Rwanda of the applicant" (27.205(a), (c)–(e)). The application gives the activity type, the start and end dates, the mission title and description, the pilot in command's licence and the locations, and an approved permit is sent by email (advisory circular §1.4, FAQ 2.5).
:::
:::step{title="A UAS Operator Certificate"}
Required where RCAA asks for one "after considering the type of operations, operational area and type of equipment" (27.205(b)). The application is a letter describing the operations, a concept of operations, a risk assessment and the applicant form ([RCAA UOC](https://www.caa.gov.rw/service-details/unmanned-operator-certificate)); its contents (27.210) include the locations, the personnel and their training, the aircraft and maintenance, in-flight procedures "including minimum distances from persons or property", the manufacturer's declaration of compliance and "any other approvals that are required". The certificate states the principal base, an address for service in Rwanda, the aircraft and the **approved geographical areas** (27.220), and lasts 12 months at most (27.230). The holder appoints a chief remote pilot (27.195), runs a safety management system (27.270), reports airworthiness quarterly (27.271), notifies RCAA of an accident within 24 hours (27.035) and keeps flight records for 12 months and maintenance records for 24 (27.250).
:::
:::step{title="An eligible aircraft"}
A permanent "specific category" label in English, operating instructions, and a declaration of compliance accepted by RCAA or an approved organisation's approval (27.200(b)).
:::
:::step{title="Security procedures and camera review"}
Security vetting (27.375), operator security procedures accepted by RCAA (27.380(a)), camera use described "for Security review and approval" (27.380(e)(4)), and inspection at any time without notice (27.380(d)). A UOC holder has a security coordinator and secure storage (27.395).
:::
:::step{title="Tracking"}
The operator subscribes to the means of tracking available in the country (27.365, 27.440(c)).
:::
:::step{title="Insurance"}
Third-party cover "commensurate with the risk", with proof submitted to RCAA and the certificate kept where the operator is managed (27.405(a)–(b)). No minimum is stated, and RCAA may waive it by class and category (27.405(c)).
:::
:::step{title="Notice on site"}
"Present your activity permit to local police and local authorities before flight" ([RCAA FAQs](https://www.caa.gov.rw/faqs)).
:::
:::

RCAA charges fees for these approvals under its published [fees and charges circular (AIC A 01/2023)](https://www.caa.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/RCAA/RCAA_CHARGES/AIC_A_01_2023_RCAA_FEES_AND_CHARGES_Final.pdf). Confirm the current amounts with RCAA; this page quotes none.

::::section{id="corridor-rules" eyebrow="Flying a line or a site" title="The rules that shape a corridor survey" lead="Power lines, roads and water mains in Rwanda pass through dense rural settlement and close to Kigali's airspace. These are the Part 27 rules a flight plan along them has to fit."}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="The standard conditions" icon="drone"}
Within visual line of sight, at or below 120 m (400 ft) above ground, by day; not within 30 m of anyone not directly associated with the operation; not over a populated area; not in prohibited or restricted areas; not over a fire, police or other emergency operation without approval; one drone per pilot (27.050).
:::
:::card{title="Consent of people and occupiers" icon="houses"}
Outside segregated airspace, no flight "within 100 m, measured horizontally, of a person who has not given consent", or "above property unless prior consent has been obtained from any persons occupying that property or the property owner; or the appropriate Authority working on behalf of the public" (27.095(a)(1)). A right-of-way flight over inhabited plots needs consent planning or an authority-level consent.
:::
:::card{title="Aerodromes" icon="flag"}
Not within 10 NM of the centre of an international controlled aerodrome or 5 NM of a domestic one (27.050), except under an agreement with the aerodrome operator and with an observer at an uncontrolled aerodrome, or with ATC authorisation at a controlled one, and only as or under a qualified pilot (27.090). [Rwanda Airports Company](https://rac.co.rw/about-us-1/airports-1) lists Kigali International and Kamembe International, Gisenyi Airport, and the Ruhengeri, Butare and Nemba airstrips.
:::
:::card{title="Height and controlled airspace" icon="map"}
Above 120 m, outside the aerodrome radii, only in segregated airspace or with a NOTAM requested at least 24 hours ahead through an authorised person (27.095(c)). In controlled airspace, ATC authorisation (27.065).
:::
:::card{title="People, sight and weather" icon="eye"}
No flight over people who are not covered or consenting, and no closer than 30 m (27.130). First-person view only with a trained observer (27.100). Below the cloud base, and no flight in cloud, at night or outside visual meteorological conditions unless a Subpart D authorisation allows it (27.115, 27.120). No alcohol within 8 hours before flying (27.150).
:::
:::card{title="The imaging footprint" icon="target"}
For Basic operations, capturing pictures or video "extending beyond the prescribed area of approved operation is prohibited" (App. 2 §2.5.1(i)). For Specific operations the approved area comes from the activity permit and the UOC (27.220(b)(4)(ii)), and keeping every image inside the approved corridor is prudent practice.
:::
:::

Special operations, such as work within aerodromes, need an authorisation and a concept of operations (27.155). Expedited authorisation is only for urgent public-benefit work, by an applicant with current registration, a UOC, a pilot licence and prior experience of that operation (27.156).

::::section{id="restricted" tone="alt" eyebrow="Restricted sites" title="High-tension lines, strategic installations and other places that need RCAA permission"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Art. 100 of Law n° 20/2018, as restated by art. 30 of Law n° 059/2024, makes it an offence to operate a drone:

- "in a prohibited or a restricted area, the particulars of which have been duly published in the Rwanda Aeronautical Information Publication, except in accordance with the conditions of the restrictions or by permission granted by the Authority"; or
- "in strategic installations, radar sites, high tension cables and communication masts, highways, stadiums, prisons, police stations, military barracks, courts of law, scenes of crime, except in accordance with the conditions of the restrictions or by permission granted by the Authority".

RCAA's advisory circular (FAQ 2.19, "What are the restricted areas in Rwanda?") starts its list with "Strategic Installation (oil and gas reserves, power plants, Atomic and Nuclear power stations, etc)", then radar sites, high tension cables, communication masts, highways, stadiums, prisons, police stations, "Military barracks (National Defense and security installations)", courts of law, scenes of crime, accidents and fires "as defined by RNP", and any other area the appropriate authority specifies.

**So every drone survey of a power line, substation, power plant, fuel or gas storage site, highway or mast needs an RCAA permission that names the line or site.** The prohibited and restricted areas published in the AIP change, so get the current list from RCAA before any flight plan.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="warn" title="Other places to plan around"}
- **Military zones in Kigali.** The UK's travel advice warns that some "may not be well lit or signposted. You could be arrested and detained if you enter these areas."
- **Mining sites.** Part 27 sets no drone rule specific to mines; occupier consent (27.095) applies, and blasting areas are hazards.
- **National parks.** Part 27 is silent; check the park's own permissions before planning.
- **The DRC border.** We do not offer field or drone work within 10 km of the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo ([see below](#afriscan)).

::::section{id="bvlos" eyebrow="Beyond visual line of sight" title="BVLOS, flight by flight or by area"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Per operation (27.105)" icon="route"}
An RCAA authorisation after an air and ground risk assessment; detect-and-avoid or another mitigation; ATC coordination with direct telephone contact; flight within radio line of sight unless specially authorised; visual meteorological conditions; and special considerations over heavily populated areas. For a foreign operator, "the State of the Operator and the State in whose airspace operation occurs" must both have approved the operation (27.105(h)(1)).
:::
:::card{title="The BVLOS area approval (Subpart I, new in 2026)" icon="calendar"}
Permanent, temporary or event-based (27.425(b)); **UOC holders only** (27.425(c)); applied for **at least 90 days** before the operation (27.430(a)), with maps and coordinates, a risk assessment and concept of operations, detect-and-avoid evidence, an ATS coordination plan, ground-risk mitigation, an emergency plan, evidence of the ability to avoid the no-fly zones the Authority specifies, and "a tool that allows a quick review of all flight paths" (27.430(b)). Conditions may limit flights "based on population density or sensitive sites" (27.450(a)(3)), and transponders, remote ID or observers may be required (27.440(d)). Valid for up to a year (27.455).
:::
:::

BVLOS is possible in Rwanda, but not quickly. For a survey with a deadline, plan the line in blocks flown within visual line of sight, or start from satellite imagery.

::::section{id="security" tone="alt" eyebrow="The security layer" title="Vetting, camera review and inspection"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Every pilot-licence and registration application goes to the competent security agencies. Licences and operator certificates are issued only to those who pass a security threat assessment drawing on intelligence databases, Interpol and terrorist watch lists, and background and criminal-record checks recur every 24 months for everyone who handles the drones (27.375). Commercial camera use is submitted for security review and approval (27.380(e)(4)), drones may be inspected at any time without notice (27.380(d)), and highly automated operations need the chair of the National Civil Aviation Security Committee to be consulted (27.110).

The texts this guide relies on set no separate defence permit, but they build security review into every RCAA step. Ask RCAA whether anything more applies to your site, and build vetting into the schedule.

::::section{id="privacy" eyebrow="Photographs and personal data" title="People are never the subject of the imagery"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Part 27 and Law n° 20/2018 {#privacy-rules}

Part 27's privacy rule (27.410) forbids using a drone to observe a person without their consent, or private real property without its owner's consent, and forbids photographing or filming an individual without consent for publication, apart from news and public events. Art. 101 of Law n° 20/2018, unchanged in 2024, makes it an offence to use a drone "to take photographs of a prohibited area without a permit or of an individual without the individual's consent".

This is one reason AfriScan maps land and assets, never people. A right-of-way register lists structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks with their distance to the line; it does not identify who lives or works there, and identifiable people are never published.
::::
::::col
### Law n° 058/2021 on personal data {#personal-data}

[Law n° 058/2021](https://rwandalii.org/akn/rw/act/law/2021/58/eng@2021-10-15) applies to controllers and processors outside Rwanda that process the data of people in Rwanda (art. 2), and defines personal data by identifiers that include "location data" (art. 3). Maps linked to named households, ID numbers or parcel owners are personal data. The articles that matter for a survey:

- **art. 29:** controllers and processors register with the supervisory authority, the National Cyber Security Authority;
- **art. 48:** a transfer abroad needs its authorisation, consent, or a listed necessity, and **art. 49** a written contract;
- **art. 50:** personal data is stored in Rwanda, unless the controller or processor holds a registration certificate authorising storage outside Rwanda.

The [Data Protection and Privacy Office](https://dpo.gov.rw/) issues those certificates and authorisations.

::::section{id="import" tone="alt" eyebrow="Bringing a drone in" title="Declare it at the border"}
"Declare your drone to Rwanda National Police at any point of entry into the country, failure to abide calls for penalty" ([RCAA](https://www.caa.gov.rw/drones)). Unless it is authorised, any drone brought in, registered abroad or not registered at all, is handed over on a seizure form until registration or authorisation is complete (App. 1 §1.2.4), and drones left unclaimed for more than six months are destroyed or auctioned (§1.2.6). Imported drone parts may not be held without RCAA's authorisation (§1.2.3). The UK's travel advice puts it plainly: "You must declare drones when you arrive. They may be confiscated or held until you leave the country."

::::section{id="checklist" tone="alt" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Rwanda" lead="Ask any operator, including us, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="An eligible, Rwanda-registered operator"}
A company registered in Rwanda, with RCAA registration certificates (9XR-) for each drone and the marks and identification plates fitted.
:::
:::step{title="A current Rwandan pilot licence for every pilot"}
A remote pilot licence or certificate for each person at the controls, with an expiry date after the last flight.
:::
:::step{title="The right certificate and permit"}
A UOC covering the type of operation and the geographical area, where RCAA requires one, and an activity permit for these locations and days.
:::
:::step{title="Restricted sites named"}
RCAA permission that explicitly covers the power lines, substations, masts, highways and other strategic installations on the route (art. 100; advisory circular FAQ 2.19).
:::
:::step{title="Aerodromes coordinated"}
ATC or aerodrome-operator authorisation for flights within 10 NM of Kigali or Kamembe, or 5 NM of a domestic aerodrome.
:::
:::step{title="Insurance lodged"}
Proof of third-party cover submitted to RCAA (27.405).
:::
:::step{title="Consent and local notice"}
Consent for flying over occupied property and within 100 m of people, or an authority-level consent (27.095), and the permit shown to the local police and local authorities.
:::
:::step{title="BVLOS covered"}
A per-operation authorisation or an area approval, which needs a UOC and at least 90 days.
:::
:::step{title="Camera use declared"}
Camera use submitted for security review, people not photographed without consent (art. 101), and imagery kept inside the approved area.
:::
:::step{title="A data clause"}
The operator's NCSA registration, where the imagery and any personal data are stored, the basis for any transfer abroad (Law n° 058/2021 arts. 48–50), how long files are kept, and who may publish what.
:::
:::step{title="No flights in the border band or in military zones"}
Nothing within 10 km of the DRC border, and nothing in or near military zones.
:::

::::section{id="afriscan" eyebrow="How we work" title="AfriScan and drone work in Rwanda"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
We start from satellite. A satellite-based survey involves no aircraft, no flight and no site visit, so it can cover a whole line, road or licence area before anyone decides whether a drone is needed, and it needs no RCAA approval. Your counsel should confirm the open space-activity question in the FAQ below for your project.

Drone surveys are subject to the RCAA approvals, security reviews and consents each job requires. Afridrone is working towards the approvals Rwanda requires. Every proposal that includes drone work sets out the approvals its flights need, who applies for each and when each must be in place, and no flight is scheduled before they are.

We map structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks on and around your line or site, and flag change between dated surveys for your teams to check. We never identify or follow people, restricted sites are left out of what we publish, and results go only to the contacts you name. See [power-line rights of way in Rwanda](/rw/power-lines) and [mining licence areas](/rw/mining).

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Where we don't work"}
In line with current government travel advice, we do not offer field or drone work within 10 km of Rwanda's border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The US State Department advises "Level 4 - Do Not Travel" in that band, and the UK advises against all but essential travel to the parts of Rusizi District within 10 km of the border, including the Rwandan islands in Lake Kivu and Kamembe Airport. The band takes in most of the Lake Kivu shore and islands and the Rubavu and Rusizi border areas.

We also take extra care near the Burundi border, which Burundi closed to Rwanda in January 2024, in Nyungwe, on the DRC-border section of Volcanoes National Park and around Kigali's military zones.

  - q: Can you tell us who is digging near our licence area?
    a: No. We map the ground, such as pits, spoil heaps, water-filled excavations, cleared ground and new tracks, with new sites flagged between dates and confirmed by a reviewer. We never identify, count or follow people. Whether an excavation near your licence area is authorised is for you and RMB to establish.
  - q: Can you check the 20 m, 10 m and 5 m buffers?
    a: Yes. RMB's 2026 guidelines say mining is not permitted within 20 m of wetlands, 10 m of main rivers and 5 m of the banks of smaller rivers. Send the river and wetland lines you work to, or tell us which ones to use, and we list the excavations, cleared ground and structures inside each buffer, with the imagery date on every record.
  - q: Does a provider need an RMB licence to work on our licence area?
    a: RMB's 2019 regulations say a mining licence holder "must not engage or employ" a mining or quarry services provider unless the provider holds an RMB licence or registration (art. 5), and they list remote sensing among the essential services. Ask any provider, including us, how that requirement is met for your contract before work starts; our proposal sets it out.
  - q: Can you see underground workings or shafts?
    a: No. Underground workings, and shafts under cover, are not visible from above. We map what shows at the surface.
  - q: Can you confirm our licence boundary or beacons?
    a: No. We work from the licence-area coordinates you give us, from your licence or the GIMCS mining cadastre, and we do not verify title or tenure. Beacons are yours to install under art. 24(c) of the mining law, and a boundary that needs surveying is a land surveyor's work.
  - q: Can your maps stand as our rehabilitation or closure report?
    a: They support it. Revegetation trends and earthworks between dates give you dated evidence for your environmental and closure reporting. They are not an ecological assessment, a closure certificate or a sign-off by RMB.
  - q: The mining law asks us to keep aerial photographs in Rwanda. How do your files fit?
    a: Art. 24(d)(ii) of Law n° 072/2024 asks licence holders to keep "aerial photographs" and other data at an address in Rwanda. Everything we deliver is a set of files handed to you, the PDF report, the GIS layers and, where the imagery licence allows, the imagery itself, so you can keep them with the rest of your licence records.

::::section{id="law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The law" title="What Law n° 072/2024 sets for the land"}
[Law n° 072/2024 of 26/06/2024 on mining and quarry operations](https://www.rmb.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/RMB/Publications/Laws/Law_n___0722024of_26062024_on_mining_and_quarry_operations.pdf) (*loi portant exploitation des mines et carrières*; Kinyarwanda *itegeko rigenga ubucukuzi bw'amabuye y'agaciro na kariyeri*) repealed Law n° 58/2018; licences granted under the old law stay valid until they expire (arts. 75, 77).

:::facts{cols="3"}
- Ownership: Minerals are vested in the State "notwithstanding personal ownership of land" (art. 3)
- Compensation first: Fair compensation to the landowner or lawful occupier before mining, after which the land is registered to the State (art. 4)
- Maximum block sizes: Exploration 400 ha, small-scale 50 ha, medium-scale 100 ha, large-scale 400 ha (art. 8)
- Terms: Exploration up to 4 years and mining up to 15 years at first (art. 13)
- Beacons: The holder installs "beacons around the mining licence area" (art. 24(c))
- Records: The holder keeps "aerial photographs" and other data at an address in Rwanda (art. 24(d)(ii))
:::

Applications go through the One Stop Center (art. 9), potential mining areas are demarcated with their names and geographic coordinates (art. 5), and each holder agrees a development and social-welfare plan with the District (art. 74). Tailings are managed as regulations provide, and damage to land and property is fairly compensated (art. 24(i)–(j)). Licences are administered by the Rwanda Mines, Petroleum and Gas Board (RMB) through the [GIMCS mining cadastre](https://gimcs.rmb.gov.rw/).

### River and wetland buffers {#buffers}

We buffer the river and wetland lines you work to at 20 m for wetlands, 10 m for main rivers and 5 m for the banks of smaller rivers, the distances in RMB's 2026 guidelines, and list each excavation, clearing and structure inside them with its distance and the imagery date. The environment law sets its own distances for other activities, such as nothing built in a swamp or its 20 m buffer (Law n° 48/2018, art. 49), and we can add those bands too.

::::section{id="boundary" tone="alt" eyebrow="Around the boundary" title="Excavations near the licence area"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Licence blocks in Rwanda are small: the law caps small-scale mining at 50 ha and medium-scale at 100 ha, so the land just outside the beacons matters as much as the land inside. We map the licence area and a ring around it at the width you choose, and flag new pits, spoil, clearings and tracks between surveys, each with its distance to your boundary.

Whether an excavation near your licence area is authorised is for you and RMB to establish. We describe the ground, never the people, and what you do with a flagged site is your decision. In the rainy season, radar comparisons show larger ground changes even under cloud, and flagged areas are followed up with optical imagery or a drone check.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What a flag is"}
An indication for your team to check, located and dated, with before-and-after views. It is not proof of who did anything, and it will not catch every change: small or covered workings can be missed.

::::section{id="compensation" tone="alt" eyebrow="Expansion and compensation" title="Record the land before the next pit"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Law n° 072/2024 requires fair compensation before mining starts (art. 4) and for damage to land and property (art. 24(j)). The expropriation law also counts "minerals and other natural resources in the public domain" among activities of public interest (Law n° 32/2015, art. 5), and all works related to mining are on the list of projects that need an environmental impact assessment (Ministerial Order of 2018, Annex I).

Before an expansion, we produce a dated register of the structures and cleared land in the new footprint, with imagery, coordinates and IDs. It supports your valuation, your compensation records and, where a lender applies them, cut-off-date records under IFC Performance Standard 5. It does not replace the census or the asset inventory, and it never establishes ownership, use or value.

::::section{id="service-providers" eyebrow="Working on a licence area" title="RMB's rules for service providers"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
[RMB Regulations N° 002/MINES/RMB/2019 of 18/07/2019](https://www.rmb.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/RMB/Publications/Regulations/Regulations_of_the_CEO_No_002.MINES.RMB.2019_of_18.07.2019_determining_the_provision_of_Services_relating_to_mining_and_quarry_operations.pdf) list as essential services "mineral or quarry exploration services, including geological, geophysics, remote sensing and geochemistry activities" and "environmental and social management services such as reclamation, re-vegetation, mine waste and water and tailings management, mine closure and rehabilitation" (art. 4). A licence holder "must not engage or employ" a mining or quarry services provider unless the provider holds an RMB licence or registration (art. 5). RMB decides an application within 60 days (art. 13) and issues the licence for five years, renewable (art. 14).

RMB still lists these regulations, and publishes its lists of providers holding service licences on its [guidelines page](https://www.rmb.gov.rw/publications/guidelines). Law n° 072/2024 provides for regulations on private mining and quarry services (art. 71), and asks licence holders to give preference to Rwandan contractors "provided that the rates, quality and time schedule for delivery are competitive" (art. 72).

::::section{id="drones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Drones on a mine site" title="Satellite first, drones where they add detail"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 27 sets no drone rule specific to mines. A flight over a licence area is a commercial Specific operation with registration, a pilot licence and an activity permit, the consent of occupiers where the flight passes over their property or near people, and care around blasting areas, which are hazards. Drone surveys are subject to the RCAA approvals, security reviews and consents each job requires, so a survey starts from satellite imagery and adds drone orthophotos and elevation models where they add detail.
::::
::::col

The Part 27 approvals, restricted sites and a client checklist, as of 27 September 2026.

### What we do

:::checklist

### What we don't do

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Exploration, geology or resource estimates
- See underground workings, or measure ground movement
- Identify, count or follow people
- Verify title or beacons, or decide whether an excavation is authorised
- Offer field or drone work within 10 km of the DRC border

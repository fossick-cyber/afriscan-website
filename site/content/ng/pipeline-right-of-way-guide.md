## Five stages, five records {#stages}

| Stage | What the law provides | What a dated baseline records |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Surveying the route** | A permit to survey the route, and notice before entering buildings or enclosed gardens, with compensation for damage | Structures along each route option, before survey teams arrive |
| **2. Acquiring the land** | Revocation of rights of occupancy for oil pipelines, with compensation and, for homes, possible resettlement | A dated register of the structures on the land at the enumeration |
| **3. The licence and the strip** | Pipeline licences and rights of way; an optional order restricting building and cultivation beside the land in the licence | Structures inside the licence strip and within any order's distance |
| **4. Operating the line** | The same restrictions, the host-community framework, and works rules near roads and railways | What appears inside and beside the strip between surveys |
| **5. Changing hands** | Transfer of assets between operators | The state of the right of way on the handover date |

## 1. Surveying the route {#survey}

The [Oil Pipelines Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=425) (1956 No. 31, as amended in 1965) provides a permit to survey the route of a proposed pipeline (ss.4–5). Before entering buildings or enclosed gardens, surveyors must give 14 days' notice, and damage done by the survey is compensated (s.6).

Where the survey work includes aerial photography for survey purposes, the [Survey Co-ordination Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=501) asks for at least three months' notice to the Director of Federal Surveys, or a shorter period if the Director allows (s.1). Whether that notice applies to drone orthophotos or to mapping from satellite imagery is a question for counsel.

**What a baseline records.** A count of the structures along each route option within the same bands, from satellite imagery, before anyone is on the ground. It lets route engineers and land teams compare options on the land they would affect, and it becomes the first dated picture of the corridor. See [route and site selection](key:route-site-selection).

## 2. Acquiring the land {#acquisition}

Under the [Land Use Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=228) (1978 No. 6), a right of occupancy can be revoked for "overriding public interest", which includes land required for "mining purposes or oil pipelines" (s.28). Compensation is then paid under the petroleum legislation "or any legislation replacing the same" (s.29(2)); community compensation may be paid to the community, its chief or a fund (s.29(3)); buildings are valued at replacement cost less depreciation (s.29(4)); and resettlement may be offered instead of compensation for a residential building (s.33). The [Petroleum Industry Act 2021](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Petroleum-Industry-Act-2021.pdf) adds its own provision on compensation for land acquisition (s.115).

On projects financed by lenders that apply IFC Performance Standard 5, a cut-off date for eligibility and a census sit on top of the national process.

**What a baseline records.** A dated register of the structures on the land, each with an ID, coordinates, a photo crop and the imagery it was seen on, and, where scoped, a category the imagery can show. The enumeration team starts from the register and matches each structure to it, so the enumeration and the imagery record point at the same building. A person reviews every entry. The register supports the enumeration and the compensation process; it does not replace either. See [dated imagery for cut-off dates](/insights/dated-imagery-cut-off-dates).

## 3. The licence and the strip {#licence}

The Petroleum Industry Act provides rights of way for upstream pipelines (s.90), rights of way reserved for the Commission (s.91), rights of way for gas pipelines in the midstream and downstream (s.127) and for petroleum liquids pipelines (s.176), and licences for gas transportation pipelines (s.135) and petroleum liquids transportation pipelines (s.190). Upstream lines fall to the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC); midstream and downstream pipeline licences to the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (NMDPRA).

The Oil Pipelines Act adds the provision most right-of-way teams know by number. On the licence holder's application, an order under **section 12** can prohibit or restrict buildings, cultivation, and industrial, mining or oil-mining activity "within a specified distance, not exceeding 100 feet, from the boundaries of the land" in the licence. Section 14 keeps pipeline works away from public roads, dams, reservoirs and government buildings (50 yards) and railways (100 yards) unless the licence expressly allows them.

:::callout{tone="legal" title="Two laws, read together"}
The Oil Pipelines Act is not among the laws the Petroleum Industry Act repeals (s.310(1)). The PIA prevails over inconsistent laws, except the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act (s.309). Which provisions govern a particular line, and whether a section 12 order exists for it, are questions for your legal team.
:::

**What a baseline records.** Send the licence strip as a polygon and the register reports the structures inside it, and within the distance of any section 12 order from its boundary (100 feet is 30.48 m), alongside the distances your company standard uses. Newly cleared ground and new tracks inside the strip can be mapped as separate layers.

## 4. Operating the line {#operating}

Most of a right of way's life is spent here, and most of its problems start here: a new building a few metres inside the strip, a farm cleared across it, a track cut to reach it, a trench dug beside it. Each is easier to deal with in the month it appears than years later.

The Petroleum Industry Act's host-community framework (Chapter 3, host community development trusts) makes the land beside many lines the ground of a formal relationship with the communities that live there. A register of structures, cleared ground and tracks, measured against the strip and shared in the right form, gives both sides the same facts to talk about.

**What a baseline records.** Re-surveys of the strip on a schedule agreed with you, with new and removed structures flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, the date each change was first seen, and a density rating per 500 m that shows where to send people first. Sites showing visible signs of artisanal refining, and oil-stained or dead vegetation near your lines, can be mapped and flagged for your environment teams to check. We map land and assets, never people.

## 5. Changing hands {#handover}

Onshore and swamp assets have been passing from international operators to Nigerian companies. On the day an asset changes hands, the new owner inherits whatever stands on its rights of way, and a record of what stood there that day is worth having before the first community meeting.

**What a baseline records.** A dated handover record from archive imagery and a new survey: the structures and their distances, tracks, cleared and disturbed ground around lines, flow stations, manifolds and well pads, and roughly when each appeared. The operator's own installations are recorded as what they are, never as encroachment. See [pipeline rights of way in Nigeria](/ng/oil-gas-pipelines#handover).

## Drones, data and the rainy season {#practicalities}

- **Satellite first.** Satellite-based right-of-way screening involves no drone flight and no site visit. Long trunklines, swamp and creek sections, and stretches where a field team is not sensible are covered this way.
- **Drones where they add detail.** Commercial drone work needs an NCAA RPAS Operator Certificate with ONSA security clearance built into the process, registered aircraft and pilots with remote pilot licences; flights over populated areas and near high-tension lines need specific authorisation, and controlled airspace around Lagos and Port Harcourt needs ATC clearance. Drone surveys are subject to the permits and authorisations each job requires. The rules are in [hiring a drone survey in Nigeria](/ng/drone-regulations).
- **Data.** Imagery that shows people or homes can hold personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023; the operator commissioning the survey is usually the controller. Military and other sensitive sites are masked in deliverables.
- **Rain and cloud.** Optical satellite imagery cannot see through cloud, so the rainy season thins out usable scenes. Radar screening can show larger ground changes, such as clearing and earthworks, even under cloud, and flagged areas are checked on optical imagery when conditions allow.

## A checklist for right-of-way teams {#checklist}

:::checklist
- The licence strip for each line as a polygon, with the licence and any section 12 order identified
- The distances to report: the strip, any order's distance, and your company standard
- A dated baseline of the strip and its surroundings, with stable structure IDs
- For new lines, route options compared by the structures they affect, before the survey permit stage
- For acquisition, a register the enumeration team can match to, on imagery with a known date
- A re-survey schedule that matches how fast each stretch changes and the imagery supply
- Your own facilities listed, so they are never counted as encroachment
- A written purpose for the register, and data-protection terms with anyone who handles the imagery
- For drone work, the NCAA, ONSA and survey-notice steps built into the programme
:::

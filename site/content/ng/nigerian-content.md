faq:
  - q: Which NOGICD schedule line covers satellite structure mapping?
    a: The Schedule has no line by that name. The closest are photogrammetric surveying services, chart and map production services, data management and software development and support, and drone survey work sits closest to photogrammetric and survey services. Which line applies to a scope is for your Nigerian content team and NCDMB to settle; ask for it to be named in the tender.
  - q: Is remote right-of-way mapping a security service?
    a: No, and it should not be bought as one. NUPRC's service-permit guidelines put security services under an NSCDC licence and require drone-based security services to show NCAA and ONSA approvals. Structure registers, change maps and imagery are remote sensing and mapping of land and assets; patrols, guarding and response stay with security contractors.
  - q: Can a foreign company register drones in Nigeria?
    a: No. Part 21 lets only Nigerian citizens, permanent residents, Nigerian-incorporated companies whose aircraft are based and primarily used in Nigeria, and government bodies register a drone (21.3.1.1(a)). A foreign-registered drone needs proof of that registration and NCAA authorisation, and foreign operators need NCAA approval under Part 10.8 or a temporary authorisation.

    a: The route or boundary file, the states it crosses, the widths or zones to report, the date the record must reflect, the deliverables and the deadline. ::::section{id="nogicd" tone="alt" width="prose" eyebrow="The Nigerian content law" title="The NOGICD Act 2010 in the sections that matter"}
The [Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010](https://ncdmb.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Nigerian-Content-Act-%E2%80%93-Nigerian-Content-Development-Monitoring-Board.pdf) applies across oil and gas contracting. The Petroleum Industry Act 2021 prevails over any inconsistent law except this one (PIA s.309). As quoted in NCDMB's [contracting-process guideline](https://ncdmb.gov.ng/images/GUIDELINES/Guidelines-for-NCDMB-Approvals-of-NOG-Contracting-Processes.pdf):

- **s.3(1)–(3):** first consideration for Nigerian independent operators; "exclusive consideration to Nigerian indigenous service companies which demonstrate ownership of equipment, Nigerian personnel and capacity" for Schedule services on land and swamp; Nigerian content compliance is "a major criterion" for awards.
- **s.10:** first consideration for services provided from within Nigeria.
- **s.11 and the Schedule:** minimum Nigerian content for listed services.
- **s.17:** contracts above the value set in the section need NCDMB approval of the advert, the prequalification criteria, the technical documents, the evaluation criteria and the bidder lists.
- **s.33:** an operator needs NCDMB approval before applying for an expatriate quota.
- **s.41(2):** Nigerian subsidiaries of multinationals must own at least half of the equipment they deploy.
- **s.106:** a "Nigerian company" has "not less than 51% equity shares by Nigerians".

NCDMB's standard clauses also deduct the Nigerian Content Development Fund contribution from every subcontract, and list licences relevant to the scope ("COREN, NIMASA, NCAA etc") among tender fatal-flaw items.

### Schedule minimums for survey, mapping and data services {#schedule}

| Service in the Schedule | Minimum Nigerian content |
|---|---|
| Photogrammetric surveying services | 45% |
| Chart and map production services | 45% |
| Survey and positioning support services | 75% |
| Software development and support | 45% |
| Data management | 50% |
| IT management consultancy | 50% |
| Security services | 95% |

From the Schedule of the Act as scanned by NCDMB. Which line a mapping, imagery or drone scope falls under is for the operator's Nigerian content team and NCDMB to settle. NCDMB's [ICT guideline](https://ncdmb.gov.ng/images/GUIDELINES/OilnGasIndICTGuidelines.pdf) (NC-LEG-02ICT/19 Rev 1, 2019) adds rules for foreign ICT original equipment manufacturers, such as selling through Nigerian companies and receiving revenue in local currency; whether it reaches a mapping service is a question for counsel.

::::section{id="registrations" eyebrow="Supplier registrations" title="The portals a Nigerian buyer will ask about"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="NOGIC Joint Qualification System" icon="file-check" eyebrow="NCDMB"}
Created under NOGICD ss.55–56 as the "Sole system for Nigerian Content registration and pre-qualification of contractors" ([NOGIC JQS](https://nogicjqs.gov.ng/)). A service company sends a letter of intent, its CAC certificate, Forms CO2 and CO7, its RC number, contacts and an "NUPRC/NMDPRA permit", then completes the profile, ownership, shareholder, services and staff modules ([service-company guideline](https://nogicjqs.gov.ng/guidelines/service-company)).
:::
:::card{title="NUPRC service permit (OGISP)" icon="clipboard" eyebrow="Upstream"}
"Any company registered in Nigeria may be issued a permit"; foreign companies prove registration with the Ministry of Trade and Investment under CAMA 2020 ([OGISP Guidelines 2024](https://ogisp.nuprc.gov.ng/Content/ApplicationGuideLines/OGISPGUIDELINE2024.pdf)). The Specialised category includes pipeline monitoring, photogrammetry surveying services, remote sensing, survey and positioning services, and ESI mapping and GIS; the Major category includes right-of-way maintenance lines and land, topographic and geodetic survey, which need SURCON membership. Since 1 January 2025 only fully incorporated companies may use the General category.
:::
:::card{title="SURCON" icon="ruler" eyebrow="Survey"}
The Surveyors Council of Nigeria registers surveyors and enlists survey firms; its enlistment form asks for the foreign-owned share ([SURCON](https://surcon.gov.ng/)). Survey categories of the NUPRC permit ask for proof of SURCON membership, and survey plans are signed by a registered surveyor.
:::
:::card{title="BPP federal contractors database" icon="building" eyebrow="Federal buyers"}
Federal bodies, such as the Transmission Company of Nigeria, buy under the [Public Procurement Act 2007](https://bpp-storage.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/07144652/Public-Procurement-Act-2007pdf.pdf). Suppliers register on the [BPP database](https://federalcontractors.bpp.gov.ng/) and print an Interim Registration Report; the [registration procedure](https://bpp-storage.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/13205604/REGISTRATION-PROCEDURE-1.pdf) sets out the modules and the exemptions for very small firms. TCN posts tenders on its [notices page](https://www.tcn.org.ng/page_notices.php), and NOSDRA on its [tenders page](https://nosdra.gov.ng/tenders/).
:::

::::section{id="aviation" tone="alt" eyebrow="Drone work" title="Aviation ownership tests and approvals"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Nigeria's drone rules carry their own nationality tests, which matter as soon as a scope includes drone flights:

- **Registration:** only Nigerian citizens, permanent residents, Nigerian-incorporated companies whose aircraft are "based and primarily used in Nigeria", and government bodies may register a drone ([Nig.CARs Part 21](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/wabn2axn/nigcars-part-21-remotely-piloted-aircraft-system.pdf), 21.3.1.1(a)).
- **Aerial work permit:** a Permit for Aerial Aviation Services requires that "the majority shareholding shall be held by Nigerian(s)" ([Part 18](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/kpjldhy3/nigcars-part-18-air-transport-economics.pdf), IS 18.3.6.1(A)). Whether a drone operator with a ROC also needs one is unresolved in the published texts.
- **Security:** ONSA security clearance is a condition of every commercial ROC, and NUPRC requires drone-based security services to show NCAA and ONSA approvals.
- **Foreign crews and drones:** NCAA approval under Part 10.8 or a temporary authorisation, an expatriate quota (with NCDMB approval first for oil and gas work) and End-User Certificates for imports.

::::section{id="data" eyebrow="Data" title="Where the data goes: the NDPA 2023"}
Imagery of rights of way and project areas can show identifiable homes, so a mapping contract is usually a data-processing contract too. Under the [Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Nigeria-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf), a transfer of personal data out of Nigeria needs a recorded basis that gives adequate protection (s.41), a processor must tell the controller of a breach so that the controller can notify the Commission within 72 hours (s.40), and controllers or processors whose data are of particular value to Nigeria's economy, society or security can be designated "of major importance", which brings registration duties (ss.44 and 65). Put the processing and storage locations, the transfer basis, the breach procedure and the retention period in the contract. More in the [drone-law guide](/ng/drone-regulations#ndpa).
::::

::::section{id="ask" tone="alt" eyebrow="For your supplier file" title="What to ask any supplier for, including us" lead="Every item here is something a Nigerian operator, TCN or a lender's adviser can ask to see. Ask for it with the proposal, not after award."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="The contracting entity"}
Who signs and invoices: its CAC registration and RC number, its Nigerian ownership, and any Nigerian company it works with on the contract.
:::
:::step{title="The Nigerian content plan"}
The Schedule line the scope falls under, the Nigerian content it will deliver, and the Nigerian staff, equipment and services behind it.
:::
:::step{title="Registrations for the category"}
NOGIC JQS registration, the NUPRC service permit in the right category, SURCON enlistment where survey outputs are signed, and BPP registration for federal buyers.
:::
:::step{title="Drone authorisations, if drones fly"}
Who holds the NCAA ROC and Operations Specifications that cover the job, ONSA clearance, the drones' registration certificates, End-User Certificates and third-party insurance.
:::
:::step{title="Data handling"}
Where imagery and results are processed and stored, the NDPA s.41 basis for any transfer, the breach procedure, the retention period and the masking of military and other sensitive sites.
:::
:::step{title="Scope boundaries"}
A written statement of what the service does not do: no security services, no leak or theft detection, no identification of people, and no survey plans unless a registered surveyor signs them.
:::
:::

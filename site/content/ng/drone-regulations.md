faq:
  - q: Does satellite-based right-of-way screening need a drone permit?
    a: No drone flies in satellite-based work, so the Part 21 approvals do not come into it. We found no rule that requires a licence to buy or analyse commercial satellite imagery in Nigeria, and whether the Survey Co-ordination Act's notice to the Director of Federal Surveys reaches satellite-derived mapping has not been settled. Your counsel should confirm both points for your project.
  - q: Is a drone under 250 g exempt?
    a: Not for commercial work. The Gazette text of Part 21 has a Nano class for drones under 250 g that still needs prior authorisation (21.2.1.1), any flight for remuneration or hire is commercial (21.1.2), and ONSA's End-User Certificate list covers drones of not more than 250 g as well as heavier ones.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly drone surveys in Nigeria?
    a: "Only Nigerian citizens, permanent residents, Nigerian-incorporated companies whose aircraft are based and primarily used in Nigeria, and government bodies may register a drone (21.3.1.1(a)). A drone registered abroad may fly only with proof of that registration and NCAA authorisation (21.3.1.2(e)). A foreign operator needs either NCAA's approval under Part 10.8, applied for at least 90 days ahead, or the temporary authorisation in 21.9.6.20, which the regulation ties to ROC holders in Nigeria."
  - q: Can a drone fly over the homes along our right of way?
    a: Not under the standard conditions, which keep flights away from populated areas and at least 30 m from anyone not involved in the operation (15 m with their consent). Flights over heavily populated areas need special consideration, and NCAA's advisory circular says a small drone is not normally permitted over populated areas. Stretches lined with homes need an operator whose Operations Specifications allow it, or satellite imagery instead.
  - q: How long is a ROC valid?
    a: NCAA's own documents give three different periods (Part 21, NCAA-AC-GEN007 and NCAA-AC-AWS061), so we publish none. Check the expiry date printed on the operator's certificate and Operations Specifications, and ask NCAA in writing if the job runs close to it.
  - q: Can a drone survey a 330 kV line?
    a: Only with a special authorisation. Part 21 lists "areas of high RF transmission/interference (e.g. radar sites, high tension wires)" among the operations that need one, requested at least 30 days ahead (21.9.6.21). Plan for it on any flight along a transmission line, or start from satellite imagery.
  - q: What happens after an incident?
    a: The operator notifies NCAA by the quickest available means, and a written report is due within 72 hours (21.9.6.19). Accidents also go to the Nigerian Safety Investigation Bureau (NSIB).

::::section{id="at-a-glance" eyebrow="At a glance" title="Six things a commercial survey flight needs" lead="A drone survey in Nigeria is lawful only when every one of these is in place for that job. Most take weeks to arrange, so they belong in the plan from the first day."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="An operator certificate" icon="file-check" eyebrow="NCAA"}
An RPAS Operator Certificate (ROC) with Operations Specifications that list the operations allowed and their limits. Any flight "for remuneration or hire" is commercial (21.1.2).
:::
:::card{title="Registered drones" icon="drone" eyebrow="NCAA"}
Every drone must be registered with NCAA, and registration needs an End-User Certificate from ONSA (21.3.1.2(b)). Registration is not a permit to fly commercially.
:::
:::card{title="Security clearance" icon="shield" eyebrow="ONSA"}
No commercial operation without ONSA security clearance, which NCAA requests during certification. The operator also runs an NCAA-approved security programme.
:::
:::card{title="Qualified pilots" icon="user-check" eyebrow="NCAA"}
A Remote Pilot Licence under Part 2 for each pilot, with a current medical. In controlled airspace the licence also counts as the radio qualification.
:::
:::card{title="Airspace and site permissions" icon="map" eyebrow="ATC · landowners"}
ATC clearance in controlled airspace, special authorisations for night and high-tension lines, and written consent from the right-of-way holder or landowner.
:::
:::card{title="Survey and data rules" icon="scale" eyebrow="OSGOF · NDPC"}
Notice to the Director of Federal Surveys before survey work, and personal data in the imagery handled under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.
:::

::::section{id="commercial" eyebrow="The categories" title="What counts as commercial, and where a survey drone fits"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 21 treats "all RPAS operation for remuneration or hire" as commercial, and defines aerial work as the use of an aircraft "for specialized services such as agriculture, construction, photography, surveying, observation and patrol" (21.1.2). NCAA's advisory circular adds that a ROC "is required for all Government (Civil) and commercial use of RPAs" (AC-AWS061 §12.2.2), and that a drone under 25 kg used for filming or inspection is not a recreational aircraft and needs a ROC (App. 6).

The Gazette text sorts drones into five mass classes, and every class needs prior authorisation (21.2.1.1):

| Class | Mass | Line of sight |
|---|---|---|
| Nano | under 250 g | VLOS |
| Micro | 250 g to under 2 kg | VLOS |
| Small | 2 kg to under 25 kg | VLOS only |
| Medium | 25 kg to 150 kg | VLOS or BVLOS |
| Large | over 150 kg | VLOS or BVLOS |

Operations are also sorted by risk (21.2.1.1(b)): an open category that needs no prior authorisation but still needs registration, a specific category that needs authorisation, and a third, highest-risk category in which the drone, the operator and the pilot each need certification. No one may own, register or operate a drone with military specifications (21.3.1.1(b)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="warn" title="Two texts, two sets of classes"}
NCAA also publishes its own edition of Part 21 ([April 2023, Amendment 4](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/l2vjnlce/nig-cars-part-21-remotely-piloted-aircraft-system.pdf)), which has three mass classes instead of five, and the [RPAS Portal](https://rpas.ncaa.gov.ng/) says specific-category drones "must weigh between 25 and 150 kg". The Gazette text is the legal instrument, so this guide follows it. Where the texts differ, ask NCAA in writing.

::::section{id="approvals" tone="alt" eyebrow="The approvals" title="What a commercial survey operator needs, one by one"}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="An RPAS Operator Certificate with Operations Specifications"}
"No person may operate an RPA except in accordance with the terms and conditions of the ROC" (21.4.1.1). The ROC is a certificate plus Operations Specifications (21.4.1.4(a)), which list the operations allowed (visual line of sight, extended, beyond visual line of sight, beyond radio line of sight) and "special limitations (e.g. day-only, population density and altitude)". The application asks for the corporate structure and major financial interests, the Accountable Officer, the Head of Operations, the Quality and Safety Manager and the Maintenance Controller, the operations and maintenance control manuals, proof of financial capability, any expatriate quota and the security clearance. The form note and the advisory circular ask for the initial application at least 90 days before operations and the operations manual at least 60 days before, and a ROC not used for 12 months after it is granted must be revalidated (AC-AWS061 App. 4).
:::
:::step{title="A registration certificate for each drone"}
Only Nigerian citizens, permanent residents, Nigerian-incorporated companies whose aircraft are "based and primarily used in Nigeria", and government bodies may register (21.3.1.1(a)). The application needs proof of ownership, the fee and an End-User Certificate (21.3.1.2(b)), and the certificate cannot be transferred (21.3.1.2(d)). Registration is not a commercial permit: commercial authorisation comes later, through document evaluation.
:::
:::step{title="ONSA security clearance"}
"No person shall operate a RPA for Commercial purposes in Nigeria without security clearance issued by … ONSA" (AC-AWS061 App. 5 §3; App. 4 §3 says the same for government-civil and corporate use). NCAA forwards the file to ONSA, and the Director General "shall refuse to grant a ROC" to an applicant who is not cleared. Part 21 requires applicants to "undergo and obtain a Security Clearance and End User Certificate from the appropriate Security Agency" (21.13.1.1(a)).
:::
:::step{title="An RPAS operator security programme"}
Submitted to NCAA for approval before any operation (21.13.1.1(b)) and "implemented and in full operation" within 30 days of approval (21.13.1.1(e)), with a Ground Security Coordinator (21.13.1.6(a)(1)). It covers the security of the command-and-control link and the pilot station, tamper-evident storage, awareness training and records.
:::
:::step{title="A Remote Pilot Licence for each pilot"}
Under Part 2 (2.3.11): aged 18 or over, a Class 3 or Class 1 medical, the knowledge areas and a skill test. Night flying needs dual night training. In controlled airspace, the licence itself counts as the qualification for aeronautical radio (21.9.6.32(c)(2)).
:::
:::step{title="Airworthiness, maintenance and safety management"}
The aircraft is approved under Part 5, with a certificate of airworthiness where applicable (21.9.1.2(c)); a drone over 25 kg is maintained and released to service by an approved maintenance organisation or authorised persons (21.9.6.29(c)). The operator runs a safety management system under Part 20 (21.6.1.1), a fatigue-management programme (21.9.6.22) and an NCAA-approved hazard-identification system (21.9.6.34).
:::
:::step{title="Insurance"}
"A minimum insurance policy, commensurate with the risk … in respect of the RPAS and third party risks", with proof lodged with NCAA (21.17.1.1(a)). NCAA may waive it by type of operation (21.17.1.1(b)), but its advisory circular treats it as mandatory for commercial, corporate and government-civil operations (AC-AWS061 §14.1.1).
:::
:::step{title="The Part 11 aerial-work application"}
Anyone intending RPA aerial work applies on form IS 11.1.1.1(F), which lists "Aerial mapping; Aerial surveying; Aerial photography" (11.1.1.1(f)). The form asks for the dates, the route, the cruising level, emergency set-down sites, recovery and clean-up contacts, the ROC and pilot licence numbers, the insurer, the camera, where any video link transmits to, the frequency band and the radio station licence. Whether it is filed for each job or once for a programme is not stated; ask NCAA.
:::
:::step{title="The PAAS and Aerial Work Certificate question"}
Part 11 says: "No person may engage in aerial work operation unless he/she is a holder of a Permit for Aerial Aviation Services (PAAS) and an Aerial Work Certificate issued by the Authority under Part 18" (11.1.1.4(a)). A PAAS is applied for 12 months ahead, needs a minimum paid-up share capital, an aviation professional on the board and a Nigerian majority shareholding, is notified in the Official Gazette and lasts three years (IS 18.3.6.1(A); 18.3.6.1(d)). Whether a drone operator with a ROC also needs a PAAS and an Aerial Work Certificate is unresolved in the published texts. Get NCAA's answer in writing.
:::
:::step{title="A flight plan for every operation"}
Filed before each operation, with NCAA and ATC told of any cancellation or change (21.9.1.2(b)(2)). A radio station licence is needed "if applicable" (21.9.1.2(e)(1)(x)).
:::
:::

NCAA charges fees for registration and certification under its [Schedule of Fees](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/k1rfvnjj/nigcars-ncaa-schedule-of-fees-and-charges.pdf) (S.I. 83, 25 September 2023), with annual and utilisation fees on top. Confirm the current amounts with NCAA; this page quotes none.

::::section{id="phases" eyebrow="The certification path" title="Five phases, and the Portal's nine steps"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
NCAA's circular on the ROC process ([NCAA-AC-GEN007, April 2023](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/zavci03g/ncaa-ac-gen-007.pdf)) sets out five phases:

:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Pre-application"}
The pre-application statement of intent, form AC-AW-061E.
:::
:::step{title="Formal application"}
Form AC-AWS-061F, with the operations manual, the maintenance control manual and the compliance statement.
:::
:::step{title="Document evaluation"}
Including the ONSA security clearance and the End-User Certificate, which the circular places before any drone is imported for commercial use (§6.1).
:::
:::step{title="Demonstration and inspection"}
NCAA sees the operation and the equipment.
:::
:::step{title="Certification"}
The ROC and Operations Specifications are issued, followed by a conformity audit twice a year.
:::
:::
::::
::::col
The [RPAS Portal](https://rpas.ncaa.gov.ng/) (read 26 September 2026) runs specific and highest-risk applications through nine steps:

1. Fill in the drone's details.
2. Upload a letter of intent.
3. Receive an email with a unique ID.
4. Pay the fee.
5. Submit the registration form.
6. The **ONSA screening exercise**.
7. Submit the documents for review.
8. Demonstration and inspection.
9. Certification.

No official timeline is published for ONSA screening or the End-User Certificate, so build them into the schedule rather than assume them.

::::section{id="corridor-rules" tone="alt" eyebrow="Flying a right of way" title="The rules that shape a corridor survey" lead="Pipelines and power lines run past towns, farms, airports and oil installations. These are the Part 21 rules a flight plan along them has to fit, from the Gazette text of 22 September 2023."}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Height, sight and daylight" icon="drone"}
Under the standard conditions a drone flies within the pilot's visual line of sight, at or below 120 m (400 ft) above ground, by day, with one drone per pilot (21.9.6.4). Above 120 m it may fly only more than 4 km from an aerodrome boundary, in Class G airspace, and in segregated airspace or with a NOTAM requested at least 24 hours ahead (21.9.6.30(c)).
:::
:::card{title="Beyond visual line of sight" icon="route"}
Needs NCAA authorisation after an operational safety risk assessment and coordination with ATC, and must stay within radio line of sight unless specially authorised (21.9.6.6). Under visual flight rules it also needs visual meteorological conditions and one of: detect-and-avoid or other mitigation, an area void of other traffic, or segregated airspace.
:::
:::card{title="People and populated areas" icon="houses"}
At least 30 m horizontally from anyone not associated with the operation, or 15 m with their consent (21.9.6.18). The standard conditions exclude flight "over a populated area", and operations "over heavily populated areas or over open air assemblies of people shall require special considerations" (21.9.6.7). Stretches of right of way lined with homes need Operations Specifications that allow it, or satellite imagery.
:::
:::card{title="Night and high-tension lines" icon="power"}
A special authorisation, requested at least 30 days ahead, is needed for night flying and for "areas of high RF transmission/interference (e.g. radar sites, high tension wires)" (21.9.6.21). Plan one for any flight along a transmission line.
:::
:::card{title="Controlled airspace" icon="map"}
ATC authorisation, a radio qualification, a listening watch and broadcasts (21.9.6.32). NCAA's All Operators Letter of 20 June 2025 prohibits flying "within controlled or restricted airspace without first notifying and obtaining clearance from the relevant ATC authorities", notes that occurrences are "prevalent in Lagos and Port Harcourt airspaces … especially in Port Harcourt due to low flying helicopter operations", and warns of suspension or revocation of operational permissions.
:::
:::card{title="Aerodromes" icon="flag"}
No flying within 4 km of an aerodrome without, for an uncontrolled aerodrome, the operator's agreement, an observer and a ceiling of 400 ft, or, for a controlled aerodrome, ATC authorisation (21.9.6.36). Recreational flyers stay 10 km away (21.1.1.5(a)(iii)).
:::
:::card{title="Landowners and right-of-way holders" icon="boundary"}
An NCAA approval confers no rights "as against the owner or occupier of any land" (AC-AWS061 §12.7.1). Get written consent from the right-of-way holder or landowner for each site.
:::
:::card{title="Incidents" icon="alert"}
Notify NCAA "by the quickest available means"; a written report is due within 72 hours (21.9.6.19). Accidents also go to the NSIB (21.9.6.19(b)).
:::

::::section{id="zones" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="Where not to fly without extra approval"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Military protected places" icon="shield"}
Entering, photographing or recording a protected place "for any purpose prejudicial to the security of Nigeria" is an offence, and the purpose can be presumed from "character or general conduct" ([Official Secrets Act, s.2](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=421)), with up to 14 years' imprisonment (s.7). Part 21 also forbids using a drone to "observe and photograph restricted places" (21.13.1.6(a)(5)). Stay clear of military sites and mask them in every deliverable.
:::
:::card{title="Oil and gas installations" icon="pipeline"}
Part 21 sets no numeric exclusion zone around flow stations, terminals or pipelines. What applies instead is the owner's consent (the privacy clause and AC-AWS061 §12.7.1), NUPRC's requirement that drone-based security services show NCAA and ONSA approvals (OGISP 2024, cl. 2.1.12), and NCAA's warning about low-flying helicopters around Port Harcourt. Good practice, though not a written rule: plan every flight through the asset owner's security department.
:::
:::card{title="Published restricted areas" icon="map"}
Prohibited, restricted and danger areas are published in the Nigeria AIP, which NAMA's AIS department produces ([NAMA](https://nama.gov.ng/purchasing-ais-products/)). Part 21 forbids flying over "prohibited or restricted airspace/area/infrastructure such as airports and navigational aids" (21.13.1.6(a)(4)). Check the AIP and NOTAMs before every job.
:::
:::card{title="Offshore and the coast" icon="water"}
Any operation over the high seas is coordinated with the air traffic services authority first (21.9.1.2(b)(1)(iv)). AfriScan maps land: shore crossings and onshore facilities are in scope, offshore facilities are not.
:::

::::section{id="import" tone="alt" eyebrow="Importing equipment" title="The ONSA End-User Certificate"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
ONSA's list of controlled items covers drones (HS 8806) in every weight band, including those of "not more than 250g", and two-way radios (HS 85256011) ([full HS list](https://euc.nsa.gov.ng/full-hs-codes/)). An End-User Certificate is part of Customs clearance, and drone registration itself needs one.

The drone guideline ([ONSA, Remotely Piloted Aircraft](https://euc.nsa.gov.ng/remotely-piloted-aircraft/)) asks for:

- a formal application letter from NCAA;
- the certificate of incorporation, the directors' profile and Form CO7;
- the passport data pages of the directors or owners;
- a letter of introduction from the acquirer;
- a proforma invoice or proof of ownership;
- a current-year tax clearance certificate.

"ALL EUC requests/applications are subject to screening by the Department of State Services", and private companies' requests "will be recommended through the Office of the Commandant General, Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC)" ([general guidelines](https://euc.nsa.gov.ng/general-guidelines/)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="The general rules"}
- Biometrics are required.
- Apply only for items needed now.
- "All EUCs are valid for only 1 year."
- "EUC applications are free of charge at every stage."
- NCAA's circular places the clearance and the certificate before any drone is imported for commercial use.

::::section{id="privacy" eyebrow="Privacy and personal data" title="Part 21's privacy clause and the NDPA 2023"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### The privacy clause {#privacy-clause}

"No person shall use a RPAS to … conduct surveillance of (i) A person without the person's consent. (ii) Private real property without the consent of the owner" (21.18.1.1). Photographing or filming an individual without consent "for the purpose of publishing" is also forbidden.

This is one reason AfriScan maps land and assets, never people. A right-of-way register lists structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks, with their distance to the line; it does not identify who lives or works there.
::::
::::col
### The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 {#ndpa}

The [NDPA](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Nigeria-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf) applies to anyone processing personal data in Nigeria, or processing the data of people in Nigeria from abroad (s.2). Imagery that shows identifiable homes can carry personal data. The sections that matter for a survey:

- **s.39:** security measures, such as pseudonymisation, encryption and regular testing.
- **s.40:** a processor tells the controller of a breach; the controller notifies the Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach likely to put people's rights and freedoms at risk, and tells the people affected immediately if the risk is high.
- **s.41:** a transfer abroad needs a recorded basis that gives adequate protection.
- **s.44:** controllers and processors "of major importance" register with the Commission, including the countries they transfer data to.
- **s.48:** fines linked to annual gross revenue, and orders for compensation.

The Commission's website blocks automated access, so its implementing guidance was not reviewed for this page; check it with counsel.

::::section{id="survey-law" tone="alt" eyebrow="Survey law" title="Notice to the Director of Federal Surveys, and SURCON"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The [Survey Co-ordination Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=501) requires at least **three months' notice** to the Director of Federal Surveys before any "survey work", "or such shorter period as the Director may allow" (s.1). Survey work includes "aerial photography for survey purposes", topographic surveys and airborne heighting, and the notice comes with a sketch map. Within a month the Director may require the surveyor to erect survey marks, to "process all aerial films … in Nigeria, and submit them for examination", to hand over two copies of every map and "the original or a copy of the negatives of all aerial photographs", and to connect the work to government survey marks within 16.09 km. Directors of a company in breach are liable too (s.2).

Work by or for the armed forces or government survey departments, areas exempted by regulation, and surveys "solely for the purpose of determining boundaries" are exempt. OSGOF's Survey Coordination Department applies the Act, and OSGOF has held a stakeholder workshop on a Survey Coordination Bill 2025 whose status we have not confirmed.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Open question"}
Whether the three-month notice applies to drone orthophoto projects, and to mapping derived from satellite imagery, is a question for your counsel. Every AfriScan proposal for Nigerian work flags it.
:::

The Surveyors Council of Nigeria (SURCON) registers surveyors and enlists survey firms, and NUPRC's service-permit "Survey" categories ask for proof of SURCON membership. Where a survey plan must be signed, a SURCON-registered surveyor signs it; a structure register is not a survey plan.

::::section{id="foreign" eyebrow="Foreign operators and crews" title="Two routes for aircraft and operators from abroad"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="NCAA approval under Part 10.8" icon="globe"}
A foreign operator flying remotely piloted aircraft in Nigeria needs NCAA authorisation with Operations Specifications, applied for at least 90 days ahead, and a flight plan for each operation ([Part 10, 10.8.1.1](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/0qll0mio/nigcars-part-10-commercial-air-transport-by-foreign-air-operators-within-nigeria.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="A temporary authorisation" icon="clock"}
For drones not registered in Nigeria, a temporary authorisation lasts 21 days and can be renewed (21.9.6.20). The regulation ties it to ROC holders in Nigeria, and the applicant must have a foreign ROC.
:::
:::

Foreign crew also bring in immigration rules: the ROC application asks for any expatriate quota, and for oil and gas work an operator needs the Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board's approval before applying for one (NOGICD Act, s.33). Imported drones and radios need End-User Certificates. See [Nigerian content and procurement](/ng/nigerian-content).

::::section{id="penalties" tone="alt" eyebrow="Enforcement" title="Penalties"}
- **Civil Aviation Act 2022, s.78:** civil penalties; NCAA may detain aircraft, "seal or close offices and or operations", and seize or take a lien over aircraft and other assets.
- **s.79:** liability for damage on the ground without proof of negligence. The Act's caps on that liability apply only if the operator proves there was no negligence, and an advance payment is due within 30 days.
- **s.80, s.81 and s.82(4):** fines or imprisonment for forgery and false marks, for dangerous flying (the pilot and the owner) and for an operator's failure to make required reports.
- **NCAA All Operators Letter 24/29:** suspension or revocation of operational permissions for flying in controlled airspace without clearance.
- **Official Secrets Act, s.7:** up to 14 years' imprisonment.
- **NDPA 2023, s.48:** fines linked to annual gross revenue.

NCAA's advisory circular AC-AWS061 §15 still cites the 2006 Civil Aviation Act, which the 2022 Act superseded.

::::section{id="open-points" eyebrow="Before you rely on a figure" title="Where NCAA's own documents differ"}
We compared the Gazette texts with NCAA's circulars, its own edition of Part 21 and the RPAS Portal. On these points they disagree, so this guide gives no figure and suggests asking NCAA in writing:

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- How long a ROC is valid: Part 21 (21.4.1.4(c)(1)), NCAA-AC-GEN007 (§9) and NCAA-AC-AWS061 (App. 4 and 5, §4) each give a different period
- How early to apply for renewal: 21.4.1.6(b) and the Part 21 form note (with AC-AWS061 §11.5) differ
- The mass classes: five in the Gazette text, three in NCAA's April 2023 edition
- The specific category: risk-based in Part 21, a 25–150 kg weight band on the Portal
- The registration certificate: NCAA's edition reads as if registration entitles the holder to operate commercially, which looks like a missing "not"
- Whether an operator with a ROC also needs a PAAS and an Aerial Work Certificate for aerial work (Part 11, 11.1.1.4(a)), and whether form IS 11.1.1.1(F) is filed per job
:::

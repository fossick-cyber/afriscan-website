faq:
  - q: Is your baseline a substitute for the census and asset inventory?
    a: No. It is a dated record of the structures in the footprint, with imagery, coordinates and IDs, that supports your census and asset inventory under IFC Performance Standard 5 and your compensation enumeration. Field teams still enumerate people and assets, and compensation needs ground verification.
  - q: Does the imagery set the cut-off date?
    a: No. The cut-off date comes from your resettlement process. Our dated record documents what was on the ground on the imagery dates around it, and re-surveys after it show which structures are new.
  - q: Do you value structures for compensation?
    a: No. Valuation stays with your valuers and the authorities. The Land Use Act values buildings at replacement cost less depreciation (s.29(4)); we supply the dated inventory of which structures stood where, with reviewer categories such as main building, outbuilding or under construction, which the valuation starts from.
  - q: Is an onshore pipeline on the EIA Act's mandatory list?
    a: Not as a named item. The Act's mandatory list names offshore pipelines over 50 kilometres, oil and gas field development, processing and storage facilities, refineries and large product depots near homes. Screening for an onshore line goes through the 2021 EIA Procedures and the Federal Ministry of Environment; your consultant will confirm the category.
  - q: Can you work as a subcontractor to our ESIA or RAP team?
    a: Yes. We supply structure baselines, estimated households, land-cover baselines and GIS layers for your own maps and reports, in the formats and coordinate reference system your team uses.
  - q: Can your evidence packs be used in legal proceedings?
    a: They support legal and community processes; we never describe them as court-grade. File fingerprints and an independent timestamp show that the files existed at that time and have not changed since. They don't prove how the imagery was captured.
  - q: How are household numbers produced?
    a: As estimates, from reviewed structure counts and stated persons-per-household assumptions. Every report sets the assumptions out, so your team can replace them with the figures from your own socio-economic survey.

:::::columns{split="1-1"}

### EIA Act mandatory studies {#mandatory-studies}

The [Environmental Impact Assessment Act](https://ead.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/EIA_Act.pdf) (Decree No. 86 of 1992, Cap E12) lists projects for which a study is mandatory. For energy and linear projects they include:

- oil and gas field development;
- "off-shore pipelines in exceed of 50 kilometres";
- separation, processing, handling and storage facilities, and refineries;
- product depots of 60,000 barrels or more within 3 km of homes, businesses or industry;
- fossil-fuel steam power stations over 10 MW;
- agricultural programmes that resettle 100 families or more.

Onshore pipelines are not a named item, so their category is settled under the [2021 EIA Procedures](https://ead.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2021-EIA-Procedures-and-Charges.pdf) with the Federal Ministry of Environment.
::::
::::col
### Land, compensation and resettlement {#compensation}

| Instrument | What it provides |
|---|---|
| [Land Use Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=228), s.28 | Revocation for "overriding public interest", which includes land for "mining purposes or oil pipelines" |
| Land Use Act, s.29 | Compensation under the mining or petroleum law in those cases (s.29(2)); community compensation to the community, its chief or a fund (s.29(3)); buildings at replacement cost less depreciation (s.29(4)) |
| Land Use Act, s.33 | Resettlement may be offered instead of compensation for a residential building |
| [Oil Pipelines Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=425), s.6 | Notice before entering buildings or enclosed gardens on a pipeline survey, with compensation for damage |
| [Petroleum Industry Act 2021](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Petroleum-Industry-Act-2021.pdf), s.115 | Compensation for land acquired for petroleum operations |
| [IFC Performance Standard 5](https://www.ifc.org/en/insights-reports/2012/ifc-performance-standard-5) | The census, the asset inventory and the cut-off date on lender-financed projects |

World Bank-financed projects in Nigeria, such as the DARES energy-access programme, apply the Bank's own Environmental and Social Standard on land acquisition and resettlement.

::::section{id="baselines" eyebrow="For ESIA teams" title="The spatial baseline, as GIS layers and maps"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
An ESIA baseline needs to show what is around the project before it starts: settlements and structures, land cover and land use, flood-prone ground and terrain, and how all of it has been changing. We deliver each as a layer your team can load into its own GIS and map in its own report:

- **Structures** in the footprint and in the rings around it that your study area sets, reviewed by a person, with categories where scoped;
- **Settlement growth trends**, showing where built-up land has expanded year by year;
- **Land use and land cover**: built-up land, cropland, bare ground, water and tree cover, from open datasets that are credited;
- **Terrain and flood exposure**: steep and erosion-prone stretches and ground that has flooded in past satellite records;
- **Estimated households**, with the assumptions stated, for consultation planning and to size the socio-economic survey;
- **Imagery history** of the footprint: what was built, cleared or changed, and roughly when.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What a baseline layer is, and isn't"}
The land-cover classes are generalised: it is not a cadastral, agricultural or ecological survey. Flood screening is not a hydraulic model. Households are estimates, not a census. Every layer names its source and date.
:::

::::section{id="cut-off-records" tone="alt" eyebrow="Resettlement and compensation" title="A dated record at the cut-off, then what arrived after it"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
At the cut-off date, we record the structures in the footprint from dated imagery, a drone survey or a satellite scene with a known capture date, never from undated basemap tiles. Each structure gets an ID, coordinates, a photo crop and, where scoped, a reviewer category that describes what the imagery shows. That register supports the census and asset inventory under IFC Performance Standard 5, and the compensation enumeration for a right of way under the Land Use Act.

After the cut-off, the footprint is re-surveyed on a schedule you agree with us. New structures are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, and each re-survey can be packaged as an evidence pack: the imagery, the registers and the maps with file fingerprints and an independent timestamp, to support legal and community processes.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Where the date comes from"}
The record reflects the imagery's capture date. For a cut-off date, we look for dated scenes close to it and tell you what exists before you commit. A new satellite capture can be requested when the archive is too old; its date depends on satellite availability and weather, so it is never guaranteed.
:::

::::section{id="independent-monitoring" eyebrow="For lenders and monitors" title="Dated evidence between site visits"}
Between monitoring visits, dated imagery shows where land clearing, construction, settlement around the site or resettlement villages have moved on, so the next visit goes to the places that changed. For a single grievance or claim, a location check puts every dated image that covers the point on one sheet, so your team can see what was there on each date. It supports an investigation; it does not decide the claim, and it does not replace the monitoring your lenders or regulators require.

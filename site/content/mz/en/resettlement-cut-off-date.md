faq:
  - q: Does the imagery set the cut-off date?
    a: No. The cut-off date is set by the host government's procedures or, where there are none, by the project, and it must be clearly established and made public (IFC Performance Standard 5). Our register records what stood in the project area on a date you choose; it supports the cut-off-date record rather than defining it.
  - q: Is the register a substitute for the census or the asset inventory?
    a: No. The census, the inventory of assets and improvements, the public consultations and the eligibility decisions stay with your resettlement team. The register tells the field teams where the structures are, how many to expect and which ones have appeared since the cut-off date.
  - q: Can you use drone orthophotos for the baseline?

Dated satellite scenes and imagery you already hold are the alternatives.
  - q: Can the register be in Portuguese for the district and the resettlement commission?

  - q: Do you identify the households?
    a: No. We map structures, not people. Where you need a planning figure, we estimate households from structure counts with the assumptions stated, for your team to replace with census data.

::::section{id="framework" eyebrow="What the rules ask for" title="Mozambique's resettlement regulation, the sector laws and PS5"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Decreto n.º 31/2012 {#regulation}

The [Regulamento sobre o Processo de Reassentamento Resultante de Actividades Económicas](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/644941485846310129/pdf/SFG2931-REVISED-RP-PORTUGUESE-P161351-Box402883B-PUBLIC-Disclosed-2-1-2017.pdf) starts with data collection: identifying and delimiting the area, quantifying the affected families, recording its physical characteristics and its *ocupação actual*, its current occupation (art. 19). It asks for at least four public consultations (art. 23). The plan is part of the environmental impact assessment process, and the district government approves it once the spatial-planning sector has issued its conformity; a technical commission oversees the process.

### The sector laws {#sector-laws}

The rights holder bears resettlement costs after prior consultation, and the people affected must be left with living conditions that are dignified and better than before ([Lei n.º 8/2026, art. 61](https://www.inp.gov.mz/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/BR_104_I_SERIE_2.o_SUPLEMENTO_2026.pdf)). For power lines, the resettlement and compensation rules apply to the servitude, and no compensation is owed to those who acquired their rights after the line was built ([Lei n.º 12/2022, art. 43(8) and (10)](https://arene.org.mz/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Lei-de-Electricidade-2022.pdf)).
::::
::::col
### IFC Performance Standard 5 {#ps5}

Lender-funded projects add [IFC Performance Standard 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf). It asks for a census, in part to discourage ineligible persons, such as opportunistic settlers, from claiming benefits, and, in the absence of host-government procedures, for the client to establish a cut-off date for eligibility (para. 12). The client is not required to compensate or assist those who encroach on the project area after the cut-off date, provided the date has been clearly established and made public (para. 23).

:::callout{tone="legal" title="What a dated register supports"}
A dated, reproducible record of the structures in the project area is useful evidence for both frameworks: it shows what stood where on the date that matters. It supports your census and your IFC Performance Standard 5 cut-off-date records. It does not set the cut-off date, and it does not replace the census, the asset inventory or the consultations.
:::

::::section{id="dated-imagery" eyebrow="The imagery" title="Why the date on the imagery matters"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A cut-off-date record is only as good as the date on its imagery. We build it on imagery with a known capture date: a drone survey, a very-high-resolution satellite scene with its acquisition date, or georeferenced imagery you already hold. Map-service basemaps, which carry no capture date, are never used for it.

Archive coverage and cloud vary by place and season, so the proposal says what exists for your area and dates before you commit. A new satellite capture can be requested when the archive is too old; its date depends on satellite availability and weather, and is never guaranteed.

In Mozambique, a drone baseline needs the Defence authorisation of the survey and the authorisation to release its data to you, and the orthophotos may not be reproduced in the plan, its annexes or anywhere else without authorisation ([Lei n.º 6/2024, arts. 13 and 16(1)(c)](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/mz/2024/mz-government-gazette-series-i-dated-2024-06-04-no-108.pdf)). Build that into the schedule.

### What it supports

:::checklist
- Census and asset-inventory planning
- Your IFC Performance Standard 5 cut-off-date records
- Community engagement and grievance handling, with dated facts
- Comparison of route and site options
:::

::::col
### What it does not do

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Set the cut-off date or decide who is eligible
- Carry out the census, the asset inventory or the public consultations
- Count crops, trees or other improvements: your field teams do that
- Identify households or people, or see roofs hidden under dense tree cover
:::

  - q: Do you measure from the pipe centreline?
    a: We measure from the line in the route file you supply, in its UTM zone, so distances are in true metres. If the file is the centreline and your concession or safety study counts a zone from the edge of an installation or from the servitude boundary, tell us and we set the bands to match. Any drawn route is sent back for your confirmation before the survey runs.
  - q: Can you report the Pande–Temane 200 m safety zone?
    a: Yes. Decreto n.º 36/2001 sets a 50 m partial protection zone and a 200 m safety zone on each side of the Pande–Temane gas pipeline corridor and around its processing facility. We report both, alongside the 100 m band, and list each structure with its distance to the line and the zone it falls in.
  - q: Does a structure inside the 50 m zone mean it is unlawful?
    a: Not necessarily, and it is not for us to decide. No DUAT can be acquired in a partial protection zone, but special licences can be issued there (Lei n.º 19/97, art. 9), and some structures predate the line. The register records what stands where, and when; its status is for your land team and the authorities.
  - q: Can you detect leaks, illegal tapping or theft?
    a: No. We map what is visible at the surface, such as structures, fresh digging, spoil heaps, tracks and cleared ground, and flag it so your field teams know where to look. The pipe, its condition and anything underground are outside what imagery can show.
  - q: Can the report go to the district government in Portuguese?

  - q: How often can the route be re-surveyed?
    a: On a schedule agreed with you, campaign by campaign. How often depends on when new imagery exists for the route, on cloud in the rainy season and, for drone stretches, on the authorisations each survey needs. After each campaign your team receives a notice of what changed and where.
  - q: Do you work offshore?
    a: No. We map land. The shore crossing, onshore pipelines and onshore plants are in scope; offshore facilities are not.

:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="“There are new houses inside the 50 m zone.”" icon="houses"}
No land-use right can be acquired in a partial protection zone, yet homesteads, stalls and outbuildings appear there, especially where the line runs beside a road or a village grows towards it. Each is easier to discuss while it is new.
:::
:::card{title="“We need to know what to count before the census.”" icon="clipboard"}
Machambas and benfeitorias are counted by your census and asset-inventory teams. A register from the air tells them where the structures and cleared plots are, how far each is from the line, and which stretches need people first.
:::
:::card{title="“Someone has been digging near the line.”" icon="excavation"}
Trenches, pits, borrow areas and spoil heaps near a buried line are found late when patrols cover the route on a cycle. We flag fresh ground disturbance between campaigns so your teams know where to check.
:::
:::card{title="“We can't get to that stretch often.”" icon="route"}
Long corridors cross land where access is slow, costly or, near some facilities, restricted. Satellite screening needs no one on site, so the baseline starts without anyone travelling.

| Instrument | What it sets for pipelines |
|---|---|
| [Lei n.º 19/97, de 1 de Outubro (Lei de Terras)](https://www.pdul.gov.mz/content/download/486/2635/file/Lei%20de%20Terras.pdf), arts. 6, 8(g) and 9 | Oil, gas and water conduits, with a strip of 50 m on each side, are a partial protection zone: public domain, where no DUAT can be acquired, only special licences |
| [Lei n.º 8/2026, de 3 de Junho (Lei do Petróleo)](https://www.inp.gov.mz/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/BR_104_I_SERIE_2.o_SUPLEMENTO_2026.pdf), art. 75 | The 50 m around petroleum infrastructure is a partial protection zone (75(3)); the safety zone is set by regulation (75(4)); compensation is due for damage to crops, buildings and improvements (75(5)); rights of passage can be constituted (75(7)) |
| Lei n.º 8/2026, arts. 6(1), 61 and 76(2) | Strategic petroleum areas are under a special protection regime; the rights holder bears resettlement costs after prior consultation; licensing needs prior approval of the environmental impact assessment |
| [Decreto n.º 36/2001, de 20 de Novembro](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/moz50003.pdf) | Along the Pande–Temane gas pipeline corridor and around its processing and compression unit: a 50 m partial protection zone (art. 1), a 200 m safety zone (art. 2), and the project operator's prior consent for building there (art. 3) |
| [Draft Land Law, June 2024](https://www.agricultura.gov.mz/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/CRPNT-do-Ante-Projecto3-da-Lei-de-Terra-vJunho.2024-CIRCULAR-v1.pdf) | Keeps the 50 m strip for conduits, but would let the widths be reduced or increased (art. 15(4)). The Council of Ministers approved a Land Law proposal on [28 October 2025](https://aimnews.org/2025/10/29/governo-aprova-proposta-de-lei-de-terras/); check its status before relying on it |

We report structures within **50 m and 100 m** of the line by default, add the **200 m** safety zone on corridors that have one, and set any other width your concession, safety study or lender requires, up to six in one survey. Decreto n.º 36/2001 was made under the earlier petroleum law, and the new law leaves safety zones to regulation, so your counsel should confirm which safety zone applies to your asset. The [50 m partial protection zone explainer](/mz/50m-protection-zone) sets out every strip in the Land Law.

The regulator for petroleum operations is the Instituto Nacional de Petróleo (INP), the Autoridade Reguladora do Petróleo under Lei n.º 8/2026 (art. 37). ARENE regulates natural gas distribution and transport at pressures up to 16 bar ([Lei n.º 11/2017](https://arene.org.mz/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Lei-que-cria-a-arene.pdf)); high-pressure transmission sits under the petroleum regime.

::::section{id="corridors" eyebrow="Where the corridors run" title="Southern gas lines, and the new ones being planned"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Most of Mozambique's onshore gas infrastructure runs through the south. The Pande and Temane fields and their processing facility sit in Inhambane Province, and the 865 km Mozambique–Secunda gas pipeline crosses Inhambane, Gaza and Maputo provinces to South Africa. Its operator aims to raise capacity and to take regasified LNG from an import terminal at Matola ([World Pipelines, August 2025](https://www.worldpipelines.com/project-news/18082025/rompco-driving-regional-energy-security-in-southern-africa/)). These are long servitudes through farmland, bush and growing villages, where roads and settlements follow the line.

New corridors are being planned too. In November 2025 the Council of Ministers approved a concession for gas reception, storage, regasification and transport in the Port of Beira and at Inhassoro ([INP](https://www.inp.gov.mz/en/20-11-2025-mocambique-aprova-concessao-para-terminal-de-gas-natural-liquefeito-e-autoriza-medidas-para-a-retoma-do-projecto-golfinho-atum/)), and an agreement for a Beira–Ndola gas pipeline to Zambia was signed in May 2025 ([AIM](https://aimnews.org/2025/05/07/gasoduto-orcado-em-15-biliao-de-dolares-liga-mocambique-e-zambia/)).

### For a new line or loop {#new-pipelines}

The cheapest structure to deal with is the one the route avoids. We count the structures along each alternative alignment within the same bands, so route engineers and the land team can compare options on the land they would affect, alongside their engineering and environmental criteria.

Once the route is fixed, a dated baseline records the corridor before settlement follows the new access road, and estimated households help plan consultation and the census. The comparison is one input to route selection, not a full alternatives assessment.

::::section{id="rights-of-way" tone="alt" eyebrow="What we map along the line" title="A register of the structures inside the faixa, then what changed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the route you supply in its UTM zone (36S or 37S in Mozambique), list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the line and its band, and cut the route into 500 m stretches. Each stretch is rated for **encroachment density**: high where more than five structures stand inside the widest band, medium for one to five, low for none. It is a count rule that tells your servitude team where to go first, not a safety or integrity rating.

After the baseline, the route is re-surveyed campaign by campaign on a schedule you agree with us. New and removed structures between dated surveys are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views, and your team receives a notice after each campaign saying what changed and where. Fresh digging, spoil heaps and new tracks on or near the line are flagged the same way, so patrols know where to look. They do not replace patrols.

In the dry season, *queimadas* can run up to above-ground installations such as valve stations and scraper traps. We send notices of satellite-detected fire hotspots near them and map the burnt area afterwards.

::::section{id="plants" tone="alt" eyebrow="Plants and project areas" title="Around processing facilities, LNG sites and resettlement villages"}
Around a processing facility or compressor station, the line becomes a boundary and the question becomes a ring: what stands inside the site, what stands in a band around it, and what sits inside the safety zone. We map all three and show how settlement around the plant, its access roads and its resettlement villages grows between surveys. For LNG in the north, mining concessions and other large projects, see [LNG, mining and large projects in Mozambique](/mz/lng-and-mining).

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Your own facilities are not encroachment"}
An operator's plants, well pads, camps and yards are part of the asset. We record them as the operator's installations, never as encroachment, and keep them out of the encroachment register unless you ask for them to be listed separately.

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Drone checks near pipelines" title="Where a drone helps, and what it needs first"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Satellite screening narrows the search; a drone survey of the flagged stretches then captures the detail your team needs, subject to the permits and authorisations each job requires. In Mozambique, IACM's RPAS directive keeps flights at or below 400 ft and within the pilot's sight, up to 500 m away, with no flights beyond visual line of sight. Flying from a moving vehicle while keeping the aircraft in sight is possible in the commercial categories, with geofencing and a demonstration flight, which suits pipeline access roads. Flights near aerodromes, public roads, people or structures need their own approvals.

The survey itself needs the Minister of National Defence's authorisation, the data need authorisation before they are released to you, and orthophotos of Mozambique may not be reproduced or published without authorisation ([Lei n.º 6/2024, arts. 13 and 16](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/mz/2024/mz-government-gazette-series-i-dated-2024-06-04-no-108.pdf)). Afridrone is working towards the operator approvals Mozambique requires, and every drone proposal lists the authorisations that survey needs.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Near plants and strategic sites"}
Lei n.º 8/2026 puts strategic petroleum areas under a special protection regime (art. 6(1)), and Lei n.º 6/2024 restricts aircraft operations within at least 5 km of strategic national-security areas (art. 11(1)). IACM's directive allows no flights over national key points, military installations or places of strategic interest. Around such sites, satellite work is the practical route, planned with your security team.

::::section{id="local-content" tone="alt" eyebrow="Local content" title="What the 2026 laws mean for this service"}
Foreign providers of services to petroleum operations must associate with Mozambican individuals or companies, with real economic substance (Lei n.º 8/2026, art. 60(2)–(3)). The Local Content Law passes its obligations down to the rights holder's contractors and subcontractors, and lists *serviços de topografia* among the services reserved for qualifying Mozambican firms ([Lei n.º 9/2026, Annex II](https://www.inp.gov.mz/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/BR_104_I_SERIE_3.o_SUPLEMENTO_2026.pdf)).

Our registers are remote-sensing analyses of structures, not topographic or cadastral surveys; drone survey work is closer to *topografia*. Where a scope sits on that line, the proposal says so and names the question for your local-content team. [Procurement notes for Mozambique](/mz/procurement) set out the detail.

faq:
  - q: Can a foreign company fly commercial drone surveys in Mozambique?
    a: "IACM's directive issues approvals for corporate and commercial operations only to companies registered and domiciled in Mozambique (DOS-09-2018 §8.3.3(a)). Lei n.º 6/2024 lets the national private sector and foreign companies, duly authorised, carry out aerial coverage \"em último recurso\" where the State lacks the means (art. 10(2)); how far that reaches is a question for counsel. For oil and gas work, the Local Content Law also lists \"serviços de topografia\" among the reserved services (Lei n.º 9/2026, Annex II)."
  - q: Does the client need any authorisation?
    a: "The client needs to be sure the operator's authorisations are in place. Lei n.º 6/2024 applies to the \"entidades utilizadoras\" that acquire or receive aerial-survey products for their own use as well as to operators (art. 2(1)), and acquiring or using products of illegal origin is an infraction (art. 16(1)(d)). Ask for the Defence authorisation of the survey and the authorisation to release the data to you."
  - q: May we put drone orthophotos in our reports or on our website?
    a: Only with authorisation. Reproducing and disseminating aerial photographs, photographic mosaics and orthophotos without the authorisation of the competent authority is an infraction (Lei n.º 6/2024, art. 16(1)(c)). Agree in the contract who will seek that authorisation and for which uses.
  - q: How long do the approvals take?
    a: IACM's directive says IACM strives to complete an operator approval in 90 to 120 days. The time for the Defence authorisation and the data-release authorisation is not published; plan for it and ask the operator for its experience. Fees exist for each step; confirm the current amounts with IACM and the Ministry.
  - q: Can a drone fly beyond the pilot's line of sight along a pipeline?
    a: No. The directive does not permit operations beyond visual line of sight. Corridors are flown in legs within sight of the pilot, up to 500 m away and at or below 400 ft, or from a moving vehicle that keeps the aircraft in sight, which needs geofencing and a demonstration flight.
  - q: Is ANAC the aviation authority?
    a: No. In Mozambique, ANAC is the Administração Nacional das Áreas de Conservação, which manages conservation areas. The civil aviation authority is IACM. A flight over a conservation area needs the written permission of the agency that manages it, as well as IACM's approval.

Registration of each aircraft, pilot licences, the operator's Letter of Approval and Certificates of Safety, under the RPAS directive DOS-09-2018. A written approval is needed before any corporate or commercial flight.
:::
:::card{title="The survey" icon="shield" eyebrow="Ministry of National Defence"}
The Minister who oversees National Defence authorises the execution of aerial surveys in national territory, and classifies and certifies the permitted devices (Lei n.º 6/2024, art. 13(1)).
:::
:::card{title="The data" icon="file-check" eyebrow="Systematic cartography"}
The entity for systematic cartography, after hearing Defence and Security, authorises releasing the survey data to their users (art. 13(2)). The original data belong to the State and are deposited after processing (art. 14).

::::section{id="iacm" eyebrow="The flight" title="IACM's RPAS directive, DOS-09-2018"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
IACM's public notice of 10 June 2020 ([Anúncio VANTS](https://www.iacm.gov.mz/anuncio-vants/)) requires drone owners and users to comply with its safety directive of 16 September 2018, and asks owners to send IACM the owner's name, the drone's details and the coordinates, area and purpose of each flight (vantsmoz@iacm.gov.mz).

The directive sorts operations into categories. Survey work done for a client is **commercial, Category C1 or C2**. A pipeline or power company flying its own drones for its own business is **Category B**, a corporate operation. Recreational flying is Category A.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="warn" title="About the text"}
The only full text of DOS-09-2018 we found is an unofficial English copy annexed to a 2019 tender. It is marked as a first draft, cites the aviation law in force before Lei n.º 5/2016, and carries a different date from IACM's notice. Treat its details as indicative and ask IACM for the signed Portuguese text before relying on a clause.

Class 1 covers aircraft up to 5 kg, Class 2 from 5.1 to 20 kg, Class 3 from 20.1 to 150 kg; Class 4 is reserved. VLOS, D-VLOS and E-VLOS are explained under [flight limits](#limits).

### What an operator needs {#operator}

:::cards{cols="3" style="plain"}
:::card{title="A Mozambican company" icon="building"}
Approvals for Categories B, C1 and C2 are issued only to companies registered in Mozambique and domiciled there (§8.3.3(a)).
:::
:::card{title="Registered aircraft" icon="drone"}
An IACM Certificate of Registration for each aircraft, with registration marks in black on a yellow background and a fireproof metal identification plate. The directive caps an operator at five registered aircraft.
:::
:::card{title="Approved procedures" icon="file-text"}
Standard operating procedures, a safety case and a maintenance programme, then inspection and a demonstration flight for Categories B and C2. For C1 the Letter of Approval follows the document review.
:::
:::card{title="Letter of Approval and Certificates of Safety" icon="file-check"}
A Letter of Approval for the operator and a Certificate of Safety for each aircraft, each valid for 12 months. A Certificate of Safety is needed for every Class 2 or larger aircraft, for every C2 operation, and for Class 1 aircraft flown in extended line of sight, in controlled airspace or near people.
:::
:::card{title="Qualified pilots" icon="user-check"}
A remote pilot licence, age 18 or over, a Class 4 medical, a radiotelephony licence, ICAO English Level 4, type training with at least 5 hours logged, and logbooks. A licence from another ICAO state can be validated.
:::
:::card{title="Safety, security and insurance" icon="shield"}
A safety officer, a risk assessment for each flight or series of flights at one place, checklists, an emergency plan and a fire extinguisher within 10 m of the pilot; background checks, locked storage and site cordons; and third-party liability insurance.
:::
:::

The directive also lists fees for registration, the Certificate of Safety, procedure approval, the Letter of Approval and renewals, and states a minimum insurance amount whose wording is ambiguous. Confirm the current figures with IACM. IACM says it strives to complete an approval in 90 to 120 days.

::::section{id="limits" tone="alt" eyebrow="The flight" title="Flight limits, and what needs a specific approval"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### The basic envelope

:::checklist
- At or below 400 ft (about 120 m) above ground level
- Within the pilot's direct sight, up to 500 m away (VLOS)
- From a moving vehicle or vessel that keeps the aircraft in sight (D-VLOS), with geofencing and a demonstration flight
- With observers up to 1,000 m from the pilot (E-VLOS), in Category C2 only and with a specific approval
- Written IACM approval before any corporate or commercial flight, and IACM informed of each planned operation
:::

For a pipeline or power-line corridor, that means flying in legs of up to 500 m within sight of the pilot, or along an access road from a vehicle, below 400 ft.
::::
::::col
### Never permitted

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS)
- Above 400 ft above ground level
- Crossing the national border
- In instrument meteorological conditions or under instrument flight rules
- Dropping anything, or carrying dangerous goods, weapons or explosives
- Over national key points, police stations, nuclear facilities, hospitals, prisons, courts, military or other government installations, or any other place of strategic interest
:::

The directive calls these flights illegal: those who perform them risk prosecution.
::::
:::::

### Prohibited unless IACM gives a specific approval

| Activity | Who may apply | Key conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Night | B, C2 | Lights; recent night flying; a Certificate of Safety for Class 2 and above |
| Extended line of sight | C2 | At most two observers; aircraft within 500 m of an observer; at least 10 m from people and structures |
| Within 5 km of an aerodrome | C2 | The aerodrome operator's written permission; at least 5 days' notice; VHF radio with position calls; at or below 400 ft |
| Controlled airspace | C2 | A Certificate of Safety and a demonstration flight; a flight plan to air traffic services; a NOTAM; day, visual conditions only |
| Over or within 50 m of people | C2, Classes 1–2 | Only people who are part of the operation, informed beforehand; at least 50 ft above ground |
| Within 50 m of structures | B, C1, C2; Classes 1–2 | The structure owner's written permission; a cordon; **no closer than 5 m laterally and 20 ft vertically to live high-tension wires** |
| Over or on a public road | C2 | The local police's permission; a 100 m cordon on each side; crossing at right angles at 100 ft or more |
| Within 50 m of a public road | B, C1, C2 | At least 10 m from the road; geofencing; day, visual conditions |
| Restricted, prohibited or danger areas | B, C1, C2 | The written permission of the agency that manages the airspace; IACM's examples include mine blasting areas, flight training areas and conservation areas |

Anything not in the directive's table cannot be applied for.

::::section{id="lei-6-2024" eyebrow="The survey and the data" title="Lei n.º 6/2024: Defence authorisation, State data and publication"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
[Lei n.º 6/2024, de 4 de Junho](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/mz/2024/mz-government-gazette-series-i-dated-2024-06-04-no-108.pdf) sets the rules for aerial surveys and aerial filming for civil purposes in national territory, and for supplying their products to the users. The Assembleia da República approved it on 28 March 2024; it was published in the Boletim da República on 4 June 2024 and took effect on publication (art. 19).

**It binds the client too.** The law applies to all operators and to the *entidades utilizadoras*, anyone who acquires or receives products of aerial surveys for their own use (art. 2(1)). People flying for leisure or personal purposes are excluded (art. 2(2)).

**Who may fly.** Aerial photography for official systematic cartography is the State's responsibility (art. 10(1)). The national private sector and foreign companies, duly authorised, may carry out aerial coverage *em último recurso*, where the State lacks the means (art. 10(2)), with security conditions that include specialists from Defence and Security and from Cartography in the technical teams (art. 10(3)). Whether *em último recurso* reaches every private survey or only official cartography is a point for counsel.

**Restrictions.** Aircraft operations, unmanned ones included, are restricted in the airspace around strategic national-security areas within a radius of at least 5 km (art. 11(1)). Those areas are the ones subject to military servitude and any others the Council of Ministers or the intelligence service designate, and aerial imagery covering them is classified information. Drones at public events and crowds need prior authorisation (art. 11(2)), and surveys may be banned in a state of emergency, siege or war (art. 11(3)).

**Safety and coordination.** Each survey needs weather information, certification of the equipment, an obstacle-free site, a flight plan with its points of interest, heights and distances from people, environmental safety and a contingency plan (art. 12(1)), and must be coordinated with the Defence and Security authorities (art. 12(2)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="The data belong to the State"}
The original films or data of aerial coverage by the national private sector and foreign companies are the property of the Mozambican State (art. 14(1)). After processing, the operator hands the systematic-cartography, National Defence and geospatial-development entities:

- the coverage scheme;
- the navigation data;
- the camera calibration certificate;
- a copy of the digital and photographic data;
- the metric-photo data;
- a detailed processing report (art. 14(2)).
:::

### Infractions and sanctions {#infractions}

Article 16(1) makes these infractions:

:::cards{cols="2" style="plain"}
:::card{title="For the operator" icon="alert"}
Surveying without prior authorisation; refusing inspection; delivering the data only partly, or not at all; surveying in strategic security areas; flying drones at public events without authorisation.
:::
:::card{title="For operator and client alike" icon="alert"}
Reproducing and disseminating aerial photographs, photographic mosaics and orthophotos without the competent authority's authorisation (16(1)(c)); acquiring and using products of aerial surveys of illegal origin (16(1)(d)).
:::
:::

Sanctions include impoundment of the aircraft, fines, temporary bans and, where sanctions are ignored, the aircraft reverting to the State; civil and criminal liability are preserved (art. 16(2)–(3)). The law itself sets no fine amounts, and its fees are set by regulation (art. 17). The Council of Ministers approved the regulation on 17 December 2024 ([AIM report on the 17 December 2024 Council of Ministers session](https://aimnews.org/2024/12/17/governo-aprova-regulamento-sobre-comercio-internacional-de-especies-ameacadas-de-extincao/), which covers several of the session's decisions); obtain its text, fees and fine amounts from the Ministry of National Defence, CENACARTA or counsel.

::::section{id="satellite" tone="alt" eyebrow="Satellite imagery" title="Does Lei n.º 6/2024 reach satellite-only work?"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The law defines aerial surveys and aerial photography by reference to sensors on board manned or unmanned aircraft. On those definitions, analysis of satellite imagery, with no aircraft flown, appears to fall outside it.

Two cautions stop us from saying more. CENACARTA's rules give it the coordination, supervision and approval (*homologação*) of geospatial production in general (DM n.º 105/2023, art. 6(l)), and the law's regulation has not been reviewed here. So we treat the point as open: every AfriScan proposal for satellite-only work in Mozambique says so, and your counsel's view settles it.

::::section{id="zones" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="Restricted zones around oil, gas, power and mining assets"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Petroleum installations" icon="pipeline"}
Strategic petroleum areas are under a special protection and inspection regime ([Lei n.º 8/2026, art. 6(1)](https://www.inp.gov.mz/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/BR_104_I_SERIE_2.o_SUPLEMENTO_2026.pdf)). Whether a given processing facility, compressor station or LNG plant is a strategic national-security area under Lei n.º 6/2024 depends on designation, so plan any flight near one with the operator's security team. On the Pande–Temane corridor, a 200 m safety zone applies on each side of the pipeline and around the processing unit ([Decreto n.º 36/2001](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/moz50003.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="Military sites" icon="flag"}
The directive allows no flights over military installations. Lei n.º 6/2024 restricts operations within at least 5 km of strategic areas, including military-servitude areas. On the ground, the Land Law makes a 100 m strip around military and other defence and security installations a partial protection zone ([Lei n.º 19/97, art. 8(j)](https://www.pdul.gov.mz/content/download/486/2635/file/Lei%20de%20Terras.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="Airports and aerodromes" icon="map"}
Flying within 5 km of an aerodrome needs a specific approval under the directive, and the Land Law protects a 100 m strip around airports and aerodromes (art. 8(i)). Check each corridor against the aerodromes near it.
:::
:::card{title="Conservation areas and the north" icon="tree"}
Conservation areas are among the directive's restricted areas, so a flight needs the written permission of the agency that manages the area (ANAC for most national conservation areas). In the northern provinces, expect the Defence authorisation to come with coordination with the security forces and the project's own security (art. 12(2)); satellite work lets the baseline start without a flight.
:::
:::

Radio equipment entering Mozambique must be homologated by INCM (Decreto n.º 37/2009). We found no drone-specific import permit; confirm customs practice for enterprise drones with a broker.

::::section{id="checklist" tone="alt" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Mozambique" lead="Ask any operator, including us, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="A Mozambican company"}
The operator is registered and domiciled in Mozambique: ask for the certidão, NUIT and alvará. For oil and gas work, check the Local Content Law tests, because *serviços de topografia* are on its reserved list (Lei n.º 9/2026, art. 28 and Annex II).
:::
:::step{title="An IACM Letter of Approval that covers the job"}
Category C1 or C2, with any specific approvals the plan needs: night, extended line of sight, controlled airspace, people, roads, aerodromes, flights from a vehicle.
:::
:::step{title="Registered aircraft with Certificates of Safety"}
An IACM Certificate of Registration and a current Certificate of Safety for each aircraft, with the marks and identification plate fitted.
:::
:::step{title="Qualified pilots"}
A remote pilot licence or IACM validation, a Class 4 medical, a radiotelephony licence, ICAO English Level 4 and logbooks.
:::
:::step{title="Defence authorisation for this survey"}
The Minister of National Defence's written authorisation of this survey (Lei n.º 6/2024, art. 13(1)(b)), and the receipts for its fees.
:::
:::step{title="Authorisation to release the data to you"}
From the systematic-cartography entity, through CENACARTA (art. 13(2)). Without it, the client risks the infraction of using products of illegal origin (art. 16(1)(d)).
:::
:::step{title="Insurance"}
A third-party liability certificate that names the project.
:::
:::step{title="Site permissions"}
The landowners and structure owners, the aerodrome within 5 km, the police for roads, the agency that manages any restricted area, and your own security manager for plants, LNG sites and the north.
:::
:::step{title="A flight plan inside the envelope"}
Within line of sight or from a vehicle, at or below 400 ft, with any extended-line-of-sight approval in hand. No flights beyond line of sight and nothing above 400 ft.
:::
:::step{title="A data clause in the contract"}
Who delivers the Article 14(2) deposit, and who may reproduce the orthophotos and mosaics, where and with whose authorisation (art. 16(1)(c)).
:::
:::step{title="Procedures and a risk assessment"}
Operating procedures, a risk assessment and an emergency plan for the site, including the 5 m lateral and 20 ft vertical clearance from live high-tension wires.
:::

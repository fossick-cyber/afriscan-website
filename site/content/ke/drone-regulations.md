faq:
  - q: Does a satellite corridor scan need KCAA authorisation?
    a: No aircraft is flown, and the UAS Regulations govern aircraft, so they do not reach the satellite work itself. The Survey Act's one-month notice speaks of "aerial photography for use in mapping", which satellite imagery arguably is not; if your project depends on that point, ask counsel to confirm it.
  - q: Which rules apply today, the 2020 Regulations or the 2025 ones?
    a: Ask KCAA to confirm in writing. Legal Notice 40 of 2026 has no commencement clause and no clause revoking the 2020 Regulations, and on 27 September 2026 KCAA's drone page still offered the 2020 Regulations, the 2020 Manual of Implementing Standards and the 2021 fees as its downloads. This page follows the 2025 text and notes where the 2020 rules differed.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly commercial drone surveys in Kenya?
    a: Not with a Kenyan operator certificate of its own, which goes only to a company registered in Kenya with its principal place of business there (reg 26(a)). The Regulations let KCAA recognise a certificate issued by another Contracting State whose requirements are at least equal to the applicable standards (reg 35), but how that works for commercial aerial survey has not been tested in the sources we checked, and the 30-day temporary permit for foreign operators in the 2020 Regulations is not in the 2025 text. Plan commercial survey flights with a company that has a Kenyan certificate.
  - q: Can a drone survey a transmission line?
    a: Only with KCAA's specific permission. Operating in or around high-tension cables, communication masts or strategic installations without it is treated as negligent or reckless operation (reg 45(2)(c)). The utility's own safety clearances come on top.
  - q: How far ahead does drone work need to be planned?
    a: A new operator certificate is applied for at least 90 days before the intended operation (reg 25(2)), and the Director of Surveys needs at least one month's written notice before aerial photography for mapping (Survey Act s.42). With a current certificate in place, the job authorisation, the site permissions and any clearances set the timetable. Satellite work can start while they are arranged.
  - q: Can the imagery show people?
    a: An image of a person needs that person's written consent (reg 60), so a drone survey is planned to avoid capturing people and anyone captured is blurred or left out. Structure registers carry no names.
  - q: Is there a corridor where drones can fly beyond line of sight?
    a: KCAA approved the Konza National Drone Corridor as Kenya's first controlled airspace dedicated to beyond-visual-line-of-sight operations, after review by a multi-agency route committee (KBC, 8 July 2025). Elsewhere, beyond-line-of-sight flying is allowed only as KCAA prescribes (reg 40), so long corridors are planned as blocks flown within visual or extended visual line of sight.

::::section{id="which-rules" tone="alt" eyebrow="Before anything else" title="Which rules apply: confirm it with KCAA" lead="The 2025 Regulations are published, but the 2020 ones have not been expressly revoked."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The Civil Aviation (Unmanned Aircraft Systems) Regulations, 2025 were made on 15 December 2025 by the Cabinet Secretary for Transport and Roads under section 82 of the Civil Aviation Act (Cap. 394), and published as Legal Notice No. 40 of 2026 in Kenya Gazette Supplement No. 50 of 6 March 2026. They are one of 36 revised regulations KCAA uploaded in its notice on the [Kenya Civil Aviation Regulations](https://www.kcaa.or.ke/published-regs-2025), alongside the Civil Aviation (Security) Regulations, 2025 (Legal Notice No. 31 of 2026) and the Civil Aviation (Aerial Work – General Operations) Regulations, 2025 (Legal Notice No. 44 of 2026).

Three things are not settled on the face of the documents:

- **Commencement and revocation.** Legal Notice 40 has no commencement clause and no revocation clause, and [KCAA's drone page](https://www.kcaa.or.ke/safety-security-oversight/unmanned-aircraft-systems) still offered the 2020 Regulations (Legal Notice No. 42 of 2020), the 2020 [Manual of Implementing Standards](https://www.kcaa.or.ke/sites/default/files/docs/uas/Manual%20of%20Implementing%20Standards%20(MIS).pdf) and the 2021 fees as its downloads on 27 September 2026.
- **The security regulations named in the text.** Regulation 4(4) refers to "The Civil Aviation Security Regulation 2024" for national security vetting; the regulations actually published are the Security Regulations, 2025.
- **Aerial work.** The Aerial Work Regulations define "aerial survey" to include imagery collected by UAVs (reg 2) and require authorisation when it is done for business or hire (reg 51). Their pilot rules are written for manned aircraft, so whether they apply to drone surveys is a question to put to KCAA.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Ask in writing"}
Before a drone job is costed or scheduled, ask KCAA or your counsel to confirm:

- which UAS Regulations KCAA applies to the operation;
- whether the Aerial Work Regulations' authorisation applies to it;
- which fee schedule applies.

KCAA's online services run through its [RPAS Operations Management System](https://rpas.kcaa.or.ke/).

::::section{id="operator" tone="alt" eyebrow="The operator" title="The RPAS Operator Certificate"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**When it is needed.** For "commercial activities, reward or hire" (reg 24(1)), with operations specifications attached that set out what the operator may do. The company names an Accountable Executive and heads of operations, maintenance, quality and safety, and runs a safety management system.

**Who can get one.** KCAA "may issue" the certificate to an applicant that (reg 26):

- (a) "has its principal place of business and it is registered in Kenya";
- (b) meets the standards;
- (c) is qualified, staffed and equipped;
- (d) has "a security clearance issued by the Ministry responsible for matters relating to defence";
- (e) has an acceptable aircraft operator security programme.

**Timing and validity.** Apply at least 90 days before the intended operation (reg 25(2)). The certificate is valid for 24 months from issue or renewal (reg 27), and an expired certificate needs a new initial application (reg 27(3)). Under the 2020 Manual of Implementing Standards, the process runs in five phases: a pre-application statement of intent after government-agency clearance, the formal application (operations manual, safety management system, quality and security manuals), document evaluation, a demonstration, and certification.

**What the public list shows.** KCAA publishes a [list of operator certificates](https://www.kcaa.or.ke/sites/default/files/docs/uas/ROCs.pdf) in a PDF created on 8 January 2024. It says nothing about any company's status today: ask for the certificate itself.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Duties that come with the certificate"}
- **A security programme** approved by KCAA (reg 62).
- **Vetting** (reg 63): background checks on everyone recruited to deploy, handle or store drones, criminal-record checks every 24 months, secure storage protected against tampering, a named security coordinator and staff training.
- **Loss or theft** reported to the relevant Government Security Agencies and KCAA (reg 44).
- **Records** kept "with all data protection laws of Kenya" (reg 58(3)).
- **Insurance**: third-party cover in force, with the certificate kept at operational management (reg 59).
- **Contractors** that KCAA has not certificated follow the operator's approved safety and security procedures, and KCAA may inspect them (reg 37).

::::section{id="security" eyebrow="Security" title="Two clearances, and the places they protect"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Clearance for people.** Anyone who "intends to engage in UAS operations" needs a security clearance as prescribed by KCAA, valid for 24 months (reg 4). Approvals can be amended, suspended or revoked on security findings.

**Clearance for the operator.** Every operator certificate needs a security clearance from the Ministry responsible for defence (reg 26(d)). The 2020 Manual lists a security clearance "already received in pre-application phase" among the documents for the formal application (§5.3.1(f)).

**Interference and interception.** Unlawful interference with an authorised operation is an offence (reg 66). KCAA may intercept or take control of a drone where there is a contravention, a serious and imminent risk, or a threat to national security (reg 67), and may seize drones (reg 18).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Near military sites"}
Security restrictions around military installations shape infrastructure itself: part of a recent 132 kV line was put underground "because of security restrictions around Laikipia Air Base" ([Capital FM, 28 August 2026](https://capitalfm.africa/ketraco-completes-nanyuki-isiolo-electricity-transmission-line-after-delays/)). Plan on no drone flight near a military installation without the defence authorities' agreement.

::::section{id="aircraft-pilots" tone="alt" eyebrow="Each drone and each pilot" title="Ownership, import, registration and licences"}
:::cards{cols="3" style="plain"}
:::card{title="Who may own a drone" icon="user-check" eyebrow="Reg 7"}
A Kenyan citizen or resident aged 18 or over; a body corporate "lawfully recognized and doing business under the laws of Kenya", including UN bodies and NGOs; national or county government; and bodies corporate of other countries the Authority may approve. Military-specification drones need KCAA approval (reg 7(3)).
:::
:::card{title="Import and sale" icon="download" eyebrow="Regs 8 and 10"}
A KCAA permit to import or export, processed "in conjunction with relevant government agencies" (Manual §1.3). Drones are sold only as the Authority prescribes, and KCAA lists the resellers it has approved.
:::
:::card{title="Registration" icon="clipboard" eyebrow="Reg 11"}
A certificate of registration, with the identification displayed "at all times". Drones under an operator certificate show the manufacturer's serial number or the KCAA mark. Drones already imported must be registered within six months of commencement (reg 167).
:::
:::card{title="Radios" icon="send" eyebrow="Communications Authority"}
Command-and-control and video links need type approval from the Communications Authority of Kenya.
:::
:::card{title="Remote pilots" icon="user-check" eyebrow="Regs 30 and 32"}
A KCAA remote pilot licence for the pilot in command and the co-pilot in categories B and C; category A skills "as prescribed". Training only at an RPAS Training Organisation KCAA has certificated.
:::
:::card{title="Categories" icon="layers" eyebrow="Reg 6"}
A (open, low risk), B (specific, medium risk) and C (high risk, where Part B also applies). The text sets no weight or distance thresholds; KCAA "may from time to time determine additional requirements" (reg 6(2)).
:::
:::

Under the Manual (§1.2), applicants open an online account; individuals submit their ID and a police clearance certificate issued within the last year, and companies their registration documents.

::::section{id="operation" eyebrow="Each flight" title="Authorisation of every operation"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
No operation may take place "without authorization as prescribed by the Authority" (reg 23). Drones registered abroad need authorisation too (reg 23(2)), and operations are authorised by category (reg 23(3)–(4)). The request covers (reg 36(2)):

- the operator and the aircraft, with copies of the registration, the operator certificate and the pilot licences;
- the radio station licence;
- the intended operation: dates, route, levels and duration;
- performance, command-and-control links, detect-and-avoid and emergency procedures;
- "confirmation of compliance with the Civil Aviation (Security) Regulations";
- the payload;
- "proof of adequate insurance coverage".

Documents not in English need an English translation and a certificate of translation (reg 36(4)).

::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What this means for a corridor"}
A long line crosses several counties, landowners and, often, aerodrome zones and high-tension lines. Each of those can add a permission to the authorisation. Satellite screening first narrows the flying to the stretches where drone detail is worth it.

::::section{id="limits" tone="alt" eyebrow="The flight" title="Operating limits that shape a corridor survey" lead="The 2025 text, unless stated. KCAA may approve more than the defaults; none of them can be waived by the client."}
| Rule | What the 2025 Regulations say |
|---|---|
| **Height and distance** | Not "above 400 feet Above Ground Level (AGL) and within 30 meters of any person, vessel, vehicle or structure which is not under the control of the person in charge" (reg 38(1)(a)); KCAA may approve more (38(2)). The 2020 Regulations and Manual said 50 m. The text joins the two limits with "and": ask counsel how KCAA applies them. |
| **Sensor footprint** | No operation "where cameras, imaging devices or other sensors capture information, pictures or videos extending beyond the prescribed area of approved operation" (reg 38(1)(c)) |
| **Weather** | Visual meteorological conditions only, unless the pilot is rated and the system approved (reg 38(1)(b) and (3)) |
| **Congested areas** | At least 30 m lateral from any person, building, structure, vehicle, vessel or animal not associated with the operation, unless authorised (reg 47) |
| **Public roads** | Not over, or less than 30 m along, a public road unless reasonable care is taken for road users (reg 48); no take-off or landing on a public road without approval, except in an emergency (reg 49) |
| **Aerodromes** | Not within 10 km of the aerodrome reference point of a category C–F aerodrome, or 7 km of a category A–B aerodrome, nor on approach and take-off paths, near navigation aids, in the aerodrome traffic zone or in holding patterns, without the written permission of the aerodrome operator and the air navigation service provider plus KCAA approval (reg 56) |
| **Controlled airspace** | Flight plans as prescribed (reg 52), with air traffic control made aware (reg 55) |
| **Night** | Within visual or extended visual line of sight, and only if expressly authorised (reg 39) |
| **Beyond visual line of sight** | Only "as prescribed by the Authority" (reg 40); autonomous and highly automated drones likewise (regs 41–42) |
| **Loss of control link** | Reported to KCAA immediately (reg 54(2)) |
| **Insurance** | Third-party cover in force, with the minimum sum notified to KCAA; KCAA may dispense with it by category (reg 59) |

### Beyond line of sight in practice {#bvlos}

KCAA approved the Konza National Drone Corridor, initiated by the Konza Technopolis Development Authority, after review by its UAS Multi-Agency Route Committee: "Kenya's first controlled airspace specifically dedicated to Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) drone operations" ([KBC, 8 July 2025](https://www.kbc.co.ke/konza-technopolis-approved-as-national-drone-corridor-for-kenya/)). KCAA has also published route-specific procedures for autonomous flights in class G airspace in western Kenya, with radio licences, route approvals and higher minimum heights for helicopters inside the approved route areas. A new beyond-line-of-sight corridor is a bespoke multi-agency approval with no published timeline, so pipeline and power-line jobs are planned as blocks flown within visual or extended visual line of sight.

::::section{id="restricted" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="High-tension lines, installations, airports and protected places"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="High-tension lines and strategic installations" icon="power" eyebrow="Reg 45(2)(c)"}
Operating "in or around strategic installations, Air Navigation Service facilities, high tension cables and communication masts, prisons, police stations, courts of law, scenes of crime, schools and hospitals", except under the conditions or with KCAA's permission, is deemed negligent or reckless operation. So every drone survey of a transmission line needs specific KCAA permission, plus the utility's own safety clearances. No public list of "strategic installations" was found; ask KCAA about pipeline stations, depots and terminals before planning.
:::
:::card{title="Published restricted areas" icon="map" eyebrow="Reg 45(2)(b) and (3)"}
Flying in the prohibited, restricted and danger areas published in the Aeronautical Information Publication is negligent or reckless unless done under its conditions or with KCAA's permission. KCAA can also ban drones from a specific area by Gazette notice in the interest of national security, so check the Gazette before each job.
:::
:::card{title="Prohibited places" icon="lock" eyebrow="Official Secrets Act, ss.2–3"}
"Prohibited place" includes government places used for public security, among them "factories, dockyards, mines… telegraph, telephone, wireless or signal stations", and places the Minister declares by order. Photographing a prohibited place, or in one, without the authority of the officer in charge is an offence (s.3(2)).
:::
:::card{title="Mining sites and private land" icon="mine" eyebrow="Permissions"}
There is no drone-specific rule for mines. The licence holder and the landowner give permission for access, and the Aerial Work Regulations expect "a statement that permission has been obtained from property owners or local officials" in a plan for a congested area or controlled airspace (LN 44/2026, reg 54).
:::

::::section{id="data" tone="alt" eyebrow="Data and images" title="What the camera may capture, and what may be done with it"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Stay inside the approved area.** Operating where the sensors capture information "extending beyond the prescribed area of approved operation" is itself a breach of the operating limits (reg 38(1)(c)), and such material "shall not be reproduced, processed, shared, distributed or published" (reg 38(4)). Flight lines and the sensor footprint are planned inside the approved polygon, and orthophotos are cropped to it before processing as a second safeguard.

**People and private property** (reg 60):

- no image of a person without that person's written consent;
- no imaging of private property or its occupants with the intent of watching them, in violation of a reasonable expectation of privacy;
- "with the approval of the Authority", an imaging drone may be used for "mapping and evaluating the earth's surface, including terrain and surface water bodies and other features", for forest, vegetation and wildlife work and for search and rescue (60(4));
- other privacy and data laws apply (60(5)), and no nuisance (60(6)).
::::
::::col
**The Data Protection Act 2019** applies to controllers and processors "not established or ordinarily resident in Kenya, but processing personal data of data subjects located in Kenya" (s.4(b)(ii)), and lists "property details" among sensitive personal data (s.2). See [data protection and aerial mapping](/ke/data-protection).

**The Survey Act.** The Director of Surveys may ask for the photographs to be produced for inspection, and for copies at the Director's cost (s.42).

**Installations.** Drone imagery of pipeline, power, military or airport installations is never published: the Official Secrets Act makes photographing a prohibited place without the authority of the officer in charge an offence. Survey imagery goes only to the client's named contacts.

::::section{id="penalties" eyebrow="Enforcement" title="Penalties and other consequences"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The Regulations sort their provisions into two penalty classes (reg 165 and its Schedule):

- **"B" provisions** carry a fine or imprisonment of up to three years for each offence. They include categorisation (6), import and export (8), sale (10), **operation authorisation (23)**, the **operator certificate (24)**, the **operating limits (38)**, night (39), **prohibited operation (45)**, congested areas (47), aerodromes (56), **privacy (60)** and interference (66).
- **"A" provisions** carry a fine or imprisonment of up to one year. They include eligibility (7), **registration (11)**, the **pilot licence (30)**, **training (32)**, incident reporting (43), flight plans (52), command and control (54), records (58) and **insurance (59)**.
- **Failing to comply with a KCAA direction**, and contraventions with no penalty of their own, are punished like "B" provisions (165(1) and (5)).
::::
::::col
**Joint liability.** The operator and the pilot are both deemed to have contravened a provision unless each proves lack of consent or connivance and due diligence (reg 165(4)).

**Other consequences.** Suspension or revocation of approvals (reg 163), seizure of drones (reg 18), and appeal to the Tribunal within 21 days (reg 164).

**Outside the Regulations.** A fine under the Survey Act for failing to give the Director of Surveys notice (s.42), and the offences in the Official Secrets Act.

The fine amounts are set in regulation 165; see the Sources below.

::::section{id="checklist" tone="alt" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Kenya" lead="Ask any operator, including one we propose, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="A current KCAA RPAS Operator Certificate"}
The number, the 24-month expiry, and operations specifications covering what the job needs: category B or C, night, extended or beyond visual line of sight, congested areas (reg 24). Ask for the certificate itself; the public list of January 2024 proves nothing about current status.
:::
:::step{title="Kenyan registration and principal place of business"}
As reg 26(a) requires; for a foreign firm, KCAA's written recognition of its certificate under reg 35.
:::
:::step{title="Security clearances"}
The defence clearance (reg 26(d)) and the personnel clearances (reg 4, 24 months), an approved security programme and a named security coordinator (regs 62–63).
:::
:::step{title="Each drone registered and marked"}
Imported under a KCAA permit, registered and marked, with radios type-approved by the Communications Authority.
:::
:::step{title="Remote pilot licences"}
KCAA remote pilot licences (reg 30) with the ratings the task needs.
:::
:::step{title="An operation authorisation for this job and this area"}
Including KCAA permission for high-tension lines, strategic installations or published restricted areas (reg 45); the aerodrome operator's, the air navigation service provider's and KCAA's permission within 10 km or 7 km of an airfield (reg 56); and approval to use the imaging device for mapping (reg 60(4)).
:::
:::step{title="Notice to the Director of Surveys"}
A dated letter giving at least one month's written notice before the aerial photography (Survey Act s.42).
:::
:::step{title="Aerial-work authorisation, if KCAA says it applies"}
The authorisation for aerial survey for business or hire in the Aerial Work Regulations (LN 44/2026, regs 51 and 54), if KCAA confirms that it applies to drones.
:::
:::step{title="Permissions on the ground"}
From the asset owner and the landowners for take-off and landing sites; county and security coordination; written authority before imaging any prohibited or protected place (Official Secrets Act s.3(2); Protected Areas Act).
:::
:::step{title="Insurance"}
Third-party liability cover naming the project (reg 59).
:::
:::step{title="A data clause"}
Flight lines and sensor footprint inside the approved polygon, and nothing outside it kept or shared (reg 38(1)(c) and (4)); people blurred or not captured (reg 60); processing and transfer under the Data Protection Act; who may publish the imagery.
:::
:::step{title="Safety management and site procedures"}
The safety management system, a site risk assessment, and emergency and contingency procedures (regs 29 and 53).
:::
:::step{title="Only where field work is safe"}
No field or drone work in Mandera, Wajir and Garissa counties, Lamu County outside Lamu and Manda islands, Tana River County, the Kilifi coast north of Malindi, the Turkana and Marsabit border areas, or West Pokot and western Turkana, where government travel advice warns against travel ([FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya/warnings-and-insurance), [Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/kenya)); and a written security plan for field work in the North Rift, where security operations were being expanded to Samburu and Isiolo in September 2026 ([Capital FM, 27 September 2026](https://capitalfm.africa/over-3000-illegal-guns-8000-rounds-recovered-in-kerio-valley-as-murkomen-warns-bandits/)).

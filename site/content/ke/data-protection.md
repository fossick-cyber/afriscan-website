faq:
  - q: Is satellite imagery personal data?
    a: It can be. Very-high-resolution satellite imagery shows roofs, yards and plots rather than faces, but where an image or a register can be linked to an identifiable person, for example through a census or a list of plot holders, it relates to that person. Drone imagery can show far more, which is why the drone rules require written consent for any image of a person.
  - q: Does the Act apply when the mapping is done outside Kenya?
    a: Yes. It applies to controllers and processors "not established or ordinarily resident in Kenya, but processing personal data of data subjects located in Kenya" (s.4(b)(ii)).
  - q: Must our project data be stored in Kenya?
    a: Not because of the regulations' localisation rule, as far as corridor mapping goes. Regulation 26 of the General Regulations requires processing through a server or data centre in Kenya, or at least one serving copy there, only for strategic-interest purposes such as civil registration, elections, public finance, protected computer systems, early childhood and basic education, and primary or secondary health care. The Cabinet Secretary can still require a controller processing outside Kenya to localise if it fails to handle a notified breach or to cooperate with the Data Commissioner (reg 26(3)).
  - q: Do we have to register with the Data Commissioner?
    a: Possibly. The Registration Regulations exempt only organisations below a turnover threshold that also have fewer than ten employees (reg 13(2)), and the exemption does not cover the purposes in their schedule, which include "property management including the selling of land", telecoms, transport services and financial services. Organisations in those sectors may already be registered. Check your own position with counsel.
  - q: Does AfriScan identify the people who live in the structures?
    a: No. The register lists structures with an ID, coordinates, a distance to the line and imagery dates. It carries no names, and we do not identify, count or follow people or vehicles in any imagery.

:::cards{cols="4"}
:::card{title="Scope" icon="globe" eyebrow="s.4(b)(ii)"}
The Act reaches controllers and processors outside Kenya that process personal data of people in Kenya, so mapping done abroad is covered.
:::
:::card{title="Sensitive data" icon="houses" eyebrow="s.2"}
"Property details" and family details are sensitive personal data. A structure register joined to a census can carry both.
:::
:::card{title="Registration" icon="clipboard" eyebrow="Registration Regulations, reg 13"}
The exemption is narrow, and it does not cover property management, including the selling of land, or telecoms, transport or financial services.
:::
:::card{title="The processor contract" icon="file-check" eyebrow="General Regulations, regs 24–25"}
A written contract with set contents, and no sub-processor without the controller's prior authorisation.
:::

::::section{id="roles" tone="alt" eyebrow="Who is responsible" title="Controller, processor and sub-processors"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The organisation that commissions a register usually decides what it is for and how it is made; a contractor that processes imagery on its behalf usually acts as its processor. Your counsel settles the roles for each job, and the contract follows from them.

**The contract.** The Data Protection (General) Regulations, 2021 require a written contract between controller and processor with set contents (reg 24).

**Sub-processors.** "A data processor shall not engage the services of a third party without the prior authorisation of the data controller" (reg 25(1)), and the processor stays liable for the sub-processor (reg 25(4)). In mapping work, the third parties are usually imagery suppliers and, for drone work, the flying company, so the proposal names them for you to authorise.

**Where the data sits.** Corridor mapping is not among the strategic-interest purposes that must be processed in Kenya (reg 26(2)). The proposal states where project data will be processed and stored and for how long, so your data protection officer can check it before work starts.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Registration, in brief"}
The Data Protection (Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors) Regulations, 2021 exempt an organisation only if it is below a turnover threshold **and** has fewer than ten employees (reg 13(2)). The exemption does not apply to the purposes in the schedule, including:

- "Property management including the selling of land";
- telecoms;
- transport services;
- financial services.

Source: the [Office of the Data Protection Commissioner](https://www.odpc.go.ke/).
:::

::::section{id="design" eyebrow="How a register is designed" title="Structures, not people"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
A structure register is built to carry the least personal data the job allows:

- **Structures, IDs, dates and distances only.** No names, no household details, no photographs of people.
- **Personal data stays in your census.** Where your team joins the register to a census or an asset inventory, the joined record is yours and sits in your systems.
- **A written processor agreement** covering the contents regulation 24 sets.
- **People blurred or left out** of any drone imagery, and nothing captured outside the approved flight area kept or shared.
- **Results only to the contacts you name.** We never publish maps of a client's assets or of the settlements around them without written permission.

::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Why property details matter"}
The Act lists "property details" among sensitive personal data (s.2). On its own, a register of structures with coordinates and dates describes land, not a person. Joined to the census, to a list of plot holders or to compensation files, it can describe a person's property. Decide at the start who holds the joined record, who may see it, and how long it is kept.
:::

**An enforcement example.** In a determination of 14 April 2026, the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner found a microfinance bank liable for publishing a former employee's images and details on social media without consent (reported by Capital FM, 19 April 2026).

::::section{id="drones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Drone imagery" title="What the drone rules add"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Images of people" icon="user-check" eyebrow="UAS Regulations 2025, reg 60"}
No image of a person without that person's written consent, and no imaging of private property or its occupants with the intent of watching them, in violation of a reasonable expectation of privacy. Other privacy and data laws apply as well (reg 60(5)).
:::
:::card{title="Only the approved area" icon="target" eyebrow="Reg 38(1)(c) and (4)"}
No operation where sensors capture information beyond the approved area, and material captured beyond it "shall not be reproduced, processed, shared, distributed or published".
:::
:::card{title="Records" icon="file-text" eyebrow="Reg 58(3)"}
Operators keep their records in compliance "with all data protection laws of Kenya".
:::
:::

Satellite imagery involves no flight, so the drone rules do not apply to it; the Data Protection Act applies to both. See [drone law in Kenya](/ke/drone-regulations#data).

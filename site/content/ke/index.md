faq:
  - q: Which widths do you measure?
    a: The width in your gazette order, easement, wayleave agreement or resettlement action plan. KETRACO's published resettlement action plans, for example, use a 30 m wayleave (15 m each side of the centreline) for a 132 kV line and 40 m (20 m each side) for a 220 kV line. For pipelines, roads and railways, send the width that applies to each section, or the reserve as a polygon, and each structure is reported with its distance to the centreline and the band it falls in.
  - q: Can you show which structures were there before the notice or the cut-off date?
    a: Only by comparing dated images. A structure can be dated to the interval between the last image that does not show it and the first image that does, so the record is only as precise as the imagery on either side of the date. Archive coverage varies by place and season, and the proposal says which dated scenes exist for your corridor before you commit.
  - q: Do we need a drone survey?
    a: Usually not. Most corridors can be screened from satellite imagery without anyone flying. Where a stretch needs more detail, drone flights are carried out by a company registered and based in Kenya with a current KCAA RPAS Operator Certificate, subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires, and after one month's written notice to the Director of Surveys. See [drone law in Kenya](/ke/drone-regulations).
  - q: What about cloud in the rainy seasons?
    a: Optical satellite imagery cannot see through cloud, so captures are planned around the rains and each register uses the clearest dated scene for each stretch. Radar comparisons can show larger changes, such as clearing, earthworks and new large buildings, even under rainy-season cloud.
  - q: Do you work in Mandera, Wajir, Garissa, Lamu, Tana River or West Pokot?
    a: From satellite imagery only. We send no one into the counties and border areas where the UK, Canadian, US or Australian government advises against travel, and no drone flies there. A satellite register needs no one on the ground, so those corridors can still be mapped.
  - q: Can you tell us which structures are unauthorised?
    a: No. We map what stands where, how far it is from the line and when it first appears in the imagery. Whether a structure is authorised, predates the wayleave or qualifies for compensation is for the wayleave holder, the National Land Commission and the courts to decide. The register gives them a dated, located list to work from.
  - q: Is the register a survey under the Survey Act?
    a: No. Surveys for registrable title documents and boundary surveys are for surveyors under the Survey Act (section 36). Our registers are monitoring and planning records built from imagery. They tell your surveyors, wayleave officers and RAP teams where to look.

::::section{id="land-act" eyebrow="Dated records for wayleaves and acquisitions" title="Kenyan land law turns on what stood where, and when" lead="The Land Act 2012 ties a wayleave, its notices and its compensation to the land and to dates. A wayleave holder needs a dated, measured record of what stands in the corridor. That is what we deliver."}
:::cards{cols="4"}
:::card{title="The wayleave runs with the land" icon="corridor" eyebrow="Land Act, s.143"}
A wayleave created for national or county government, a public authority or a corporate body attaches to the servient land and binds every owner and occupier from time to time, however they hold or occupy it.
:::
:::card{title="Notice reaches everyone on the route" icon="houses" eyebrow="Land Act, s.144(4)"}
Before a wayleave is created, the applicant serves notice on all occupiers, including holders of customary pastoral rights, the county, and everyone in actual occupation of urban and peri-urban land along the route.
:::
:::card{title="Survey damage is compensated too" icon="excavation" eyebrow="Land Act, s.148"}
Compensation covers the use of the land and damage to trees, crops and buildings, including damage from the preliminary work that surveyed or fixed the route, paid promptly by the applicant.
:::
:::card{title="Valued at the notice date" icon="calendar" eyebrow="Land Act, ss.2 and 107A(9)"}
"Full" compensation restores the value of the land and its improvements as at the date of the notice of intention to acquire. On compulsory acquisition, occupants in good faith without title are compensated on six years' occupation and for improvements made before the notice is published in the Gazette.
:::
:::

A "building" in the Act is any structure or erection of any kind, permanent or temporary, movable or not, finished or not (section 2), so kiosks, stalls, mabati sheds and jua kali workshops all count. The [wayleave law guide](/ke/wayleave-law-guide) sets out the procedure from notice to Gazette order, with the sections behind each step.

Work on the standard gauge railway from Naivasha to Kisumu and Malaba was launched in March 2026 ([Capital FM, 21 March 2026](https://capitalfm.africa/kisumu-malaba-sgr-launch-trade-link/)). A new eastern products pipeline from Mombasa to Nairobi and a cross-border line from Eldoret towards Uganda are planned.

About 13,000 km of national backbone fibre is in place ([Capital FM, 15 October 2025](https://capitalfm.africa/state-spends-sh34-4bn-expanding-fiber-optic-network/)), and the Digital Superhighway programme aims for 100,000 km ([Capital FM, 30 January 2026](https://capitalfm.africa/ps-isaboke-kenyas-digital-superhighway-on-the-right-track/)). Much of it runs in road reserves, where utilities must relocate on the road authority's written request (Kenya Roads Act, s.27).

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Satellite first, drones where they add detail" title="Two routes to the picture, and the rules for each"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Satellite imagery and your own data.** A register starts from your route file and dated satellite imagery, open building datasets or the orthophotos you already hold. Most corridors can be screened this way without anyone flying, however long the route and whichever counties it crosses.

**Drone detail.** Under the Civil Aviation (Unmanned Aircraft Systems) Regulations 2025, commercial drone work needs an RPAS Operator Certificate that KCAA issues only to a company registered in Kenya, with its principal place of business there and a security clearance from the Ministry responsible for defence. So where a stretch needs drone detail, the flights are carried out by such a company, subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires: KCAA authorisation of the operation, specific permission near high-tension lines, and one month's written notice to the Director of Surveys under the Survey Act. The proposal names the company that will fly, so your team can check its certificate.

:::::columns{split="2-1"}

Satellite registers cover any corridor in Kenya. Field and drone work does not go where the UK, Canadian, US or Australian government advises against travel ([FCDO, updated 4 September 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya/warnings-and-insurance); [Global Affairs Canada, updated 24 September 2026](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/kenya)):

- Mandera, Wajir and Garissa counties;
- Lamu County, except Lamu Island and Manda Island;
- Tana River County;
- the Kilifi coast north of Malindi;
- Turkana and Marsabit within 110 km of the South Sudan and Ethiopia borders;
- West Pokot and western Turkana.

Corridors and project areas there are mapped from satellite imagery only. Elsewhere, any field or drone work is planned around the travel advice current at the time.

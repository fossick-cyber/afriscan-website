::::section{id="road-reserves" eyebrow="Road reserves" title="What stands on the road reserve, segment by segment"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The Kenya Roads Act says nobody may "erect, construct or lay, or establish any structure or other thing, on or over or below the surface of a road reserve or land in a building restricted area" without the road authority's written permission (section 49(1)). We map what stands there, by segment: kiosks, stalls, mabati structures, jua kali sheds, walls and houses inside the reserve, and, where you send it, inside the building restricted area beyond it.

Each structure the review confirms is listed with its distance from the centreline or the reserve edge, its chainage and its coordinates, and each 500 m segment is rated by the number of structures it holds. Repeat scans flag new and removed structures between dated images, confirmed by a reviewer, so the road authority's teams can follow up through their own process, and so a widening or dualling design can be checked against what actually stands beside the road.

Its first rails and sleepers were laid in July 2026. New lines like this need dated records at three moments.

::::section{id="fibre" eyebrow="Fibre routes" title="New works along buried fibre, found between dated images"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
About 13,000 km of national backbone fibre is in place ([Capital FM, 15 October 2025](https://capitalfm.africa/state-spends-sh34-4bn-expanding-fiber-optic-network/)), the Digital Superhighway programme aims to "roll out 100,000 km of fibre optic cable nationwide" ([Capital FM, 30 January 2026](https://capitalfm.africa/ps-isaboke-kenyas-digital-superhighway-on-the-right-track/)), and a government-funded project is extending fibre to more than 53,000 public institutions.

Much fibre runs in road reserves. Satellites can't see a cut cable, but they can show new excavations, earthworks, tracks and structures along the route between dated images, so patrols go where the ground has changed. Each flag carries its location, its distance to the route and the dates of the images on either side of the change, and a reviewer confirms it before it reaches you.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Relocation on request"}
The Kenya Roads Act requires pipe-borne and wire-borne utilities in a road reserve, expressly including "fibre optic cable", to be relocated "at no cost to the Authority" on the road authority's written request, and removed within sixty days of notice; otherwise the authority removes them at the provider's cost (section 27).

When a relocation notice arrives, we compare alternative routes for the cable by the structures and excavation each would cross, and record what stands along the new route before the work starts.
:::

::::section{id="wayleaves" tone="alt" eyebrow="Wayleaves along the way" title="Counties, forests and other crossings"}

In August 2025 a government Principal Secretary cited the "high fees and levies demanded by county governments including wayleave acquisition challenges" as a hurdle for fibre builders ([KBC, 15 August 2025](https://www.kbc.co.ke/counties-urged-to-ease-wayleave-fee-hurdles-to-boost-ict-investments/)), and routes that leave the road reserve need their own wayleaves across public, community and private land under the Land Act (sections 143–148). A route comparison that counts the structures, parcels of cleared land and forest cover each option crosses helps choose between staying in the reserve and leaving it.

We describe the ground. The permissions, the fees and the negotiations with counties and landowners stay with your wayleave team.

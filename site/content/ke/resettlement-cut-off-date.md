### Compulsory acquisition under the Land Act {#land-act}

Compulsory acquisition runs under Part VIII of the [Land Act 2012](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2012/6/eng@2025-11-04) (sections 107–133), through the National Land Commission. Its rules tie compensation to dates:

- **"Full" compensation** restores the value of the land and its improvements "as at the date of the notice of intention to acquire the land" (s.2), and applies to compulsory acquisition and to the creation of wayleaves, easements and public rights of way.
- **"Prompt"** means within a reasonable time and in any case not more than one year after taking possession (s.2).
- **Occupants in good faith without title** are compensated on six years' uninterrupted occupation immediately before the notice of intention is published, and for improvements made before its publication in the Gazette (s.107A(9)).
- **Geo-referencing:** "All land to be compulsorily acquired shall be geo-referenced and authenticated" by the survey authority (s.107(8)).
- **Lapse:** an acquisition not completed within 24 months of the notice of intention lapses (s.111(1B)).
- **Disputes** go first to the Land Acquisition Tribunal (s.133C(6)), then to the Environment and Land Court.
- Taking possession must not leave people homeless (s.120(1)).

### Resettlement plans and lenders {#rap}

We found no standalone resettlement statute in Kenya. Resettlement rests on the Land Act, on the Mining Act's rule that people who prefer to be compensated by resettlement are settled on suitable alternative land at the licence holder's cost (s.153(8)–(9)), and on lender standards such as IFC Performance Standard 5 and the World Bank's ESS5, which ask for a census and a cut-off date.

Published resettlement action plans for transmission lines define the cut-off date as the completion of the census and asset inventory, and provide for re-ratifying it by Gazette notice after a delay of two years or more. Some of those dates were fixed years before construction. See [power-line wayleaves](/ke/power-line-wayleaves#cut-off).

:::callout{tone="legal" title="What a dated register supports"}
A dated, reproducible record of the structures in the footprint shows what stood where on the date that matters. It supports your census and asset inventory, your notices and your IFC Performance Standard 5 cut-off-date records. It does not set any date, value any improvement or replace the census.
:::

A summary for orientation as of 27 September 2026, not legal advice. The [wayleave law guide](/ke/wayleave-law-guide) has the procedure in full.

::::section{id="how" eyebrow="How it works" title="Before the census, on the date, and after"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Before the census.** How many structures stand in the footprint and the buffers around it, and where they cluster, so the census and asset-inventory teams are sized and routed before they go. On a long wayleave, the busiest 500 m segments come first.

**On the date.** A register of the structures on dated imagery, with IDs, coordinates and reviewer categories such as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction. Where the record may be tested later, it is packaged as an evidence pack with file fingerprints and an independent timestamp.

**After.** Repeat scans of the footprint and any resettlement site, with new structures since the cut-off date flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, and a location check for single grievances and claims: every dated image covering one point, on one sheet.

**The imagery.** A cut-off record is only as good as the date on its imagery, so we build it on imagery with a known capture date: a dated very-high-resolution satellite scene, a drone survey flown with the approvals each job requires, or georeferenced imagery you already hold. Map-service basemaps, which carry no capture date, are never used for it. A new satellite capture can be requested when the archive is too old; its date depends on satellite availability and weather and is never guaranteed.

### What it supports

:::checklist
- Census and asset-inventory planning
- Notices to occupiers along a wayleave (Land Act s.144(4))
- Your IFC Performance Standard 5 cut-off-date records
- Grievance handling and tribunal cases, with dated facts
- Comparison of route and site options before a notice is issued
:::
::::
::::col
### What it does not do

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Set a notice, Gazette or cut-off date, or decide who is eligible
- Carry out the census, the asset inventory or public participation
- Value land, structures, crops or trees
- Identify households or people, or show occupation
- Replace the survey authority's geo-referencing and authentication

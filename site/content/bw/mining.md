  - q: Can you measure how much sand or ore has been removed?
    a: Satellite imagery shows how far disturbed ground has spread between dates, not its depth. Volumes of pits, spoil heaps and stockpiles come from drone elevation models, where a drone survey is flown or you supply one, and they are indicative for your own planning, not certified quantities.
  - q: Can your baseline replace the surface-rights plan or the EIA?

::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="Concessions are large, and the ground on them keeps changing"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="“The application needs the land described.”" icon="clipboard"}
A mining licence application needs surface rights from the land authority or landowner "with a stamped sketch plan showing coordinates", authorisation of the environmental impact assessment by the Department of Environmental Affairs, archaeological clearance with location coordinates and, inside a conservation area, clearance from the Department of Wildlife and National Parks, together with a proposed rehabilitation programme ([gov.bw](https://www.gov.bw/mining/mining-license-application)).
:::
:::card{title="“Is sand being dug outside the permitted stretches?”" icon="excavation"}
The Minister of Minerals and Energy told Parliament in March 2026 that river systems "have been adversely impacted by over mining", that no river sand is mined south of Dibete under a moratorium, extended to the Mahalapye area in 2025, and that permits along the Mahalapye River are being reopened after a study by the Botswana Geoscience Institute ([Committee of Supply speech, 4 March 2026](https://www.parliament.gov.bw/documents/BUDGET-2026-27-MARCH---Ministry-of-Minerals-and-Energy-Committee-of-Supply-Final-Speech_12_59_20_04_08_2026.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="“The expansion needs a footprint record first.”" icon="calendar"}
The same speech reports underground development at diamond mines, copper expansions that open new mines beside existing ones, and manganese, graphite and iron projects at different stages. Each new pit, haul road, camp and dump takes land, and the land needs a dated record before the census and compensation start.
:::
:::card{title="“Nobody has walked the whole lease this year.”" icon="boundary"}
Lease areas are large, and some include game parks. New tracks, cleared ground and structures at the edge, or pits opening inside it, are found on the next visit if they are found at all.
:::

::::section{id="baselines" tone="alt" eyebrow="Before the licence or the expansion" title="A baseline of the land, dated and reviewed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Environmental assessment in Botswana starts with a project brief to the Department of Environmental Affairs, which decides whether no further study, an environmental management plan or a full assessment is needed. The practitioner then publicises the activity in the media, in the official languages, for at least 21 days, holds meetings with the affected communities, and submits a scoping report before the assessment itself; the application's supporting documents include proof of land ownership, such as a prospecting licence ([gov.bw](https://www.gov.bw/environmental-management/application-environmental-assessment-ea-authorizations)).

For each of those steps we give your practitioner and land team the same dated picture of the licence area and the ground around it: a land-use and land-cover map of built-up land, cropland, bare ground, water and tree cover, a layer of the structures on it with coordinates and IDs, and the imagery history of what was built, cleared or dug before, and roughly when. Reviewers mark structures by hand where roofs are hard to read, and categorise them as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction when a resettlement register is needed.

We are not an environmental assessment practitioner, and we do not do archaeological or heritage surveys. The baseline supports those who do.

::::section{id="excavation" eyebrow="Pits, sand and disturbed ground" title="Where the ground has been dug since the last survey"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We map pits, disturbed ground, spoil heaps and water-filled excavations inside and around your licence or permit area, and along the river stretches you name, with new sites flagged between dates and confirmed by a reviewer. In the rainy season, radar comparisons show larger ground changes even under cloud, and flagged areas are followed up with optical imagery or a drone check. New tracks and access roads into the area are mapped the same way, because they often come first.

The Minister says the Mines and Minerals (Amendment) Act, 2024, enacted in October 2025 and now in force, "had brought revised punitive measures and fees to deter those who were tempted to undertake illegal mining", and that the Ministry is committed to the forfeiture of equipment used in it; the Ministry and other stakeholders run random and joint inspections ([BOPA, 25 August 2026](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/92924)). A dated map of new excavations shows your teams and the inspectors where to look first. It is an indication to check, not proof of who did anything.

**Small concessions.** A minerals permit covers a concession of no more than 0.5 km² for minerals other than diamonds, and permits for industrial minerals are reserved for citizens unless the Minister decides otherwise in the public interest ([gov.bw](https://www.gov.bw/mining/minerals-permit)). For a permit holder, the same map shows whether digging has spread beyond the permitted area.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Concession oversight, in the Ministry's words"}
As part of optimised regulation of small mines, the Ministry of Minerals and Energy means "to increase monitoring of concessions through the use of new technology such as drones", which it says can also give feedback on "the actual amount of material that has been mined" (Committee of Supply speech, 4 March 2026).

We map the extent of disturbed ground between dates from satellite imagery, and calculate volumes from drone elevation models where they exist. Volumes are indicative, not certified.
:::

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Land, never people"}
What we map is the ground. We do not identify, count or follow people, and underground workings and covered shafts are not visible from above.

When a pit, dump or haul road expands, we record the structures inside the new footprint on a date you fix. Where lenders apply IFC Performance Standard 5, people who move into the project area after a cut-off date that "has been clearly established and made public" need not be compensated ([PS5, para. 23](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf)), so the dated register supports the census and asset inventory. It does not replace them, and its categories do not establish use, ownership or value.

::::section{id="rehabilitation" eyebrow="Rehabilitation, care and maintenance" title="Dated evidence of rehabilitation, and of what happens on quiet sites"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Every mining licence application carries a proposed rehabilitation programme. We track revegetation on rehabilitated land between dates, from Copernicus Sentinel data and, where flown, drone elevation models, and compare cut and fill between drone visits. That shows the trend; it is not an ecological assessment or a closure sign-off.
::::
::::col
The Minister's March 2026 speech lists mines under care and maintenance or in liquidation, and shafts that have been backfilled. Sites without daily operations change too: we flag new excavations, structures and tracks on them between dates, so the owner, the liquidator or the Ministry knows what to inspect.

::::section{id="drones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Your drone imagery, and drone surveys" title="Drones on and around mine sites"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We found no drone rule specific to mines in Botswana. The general rules apply: the Civil Aviation (Remotely Piloted Aircraft) Regulations, 2024 require the consent of "the relevant Authority" to launch or recover a drone from any public or private property (reg. 100), CAAB's checklist asks commercial operators for the property owner's letter of authorisation, and each commercial operation needs its own CAAB authorisation, including a security review of how the camera will be used. Where a lease area takes in a game park or wildlife sanctuary, CAAB's 2016 bye-law, whose status after 2024 is to be confirmed with CAAB, restricts flights over it and over wildlife.

If your survey team already flies the mine, send the orthophotos and we run the analysis on them. New drone surveys are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires.
::::
::::col

Ownership and registration, the RPAS Operator Certificate, security vetting, the flight limits and a checklist for clients, last reviewed 27 September 2026.

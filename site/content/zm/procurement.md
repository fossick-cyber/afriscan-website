faq:
  - q: Can a foreign supplier bid in a Zambian public tender?
    a: It depends on the method. Open national bidding for goods, works and non-consulting services is limited to citizen and local bidders, with priority for citizen bidders (Public Procurement Act s.39(2)), and open national selection of consultants is limited in the same way (s.41(3)). International selection is used only where the service is not available from three suppliers in Zambia, where an agreement requires it, or in "a specialised industry", and a foreign bidder in it "shall partner with a citizen bidder". The tender document states which method applies.
  - q: Does SI 68 of 2025 apply to remote-sensing and mapping work?
    a: The regulations reserve a share of mining procurement for local companies in core goods and services, and reserve some non-core goods and services critical to mining exclusively for them. Their schedules list items such as engineering services, geological and geotechnical services, information systems and consultancies. Which category covers satellite and drone mapping is a question for counsel, and the tender or the mining company's procurement team will say how it applies the rules.
  - q: Does a satellite mapping contract raise the drone-approval questions?
    a: No. The ROC, the Air Services Permit, the special authorisation and the Air Force clearance apply only where an aircraft is flown. Where a scope includes drone work, the proposal separates it, names the Zambian company that will fly it and gives its approval details, so your evaluation can treat each part on its own terms.
  - q: Where will our project data be stored?
    a: The Data Protection Act requires personal data to be processed and stored in Zambia (s.70), and storage abroad needs a separate authorisation from the Data Protection Commission. Our registers carry structure IDs and coordinates rather than names, and each proposal states the controller and processor roles and where project data will be processed and stored for your contract.
  - q: What do you need from us to prepare a proposal?
    a: The route, network or boundary file, the province and district, the widths or zones to report, the date the record must reflect, the deliverables and the deadline. With a tender, send the documents, the portal and the reference number your process uses, and any supplier-registration or data-protection forms.

::::section{id="registration" tone="alt" eyebrow="Supplier registration" title="ZPPA e-GP, and where the main public buyers advertise"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Public entities in Zambia buy through the Zambia Public Procurement Authority's [e-GP system](https://eprocure.zppa.org.zm/epps). ZPPA's [supplier registration guide](https://www.zppa.org.zm/documents/20182/21184/eGP_Supplier_Reg.pdf/515c7350-6fca-4835-a17b-c1f3848254bb) (dated 2016) sets out a manual route for foreign suppliers "that have not registered with the relevant authorities in Zambia": the organisation's details, its shareholders, its "CEEC Category" and supporting documents, with up to five UNSPSC codes per supplier. Validation follows before the supplier can bid.

State-owned utilities and corridor operators publish their own tenders as well, and large mining houses and private utilities run their own supplier registration and e-tendering. State the company, tax and registration documents your process needs at award in the tender, and the proposal answers each one.
::::
::::col
### Where public buyers publish

| Buyer | Portal |
|---|---|
| All public entities | [ZPPA e-GP](https://eprocure.zppa.org.zm/epps) · [ZPPA](https://www.zppa.org.zm/) |
| ZESCO | [ZESCO tenders](https://www.zesco.co.zm/tender.php) |

Some state tenders are open only to citizen and local suppliers, and some accept sealed paper bids only. Read the bidding method and the submission rules in each notice.

::::section{id="bidders" eyebrow="The Public Procurement Act" title="Citizen, local and foreign bidders" lead="The Public Procurement Act 2020 (No. 8 of 2020), as amended by Act No. 17 of 2023, sorts bidders by status and reserves national competition for citizen and local bidders."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
| Status | Meaning in the Act |
|---|---|
| **Citizen bidder** | For a firm, one "wholly owned by a citizen" |
| **Local bidder** | "A non-citizen bidder or supplier who is registered to undertake business activities in the Republic" |
| **Foreign bidder** | A non-citizen bidder not so registered |

**Goods, works and non-consulting services (s.39).** "Participation in open national bidding shall be limited to citizen and local bidders, except that citizen bidders shall be given priority" (s.39(2)), and a winning local bidder "shall partner with a citizen supplier" (s.39(3)).

**Consulting services (s.41).** Open national selection is limited to citizen and local bidders (s.41(3)), and a local bidder awarded a contract "shall partner with a citizen supplier" (s.41(4), inserted in 2023). International selection is used only where the service is not available from three suppliers in Zambia, where an agreement requires it, or in "a specialised industry", and a foreign bidder in international selection "shall partner with a citizen bidder".

**Partnering and subcontracting.** Foreign bidders partner with a citizen supplier (s.89(1)(h)), and since 2023 foreign and local bidders subcontract a percentage to citizens for procurements of works (s.93). Regulation 20 of the [Public Procurement Regulations 2022](https://www.zppa.org.zm/documents/20182/21181/Public+Procurement+Regulations+2022.pdf/9f9fd0d3-860e-4fbf-bed6-d9378d0c04ab?version=1.0) requires foreign bidders to subcontract at least twenty per cent of the total value above the simplified thresholds; whether that still applies to services after the 2023 amendment is for counsel.

**Preference.** The [Public Procurement (Preference and Reservation Schemes) Regulations, SI 45 of 2025](https://www.zppa.org.zm/documents/20182/293897/S.+I.+No.+45+of+2025+The+Public+Procurement+%28Preference+and+Reservation+Schemes%29+Regulations.pdf/58b9f0a9-aba5-4665-842a-5cc971f837bb), gazetted on 27 June 2025, set margins of preference for local suppliers, citizens, citizen enterprises owned by women, youth or persons with disabilities, small citizen businesses and citizen–local joint ventures or subcontracts. Local preference applies only "where a foreign bidder is permitted to participate".
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="The Acts"}
- [Public Procurement Act 2020](https://www.zppa.org.zm/documents/20182/21184/The+Public+Procurement+Act+No.+8+of+2020.pdf/7ebd55aa-03cd-41c5-a906-f319d162d615?version=1.0)
- [Public Procurement (Amendment) Act 2023](https://www.zppa.org.zm/documents/20182/197698/Act+No.+17+of+2023%2C+The+Public+Procurement.pdf/295bb334-92d3-42fb-8367-c430c938f2ba)
- The Citizens Economic Empowerment Act 2006 (Cap. 419) defines citizen-owned, citizen-empowered and citizen-influenced companies; ZPPA's registration asks for a "CEEC Category".

::::section{id="mining" tone="alt" eyebrow="Mining local content" title="SI 68 of 2025: procurement reserved for local companies"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The [Geological and Minerals Development (Local Content) (Preference for Goods and Services in the Mining Sector) Regulations 2025](https://www.mmmd.gov.zm/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Geological-and-Minerals-Development-Preference-for-Zambian-Goods-and-Services-Regulations-2025.pdf), SI 68 of 2025, were gazetted on 14 October 2025 and came into force on 1 January 2026. They apply to mining and mining-related companies:

- **Reservation (reg. 4).** Within six months, at least 20 per cent of the annual procurement budget is reserved "for a local company involved in the supply or provision of a core mining good or core mining service", rising to 25 per cent within one year, 35 per cent within two and at least 40 per cent within five.
- **Preference (reg. 5).** A margin of preference for local companies.
- **Exclusive reservation (reg. 6).** Non-core goods or services "critical to the operation of the mining industry" are "exclusively reserved for a local company".
- **Who counts.** A "local company" is "a citizen empowered company or a citizen owned company" under the Citizens Economic Empowerment Act.

The schedules name core items such as engineering services, geological and geotechnical services and consultancies on core goods and services, and non-core items such as information systems, information technology and communication, and consultancies in non-core goods and services. Which of them covers satellite and drone mapping is a question for counsel. Upstream petroleum has its own preference rule for Zambian goods, services and citizens (Petroleum (Exploration and Production) Act 2008, s.63).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="For mining buyers"}
Ask each supplier how it meets the regulations for your contract: its own local-company status, with evidence, or the local company it works with and that company's share of the work. The regulations carry penalties for non-compliance by the mining company, so the evidence matters to you.
:::

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Drone work" title="Who may fly, and what to ask for"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Commercial drone work in Zambia needs a ZCAA RPAS Operator Certificate with current OpSpecs and an Air Services Permit, every drone needs a Zambian registration and Letter of Approval, and every pilot a ZCAA Remote Pilot Licence. ZCAA guidance adds, for each operation, a special authorisation, a ZCAA inspector on site and Zambia Air Force airspace clearance. The drones must be on the Zambian register and ZCAA's company form asks for Zambian company papers, so a foreign supplier flies through a Zambian operator with its own ROC.

For evaluation, split any scope into its satellite or desk-based part, which needs no aviation approval, and its drone part, which needs all of them. Ask for the flying company's ROC and OpSpecs, its Air Services Permit, its insurance and its plan for the special authorisation and clearance on your timeline.

Drone surveys are subject to the ZCAA approvals and Zambia Air Force clearance each operation requires. Afridrone is working towards the ZCAA approvals Zambia requires, and every AfriScan proposal with a drone element names the Zambian company that will fly it.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="data" tone="alt" eyebrow="The Data Protection Act 2021" title="Personal data, storage in Zambia and breach notice"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Imagery of homes linked to names, census lists or grievance files is personal data: the [Data Protection Act 2021](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%203%20The%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202021_0.pdf) covers location data and anything from which a person "can be directly or indirectly identified" (s.2). What it asks of controllers and processors:

- **Registration** with the Data Protection Commission (s.19); the Commission decides within 14 days (s.20), and the certificate is displayed at the principal place of business (s.21).
- **Storage in Zambia.** "A data controller shall process and store personal data on a server or data centre located in the Republic" (s.70(1)). The Minister may prescribe categories that may be stored abroad (s.70(2)), and sensitive personal data always stay in Zambia (s.70(3)).
- **Transfers** abroad only on the conditions in s.71.
- **Breach notice** to the Commissioner within 24 hours, and to the people affected as soon as practicable (s.49).
- **Impact assessments** (s.46), a data protection officer (s.48) and retention rules (s.51).
::::
::::col
The [Data Protection Commission](https://www.dataprotection.gov.zm/)'s 2025 guide to registering data controllers and processors adds that storage abroad, including "A cloud-based system which stores data outside Zambia", needs "a separate authorization", that representatives of foreign entities have their own part of the process, and that registration may be refused if the applicant's entity is not legally registered or authorised to operate in Zambia.

**What to put in the contract:** the controller and processor roles, where project data will be processed and stored and any Commission authorisation for storage abroad, breach notice, retention and deletion, who may receive results, and the masking of personal and security-sensitive details in deliverables. Our registers carry structure IDs and coordinates rather than names, which keeps most of a survey's data out of scope; the proposal states the arrangements for your contract.

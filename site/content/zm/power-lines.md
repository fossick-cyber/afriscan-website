faq:
  - q: Which wayleave width applies to our line?
    a: "For one line, the minimum in Table 2.1 of the Electricity (Wayleave and Clearances) Regulations 2026: for example 22 m for 22 kV, 30 m for 66 kV and 88 kV, 32 m for 132 kV, 44 m for 220 kV and 275 kV, 48 m for 330 kV and 50 m for 400 kV. In a recognised forestry area Table 2.4 sets wider strips, from 50 m for 11 and 33 kV to 80 m for 400 kV. Parallel lines share wider wayleaves, and a shared wayleave narrower than the Schedule needs the ERB's prior written approval. Send us the widths you hold for each line or section, or your wayleave polygons, and we use those."
  - q: Does the ERB's 2011 Wayleave Code of Practice still apply?
    a: The 2026 Regulations contain no express revocation of it. ERB's September 2026 draft siting guidelines cite the 2026 Regulations where the 2022 edition cited the Code. Ask your legal team which your internal standards should follow; we measure at whichever widths you choose.
  - q: Can you measure the clearance between a conductor and a roof?
    a: No. The Regulations set vertical clearances above buildings, such as 6.0 m for 132 kV, 8.0 m for 330 kV and 9.4 m for 400 kV, but those are measurements for your line engineers. We show where structures stand in the wayleave and how far each is from the centreline, and where tall vegetation stands, from drone elevation models where flown and open canopy-height data for wider context.
  - q: Crops are allowed in the wayleave. Can you tell crops from trees?
    a: We map where vegetation is taller, where it has been cleared and where it has grown back between dates, which is what matters for a rule on height. ZESCO's practice, as described in a 2024 environmental and social assessment for a 330 kV interconnector, allows seasonal crops below 2 m and not taller ones. We do not identify crop types or individual plots, and a height check near the 2 m line needs drone elevation data.
  - q: A developer wants the wayleave position within 1 km of our line. Can you help?
    a: Yes. Under Schedule 1 of the Regulations, a developer planning development within 1 km of an existing or planned line must first get the wayleave position and clearances in writing from the licensee, who must advise within 45 days. A current register of what stands in and near the wayleave around the development site gives both sides the same picture.
  - q: Do you detect vandalism or cable theft?
    a: No. Satellites cannot see theft, vandalism or people. We flag new structures, fresh excavation and new tracks near towers and along the wayleave between surveys, so your teams know where to check. What happened there, and why, is for your field teams to establish.

:::card{title="“Houses keep going up in our wayleave.”" icon="houses" eyebrow="Electricity Act 2019, s.26"}
"A person shall not, without the permission of a licensee, erect a building or structure in a position or manner that is likely to interfere with the supply of electricity through a transmission or distribution line" ([Electricity Act 2019](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/The%20Electricity%20Act%20No.%2011%20of%202019.pdf), s.26). Structures still appear between patrols, and each one is harder to resolve once it is lived in.
:::
:::card{title="“We have to keep the wayleave clean.”" icon="leaf" eyebrow="Electricity Act 2019, ss.23(7) and 25"}
"A licensee shall maintain a wayleaves in good state of cleanliness and repair" (s.23(7)). Trees and undergrowth that obstruct or interfere with a line may be cut after at least 14 days' notice, or without notice in an emergency, subject to the Forests Act 2015 (s.25).
:::

The environmental and social study for a recent Zambian interconnector counted the houses in one section's wayleave and identified the structures in an additional wayleave "based on satellite imagery interpretation". A reviewed, dated layer of that count can be compared at every later survey.
:::

Anyone planning development within 1 km of an existing or planned line must first get the wayleave position and clearances from the licensee in writing, and the licensee must advise within 45 days. Every request is a moment to know what already stands there.

### Minimum width for one line (Table 2.1)

| Voltage | Wayleave |
|---|---|
| 11 kV | 10 m urban, 15 m rural |
| 22 kV | 22 m |
| 33 kV | 18 m urban, 22 m rural (H-pole 30 m) |
| 66 kV and 88 kV | 30 m |
| **132 kV** | **32 m** |
| 220 kV and 275 kV | 44 m |
| **330 kV** | **48 m** |
| **400 kV** | **50 m** |

The Regulations' worked examples put three parallel 132 kV lines at 57.0 m and three parallel 330 kV lines at 102.8 m.
::::
::::col
### In a recognised forestry area (Table 2.4)

| Voltage | Each side | Total |
|---|---|---|
| 11 and 33 kV | 25 m | 50 m |
| 66 and 88 kV | 33 m | 66 m |
| 132 kV | 36 m | 72 m |
| 220 kV | 38 m | 76 m |
| 330 kV | 39 m | 78 m |
| 400 kV | 40 m | 80 m |

A "wayleave" is "a parcel of land with a predetermined uniform width over its length" ([SI No. 2 of 2026](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/Electricity-Wayleave-and-Clearances-Regulation-2026.pdf)), including easements, wayleave agreements, statutory wayleaves under the Electricity Act and wayleaves over State-leased or customary land.

Shared wayleaves narrower than the Schedule need the ERB's prior written approval (reg. 5(2)), and existing approved installations need not be modified unless safety requires it (reg. 6). No line may be placed over an explosives magazine, a fire hydrant, a water well or a borehole, and there is no digging for tree planting within 4 m of an underground cable.

::::section{id="existing-lines" eyebrow="Existing lines" title="A register of the structures inside the wayleave, then what changed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the line route you supply in its UTM zone, at the wayleave width for its voltage or the widths you hold for each section, and list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the centreline, its band, its chainage and its coordinates. Each 500 m stretch is rated high, medium or low for **encroachment density**, by a count rule, so your wayleave officers know where to go first. It is not a safety or clearance rating.

After the baseline, lines are re-surveyed on a schedule you agree with us. New and removed structures between dated surveys are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views, and your team receives a notice after each survey saying what changed and where. Fresh digging near towers, new tracks across the wayleave and cleared ground that often comes before building are flagged the same way.

When a structure is found, the Electricity Act gives the licensee a route: it may ask for removal or adjustment, and then apply to the planning authority under the Urban and Regional Planning Act 2015 (s.26). A dated register, with the date each structure first appears in the imagery, is the starting point for that conversation.

::::section{id="new-lines" tone="alt" eyebrow="New lines and interconnectors" title="Count the structures before the wayleave is acquired"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Zambia's transmission grid interconnects with the utilities of six neighbouring countries, and new 330 kV and 400 kV lines are planned and tendered to extend and reinforce it. Every new line needs a route, a wayleave and a record of what stood on the land when the wayleave was acquired. Under the Electricity Act, the licensee gives notice to the landowner and occupier for consent, they may object and the Minister decides within 60 days (s.23), and compensation for damage follows the Lands Acquisition Act.

The environmental and social study for a recent Zambian interconnector counted the houses in one section's wayleave, noted that a few more may have been built since the cut-off date, and identified the structures in an additional wayleave "based on satellite imagery interpretation". We produce that count as a reviewed GIS layer, dated, so it can be compared later: a register of the structures along each alignment option, then a baseline once the route is fixed, with reviewer categories such as main building, outbuilding or under construction.

ZESCO's practice, as described in a 2024 assessment for a 330 kV interconnector, is to acquire the entire wayleave. That makes the baseline the record every later survey is compared against.

::::section{id="drones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Drone detail" title="Drones along a line in Zambia"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Where you want flagged spans flown, drone surveys are subject to the ZCAA approvals and Zambia Air Force clearance each operation requires. Three rules matter along a line. Part 18 bars flights within 50 m of a structure unless the Director-General has approved it in the operator's Operations Manual or the owner gives permission, which applies to each tower and each house in the wayleave. It bans flights adjacent to or above a national key point or strategic installation outright, and the consolidated Protected Places and Areas Act prints orders that make several switching stations on the Copperbelt protected places (whether each is still in force is for Zambian counsel). And the Electricity Act makes it an offence to "cut, injure or interfere with any apparatus" (s.44), so the flight plan must rule out contact.

ZCAA's published list of ROC holders includes a utility that flies its own drones. If your team already flies, send the orthophotos or GeoTIFFs and we run the same register, change and vegetation analysis on them, with a person reviewing every result.
::::

### What we do

:::checklist
- Map structures, cleared ground, fresh excavations and tracks visible from the air

### What we don't do

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Measure conductor clearances or inspect towers and hardware
- Detect theft or vandalism, or identify who did anything
- Identify, count or follow people
- Decide whether a structure has the licensee's permission, or survey wayleave boundaries

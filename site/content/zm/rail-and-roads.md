faq:
  - q: How wide is the railway strip?
    a: The Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act prohibits building on the "railway strip" without the Authority's written permission. We have not confirmed a width for the railway strip, so we measure from the reserve boundary or centreline you supply, at the widths in your own records, and can add the 100 m band within which a mining-right holder needs the railway administration's consent, or the 60 m and 10 m distances in the Railways Act, as further bands.
  - q: Which road-reserve widths do you use?
    a: The ones you supply. Road-reserve widths come from the Public Roads Act and the road authority's own records, so send the reserve polygons or the width for each road class and we report each structure's distance and band. Up to six widths can be reported in one survey.
  - q: Do you decide which structures must move?
    a: No. We record what stands where and when it first appears in the imagery. Whether a structure has the railway or road authority's permission, predates the reserve or has to move is for the authority, the owner and, if it comes to that, the courts. The register gives them a dated, located list to work from.
  - q: Can the register support a rehabilitation programme's cut-off date?
    a: Yes. A dated record of the structures inside the reserve and the works footprint on the cut-off date supports the census and asset inventory, and re-surveys show what has appeared since. It does not replace the census, and categories do not establish use, ownership or value.
  - q: Can you track construction progress along the line?
    a: From dated drone orthophotos and elevation models we compare works between visits, with maps of where ground has been cut or filled, subject to the ZCAA approvals and Zambia Air Force clearance each operation requires. Drone flights over, along or within 50 m of a public road need specific approval or a road closure. It is not a certified survey or a quantity surveyor's valuation.
  - q: Can a drone fly along a railway or over a bridge?
    a: Only within the ZCAA's rules, and some crossings need more. The consolidated Protected Places and Areas Act prints orders that make land within 0.8045 km of the Kafue road and rail bridges and of the Luangwa Bridge protected areas (whether each is still in force is for Zambian counsel), and the State Security Act treats roads, railways and bridges as necessary services. Satellite work needs no flight; drone work near a bridge or rail installation is planned with the authority concerned.

::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="What stands in the reserve before the works start"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="“Nothing may be built on the railway strip.”" icon="rail" eyebrow="Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act, s.62"}
"Except with the written permission of the Authority, a person shall not erect any building or structure or execute any works on the railway strip" ([Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Tanzania-Zambia%20Railway%20Act.pdf), s.62). Unauthorised works can be ordered removed or modified within 30 days, after which the Authority may do the work and recover the cost.
:::
:::card{title="“The corridor is being rebuilt.”" icon="route" eyebrow="Rehabilitation"}
Zambia's railway to the Indian Ocean was placed under a concession to rehabilitate, modernise and operate its freight services in September 2025, and rail links across the region are attracting lender finance. Rehabilitation brings works footprints, access roads, borrow pits and camps, and a record of what stood in the reserve before they started.
:::
:::card{title="“We need a baseline before the census.”" icon="calendar" eyebrow="Cut-off dates"}
Where rehabilitation or widening affects homes, fields or businesses, the resettlement plan fixes a cut-off date. A dated register of the structures inside the reserve on that date supports the census, and re-surveys show what appears later.
:::
:::card{title="“Signals, crossings and fire risk.”" icon="alert" eyebrow="Railways Act (Cap. 453)"}
For the Zambia Railways network, the [Railways Act](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Railways%20Act.pdf) lets a railway company seek a High Court order for the removal of a building that obscures a signal or endangers rail services (s.14(3)), defines a firebreak at least 10 m wide from the railway land boundary, and excludes liability for fire damage to buildings within 60 m of the rails (s.69(1)).
:::

::::section{id="reserve-baselines" tone="alt" eyebrow="Reserve baselines before rehabilitation" title="A register of what stands in the reserve, at the widths in your records"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the railway or road you supply in its UTM zone, from the centreline or the reserve boundary, at the widths in your records, and list each structure the review confirms with its distance, its band, its chainage and its coordinates. Each 500 m stretch is rated high, medium or low for encroachment density by a count rule, so your land team knows where the reserve is most built up. It is not a safety rating.

We have not confirmed a width for the Tanzania-Zambia railway strip or the road-reserve widths in the Public Roads Act, so every register states the widths you supplied and where they came from. The same survey can add the other distances that matter along a line of rail: the 100 m from any railway track within which a mining-right holder needs the railway administration's consent (Minerals Regulation Commission Act 2024, s.35(1)(d)), and the 60 m and 10 m distances in the Railways Act.

Before rehabilitation, the baseline records the reserve as the works found it: structures with reviewer categories such as main building, outbuilding or under construction, tracks and informal crossings across the line, cleared ground and existing borrow pits. Once a cut-off date is set, the same register supports the resettlement census.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="roads" tone="alt" eyebrow="Road reserves" title="Road reserves and lender-financed road programmes"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Road reserves fall under the Public Roads Act 2002, amended in 2022 and 2026. We did not retrieve its reserve widths, so every road register uses the widths in your records and says so. Lender-financed road programmes bring environmental and social standards with them, and where a lender applies IFC Performance Standard 5, anyone affected by widening or realignment is counted against a cut-off date.

For a road, we buffer the centreline or reserve boundary you supply, list each structure inside the reserve and in a band beyond it, and flag new structures and cleared ground between dated surveys. Where a road is being widened, a register of the structures along each side, with reviewer categories, gives the RAP team a dated starting point, and route options for a realignment can be compared by the structures each would affect.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Near roads, drones need care"}
Part 18 bars drone flights over, along or within 50 m of a public road without specific approval or a road closure, and take-off and landing on public roads is reserved to civil defence and law enforcement. Satellite work needs neither.

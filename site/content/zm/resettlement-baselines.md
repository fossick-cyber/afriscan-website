### Compensation under Zambian law

We found no stand-alone resettlement statute in Zambia. Compensation runs through the [Lands Acquisition Act (Cap. 189)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Lands%20Acquisition%20Act.pdf), under which the President may acquire land compulsorily (s.3) with compensation (s.10) and a Compensation Advisory Board, and which excludes compensation for "undeveloped land or unutilised land" except for unexhausted improvements (s.15). The sector laws add their own rules: compensation for wayleave damage under the Electricity Act (s.23(6)), "fair and reasonable compensation" for disturbance and for damage to crops, trees and buildings under the Minerals Regulation Commission Act 2024 (s.40), and "adequate compensation" under the Zambia Tanzania Pipeline Act (s.8).

A recent environmental and social assessment for a Zambian interconnector describes the process: "As required by Zambian regulations, the ESMP and RAP are submitted as standalone reports", and the Ministry of Lands advises on the RAP at ZEMA's request and reviews and approves the compensation measures and costs.
::::
::::col
### The cut-off date under PS5 {#ps5}

[IFC Performance Standard 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf), paragraph 23: "The client is not required to compensate or assist those who encroach on the project area after the cut-off date for eligibility, provided the cut-off date has been clearly established and made public."

The condition matters as much as the rule. A cut-off date protects a project only when it has been clearly set and made public, and when there is a record of what stood in the footprint on that date. Lenders apply IFC Performance Standard 5 or the World Bank's ESS5; the register serves either.

Zambia's National Resettlement Policy (2015) lists "Encroachment of populations on land meant for other activities" among the causes of involuntary displacement. A dated register records the footprint as it was, so later questions are about facts, not memories.

New and removed structures between the cut-off date and each later survey, flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views. For a single plot or claim, a sheet of every dated image covering it.

That is the work a reviewed, dated register makes repeatable: the same method, the same IDs and the same bands at every survey.

::::section{id="dated-imagery" eyebrow="The imagery" title="Why the date on the imagery matters"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A cut-off record is only as good as the date of the imagery behind it. We use dated sources only: drone surveys on the day, satellite scenes with a known capture date, or imagery your team already holds with its date. Map-service basemaps, whose dates are not known, are never used as a cut-off record.

Cloud in the rainy season can delay a new capture, and archive coverage varies by place, so we tell you which dated scenes exist for your footprint before you commit, and how close to the cut-off date they are. Where the archive is too old, a new capture can be requested; its date depends on satellite availability and weather.

Drone surveys on the cut-off date are subject to the ZCAA approvals and Zambia Air Force clearance each operation requires, including a special authorisation and a ZCAA inspector on site, so they are planned into the resettlement timeline from the start. See [drone law in Zambia](/zm/drone-regulations).

::::section{id="data" eyebrow="Data protection" title="Structures in the register, names in your systems"}
Personal data under the [Data Protection Act 2021](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%203%20The%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202021_0.pdf) include location data and anything from which a person can be directly or indirectly identified (s.2), so imagery of homes linked to names, census lists or grievance files is personal data. A data controller must register with the Data Protection Commission (s.19) and "shall process and store personal data on a server or data centre located in the Republic" (s.70(1)), and sensitive personal data always stay in Zambia (s.70(3)).

Our registers carry structure IDs and coordinates, not names, so the links to households stay in your census database. Each proposal sets out the controller and processor roles and where project data will be stored. More in our [notes for procurement and compliance teams](/zm/procurement#data).

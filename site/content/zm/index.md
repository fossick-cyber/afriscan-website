faq:
  - q: Which widths do you measure in Zambia?
    a: The ones that apply to your asset. For power lines we default to the minimum wayleave for the voltage in the Electricity (Wayleave and Clearances) Regulations 2026, such as 32 m for 132 kV, 48 m for 330 kV and 50 m for 400 kV, or the wider strip in a recognised forestry area. For pipelines, railways and roads we measure from the centreline or reserve boundary you supply, at your easement or reserve widths, and for licence areas from the boundary. Up to six widths can be reported in one survey, each structure with its distance and band.
  - q: Does a satellite survey need ZCAA approval?
    a: No aircraft is flown, so the drone rules in Part 18 of the Zambia Civil Aviation Requirements do not apply to the satellite work itself, and satellite-based wayleave and right-of-way screening is available across Zambia (Zambian counsel should confirm this for any corridor or site declared a protected place or area). Drone stretches, where you want them, are planned separately and are subject to the ZCAA approvals and Zambia Air Force clearance each operation requires.
  - q: Can you tell us which structures are unauthorised?
    a: No. We map what stands where, how far it is from your line or boundary, and when it first appears in the imagery. Whether it has the licensee's permission under the Electricity Act, the railway authority's written permission or the licence holder's consent, or predates the wayleave, is for your land and legal teams and the authorities to decide. The register gives them a dated, located list to work from.
  - q: Our corridor is a declared protected area. Can you still work on it?
    a: Some pipeline corridors, pumping stations, mine workings and bridges in Zambia are covered by orders printed in the consolidated Protected Places and Areas Act; whether each is still in force is for Zambian counsel. We work there only for, or with the written authority of, the operator or licence holder, and security sites are masked in every deliverable.
  - q: Do you detect vandalism or theft on lines and pipelines?
    a: No. Satellites cannot see theft, vandalism or people. We map land change, such as new structures, fresh excavations, cleared ground and new tracks, so your patrols know where to look first.
  - q: How is personal data handled under the Data Protection Act?
    a: Our registers describe structures, not people, and carry no names. Imagery of homes becomes personal data once it is linked to names, census lists or grievance files, and the Data Protection Act 2021 then requires registration and storage in Zambia unless an exception applies. Each proposal sets out the controller and processor roles, where project data will be stored and how results are shared. See [working with AfriScan in Zambia](/zm/procurement#data).
  - q: Is the register a land survey?
    a: No. The Land Survey Act requires the Surveyor-General's written permission before aerial photography is used for land survey, and our registers are not land surveys or cadastral plans. They are monitoring and planning records built from imagery, which show your surveyors and wayleave officers where to look.

::::section{id="the-law" eyebrow="The widths are in the law" title="What Zambian law says about building in wayleaves, rights of way and reserves" lead="Zambia's rules set widths, distances and consents that a dated structure register can be measured against. These are the four that come up most."}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Power-line wayleaves have minimum widths" icon="power" eyebrow="SI No. 2 of 2026"}
The [Electricity (Wayleave and Clearances) Regulations 2026](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/Electricity-Wayleave-and-Clearances-Regulation-2026.pdf) set minimum wayleave widths by voltage, for example 32 m for a 132 kV line, 48 m for 330 kV and 50 m for 400 kV, and wider strips where a line crosses a recognised forestry area. Under the [Electricity Act 2019](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/The%20Electricity%20Act%20No.%2011%20of%202019.pdf), no one may put up a building or structure that is likely to interfere with a line without the licensee's permission (s.26).
:::
:::card{title="New pipeline routes avoid settlements" icon="pipeline" eyebrow="ERB siting guidelines"}
The Energy Regulation Board's [Guidelines for Siting Petroleum Infrastructure](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/REVISED_GUIDELINES_FOR_SITING_OF_PETROLEUM_INFRASTRUCTURE-_AUGUST-2022.pdf) say a new petroleum pipeline route shall be such that the pipeline "does not pass through settlements", and must leave enough land for a right of way under Zambian Standard ZS 704 (s.5.7).
:::
:::card{title="Mining needs consent near homes and railways" icon="mine" eyebrow="MRC Act 2024, s.35"}
Under the [Minerals Regulation Commission Act 2024](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2014%20of%202024%20The%20Mineral%20Regulation%20Commission.pdf), a mining-right holder needs written consent before working within 180 m of an occupied house, within 100 m of a railway track, or on village and customary land.
:::
:::card{title="The railway strip is kept clear" icon="rail" eyebrow="TAZARA Act, s.62"}
Under the [Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Tanzania-Zambia%20Railway%20Act.pdf), no building, structure or works may be put on the railway strip without the Authority's written permission.
:::
:::

Each of those rules turns on the same facts: what stands on the ground, where, how close to the line or boundary, and since when. That is what a dated register records. For the widths and consents in detail, read our [guide to wayleaves, reserves and rights of way in Zambia](/zm/wayleave-guide).

::::section{id="drones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Drones in Zambia" title="Satellite first, drones under ZCARs Part 18"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Satellite and your own imagery.** A survey starts from your route file and dated satellite imagery, open building datasets or the orthophotos you already hold. No aircraft flies, so no special authorisation or Air Force clearance is needed, however long the wayleave.

**Drone detail.** Drone surveys in Zambia need ZCAA approvals for the operator, every aircraft and every pilot: an RPAS Operator Certificate with its operations specifications, an Air Services Permit for commercial work, a registration and Letter of Approval for each drone, and a Remote Pilot Licence for each pilot. Each operation also needs a ZCAA special authorisation, a ZCAA inspector on site and Zambia Air Force airspace clearance. Flights within 50 m of structures, people or public roads need specific approval or permission, which matters along any wayleave lined with homes.

Afridrone is working towards the ZCAA approvals Zambia requires. Every drone proposal names the Zambian company that will fly it and its approvals, so your team can check them.

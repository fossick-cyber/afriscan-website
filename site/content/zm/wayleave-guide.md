  - q: Is a structure inside a wayleave always unlawful?
    a: Not necessarily. The Electricity Act prohibits a building or structure that is "likely to interfere" with a line without the licensee's permission, the Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act prohibits building on the railway strip without the Authority's written permission, and consents under the Minerals Regulation Commission Act can be given. Whether a particular structure has permission, predates the wayleave or must move is for the licensee, the authority, the landowner and, if it comes to that, the courts.   - q: Do the 2026 Regulations make existing lines wider?

## One word, several kinds of strip {#what-a-wayleave-is}

In Zambia, "wayleave" is the everyday word for the strip of land a line needs, and the 2026 electricity regulations give it a legal meaning: "a parcel of land with a predetermined uniform width over its length". The definition covers easements and wayleave agreements, statutory wayleaves under ss.22–23 of the Electricity Act, and wayleaves over State-leased or customary land under the Lands Act ([SI No. 2 of 2026](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/Electricity-Wayleave-and-Clearances-Regulation-2026.pdf)).

Other assets use other words. A pipeline has a right of way, which the Energy Regulation Board defines as easements "agreed and signed upon by both the landowner and Pipeline Company". A railway has its strip or reserve, a road its reserve, and a mine its licence area and the consent distances around its workings. The land underneath usually stays with its owner or occupier, often under customary tenure in a chiefdom, and goes on being farmed, grazed and lived on around the line. That is why structures appear in wayleaves between inspections, and why the records matter.

| Strip | The rule | Width or distance |
|---|---|---|
| **Power-line wayleave** | No building or structure likely to interfere with the line without the licensee's permission (Electricity Act 2019, s.26) | By voltage in SI No. 2 of 2026: for example 32 m for 132 kV, 48 m for 330 kV, 50 m for 400 kV; wider in recognised forestry areas |
| **Petroleum pipeline right of way** | New routes "shall be such that the pipeline does not pass through settlements" (ERB siting guidelines, s.5.7.3) | Right of way to meet Zambian Standard ZS 704; no width in the Zambia Tanzania Pipeline Act |
| **Railway strip** | No building, structure or works without the Authority's written permission (Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act, s.62) | Use the width in the Authority's records |
| **Road reserve** | Public Roads Act 2002, amended in 2022 and 2026 | As in the road authority's records |
| **Mining consent areas** | Written consent before working near homes, railways, dams and village land (Minerals Regulation Commission Act 2024, s.35) | 180 m from a house, 100 m from a railway track, 90 m from a dam, 45 m from cropped land |

## Power-line wayleaves {#power-lines}

The [Electricity (Wayleave and Clearances) Regulations 2026](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/Electricity-Wayleave-and-Clearances-Regulation-2026.pdf), SI No. 2 of 2026, were made under s.52 of the Electricity Act, signed by the Minister of Energy on 23 December 2025 and gazetted on 2 January 2026. They cover lines of 0.4 to 400 kV and "the entire Zambian electricity supply industry".

|---|---|---|
| 11 kV | 10 m urban, 15 m rural | 50 m |
| 22 kV | 22 m | |
| 33 kV | 18 m urban, 22 m rural (H-pole 30 m) | 50 m |
| 66 kV and 88 kV | 30 m | 66 m |
| 132 kV | 32 m | 72 m |
| 220 kV | 44 m | 76 m |
| 275 kV | 44 m | |
| 330 kV | 48 m | 78 m |
| 400 kV | 50 m | 80 m |

The Regulations work through parallel lines too: three parallel 132 kV lines take 57.0 m, three parallel 330 kV lines 102.8 m. They also set vertical clearances above buildings, such as 6.0 m for 132 kV, 8.0 m for 330 kV and 9.4 m for 400 kV, bar lines over an explosives magazine, a fire hydrant, a water well or a borehole, and bar digging for tree planting within 4 m of an underground cable.

**The 1 km developer duty.** Schedule 1 makes anyone planning development "within a radius of 1km to an existing or non-existing, but already planned, overhead line or underground asset or wayleave" a developer, other than the licensee that owns the wayleave and the Defence Force. At project initiation, the developer must "obtain prior written identification and specification of any wayleave … and the required clearances", and the licensee must advise "in writing, within 45 days". The duty is framed around the development, not its type, so a housing scheme, a mine, a solar plant or a road near a line can all be caught by it; ask the licensee if in doubt.

**What the Electricity Act adds.** Section 23 lets a licensee place lines across "any land other than land covered by a building", after notice for consent to the landowner and occupier, who may object; the Minister decides within 60 days, and compensation for damage follows the Lands Acquisition Act. The licensee "shall maintain a wayleaves in good state of cleanliness and repair" (s.23(7)). Trees or undergrowth that obstruct or interfere with a line may be cut after at least 14 days' notice, or without notice in an emergency, subject to the Forests Act 2015 (s.25). Section 26 prohibits building "in a position or manner that is likely to interfere with the supply of electricity" without the licensee's permission; the licensee may ask for removal or adjustment and then apply to the planning authority under the Urban and Regional Planning Act 2015. Interfering with apparatus is an offence (s.44).

**The 2011 Code of Practice.** The 2026 Regulations contain no express revocation of the ERB's 2011 Wayleave Code of Practice. The ERB's September 2026 draft siting guidelines cite the 2026 Regulations where the 2022 edition cited the Code.

**Practice on new lines.** ZESCO's policy, as set out in a 2024 environmental and social assessment for a 330 kV interconnector, "is to acquire the entire wayleave", and seasonal crops below 2 m are allowed in it while taller crops are not. More: [power-line wayleaves in Zambia](/zm/power-lines).

## Petroleum pipeline rights of way {#pipelines}

The Energy Regulation Board licenses "pipeline transportation of crude oil, petroleum products and natural gas" under the Energy Regulation Act 2019. Its [Guidelines for Siting Petroleum Infrastructure](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/REVISED_GUIDELINES_FOR_SITING_OF_PETROLEUM_INFRASTRUCTURE-_AUGUST-2022.pdf) (First Revision, August 2022) set the route rules in s.5.7:

- the route must leave adequate land "for the right of way to meet the requirements of a Zambian Standard ZS 704: Transportation Pipeline systems for liquid hydrocarbons – Code of Practice" (5.7.1);
- the pipeline is "interred throughout" except at service points (5.7.2);
- "The proposed route for a Petroleum pipeline shall be such that the pipeline does not pass through settlements" (5.7.3);
- electricity safety distances apply (5.7.4), and ecologically sensitive areas are avoided "unless approved by ZEMA" (5.7.5).

The [Second Revision, First Draft](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/files/Guidelines-for-Siting-Petroleum-Infrastructure_Second-Revision_First-Draft.pdf) (September 2026) keeps 5.7.3, ties 5.7.4 to the 2026 wayleave regulations ("the developer will need to provide documentary proof from the relevant utility Company") and 5.7.5 to the EIA Regulations 2026. The ERB's [construction-permit checklist](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/files/checklists/Construction_Permits/Construction-Permit-Checklist-Petroleum-Pipeline.pdf) (LN.FM-008, Rev 01) asks for environmental clearance, zoning approval, wayleave clearances for roads, electricity, water and railways, proof of land ownership or lease, a topographical map with WGS 84 coordinates and "Distances from facilities which are incompatible, residential, sensitive ecological area".

The [Zambia Tanzania Pipeline Act (Cap. 455)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Zambia%20Tanzania%20Pipeline%20Act.pdf) gives the pipeline company entry to survey an intended route, with seven days' notice before entering a building or a dwelling's garden (s.4), wayleaves over unalienated land (s.5), and statutory orders across private land, but the pipeline may not go "into, through, or against any building, or in any private land covered with buildings, without the consent of the owner and lawful occupier" (s.6(2)). It sets no strip width and no building ban. Separately, the consolidated [Protected Places and Areas Act (Cap. 125)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Protected%20Places%20and%20Areas%20Act.pdf) prints an order making the area within 137.16 m on each side of that pipeline a protected area; whether it remains in force in the 2019 Revised Edition is for counsel. More: [pipeline rights of way in Zambia](/zm/pipelines).

## Railway strips {#railways}

On the Tanzania-Zambia railway, "Except with the written permission of the Authority, a person shall not erect any building or structure or execute any works on the railway strip" ([Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act, Cap. 454](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Tanzania-Zambia%20Railway%20Act.pdf), s.62). Unauthorised works can be ordered removed or modified within 30 days, after which the Authority may do the work itself and recover the cost, and the Authority is not liable for fire damage to buildings within the strip (s.83). We have not confirmed a width for the railway strip, so a register measures from the boundary or centreline in the Authority's records.

For the Zambia Railways network, the [Railways Act (Cap. 453)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Railways%20Act.pdf) lets a railway company seek a High Court order for the removal of a building that obscures a signal or endangers rail services (s.14(3)), defines a firebreak as at least 10 m wide from the railway land boundary, and excludes liability for fire damage to buildings within 60 m of the rails (s.69(1)). And any mining-right holder needs the railway administration's consent to work within 100 m of any railway track. More: [rail and road reserves in Zambia](/zm/rail-and-roads).

## Road reserves {#roads}

Road reserves fall under the Public Roads Act 2002, amended in 2022 and 2026. We did not retrieve its reserve widths for this guide, so we do not quote them: use the widths in the road authority's records, and ask for them in writing for the road classes your project touches. A mining-right holder also needs consent to work on streets and roads (Minerals Regulation Commission Act, s.35).

## Mining licence areas and consent distances {#mining}

Under the [Minerals Regulation Commission Act 2024](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2014%20of%202024%20The%20Mineral%20Regulation%20Commission.pdf), which repeals the Mines and Minerals Development Act 2015, a rights holder may not work without written consent within 180 m of an inhabited, occupied or temporarily uninhabited house or building (the owner's or occupier's), within 45 m of cleared or cropped land, within 90 m of a cattle dip tank, dam or private water, within 90 m of a building or dam owned by the Republic (the appropriate authority's), on village or customary land (the chief's and the local authority's), or within 100 m of any railway track (the railway administration's) (s.35). The reverse applies too: an owner or occupier of land under an exploration licence "shall not erect a building or structure on the land without the consent of the holder of the exploration licence", and the Commission may grant consent that is unreasonably withheld (s.37(2)). Compensation for disturbance and damage must be "fair and reasonable", with claims within three years (s.40). More: [mining licence areas in Zambia](/zm/mining).

## Customary land, acquisition and planning {#land}

The [Lands Act (Cap. 184)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Lands%20Act.pdf) recognises customary holdings; conversion to leasehold takes effect "only after the approval of the chief and the local authorities", and land in customary areas is not alienated without consulting the chief and the local authority. It has no wayleave provisions of its own. Compulsory acquisition and compensation run through the [Lands Acquisition Act (Cap. 189)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Lands%20Acquisition%20Act.pdf), which excludes compensation for "undeveloped land or unutilised land" except for unexhausted improvements (s.15). Development permission comes under the [Urban and Regional Planning Act 2015](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/The%20Urban%20and%20Regional%20Planning%20%20Act%2C%202015.pdf), which is also the route for removing a structure under s.26 of the Electricity Act. For resettlement and the cut-off date, see [resettlement baselines in Zambia](/zm/resettlement-baselines).

## Protected corridors and sites {#protected}

Some corridors and installations are declared protected areas or places under the Protected Places and Areas Act. The consolidated text on Parliament's website prints orders for the oil pipeline to Tanzania (137.16 m on each side), two of its pumping stations and 91.44 m beyond their fences, the oil refinery at Ndola, "all places, excavations or works" where mining is carried on, the Kafue road and rail bridges and the Luangwa Bridge (0.8045 km around each), the Kafue Gorge, and several switching stations on the Copperbelt. Whether each order is still in force in the 2019 Revised Edition is for counsel. Work on these assets is done only for, or with the written authority of, the operator, and security sites are masked in any record that leaves the operator. For the drone rules on and near these sites, see [drone law in Zambia](/zm/drone-regulations#protected-places).

## Records and personal data {#data}

A register of structures can quickly become a record about people: imagery of homes linked to names, census lists or grievance files is personal data under the [Data Protection Act 2021](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%203%20The%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202021_0.pdf). A data controller registers with the Data Protection Commission (s.19), stores personal data on a server or data centre in Zambia (s.70(1)), transfers it abroad only on the conditions in s.71, and notifies the Commissioner of a breach within 24 hours (s.49). Keep the names in your own systems and the register on structure IDs and coordinates.

## A checklist for wayleave holders {#checklist}

:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Know your widths"}
The SI No. 2 of 2026 width for each line and voltage, the forestry widths where lines cross recognised forestry areas, and the widths in your easements, reserve records and design standards, in one GIS layer.
:::
:::step{title="Keep a dated record of the strip"}
What stood in the wayleave or reserve when it was acquired, on any cut-off date, and at each later survey, with the imagery date on every record.
:::
:::step{title="Answer developers in writing"}
A process for the 1 km developer requests under Schedule 1, answered within 45 days with the wayleave position, the clearances and a current picture of the strip.
:::
:::step{title="Track what is new"}
Re-surveys on a schedule that fits the pressure on each stretch, with new structures, excavations, cleared ground and tracks flagged between dates.
:::
:::step{title="Follow the removal route"}
A request to the owner under s.26, then the planning authority; for a railway strip, the notice under s.62. Keep the dated record with each case.
:::
:::step{title="Respect protected sites"}
Know which of your corridors and installations are declared protected, work there only with the operator's authority, and mask security sites.
:::
:::step{title="Keep personal data in Zambia"}
Registration with the Data Protection Commission, storage under s.70, and names kept apart from the structure register.
:::
:::

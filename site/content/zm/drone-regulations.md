faq:
  - q: Does a satellite corridor survey need ZCAA approval?
    a: No aircraft is flown, so Part 18 does not apply to the satellite work itself, and we found no Zambian law that restricts the analysis of satellite imagery. Zambian counsel should confirm that position for deliverables that map a declared protected place or area. Where a corridor or site has that status, we work for, or with the written authority of, its operator, and security sites are masked in every deliverable.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly commercial drone surveys in Zambia?
    a: Part 18 has no express nationality bar, but commercial work needs a ZCAA RPAS Operator Certificate and an Air Services Permit, the drones must be on the Zambian register, ZCAA's company registration form asks for Companies Act papers and a Zambian identity document for the authorised person, pilots need ZCAA licences, and foreign pilot training counts only with the Director-General's prior approval. In practice a foreign firm works through a Zambian operator with its own ROC, or sets up its own Zambian company and goes through certification.
  - q: We fly our own drones for our own lines or mine. Do we need an Air Services Permit?
    a: Part 18 requires the permit "in the case of commercial operations" (18.4.1(a)(2)), and a utility or mine flying its own drones as an aid to its own business is a corporate operation under the definition in 18.1.1(h). ZCAA's ROC guidance, however, says a permit is required "for all operations". Ask the ZCAA in writing which applies to your programme. You need the ROC either way.
  - q: Can a drone fly along a pipeline corridor, over a pump station or over a mine?
    a: "Part 18 bans flights adjacent to or above a national key point or strategic installation, with no approval route (18.5.10(b)). Separately, the consolidated Protected Places and Areas Act prints orders that declare the whole corridor of the oil pipeline to Tanzania a protected area, its pumping stations protected places and every mine working a protected place. Whether each order is still in force is for counsel, and whether a drone overflight counts as being in a protected place is not settled. Plan any such flight only with the operator's or licence holder's written authority and pass, and police coordination."
  - q: How close can a drone fly to houses and roads?
    a: Without specific ROC approval, a drone may not fly within 50 m of a structure unless the owner gives permission (18.5.14), over or within 50 m of people who are not part of the operation (18.5.13), or over, along or within 50 m of a public road unless the road is closed (18.5.15). Along a wayleave lined with homes, that decides the flight plan.
  - q: Can a drone fly beyond the pilot's line of sight along a power line?
    a: "Yes, but only by an operator whose Operations Manual the Director-General has approved for it (18.5.11): in visual conditions below 400 ft, after demonstrating command-input flight, waypoint following, interruptible commands, a moving-map position and obstacle and collision avoidance, with a C2-link safety case and a 20 per cent energy reserve. The pilot needs a B-VLOS rating and a Class 4 medical. Unlike Mozambique, Zambia has an approval route for it."
  - q: What goes into a special authorisation application?
    a: "ZCAA's form F-002 asks for the type of operation, the dates, the area and its GPS coordinates, the height above ground, the pilot's licence number, consent letters from the relevant authorities, the registration and insurance, detailed maps, pilot licences and IDs, and photos of the drone. For commercial operations the operator's ROC is required, and the applicant arranges and provides for the ZCAA inspector who oversees the operation."

:::

A ZCAA **Remote Pilot Licence** in the right category and ratings, valid for one year, with a radiotelephony certificate and ICAO English level 4.

A **special authorisation** on form F-002, a **ZCAA inspector** on site, **Zambia Air Force airspace clearance**, liaison with the **Zambia Police** in each area and air traffic coordination with ZACL where needed.
:::
:::card{title="The site" icon="shield" eyebrow="Owners, occupiers, Cap. 125"}
Consent letters from the relevant authorities, the owner's permission for flights within 50 m of a structure and, for a declared protected place or area, the occupier's pass or written authority.

Since 18 March 2026 the ZCAA handles drones through a dedicated **UAV & Aircraft Certification Unit**, and only the revised application forms are accepted ([AIC 005/2026](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/document-repository/get-folder/767/download)). ::::section{id="operator" eyebrow="The operator" title="The RPAS Operator Certificate and its OpSpecs"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Who needs one.** Part 18 requires an ROC "in the case of commercial, corporate and non-profit operations" (18.4.1(a)(1)). A corporate operation is "a non-commercial operation or use of RPAS by an entity for professional or aerial work as an aid to the conduct of business of that entity" (18.1.1(h)), so a utility or a mine that flies its own drones needs one too. Only private flying escapes it, and private use is limited to reduced line of sight with a Class 1A or 1B drone (18.1.4).

**What the application contains.** The registration and Letter of Approval of each drone, and an original Operations Manual; ZCAA's list of requirements also names a valid Air Services Permit ([ZCAA ROC guidance](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/admin/rpas/download/14)). Certification runs in five phases, so allow for it in any programme that depends on a new certificate.

**How long it lasts.** One year. Renewal is due at least 60 days before expiry, the certificate cannot be transferred (18.4.10), and no drone may be listed on more than one ROC.

**The company behind it.** ZCAA's form for registering a company's drone (F-001B, Rev 0, November 2025) asks for the latest register of directors under the Companies Act 2017 (Cap. 388) and the Certificate of Incorporation, a resolution signed by at least three directors, members or trustees, a Zambian identity document, passport or driving licence for the authorised person, an affidavit of ownership and de-registration, the ZRA clearance and 12 months of insurance cover. Every annexure must be attested by a Commissioner for Oaths.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Duties that come with the certificate"}
- **Security** (18.4.7): background checks, criminal-record checks every two years, secure storage, a security coordinator and ZCARs Part 9 security training.
- **Records** kept for five years.
- **Audits**: the ZCAA may audit the holder, its partners and its subcontractors.
- **Insurance**: "at all times be adequately insured for third party liability" (18.4.11). No minimum amount is published.
:::
::::
:::::

ZCAA publishes the [list of ROC holders](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/admin/rpas/download/4). The copy posted on 13 February 2026, dated 20 November 2025, names 11 operators, among them a utility and a mining company that fly their own drones. Check any operator against the current list, and ask for the OpSpecs pages that cover your job.

::::section{id="air-services-permit" tone="alt" eyebrow="The air service" title="The Air Services Permit, now granted by the Director-General"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 18.4.1(a)(2) requires an Air Services Permit "in the case of commercial operations", and the Civil Aviation Act makes running an air service without one an offence (s.28(2)). On 10 June 2026, [AIC 007/2026](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/document-repository/get-folder/777/download) announced that the Minister "has delegated authority for the granting, renewal, variation, suspension and revocation of Air Services Permits to the Director-General", and that applications "shall henceforth be submitted directly to the Civil Aviation Authority".

There is one inconsistency to settle for in-house programmes. ZCAA's ROC guidance says "for all operations, a valid ROC including the Operations Specifications and an Air Services Permit … is required", while Part 18 limits the permit to commercial operations. If your organisation flies its own drones, ask the ZCAA in writing which applies.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="For contracts"}
Ask for the permit's number and expiry date alongside the ROC and OpSpecs, and make it a condition that both stay valid for the length of the contract. A certificate without the permit is not enough for commercial work.

::::section{id="aircraft" eyebrow="Each aircraft" title="Registration, Letter of Approval, sale and maintenance"}
:::cards{cols="2" style="plain"}
:::card{title="Certificate of Registration" icon="clipboard"}
"No RPA shall be operated, unless such RPA holds a valid certificate of registration issued by the Director General" (18.2.4). A registered drone has Zambian nationality, and a transfer must be notified within 30 days. The ZCAA inspects each drone first to decide whether it is a toy or a professional drone; toy drones need no registration. Registration does not by itself confirm the right to a Letter of Approval (form note 2.9). All new registrations use the **"9I" prefix** from 1 January 2026 ([AIC 005/2026](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/document-repository/get-folder/767/download)).
:::
:::card{title="RPA Letter of Approval" icon="file-check"}
Required for any operation and valid for 12 months (18.2.1–18.2.3), on the strength of design-standard or system-safety documentation. The drone needs an altimeter unless it flies only in reduced line of sight.
:::
:::card{title="Buying a drone in Zambia" icon="building"}
"No RPA shall be sold within the Republic of Zambia unless the seller has written approval by the Director General" (18.1.7). ZCAA publishes the [approved resellers](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/admin/rpas/download/16).
:::
:::card{title="Maintenance" icon="shield"}
An approved maintenance programme (18.6). For Class 3 and larger drones, a maintenance technician who is a Zambian citizen or holds a permanent residence permit, or a temporary work permit with a letter of employment; for Class 2 and smaller, the operator may maintain its own drones if it shows it can.

::::section{id="pilots" tone="alt" eyebrow="Each pilot" title="The ZCAA Remote Pilot Licence"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Part 18.3 sets out three categories, aeroplane, helicopter and multirotor (RPL(A), RPL(H) and RPL(MR)), with ratings for visual line of sight, extended visual line of sight and beyond visual line of sight. An applicant must:

- be 18 or over;
- have a Class 4 medical for B-VLOS work or Class 3 and larger drones, and otherwise a medical assessment report;
- hold a restricted radiotelephony certificate and ICAO English at level 4;
- pass the theory exam at a ZCAA-approved training organisation within the 90 days before the skill test;
- take the skill test within 60 days of finishing flight training.
::::
::::col
**Validity.** One year, with a revalidation check in the 60 days before expiry.

**Foreign training.** Theory or flight training done abroad counts only with the Director-General's prior approval (18.3.3(d), 18.3.4(d)). A pilot's licence from another country does not carry over by itself.

**Every operation.** ZCAA's guidance says a Remote Pilot Licence "is required to operate a drone for all operational authorizations". ZCAA also lists the declared training organisations that run the courses.

::::section{id="each-operation" eyebrow="Each operation" title="Special authorisation, the ZCAA inspector and Air Force clearance" lead="These steps come from ZCAA guidance and forms published in 2025 and 2026, not from the text of Part 18. They apply to every operation, and they are what makes a drone job in Zambia take planning."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**The special authorisation.** ZCAA form [CAA/UAC/F-002](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/admin/rpas/download/6) (Rev 0, November 2025) asks for:

- the type of operation (VLOS, EVLOS, BVLOS or R-VLOS), the dates, the area with its GPS coordinates, the height above ground and the pilot's licence number;
- "Consent Letters from relevant competent Authorities in Zambia";
- the registration, the insurance, detailed maps, pilot licences and IDs, and photos of the drone.

For commercial operations, "The ROC Certificate of the drone operator" is required ([ZCAA guidance](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/admin/rpas/download/13)).

**The inspector.** Before any drone operation starts, the operator arranges with the ZCAA for an inspector to provide safety oversight of it, and provides the inspector's transport, full board and a subsistence allowance for each night.

**Air Force, police and air traffic.** ZCAA guidance tells operators to "Obtain Zambia Airforce (ZAF) Airspace Clearance and other required permits before you embark on any RPAS operation in Zambia", to liaise with the Zambia Police "in each and every area the RPAS will be flying" and give the ZCAA the police contact details, and to arrange with ZACL for a controller to coordinate with air traffic control wherever necessary ([ZCAA guidance](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/admin/rpas/download/12)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="The 2026 clearance portal"}
[AIC 004/2026](https://www.caa.co.zm/api/document-repository/get-folder/766/download) (27 January 2026) launched a joint Zambia Air Force and ZCAA airspace-clearance portal, with applications due at least 48 hours before the flight. The circular is addressed to "air operators" and does not say whether drone operators must use it. Ask the ZCAA which route applies to your operation.

::::section{id="flight-rules" tone="alt" eyebrow="The flight" title="Flight rules that shape a corridor or site survey" lead="Part 18.5, as published in Revision 0. Where a rule has an approval route, only an operator whose ROC covers it can use it."}
### Limits that need Director-General approval

Only an operator whose ROC carries the Director-General's approval may fly "above 400 ft above the surface", "within a radius of 10 km from an aerodrome" or "within restricted or prohibited airspace" (18.5.10(c)). Since the Civil Aviation (Amendment) Act 2026, flying in a declared danger, restricted or prohibited area "shall be considered to be an unauthorised operation" (new s.8A). ZCAA's AIC A008.26 (July 2026) lists the aerodromes that hold its aerodrome certificates or licences, which is worth checking against the route before any drone plan is costed; the 10 km rule itself refers to any aerodrome.

### Banned outright {#banned}

No drone may "be flown adjacent to or above a nuclear power plant, prison, police station, crime scene, court of law, national key point or strategic installation", and towing, aerobatic displays and flying in formation or swarm are banned (18.5.10(b)). There is no approval route. Part 18 does not define "national key point" or "strategic installation", so ask the ZCAA whether a pumping station, fuel terminal, power station or substation near your route counts before you plan a flight near it.

### The 50 m rules {#fifty-metres}

| Near | Rule | Unless |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | Not overhead, or within 50 m laterally (18.5.13) | Approved by the Director-General in an ROC operator's Operations Manual, or the people are part of the operation |
| **Structures** | Not within 50 m (18.5.14) | Approved by the Director-General in an ROC operator's Operations Manual, or the owner gives permission |
| **Public roads** | Not over, along or within 50 m (18.5.15) | Approved by the Director-General in an ROC operator's Operations Manual, or the road is closed |
| **Take-off and landing** | Not on a public road (18.5.2) | Civil defence and law enforcement only |

Near villages and compounds along a wayleave, the structure rule means either an ROC approval or the owners' permissions, which is a deliverable in its own right on a long line.

### Line of sight, night and controlled airspace

| Mode | What it means |
|---|---|
| VLOS | Below 400 ft above ground, within 500 m of the pilot |
| E-VLOS | Below 400 ft, with an observer keeping contact within 1,000 m of the pilot; at least one trained observer aged 18 or over (18.4.4(b)) |
| R-VLOS | Within 500 m of the pilot and below the highest obstacle within 300 m |
| B-VLOS | Only by an operator approved by the Director-General in its Operations Manual, in visual conditions below 400 ft, with a C2-link safety case (18.5.8) and a 20 per cent energy reserve (18.5.23(e)); see 18.5.11 |

**Night** flights are allowed only in reduced line of sight, or by an operator approved for night work with the same capability as for B-VLOS, strobes and a beacon or navigation lights (18.5.12). **Controlled airspace** (an ATZ or CTR) is open only to operators with an ROC, in visual conditions below 400 ft, with a mode C or S transponder unless exempted, a QNH altimeter and strobes, after notifying ATC and receiving its confirmation (18.5.3). Except in reduced line of sight, the pilot carries an air-band radio and makes position calls with the registration as call-sign (18.5.16).

### On site

Before flying: NOTAM and AIP checks, a weather assessment and crew roles "defined in writing" (Schedule 18.5.17). On site: a first-aid kit and fire extinguishers within 300 m of take-off and landing (18.5.24–25); no alcohol within 8 hours of duty and a limit of 0.02 g per 100 ml (18.5.7); accidents and incidents reported under ZCARs Part 8. Near power lines, remember that the Electricity Act makes it an offence to "cut, injure or interfere with any apparatus" (s.44(1)(d)), so the flight plan must rule out any contact.

**What this means for a corridor.** Standard work is flown in VLOS legs of up to 500 m below 400 ft. Unlike Mozambique, Zambia has an approval route for B-VLOS, but only through an operator whose Operations Manual the Director-General has approved for it.

::::section{id="protected-places" eyebrow="Protected places and areas" title="Pipeline corridors, pumping stations, mine workings and bridges" lead="The Protected Places and Areas Act (Cap. 125) works on the ground, not in the air, but its orders cover exactly the assets corridor surveys are about."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**How the Act works.** In a *protected place*, no one may "be in those premises" without a pass or permit from the named authority or an authorised officer's permission (s.5). In a *protected area*, anyone present must obey an authorised officer's directions and may be searched (s.6). "Premises" includes any land. Breaking either rule is an offence punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years (ss.5(3) and 6(3)).

**Orders printed in the consolidated Act.** The copy of Cap. 125 on [Parliament's website](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Protected%20Places%20and%20Areas%20Act.pdf) carries these presidential orders among others:

| Order | What it protects |
|---|---|
| Protected Areas (Tanzama Oil Pipeline) Order, SI 195 of 1970 | "All that area along the Tanzama Oil Pipeline lying within 137.16 metres on each side" |
| Protected Places (Pipeline) Orders, SIs 406 and 407 of 1968 | The central pumping station and tank area at Bwana Mkubwa, Ndola, and the pumping station at Chinsali |
| Protected Areas (Pipeline) Orders, SIs 409 and 410 of 1968 | Land within 91.44 m of the security fences of those two stations |
| Protected Areas (Oil Refinery) Order, SI 236 of 1971 | Land within 91.44 m of the fence of the oil refinery at Bwana Mkubwa, Ndola |
| Protected Places (Mines) Order, SI 57 of 1974 | "All places, excavations or works" where any operation connected with mining is carried on, with their buildings and plant; passes from the licence holder's representative |
| Emerald and tourmaline mineralisation orders, SI 32 of 1979 and SI 131 of 1982 | Defined gemstone areas; entry to the emerald area only with a Minister of Home Affairs permit |
| Kafue Gorge, Kafue road and rail bridges, and Luangwa Bridge orders (SIs 267 of 1968, 292 of 1970 and 98 of 1970) | The Kafue Gorge area, and land within 0.8045 km of each bridge |
| Switching-station and military orders (1962 to 1970) | Several power switching stations on the Copperbelt, and defence sites |

Whether each order is still in force in the 2019 Revised Edition is for Zambian counsel to confirm, and so is whether a drone flight over a protected place counts as "being in" it.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="The State Security Act"}
Under the [State Security Act (Cap. 111)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/State%20Security%20Act.pdf) s.3, it is an offence, "for any purpose prejudicial to the safety or interests of the Republic", to approach, inspect, pass over, be in the vicinity of or enter a protected place, or to make records that could be useful to a foreign power. Interfering with a "necessary service", which includes electricity, fuel, mining, communications, transport and "any road, railway, bridge", is also an offence without lawful excuse. It explains why these assets are treated as sensitive.
:::

:::callout{tone="scope" title="How we work on protected sites"}
Pipeline-corridor, pumping-station and mine-site work is done only for, or with the written authority of, the operator or licence holder, with police coordination for any flight, and security sites are masked in every deliverable.
:::

For a park, ask the Department of National Parks and Wildlife; near a border, the Air Force clearance applies.

:::
:::card{title="Buying locally, and ex-military drones" icon="drone"}
Drones bought in Zambia come only from ZCAA-approved resellers (18.1.7). A drone of military origin needs Air Force confirmation that no armaments are fitted.
:::

::::section{id="data" eyebrow="Beyond aviation" title="Aerial photos, security sites and personal data"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Not a land survey" icon="ruler"}
"Aerial photography shall not be used for the purposes of land survey without the written permission of the Surveyor-General" ([Land Survey Act, s.38(1)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Land%20Survey%20Act.pdf)). Our orthophotos and registers are monitoring and planning records built from imagery, not land surveys or cadastral work.
:::
:::card{title="Security sites masked" icon="lock"}
Cap. 125 s.9 lets the Minister order an occupier to safeguard information about a protected place or area, and the State Security Act covers records made for prejudicial purposes. Deliverables leave protected and security sites out unless the occupier authorises otherwise.
:::
:::card{title="Personal data stays in Zambia" icon="shield"}
Imagery of homes linked to names, census lists or grievance files is personal data under the [Data Protection Act 2021](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%203%20The%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202021_0.pdf): registration with the Data Protection Commission (s.19), storage in Zambia (s.70), transfers only on the s.71 conditions and breach notice to the Commissioner within 24 hours (s.49).
:::
:::

We found no Zambian equivalent of Mozambique's rule that aerial data belongs to the State and needs a defence authorisation for each survey. Zambian counsel should confirm that for your project. Part 18 itself reminds pilots to "observe all statutory requirements relating to liability, privacy and any other laws". More on data and contracts in our [notes for procurement teams](/zm/procurement#data).

::::section{id="penalties" tone="alt" eyebrow="Enforcement" title="Penalties"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Aviation.** Part 18 has no penalty table of its own; enforcement comes from the Civil Aviation Act. An authorised officer may detain an aircraft likely to be flown in breach of the Act, regulations, notices, circulars or directives (s.129), the Director-General may impose administrative penalties for non-criminal failures (s.147), running an air service without a permit is an offence (s.28(2)), and flying in a declared area is an unauthorised operation (s.8A, 2026).
::::
::::col
**Other statutes.** Fines in penalty units, or imprisonment, under the Protected Places and Areas Act (up to five years), the State Security Act (20 to 30 years), the Data Protection Act for processing without registration (up to five years), the Electricity Act for interfering with apparatus (up to five years) and the Zambia Tanzania Pipeline Act for wilfully damaging the pipeline (up to two years). We do not publish fine amounts: check the statute.

::::section{id="foreign-operators" eyebrow="For foreign and international buyers" title="Can a foreign operator fly in Zambia?"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 18 contains no express nationality bar, but a foreign company meets obstacles at every step:

- commercial work needs a ZCAA ROC and an Air Services Permit;
- the drones must be on the Zambian register, and ZCAA's company form asks for Companies Act 2017 papers and a Zambian identity document for the authorised person;
- pilots need ZCAA licences, and foreign training counts only with the Director-General's prior approval;
- technicians for Class 3 and larger drones must be citizens or permit holders.

The temporary-import route admits foreign-registered drones, but commercial work still needs the operator's ROC. The practical routes are a Zambian operator with its own ROC, or your own Zambian company going through certification.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Ask which company will fly"}
When a proposal includes drone work, ask which company will fly it, its ROC number and OpSpecs, and its Air Services Permit, and check them against ZCAA's published list.

::::section{id="checklist" tone="alt" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Zambia" lead="Ask any operator, including one we propose, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="A current ROC and OpSpecs that cover the job"}
Checked against ZCAA's list, valid for the contract period, and covering what the plan needs: E-VLOS or B-VLOS, night, controlled airspace, within 10 km of an aerodrome, and flights near people, structures or roads.
:::
:::step{title="A valid Air Services Permit"}
Its number and expiry date, for commercial work.
:::
:::step{title="Each drone registered and approved"}
A Zambian Certificate of Registration ("9I" prefix for registrations from 2026) and a Letter of Approval valid for 12 months, bought from an approved reseller or imported with ZRA clearance.
:::
:::step{title="Each pilot qualified for the flight"}
A ZCAA Remote Pilot Licence in the right category and ratings, valid for the year, with a logbook and a radiotelephony certificate.
:::
:::step{title="The special authorisation for this operation"}
Form F-002, and the arrangements for the ZCAA inspector.
:::
:::step{title="Air Force, police and air traffic"}
Zambia Air Force airspace clearance, the police contacts for each area, and ZACL coordination where the route needs it.
:::
:::step{title="Consent letters"}
From the asset owner (the 50 m rule), landowners and occupiers, the chief and the local authority on customary land (good practice), the Department of National Parks and Wildlife in a park, your own security department and, for any declared protected place or area, the occupier's pass or written authority.
:::
:::step{title="Third-party liability insurance"}
A current certificate for the operation.
:::
:::step{title="The Operations Manual procedures"}
Risk assessment, emergency plan, and first aid and extinguishers on site.
:::
:::step{title="No flights near key points"}
Nothing adjacent to or above a national key point or strategic installation.
:::
:::step{title="Data terms"}
The operator's Data Protection Commission registration, where data will be stored (s.70) and any authorisation for storage abroad, breach notification (s.49), retention, and masking of security sites.

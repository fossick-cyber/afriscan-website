We give you the evidence to design and report against, namely the structures along each option, their distances to the centreline and the stretches where they cluster. Whether a route meets the guideline is for your engineers and the ERB.
  - q: Which widths do you measure along a pipeline?
    a: The Zambia Tanzania Pipeline Act gives no strip width, and the ERB guidelines refer the right of way to Zambian Standard ZS 704, so we measure at the widths in your easements, your design standard or your ESIA scope, from the centreline you supply. Where a protected-area order sets a distance, such as the 137.16 m on each side in the order for the oil pipeline to Tanzania, it can be one of the bands. Up to six widths can be reported in one survey.
  - q: Can you detect leaks, taps or fuel theft?
    a: No. We map what is visible at the surface, such as structures, fresh digging, spoil heaps, tracks and cleared ground, and flag it so your patrols know where to look. ::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="What a pipeline route in Zambia has to show"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="“The route must avoid settlements.”" icon="houses" eyebrow="ERB siting guidelines, s.5.7.3"}
The ERB's [Guidelines for Siting Petroleum Infrastructure](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/REVISED_GUIDELINES_FOR_SITING_OF_PETROLEUM_INFRASTRUCTURE-_AUGUST-2022.pdf) (First Revision, August 2022) say the proposed route "shall be such that the pipeline does not pass through settlements". The [Second Revision, First Draft](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/files/Guidelines-for-Siting-Petroleum-Infrastructure_Second-Revision_First-Draft.pdf) of September 2026 keeps that rule.
:::
:::card{title="“The permit asks for distances.”" icon="ruler" eyebrow="ERB checklist LN.FM-008"}
The ERB's [construction-permit requirements for a petroleum pipeline](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/files/checklists/Construction_Permits/Construction-Permit-Checklist-Petroleum-Pipeline.pdf) ask for "Distances from facilities which are incompatible, residential, sensitive ecological area", a topographical map with WGS 84 coordinates, proof of land ownership or lease, and wayleave clearances for roads, electricity, water and railways.
:::
:::card{title="“The line crosses private land.”" icon="boundary" eyebrow="Zambia Tanzania Pipeline Act, s.6(2)"}
Under the [Zambia Tanzania Pipeline Act](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Zambia%20Tanzania%20Pipeline%20Act.pdf), a pipeline may not go "into, through, or against any building, or in any private land covered with buildings, without the consent of the owner and lawful occupier". The ERB defines the right of way as easements "agreed and signed upon by both the landowner and Pipeline Company".
:::
:::card{title="“We need to know what changed since construction.”" icon="compare" eyebrow="Existing lines"}
Structures, fresh digging and new tracks appear along a right of way between patrols. A dated register, re-surveyed on a schedule, shows your right-of-way team what is new and where.
:::

::::section{id="new-routes" tone="alt" eyebrow="New pipelines" title="Count the structures along every option before the route is fixed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
New cross-border fuel pipelines to the Indian Ocean coast are at the planning stage in Zambia, and every new route will need an ERB construction permit, which asks for environmental clearance and proof of land ownership or lease, and a land process with the owners and occupiers along the line. The cheapest moment to avoid a settlement is before the alignment is drawn.

For each alignment option, we list the structures within the widths you set, measure each one to the centreline, rate every 500 m for encroachment density, and compare the options side by side. The same run screens each option for steep and erosion-prone ground, drainage crossings and historically flooded land, so your engineers see the land and terrain picture together.

The guidelines ask for more than a clear line. The route must leave enough land "for the right of way to meet the requirements of" ZS 704 (s.5.7.1), keep electricity safety distances (s.5.7.4; the 2026 draft ties them to the [Electricity (Wayleave and Clearances) Regulations 2026](https://www.erb.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/Electricity-Wayleave-and-Clearances-Regulation-2026.pdf) and asks for documentary proof from the utility), and avoid ecologically sensitive areas "unless approved by ZEMA" (s.5.7.5). Where the line meets a power-line wayleave, a railway strip or a road reserve, our register shows what stands at each crossing.

Once the route is fixed, a dated baseline of the structures in the right of way, with reviewer categories, supports landowner engagement, compensation and, where lenders apply IFC Performance Standard 5, the cut-off-date records.

::::section{id="existing-lines" eyebrow="Existing lines" title="A right-of-way register, then what changed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the pipeline route you supply in its UTM zone, at the widths you give us, and list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the centreline, its band, its chainage and its coordinates. Each 500 m stretch is rated high, medium or low for encroachment density, so your right-of-way team knows where to go first. The rating is a count rule, not a safety or integrity rating.

After the baseline, the line is re-surveyed on a schedule you agree with us. New and removed structures are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views, and fresh digging, spoil heaps, trenches and new tracks on or near the right of way are flagged so your patrols know where to check. Around depots and terminals the line becomes a boundary: we list what stands inside the site and in a ring around its fence.

::::section{id="protected-corridors" tone="alt" eyebrow="Protected corridors" title="Pipelines that are declared protected areas"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Some pipeline corridors and installations in Zambia are declared protected areas or places under the [Protected Places and Areas Act (Cap. 125)](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Protected%20Places%20and%20Areas%20Act.pdf). The consolidated text on Parliament's site prints an order of 1970 (SI 195 of 1970) that makes the land within 137.16 m on each side of the oil pipeline to Tanzania a protected area, orders making two of its pumping stations protected places with protected areas 91.44 m beyond their fences, and an order for the land around the oil refinery at Ndola. Whether each order is still in force in the 2019 Revised Edition is for Zambian counsel to confirm.

In a protected area, anyone present must follow an authorised officer's directions; a protected place may not be entered without a pass. We work on such corridors only for, or with the written authority of, the operator, and security sites are masked in every deliverable.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Why the sensitivity"}
The State Security Act makes it an offence, for a purpose prejudicial to the Republic, to approach, inspect or pass over a protected place, and treats fuel as a "necessary service". Consent and a clear scope matter on these assets.

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Drone detail" title="Drones along a pipeline in Zambia"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Where a stretch needs more detail than satellite imagery shows, a drone survey captures it, subject to the ZCAA approvals and Zambia Air Force clearance each operation requires: an operator with an RPAS Operator Certificate and an Air Services Permit, registered and approved drones, pilots with ZCAA licences, and a ZCAA special authorisation with an inspector on site for each operation.

Three rules shape a pipeline flight plan. Part 18 bans flights adjacent to or above a national key point or strategic installation outright, so pumping stations and terminals need an answer from the ZCAA first. Flights within 50 m of a structure need approval or the owner's permission. And on a protected corridor, the operator's pass and police coordination come before anything else.

Afridrone is working towards the ZCAA approvals Zambia requires, and every drone proposal names the Zambian company that will fly it.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Drone law in Zambia"}
The ROC, the Air Services Permit, the per-operation authorisation, Air Force clearance and an 11-point client checklist, as of 27 September 2026.

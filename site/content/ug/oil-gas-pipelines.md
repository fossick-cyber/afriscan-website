faq:
  - q: Does your survey replace the six-monthly route survey the licensee owes PAU?
    a: No. Regulation 87(7) puts the survey and the report to PAU on the licensee. A dated register of what changed along the route supports that survey and tells your teams where to walk first; it does not replace it, and it does not replace in-line inspection or patrols.
  - q: Which bands do you report along a Ugandan pipeline?
    a: Whichever you set. The usual set follows the 2016 Midstream Regulations, within 6 m of the pipe and within 30 m of the right of way, plus the right of way itself, which you send as a polygon or as a width either side of the centreline. We add any width a lender, an ESIA or your company standard sets, up to six in one survey.
  - q: Can you tell us whether someone had PAU's consent for an activity?
    a: No. Imagery shows that ground was dug, cleared or built on, and when; it cannot show a consent. Whether an activity next to the line was approved under regulation 68 or 91 is for your land team and PAU to establish.
  - q: Can you detect leaks, taps or oil theft?
    a: No. Imagery cannot see a leak, a tap or the condition of a buried pipe. We flag what is visible at the surface, such as new structures, fresh digging, spoil, new tracks and cleared ground, for your teams to check.
  - q: Can drones fly along the pipeline?
    a: Only with the approvals and security clearances each job requires. Every operation needs UCAA approval and Chief of Defence Forces clearance, and we read regulation 68 as needing PAU's and the licensee's consent for flights along a pipeline. Satellite mapping needs none of that.

::::section{id="law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The bands in Ugandan law" title="What the Midstream Act and the 2016 Regulations draw around a pipeline" lead="A summary of the public rules as of 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
| Rule | What it says | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **Activity next to a pipeline** | "A person shall not undertake any activity in an area adjacent to a pipeline system without the written consent of the Authority, in consultation with the licensee." The Regulations do not define the adjacent area | [Petroleum (RCTMS) Regulations 2016](https://www.pau.go.ug/cms/2025/07/Midstream_General_Regulations-2016.pdf), S.I. 36 of 2016, reg 68 |
| **6 m, where there is no pipeline right of way** | No ground disturbance by anyone other than the licensee "within six metres from the pipeline system where there is no pipeline right of way", without PAU approval. Ground disturbance includes excavating, digging, trenching, ploughing, drilling, tunnelling, backfilling, blasting, topsoil stripping, levelling, quarrying, clearing and grading | Regs 91 and 2 |
| **30 m from the right of way** | Precautions to find out whether a pipeline exists before "any work … within thirty meters from the pipeline right of way"; 10 days' written notice to the licensee and PAU before ground disturbance in the controlled area; locating and marking within 14 days; PAU's response within 21 days; temporary fencing | Regs 92 to 94 |
| **6 m from the pipe** | The pipe is located by hand before mechanical excavation within 6 m; after that, machines work within 6 m only "under the direct supervision of a competent person employed by the licensee" | Reg 97 |
| **Crossings** | No vehicle or equipment crosses a pipeline except on the travelled part of a public road, without PAU approval | Reg 98 |
| **Route surveys** | The licensee informs owners, occupiers and communities of the pipeline, with PAU's approval, and "shall, survey the pipeline routes every six months" and report to PAU | Reg 87(2), (7) and (8) |
| **Safety zones** | A safety zone around every midstream facility, its extent set by PAU, in which "unauthorised vessels, vehicles, crafts, fishing gear or other objects shall not be present" | [Midstream Act 2013](https://www.pau.go.ug/cms/2025/07/petroleum_rctms_act_2013.pdf) (Act 4 of 2013), s.67 |
| **Upstream installations** | The same safety-zone scheme for upstream facilities | [EDP Act 2013](https://www.pau.go.ug/cms/2025/07/petroleum_edp_act_2013.pdf), s.144 |

The six-metre rule in regulation 91 applies only "where there is no pipeline right of way"; near any pipeline, regulation 97 lets machines dig within 6 m only under the licensee's supervision. We report each band separately, so your team can apply the rule that fits each stretch.

We report structures within **6 m of the pipe**, inside the **right of way** and within **30 m of its edge** by default, measured from the centreline and the right-of-way polygon you send. PAU approves a surveyed route within 30 days (reg 60), so the approved route is on record from the start.

::::section{id="operating-lines" eyebrow="Operating lines" title="A baseline, then scheduled re-surveys that feed the route survey"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the route in its UTM zone, list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the pipe, its band and its chainage, and cut the route into 500 m stretches. Each stretch is rated for **encroachment density**: high where more than five structures stand inside the widest band, medium for one to five, low for none. It is a count that tells your right-of-way team where to go first, not a safety or integrity rating.

After the baseline, the route is re-surveyed on a schedule you agree with us. New and removed structures between dated surveys are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views, and your team receives a change notice after each survey. Fresh digging, spoil, trenches and new tracks near the line are flagged the same way, so the six-monthly route survey starts from a list of places that changed.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="facilities" tone="alt" eyebrow="Petroleum facilities" title="Well pads, processing sites, pump stations and terminals"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Around a facility, the questions are what has appeared at the perimeter and inside the safety zone PAU has set, and what has changed since the last survey. We map new structures, cleared ground, new tracks and stockpiles in a ring around each site, compare them between dates and list them with their distance to the site boundary.

Your own plant, well pads and pump stations are never recorded as encroachment. The register shows third-party structures and land change around them, and your team decides what each one means.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Drone flights near facilities"}
Pump stations, processing facilities, well pads and storage sites fall under regulation 43 of the drone regulations, the operator's own rules and CDF clearance. Satellite imagery covers them without a flight. See [drone law in Uganda](/ug/drone-regulations#sensitive-sites).

### What it supports

:::checklist
- The licensee's six-monthly route survey, with a list of what changed
- Right-of-way management and community engagement, with dated facts
- Lenders' environmental and social monitoring between site visits
- Cut-off-date and valuation records for new lines and sites
:::
::::
::::col
### What it does not do

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Detect leaks, taps, theft or the condition of the pipe
- Replace in-line inspection, patrols or the licensee's route survey
- Decide whether an activity had PAU's consent, or who is entitled to compensation
- Serve as a cadastral survey: boundaries come from your registered surveyor
- Map under cloud: optical imagery needs clear skies, so captures are planned for clearer spells
:::

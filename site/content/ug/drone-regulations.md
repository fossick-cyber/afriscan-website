faq:
  - q: Does satellite-based corridor mapping need a drone approval in Uganda?
    a: No drone flies in satellite-based work, so the approvals in S.I. 96 of 2022 do not come into it, and nobody has to fly near power lines, roads or homes. We found no Ugandan rule that restricts the analysis of commercial satellite imagery; your counsel should confirm that for your project. The Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 still governs any personal data in the results.
  - q: Is Chief of Defence Forces clearance needed for every flight?
    a: For every operation, according to UCAA's advisory circular UCAA-AC-UAS001, which sends each request to the Chief of Defence Forces, who convenes a Tripartite Committee, before UCAA issues its "Safety Approval and Security Clearance". UCAA's 2024 checklist lists CDF Security Clearance for Category B and leaves it out of the Category C list; because the circular covers every operation, plan for it in Category C as well.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly drone surveys in Uganda in its own name?
    a: We found no route for that in the Regulations. Only Ugandan citizens, lawfully resident foreign individuals, Uganda-registered companies and Government may own a drone (reg 8), every drone must be registered in Uganda (reg 9), and for an operator certificate UCAA considers whether the applicant has a principal place of business in Uganda (reg 20). Regs 85 and 86 allow an application for exemption; whether UCAA would grant one to a foreign-owned operator is not known.
  - q: Can a drone fly along a high-voltage line?
    a: Only with UCAA's specific permission. Regulation 43(2)(b) treats operating "in or around … high tension cables and communication masts" and strategic installations as negligent or reckless unless the authority grants specific permission. Plan for that permission on any flight along a transmission line, or start from satellite imagery.
  - q: How close to homes along a corridor may a drone fly?
    a: Not within 50 m of any person, building, structure, vehicle, vessel or animal not associated with the operation, unless authorised (reg 44), and not over or along a public road or within 50 m of one, unless approved or the road is closed (reg 45). Where a corridor is lined with homes, satellite imagery is usually the practical choice.
  - q: How long do the approvals take?
    a: An application for a UAS operator certificate is due at least ninety days before the intended operation (reg 19(3)). No timeline is published for CDF security clearance. So we plan satellite work first and give no start date for flights until the approvals for that job are in hand.
  - q: Will you publish drone imagery of our site?
    a: No. Regulation 32 limits capture to the approved area and bars sharing or publishing imagery "in a manner contrary to the laws of Uganda". Ugandan drone orthophotos are delivered only to the people you name in writing, with people and vehicles blurred, and none appears on this website.

Some have no published timeline, so they belong in the plan from the first day."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="UCAA approval of the operation" icon="file-check" eyebrow="UCAA · reg 29"}
"A person shall not operate an unmanned aircraft system without seeking the approval of the authority." The request names the aircraft and its registration, the pilot, the purpose, date, route, level and duration, the payload and the insurance.
:::
:::card{title="CDF security clearance" icon="shield" eyebrow="Chief of Defence Forces"}
UCAA's published procedure sends each operation to the Chief of Defence Forces, who convenes a Tripartite Committee and clears or rejects it. Only then does UCAA issue its "Safety Approval and Security Clearance".
:::
:::card{title="A line-ministry letter" icon="file-text" eyebrow="Regs 6 and 7"}
A "letter of no objection … regarding the intended operations" from the relevant line ministry, filed with every Category B and Category C application.
:::
:::card{title="District sign-off and notice" icon="flag" eyebrow="RDC · reg 31"}
UCAA's checklist asks for a safety risk assessment signed by the area Resident District Commissioner. The Regulations add the permission of the appropriate authorities and notice to the community in the area of operation.
:::
:::card{title="A registered, eligibly owned drone" icon="drone" eyebrow="Regs 8 and 9"}
Every drone must be registered with UCAA, and only Ugandan citizens, lawfully resident foreign individuals, Uganda-registered companies and Government may own one.
:::
:::card{title="Vetted pilots, insurance and security" icon="user-check" eyebrow="Regs 37, 56, 72, 77"}
A remote pilot authorisation or licence issued only after a security threat assessment, third-party insurance, and an operator security programme approved by UCAA.
:::

::::section{id="categories" class="compare" eyebrow="The categories" title="Three categories, and where a mapping flight fits" lead="Regulation 4 and Schedule 2 sort operations by risk. Every category must stay within the territorial borders of Uganda."}
| Category | Main limits | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **A, basic** (low risk) | Visual line of sight; at most 30 ft (10 m) above ground; 50 m from persons, buildings or objects not involved; within 200 m of the control station; 06:00 to 18:00; at most 20 km/h | UCAA approval and a valid authorisation for the drone (reg 5); a report to local authorities first; registration with UCAA or flight under an authorised club. Recreational and sports flying only inside a registered club |
| **B, standard** (medium risk) | Visual line of sight; at most 400 ft (122 m) above ground; 50 m lateral distance; at most 5 kg including payload; in non-segregated airspace away from controlled airspace, and away from notified prohibited, restricted or danger areas unless expressly authorised | A remote pilot authorisation; a report to local authorities first; compliance with the state security requirements; a concept of operations; a safety risk assessment accepted by UCAA. Reg 6 documents: the application form, the line-ministry letter, the user manual, a pilot training certificate or licence, a Class III medical, the location and map, third-party insurance, a dangerous goods manual where applicable and the safety risk assessment |
| **C, complex** (high risk) | Beyond visual line of sight, with suitable equipment and a suitably rated pilot, only in airspace not classified prohibited, restricted or danger | A UAS operator certificate (UOC) and operations specifications; a remote pilot licence endorsed for the type; a certificate of airworthiness where applicable; a safety management system. Reg 7 adds an operations manual, a security programme, a training programme, an SMS manual and a type certificate where applicable |

**Where a corridor survey fits.** A small mapping drone flown within sight, at or below 400 ft and under 5 kg, is a Category B operation. A flight beyond visual line of sight along a corridor is Category C. A flight within sight with a drone heavier than 5 kg does not fit Category B; confirm with UCAA how it is classed before planning one.

::::section{id="ownership" tone="alt" eyebrow="Ownership and registration" title="Who may own a drone, and registration before any flight"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Regulation 8 limits ownership to:

- "a citizen of Uganda of a minimum age of 18 years";
- "an individual citizen of a foreign state who is lawfully admitted for residence in Uganda of a minimum age of 18 years";
- "a company registered in Uganda";
- a Government entity.

Only Government may own drones of military specification (reg 8(3)), and a transfer of ownership needs UCAA's prior approval (reg 8(2)). "A person shall not operate a UAS within Uganda unless the UAS has been registered by the authority" (reg 9).

UCAA may deregister a drone "in the interest of national security" (reg 13(d)), seize it (reg 14) and apply to court for its destruction or disposal (reg 15).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="What this means for a survey buyer"}
A drone flown for your survey must be registered in Uganda and owned by an eligible person or company. Ask for the UCAA certificate of registration of each aircraft before the first flight.

::::section{id="uoc" eyebrow="Operator certificate" title="The UAS operator certificate for complex operations"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
A UOC is needed to operate in Category C (reg 18). The application is made "at least ninety days before the intended operation" (reg 19(3)). The certificate states the authorised operations, the type or model of drone and "the area of operation" (reg 22). It is valid for 12 months (reg 23), and renewal is applied for at least 60 days before it expires (reg 26).
::::
::::col
UCAA "shall consider" (reg 20):

- the ownership of the drones, and "whether the UAS is registered in Uganda";
- "whether the applicant has a principal place of business in Uganda";
- the organisation, staffing and training, and qualified pilots;
- "an approved aircraft operator security program in accordance with the Civil Aviation (Security) Regulations, 2022";
- aviation safety.

### What the approval request covers {#request}

Regulation 29 requires UCAA's approval for every operation. The request covers the aircraft and its registration, the pilot's credentials, the purpose, date, route, level and duration, performance, command-and-control and detect-and-avoid, emergency procedures, compliance with the Civil Aviation (Security) Regulations 2022, "payload information or description", and insurance.

### UCAA's published procedure {#procedure}

UCAA's advisory circular UCAA-AC-UAS001 (Issue 1, July 2020, filed on its drone page as the updated clearance procedure) sets out the main steps for an operation:
::::
::::col
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="A letter of no objection"}
From the relevant line ministry, about the intended operation.
:::
:::step{title="The application to UCAA"}
Form AC-UAS 001 with the letter, to UCAA's Director Safety, Security and Economic Regulations.
:::
:::step{title="UCAA asks for CDF Security Clearance"}
Copying the Tripartite Committee, and the Uganda Communications Commission if applicable.
:::
:::step{title="The Chief of Defence Forces decides"}
The CDF convenes the Tripartite Committee and clears or rejects the operation.
:::
:::step{title="UCAA's safety evaluation"}
UCAA evaluates the operation and issues "Safety Approval and Security Clearance". Only then may operations start.
:::

:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="The 2024 checklist: Category B" icon="clipboard" eyebrow="Specific operations"}
The application form, the line-ministry letter, CDF Security Clearance, the manual, the remote pilot authorisation or licence, a Class III medical, the map, third-party insurance, a dangerous goods manual if applicable, and a "Safety Risk Assessment signed by the area RDC".
:::
:::card{title="The 2024 checklist: Category C" icon="clipboard" eyebrow="UOC"}
The same documents without "CDF Security Clearance" in the list, plus a security programme, a training programme, a safety management system, an operations manual, a dangerous goods manual and a certificate of airworthiness if applicable. The advisory circular sends every operation to the CDF, so plan for that clearance in Category C too.
:::

**Timelines.** The Regulations give at least ninety days for a UOC application. No timeline is published for CDF clearance. Plan satellite work first, and give drone work a start date only once the approvals for that job are in hand.

::::section{id="corridor-rules" eyebrow="Flying a corridor" title="The rules that shape a pipeline or power-line survey" lead="Corridors run past homes, roads, towers, masts and aerodromes. These are the operating rules a flight plan along them has to fit."}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Launch sites, permission and community notice" icon="houses" eyebrow="Reg 31"}
No launch or recovery "from any public or private property without notifying the authority". Before starting, the operator seeks "the permission of the appropriate authorities and inform[s] the community within the area of operation".
:::
:::card{title="High-voltage lines, masts and strategic sites" icon="power" eyebrow="Reg 43(2)(b)"}
Operating "in or around strategic installations, air navigation service facilities, high tension cables and communication masts, prisons, police stations, courts of law, scenes of crime, schools, hospitals" and any designated prohibited, restricted or danger area counts as negligent or reckless, "except where the authority grants specific permission". UCAA may also prohibit drones in any area "in the interest of safety and security" (reg 43(3)).
:::
:::card{title="People, buildings and roads" icon="route" eyebrow="Regs 44 to 46"}
Not within 50 m of "any person, building, structure, vehicle, vessel or animal not associated with the operations", unless authorised. Not over a public road, along its length or within 50 m of it, unless approved or the road is closed, and no take-off or landing on a road without approval.
:::
:::card{title="Aerodromes" icon="flag" eyebrow="Reg 52"}
Not within 4 km of the aerodrome reference point, on approach and take-off paths, near navigation aids, in the aerodrome traffic zone or in holding patterns, without the written permission of the aerodrome operator and the air navigation service provider and UCAA approval. This applies at Entebbe and at every aerodrome UCAA lists.
:::
:::card{title="Controlled airspace, cloud and night" icon="map" eyebrow="Regs 48, 51, 54, 60"}
Flight plans in controlled airspace, air traffic control notified and kept in contact, and the ATC unit's authorisation. No flying in cloud, at night or outside visual meteorological conditions unless authorised and under an ATC clearance.
:::
:::card{title="Category B conditions" icon="drone" eyebrow="Reg 58"}
Within visual line of sight, at or below 400 ft by day, and 50 m from uninvolved persons. No flying over fire, police or emergency operations without approval.
:::
:::card{title="Beyond visual line of sight" icon="corridor" eyebrow="Reg 64"}
A detect-and-avoid system and UCAA authorisation after an accepted safety risk assessment; within radio line of sight unless specially authorised; visual meteorological conditions throughout; an area "void of other traffic" in "specifically delimited or segregated airspace". Highly automated operations need prior authorisation, in consultation with the National Aviation Security Committee (reg 65).
:::
:::card{title="Capture, records and insurance" icon="file-check" eyebrow="Regs 32, 55 to 57"}
No night flying unless cleared, and sensors must not capture anything "extending beyond the prescribed area of approved operation". Records are kept for Category B and C operations, third-party insurance is required, and nothing may be dropped without authorisation.
:::

::::section{id="sensitive-sites" tone="alt" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="Where a flight needs more than the standard approvals"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Military and strategic sites" icon="shield"}
Regulation 43(2)(b) and CDF clearance apply. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office also notes that "it is forbidden to take photos of military, official or diplomatic sites", including Owen Falls Dam at the source of the Nile near Jinja ([FCDO, 2 September 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda)). Keep flights away from them and mask them in every deliverable.
:::
:::card{title="Pipelines and petroleum facilities" icon="pipeline"}
In a safety zone around a midstream facility, whose extent the Petroleum Authority of Uganda (PAU) sets, "unauthorised vessels, vehicles, crafts, fishing gear or other objects shall not be present" ([Midstream Act s.67](https://www.pau.go.ug/cms/2025/07/petroleum_rctms_act_2013.pdf)); the upstream Act has the same scheme (s.144). The Act does not say whether a drone counts, so we treat safety zones as closed to drones without the consent of PAU and the licensee. Regulation 68 of the [2016 Midstream Regulations](https://www.pau.go.ug/cms/2025/07/Midstream_General_Regulations-2016.pdf) needs PAU's written consent for "any activity in an area adjacent to a pipeline system", and we plan any flight along a pipeline on that basis.
:::
:::card{title="Power lines, substations and masts" icon="power"}
High tension cables and communication masts are named in reg 43(2)(b), so any flight along or around them needs UCAA's specific permission. We treat substations as strategic installations.
:::
:::card{title="Mining licence areas" icon="mine"}
Flights need the consent of the licence holder and the landowners. Around busy artisanal workings the 50 m standoff from people and structures (reg 44) is hard to keep, so satellite imagery is the better tool there.
:::
:::card{title="Roads, gatherings and emergencies" icon="alert"}
Regulations 44 to 46 and 58(2) keep flights away from roads, people and emergency operations. Avoid gatherings: UK and US travel advice both warn against demonstrations.
:::
:::card{title="Borders" icon="globe"}
All three categories stay "within the territorial borders of Uganda" (Sch. 2). A cross-border flight needs the approval of both States (reg 30; reg 64(9)(a) for flights beyond visual line of sight).
:::

::::section{id="security" eyebrow="Security" title="Security programmes, vetting and interception"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### The operator security programme {#security-programme}

"A person shall not carry out standard or complex operations without an operator security programme approved by the authority" (reg 72(1)). The programme covers secured premises, the protection of IT and communications, "details of the camera usage" (reg 72(2)(d)), searches and "recurrent background checks". Operators are "subject to security inspection at any time during operations, without prior notification" (reg 72(4)).
::::
::::col
### Vetting and interception {#vetting}

With every Category B or C registration application, UCAA passes the applicant's details to "competent security agencies". The check includes "Interpol and international databases, terrorist watch lists", and a security risk leads to rejection, or to variation, suspension or revocation (reg 77). Remote pilot licences and authorisations are issued only after "a security threat assessment conducted by the competent security agencies" (reg 77(2)). UCAA, "with the assistance of the national security organs", may take control of, land or confiscate a drone that, for example, "prejudices national security" (reg 76).

::::section{id="privacy" tone="alt" eyebrow="Privacy and personal data" title="Regulation 79, regulation 32 and the Data Protection and Privacy Act"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### The drone privacy rules {#privacy-rules}

Regulation 79 forbids using a drone to keep a person under observation without that person's consent, or private property without the owner's consent, and forbids photographing or filming an individual without consent except at newsgathering or public events. Regulation 27(2) bars any operation "that constitutes a nuisance … or infringement of privacy".

Regulation 32 limits capture to the approved area and says that what is captured "shall not be reproduced, processed, shared, distributed or published in a manner contrary to the laws of Uganda". The camera's use is declared in the operator's security programme (reg 72(2)(d)).

This is why AfriScan maps land and assets, never people. A corridor register lists structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks, with their distance to the line; it does not say who lives or works there.
::::
::::col
### The Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 {#dppa}

The [Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019](https://ulii.org/akn/ug/act/2019/9/eng@2023-12-31) (Cap. 97) and the [Data Protection and Privacy Regulations 2021](https://ulii.org/akn/ug/act/si/2021/21/eng@2021-03-12) (S.I. 21 of 2021) govern personal data, and imagery showing identifiable people or homes can contain it. The [Personal Data Protection Office](https://pdpo.go.ug/) runs organisation registration and breach reports, and states that "organisations must report data breaches that may compromise personal data immediately". Processing or storing personal data outside Uganda needs adequate protection in the destination or the data subject's consent.

::::section{id="import" eyebrow="Importing equipment" title="Bringing a drone into Uganda"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Import needs UCAA's written authorisation, a customs declaration, and inspection and clearance by the Joint Security Office (reg 16). The advisory circular adds a defence step:

1. URA Customs writes to UCAA.
2. UCAA checks preliminary compliance and writes to the Chief of Defence Forces.
3. "CDF convenes the Tripartite Committee."
4. The CDF clears or rejects the import.
5. UCAA clears it with URA and notifies the applicant.
6. Only then may the applicant apply for approval of an operation.

Manufacturing, assembling, testing and selling drones need approval (reg 17), and dealers keep records (reg 55(2)). A radio licence from the Uganda Communications Commission is needed where applicable (reg 69(2)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="warn" title="Hand luggage"}
Treat a drone carried in hand luggage as an import, and confirm the route with UCAA before travelling with one.

::::section{id="foreign" tone="alt" eyebrow="Foreign operators" title="Why a foreign firm does not fly in its own name"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
We found no route in the Regulations for a foreign company to operate drones in Uganda in its own name. Regulation 8 limits ownership to Ugandans, lawfully resident foreign individuals, Uganda-registered companies and Government; regulation 9 requires Ugandan registration; regulation 20 has UCAA consider Ugandan registration and a Ugandan principal place of business; and regulation 30 covers only cross-border commercial flights by UOC holders. Regulations 85 and 86 allow an application for exemption from any provision, if an equivalent level of safety is kept. Whether UCAA would use that route for foreign ownership is not known.
::::
::::col
That leaves two practical routes for survey work:

- **a Ugandan company** that owns and registers the drones, starting with Category B approvals and later a UOC;
- **a Ugandan operator with its own approvals** that flies the survey, while the imagery is processed and analysed elsewhere.

Either way, each job still needs its line-ministry letter, CDF clearance and the RDC-signed risk assessment. On petroleum projects there is a further gate: "land surveying" is on both national content reserved lists, so the flying partner must qualify as a Ugandan company, registered entity or citizen. See [national content and procurement in Uganda](/ug/national-content).

::::section{id="enforcement" eyebrow="Enforcement" title="Liability and UCAA's powers"}
The Regulations set fines and prison terms for breaches (reg 84) and for unlawful interference with an authorised drone (reg 75(2)). The operator and the pilot are "deemed to have contravened" a provision unless they prove the contravention happened without their consent or connivance and that they "exercised all due diligence" (reg 84(2)), so every approval should be in writing and kept on file. UCAA can seize (reg 14), apply for destruction (reg 15), intercept and confiscate (reg 76), and revoke approvals "in the interest of public safety or national security" or "in the public interest" (reg 83).

::::section{id="open-points" tone="alt" eyebrow="Before you rely on a reading" title="What the published texts leave open"}
These points are not settled by the Regulations, the advisory circular or the checklist. Ask UCAA, and PAU where a petroleum site is involved, in writing:

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Which line ministry signs the letter of no objection for a private corridor survey: the Regulations say only "relevant line ministry"
- How long CDF clearance takes, and who sits on the Tripartite Committee: neither is published
- Whether a flight within sight with a drone over 5 kg is treated as Category C
- Whether "crafts" or "other objects" in the petroleum safety-zone rules covers drones
- Whether the exemption in regulations 85 and 86 is ever granted to a foreign-owned operator

::::section{id="checklist" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Uganda" lead="Ask any operator, including anyone we work with, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}

A UCAA certificate of registration for each aircraft (reg 9), owned by an eligible person or company (reg 8).

:::step{title="The right category"}
Category B (within sight, at or below 400 ft, under 5 kg, away from controlled airspace), or Category C with a UOC and a remote pilot licence endorsed for the type.
:::

A remote pilot authorisation (Category B) or licence (Category C), a Class III medical, and completed security vetting (reg 77).
:::

UCAA's Safety Approval and CDF Security Clearance (AC-UAS001); the line-ministry letter of no objection (regs 6 and 7); the RDC-signed safety risk assessment; within 4 km of an aerodrome, the written permission of the aerodrome operator and the air navigation service provider (reg 52); around high-voltage lines, masts or strategic installations, UCAA's specific permission (reg 43(2)(b)).
:::
:::step{title="An approved security programme"}
One that declares the camera's use (reg 72), with sensors limited to the approved area (reg 32).
:::
:::step{title="Third-party insurance"}
In place for the operation (reg 56).
:::
:::step{title="Notice and consent"}
The permission of the appropriate authorities and notice to the community (reg 31(2)); a report to local authorities before operating (Sch. 2); the owner's consent before keeping private property under observation (reg 79(2)(a)(ii)).
:::
:::step{title="A data plan"}
Who receives the imagery, what is blurred, where it is stored, and that nothing is published.
:::
:::step{title="Approvals in writing, kept on file"}
Under reg 84(2) the operator and the pilot are "deemed to have contravened" the Regulations unless they prove the contravention happened without their consent or connivance and that they "exercised all due diligence", so written approvals are the first evidence of due diligence.
:::
:::step{title="On petroleum sites"}
The consent of PAU and the licensee for safety zones and adjacent areas (Midstream Act s.67; Midstream Regulations reg 68), and the contractor's national content status for the contract.
:::

**Satellite first.** Satellite mapping needs none of the approvals on this page. It involves no aircraft, no flight near lines, roads or homes and no site visit, so a whole pipeline, transmission or road corridor can be mapped before anyone decides whether a drone is needed. We found no Ugandan rule that restricts the analysis of commercial satellite imagery; your counsel should confirm that for your project.

**Our position on drones.** We do not yet hold Ugandan drone approvals, and we are working towards them. Until then, any drone survey for a Ugandan project is flown by a Ugandan operator under its own approvals, we process the data, and the proposal says which approvals the job relies on. Drone work is always subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires, and no flight is scheduled before they are in place.

**Your own drone data.** Where your teams or contractors already fly the corridor, we run the same structure, change and corridor analysis on the orthophotos you send.

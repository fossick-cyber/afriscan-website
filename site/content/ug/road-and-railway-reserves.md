faq:
  - q: Which reserve width do you use?
    a: The one your road authority applies to the road's class. The 2026 road regulations set maximum widths by class, from 10 m on park and community access roads to 40 m on expressways. Tell us whether the width is the whole reserve or the width either side of the centreline, and any building line that applies, and we report each band.
  - q: Can you show when a structure was built?
    a: Roughly. We look back through the archive imagery of each spot and report the first dated scene on which the structure appears, and the last one on which it does not. How far back the archive goes, and how often, varies by place, so the report states the dates behind each answer.
  - q: Will you tell us which structures are unauthorised?
    a: No. We map what stands in the reserve and since when. Whether it had authorisation, and what follows under the Roads Act, is for the road authority and the courts.
  - q: Do you cover fibre routes in road reserves?
    a: Yes. Fresh excavations, trenches and works along a fibre or utility route inside a reserve are flagged between dated surveys, and new structures along it are listed. Imagery cannot see a cable or detect theft.
  - q: Can you compare alignments for a new road or railway?
    a: Yes. We count the structures along each candidate alignment, band by band, so the land and resettlement impact of each option can be weighed before one is chosen, and then record a dated baseline of the chosen corridor.
  - q: Can a drone survey a road reserve?
    a: With difficulty. A drone may not fly over or along a public road or within 50 m of one unless approved or the road is closed (reg 45 of S.I. 96 of 2022), on top of the approval and CDF security clearance every operation needs. Satellite imagery covers the reserve without a flight.

::::section{id="law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The rules" title="The Roads Act 2019 and the 2026 road regulations" lead="A summary of the public rules as of 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
| Section | What it says |
|---|---|
| **s.15, road reserves** | The Minister declares road reserves by statutory instrument and may set different widths for different classes of road |
| **s.16(2)(b), utilities in the reserve** | Written authorisation is needed for "pipelines, telephone lines, electric supplies, optic fibre cables and posts" in a road reserve |
| **s.16(4)–(6), relocation** | When the road authority needs the reserve, a utility relocates its lines at its own cost, without any compensation, on at least 90 days' notice before road works start, or a shorter period the road authority specifies in the notice |
| **s.16(8), unauthorised structures** | "A person who constructs any structure in or on a road reserve … without authorisation … is not entitled to any compensation" |
| **s.17, building lines** | Building lines and no-build distances measured from the centre line of the road |
| **s.24, removal** | The road authority may remove encroachments from a reserve, with or without notice, at the cost of the person responsible |

The [Roads Act 2019](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ug/2019/ug-government-gazette-dated-2019-09-25-no-48.pdf) (Act 16 of 2019; Cap. 346) was assented to on 4 September 2019 and published in the Uganda Gazette of 25 September 2019.

### Maximum reserve widths by road class {#widths}

Schedule 12 of the [Roads (Management of Public Roads) Regulations 2026](https://ulii.org/akn/ug/act/si/2026/53/eng@2026-06-26) (S.I. 53 of 2026, reg 23) sets the maximum reserve width for each class:

| Road class | Maximum reserve width |
|---|---|
| Expressway | 40 m |
| National roads, classes A, B and C | 25 m |
| Urban roads (U) | 30 m |
| District roads, classes I, II and III | 15 m |
| Park roads (P) | 10 m |
| Community access roads (CAR) | 10 m |

:::callout{tone="legal" title="Total width or either side?"}
The Schedule's note on how these widths are measured begins "measured at right angle on either …". Before a width is applied to a survey, confirm with the road authority whether it is the whole reserve or the width either side of the centre line. We report whichever reading your authority applies.
:::

::::section{id="reserves" eyebrow="What we map" title="Structures in the reserve, the building line, and when each appeared"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the road's centreline, or the reserve boundary you send, by the reserve width and the building line, list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the centreline and its band, and cut the road into 500 m stretches rated for **encroachment density**: high where more than five structures stand inside the widest band, medium for one to five, low for none.

For each structure that matters, we look back through dated archive imagery and report roughly when it first appeared: the first dated scene on which it stands and the last on which it does not. That date matters as evidence, because section 16(8) denies compensation for structures built in a reserve without authorisation. Re-surveys on a schedule you agree with us then flag new and removed structures, automatically and then confirmed by a reviewer.

::::section{id="fibre" eyebrow="Fibre and utility routes" title="Works along roads, flagged between surveys"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Pipelines, telephone and electric lines and optic fibre cables need written authorisation to be placed in a road reserve, and are relocated at the owner's cost, without compensation, on at least 90 days' notice before road works start, or a shorter period the road authority specifies in the notice, when the authority needs the reserve (Roads Act s.16). For fibre and utility operators, and for road authorities checking works in their reserves, we flag fresh excavations, trenches and earthworks along the route between dated surveys, and list new structures along it.

Imagery cannot see a buried cable or detect cable theft. It shows where the ground along the route has been disturbed, and when, so field teams know where to check.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Drones and roads"}
A drone may not fly over or along a public road, or within 50 m of one, unless approved or the road is closed (reg 45), and may not take off or land on a road without approval (reg 46). Satellite imagery covers road reserves without a flight.

faq:
  - q: Does the PPDA reservation scheme apply to donor-funded contracts?
    a: Not always. Guideline 12 of 2024 covers procurement funded by the Government of Uganda and by donors, but it "shall not apply to procurements funded by Development Partners where the conditions of funding limit the application of the reservations" (§1.3). Check the financing agreement and the bidding document for each contract.
  - q: Can a foreign company register on PAU's National Supplier Database?
    a: Yes, online. PAU's guideline asks a foreign entity for its certificate of incorporation, tax clearance, social-security compliance and shareholding and director information, notarised in its home country and then attested by the relevant mission. Verification takes up to two months and a listing lasts three years, with annual profile updates.
  - q: Is a Ugandan subsidiary of a foreign firm a "Ugandan company"?
    a: Only if it meets the definition in the petroleum national content regulations. That means being incorporated under the Companies Act 2012, providing value addition to Uganda, using available local raw materials, employing at least 70 % Ugandans, and being approved by PAU.
  - q: Can a foreign company own drones in Uganda?
    a: No. Regulation 8 of S.I. 96 of 2022 limits drone ownership to Ugandan citizens, lawfully resident foreign individuals, Uganda-registered companies and Government, and every drone must be registered in Uganda. See [drone law in Uganda](/ug/drone-regulations#foreign).

    a: The route or boundary file, the districts it crosses, the widths or zones to report, the date the record must reflect, the deliverables and the deadline. ::::section{id="petroleum" tone="alt" eyebrow="Oil and gas" title="The petroleum national content regulations"}
| | Midstream | Upstream |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrument** | [Petroleum (RCTMS) (National Content) Regulations 2016](https://www.pau.go.ug/cms/2025/07/Midstream-National-Content-Regulations-2016.pdf), S.I. 34 of 2016 | [Petroleum (EDP) (National Content) Regulations 2016](https://www.pau.go.ug/cms/2025/07/Upstream-National-content-Regulations-2016.pdf), S.I. 44 of 2016 |
| **The duty** | "Every licensee, contractor and subcontractor shall reserve contracts for goods and services specified in the Schedule to be provided by Ugandan companies, registered entities and Ugandan citizens" (reg 11) | The same duty for licensees, operators, contractors and subcontractors (reg 10) |
| **The Schedule** | 15 items | 16 items |
| **Exceptions** | With PAU's approval, where local capacity is lacking (reg 11(4)) | No written exception clause |
| **Items that touch mapping work** | Land surveying; environment studies and impact assessments; communications and information technology services | The same three |

**A "Ugandan company"** (reg 4 in both) is incorporated under the Companies Act 2012, "provides value addition to Uganda", "uses available local raw materials", "employs at least 70% Ugandans" and is approved by PAU. A **registered entity** is a Ugandan-owned business name or partnership.

**Bidding.** In the midstream regulations, national content is a qualification requirement, upcoming contracts are advertised in at least two national newspapers at least a month ahead, and contract terms are capped (reg 12).

### The crude export pipeline {#crude-export-pipeline}

The [2021 special-provisions Act](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ug/2021/ug-government-gazette-acts-supplement-dated-2021-12-24-no-92.pdf) for the crude export pipeline requires its suppliers to be registered on PAU's National Supplier Database, with exceptions where goods, works or services "are not available in Uganda" and for time-limited emergency services (s.15). Contracts listed in the project's national content appendix are reserved for "Ugandan parties"; where capacity is insufficient, PAU may consent to international procurement, preferably through a Ugandan joint venture (s.24). Where goods or services are not available in Uganda, a foreign supplier may deliver through "a Ugandan joint venture with Ugandan parties approved by the Authority and registered on the national supplier database" (s.25).

::::section{id="nsd" eyebrow="Registration" title="PAU's National Supplier Database"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The [National Supplier Database](https://nsd.pau.go.ug) (NSD) is PAU's register of suppliers to the oil and gas sector. Under PAU's guideline (updated 1 June 2026):

- registration is online only and open all year;
- verification takes up to two months;
- a listing lasts three years, with an annual profile update;
- a foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, tax clearance, social-security compliance and shareholding and director information are notarised in its home country and then attested by the relevant mission.

::::section{id="public-procurement" tone="alt" eyebrow="Public procurement" title="The PPDA reservation schemes"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
[PPDA Guideline No. 12 of 2024](https://www.ppda.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GUIDELINE-NO.12-OF-2024-ON-RESERVATION-SCHEMES-TO-PROMOTE-THE-PARTICIPATION-OF-LOCAL-PROVIDERS-IN-PUBLIC-PROCUREMENT.pdf), on reservation schemes to promote the participation of local providers, commenced on 22 March 2024. It covers procurement by central and local government above set values, funded by the Government or by donors, except where a development partner's funding conditions limit the reservations (§1.3).

- **National providers:** Ugandan citizens, and providers registered in Uganda and wholly owned and controlled by Ugandans. Consultancy, non-consultancy and works contracts up to set values are reserved for them.
- **Resident and EAC providers:** companies incorporated in Uganda but not national, and providers operating in the East African Community for at least ten years. A further band of contracts is reserved for them, and both must have been registered for at least ten years when they bid (§1.3(iv)).
- **Opening to others:** where the reserved group makes no bid, or a market survey finds no national, resident or EAC provider, the process opens to foreign providers (§2.1(iii), §2.2(iii)–(iv)).
::::
::::col
- **Subcontracting on large works:** foreign bidders on large road and other public works contracts must "sub-contract a minimum of at least 30% of the value of the works to national or resident providers" (§4.1).
- **Local consultants:** a reservation for local inputs and "local consultants where available in works contracts including … EPC projects and Turnkey" (§4.2).

The [National Local Content Act 2023](https://bills.parliament.ug/) was assented to on 23 June 2023. Its bill set out "to impose local content obligations on a person using public money or utilizing Uganda's natural resources or carrying on an activity requiring a license; to prioritize Ugandan citizens, Ugandan and resident companies in public procurement". Its final text was not reviewed for these notes; check it with counsel.

Supplier registration for public tenders is on [e-GP Uganda](https://egpuganda.go.ug/). Some utility tenders for wayleave demarcation and survey equipment in 2026 used open domestic bidding.

### Drone ownership {#drone-ownership}

Only Ugandan citizens, lawfully resident foreign individuals, Uganda-registered companies and Government may own a drone (reg 8 of S.I. 96 of 2022), every drone must be registered in Uganda (reg 9), and each operation needs UCAA approval, Chief of Defence Forces clearance, a line-ministry letter and a district sign-off. On petroleum projects, the flying partner must also qualify under the national content rules, because land surveying is reserved. See [drone law in Uganda](/ug/drone-regulations).
::::
::::col
### Personal data {#personal-data}

The [Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019](https://ulii.org/akn/ug/act/2019/9/eng@2023-12-31) and its 2021 Regulations govern personal data, which imagery of homes and census records can contain. The [Personal Data Protection Office](https://pdpo.go.ug/) runs organisation registration and breach reporting. Processing or storing personal data outside Uganda needs adequate protection in the destination or the data subject's consent, so agree with any supplier where the work is done and stored.

Where an output must be signed as a survey, who the registered surveyor is. A structure register is not a cadastral survey.

Where imagery and results are processed and stored, the basis for any transfer out of Uganda, who receives the results, and what is blurred.

UETCL's [Grid Development Plan 2025–2040](https://uetcl.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/GDP25-40.pdf) records that "acquisition of way leaves for construction of transmission lines continues to be a challenge", and lists "rampant vandalism" alongside it. Wayleave cases also reach the High Court. A dated record of what stood where does not settle a dispute; it gives the utility, the land team and the occupants the same facts.

### The Electricity Act {#electricity-act}

A licensee may place lines "over, along, across, or upon any land", with a right of use and "prompt, fair, and adequate compensation" (s.67). Disputes go through the Land Act and Land Acquisition Act procedures (s.70), and land is compulsorily acquired where more than a right of use is needed (s.71). This is how UETCL's resettlement framework summarises the Electricity Act (Cap. 145; Cap. 157 on ULII). No statutory wayleave width was found.

The [Electricity (Amendment) Act 2022](https://bills.parliament.ug/attachments/The%20Electricity%20(Amendment)%20Act,%202022.pdf), assented to on 16 May 2022, added offences for cutting, removing, storing or moving supply lines or material without consent, and for receiving them, and raised the penalties for other offences.

### A 60 m corridor on a new 400 kV line {#corridor-60m}

UETCL's [resettlement policy framework](https://uetcl.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/RPF-Wobulenzi-Masaka-Mutukula-29January2026.pdf) for a new 400 kV interconnector (29 January 2026, Table 3.1) sets a **60 m corridor**:

- a **10 m right of way**, acquired outright, with ownership passing to UETCL;
- **25 m of wayleave on each side**, where the owner keeps the land and the wayleave is registered as an easement and compensated;
- in the wayleave, "No construction of permanent or temporary structures", and "Vegetation and crops must not exceed 2 meters in height".

UETCL commits to "ongoing monitoring and enforcement of land use restrictions". The corridor is specific to that project; other lines follow their own frameworks.

::::section{id="structures" eyebrow="Structures in the wayleave" title="A register, band by band, and what changed since the last survey"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the line in its UTM zone, list each structure the review confirms with its distance to the centreline and its band (right of way, wayleave, or outside), and cut the line into 500 m stretches rated for **encroachment density**: high where more than five structures stand inside the widest band, medium for one to five, low for none. It is a count that tells your wayleave team where to go first, not a safety rating.

Re-surveys on a schedule you agree with us flag new and removed structures between dated surveys, automatically and then confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views and a change notice after each survey. Where a structure's date matters, the imagery history of that spot shows roughly when it first appeared.

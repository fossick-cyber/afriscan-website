faq:
  - q: Does a satellite-based survey need any of these permits?
    a: No drone flies in a satellite-based survey, so the drone rules on this page do not govern it. Data-protection law can still apply to imagery that shows people or homes. In Mozambique, whether Lei n.º 6/2024 reaches satellite-only products is an interpretation for counsel to confirm; its definitions refer to sensors carried by aircraft.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly a drone survey in these countries?
    a: Not simply. Mozambique's RPAS directive issues operation approvals only to companies registered and domiciled in Mozambique. South Africa's Air Service Licence requires South African residence or majority South African control. Nigeria limits drone registration to Nigerians and Nigerian companies, with separate routes for foreign operators. In practice a local operator or a local partner is part of most plans.
  - q: Who is responsible for the permits, the client or the operator?
    a: The operator carries the aviation approvals. The client usually has to help with site permissions, such as landowner consent, access to its own facilities and its security department's clearance, and in Mozambique the client is also bound by the rules on receiving and using aerial survey data. Agree who obtains each permission in the contract.
  - q: How far ahead should a drone survey be planned?
    a: Weeks at least, and months where a new approval is needed. Nigeria's rules ask for an operator application at least 90 days before operations and special authorisations at least 30 days ahead; Mozambique's IACM aims to process a new operation approval in 90 to 120 days; survey and site permissions come on top. Satellite work can start while the drone permissions are arranged.
  - q: Does AfriScan or Afridrone hold these approvals?
    a: Not yet. Afridrone is working towards the operator approvals each country requires, and every drone proposal sets out the permits and authorisations that particular flight needs, who holds them and how they fit the schedule.

::::section{id="approvals" tone="alt" class="compare" eyebrow="The approvals" title="What a commercial survey operation needs in each country"}
| Requirement | Mozambique | South Africa | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The operator** | Written IACM approval before any flight; a Letter of Approval for Category C1 or C2 commercial work ([DOS-09-2018](https://www.villagereach.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/VillageReach-RFP-Amendment-1-UAV-provider-Mozambique.pdf), §8.1(f), §8.3.2) | A UAS Operator Certificate (UASOC, formerly ROC) with Operations Specifications ([CAR 101.04.1](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202303/48228rg11556gon3170.pdf)) | An RPAS Operator Certificate (ROC) with Operations Specifications ([Nig.CARs Part 21](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/wabn2axn/nigcars-part-21-remotely-piloted-aircraft-system.pdf), 21.4.1) |
| **A second licence** | None at the aviation layer; the survey and the data are authorised separately (below) | An Air Service Licence from the Air Service Licensing Council for commercial work ([CAR 101.04.1(1)(b)](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202303/48228rg11556gon3170.pdf); [Act 115 of 1990](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201503/act-115-1990.pdf)) | Part 11 asks for an RPA aerial-work application; whether a drone operator also needs a Permit for Aerial Aviation Services and an Aerial Work Certificate is unresolved ([Part 11](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/1lrh3ff2/nigcars-part-11-aerial-work.pdf)) |
| **The survey itself** | Authorisation from the Minister of National Defence for each aerial survey ([Lei n.º 6/2024](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/mz/2024/mz-government-gazette-series-i-dated-2024-06-04-no-108.pdf), art. 13(1)(b)) | None in the aviation rules; key-point, protected-area and landowner permissions apply to the site | Three months' notice to the Director of Federal Surveys before aerial photography for survey purposes, unless a shorter period is allowed ([Survey Co-ordination Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=501), s.1) |
| **Security** | Surveys coordinated with the Defence and Security authorities (Lei n.º 6/2024, art. 12(2)); crew background checks (DOS-09-2018, §14) | Background and criminal-record checks, a security coordinator and security training (CAR 101.04.8) | ONSA security clearance is a condition of every commercial ROC ([AC-AWS061](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/jjah0hhl/ncaa-ac-aws061.pdf)); an ONSA End-User Certificate for imported drones |
| **Each aircraft** | IACM registration; a Certificate of Safety, valid 12 months, for larger aircraft and Category C2 work (DOS-09-2018, §9, §12) | ZT- registration and a UAS Letter of Approval, valid 12 months (CAR 101.02) | NCAA registration, which needs the End-User Certificate (21.3.1.2) |
| **Each pilot** | A remote pilot licence, a Class 4 medical, a radiotelephony licence and ICAO English Level 4 (DOS-09-2018, §10) | A Remote Pilot Certificate under Part 71, with a Part 67 medical ([33rd Amendment](https://caasanwebsitestorage.blob.core.windows.net/legal-documents/Promulgated%2033RD%20Amendment%20Regulation%202026%20(Final).pdf), 2026) | A Remote Pilot Licence under Part 2 ([s.2.3.11](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/mvmk433o/nigcars-part-2-personnel-licensing.pdf)) |
| **Insurance** | Third-party liability (DOS-09-2018, §13) | Third-party liability at all times (CAR 101.04.12) | Insurance commensurate with the risk, with proof lodged with NCAA (21.17.1.1) |

Mozambique's directive is quoted from the only full text available, an unofficial English copy of DOS-09-2018 annexed to a 2019 tender; confirm clause numbers with IACM before relying on them.

::::section{id="flight" class="compare" eyebrow="The flight" title="The limits that shape a corridor survey" lead="Pipelines and power lines run past homes, roads, airstrips and plant. These are the default limits a flight plan along them has to fit, and where an approval route exists."}
| Limit | Mozambique | South Africa | Nigeria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Height** | 400 ft above ground; flights above it are prohibited outright | 400 ft unless authorised (CAR 101.05.10) | 120 m (400 ft) above ground, by day (21.9.6.4) |
| **Line of sight** | Pilot within sight of the aircraft, up to 500 m; flights beyond line of sight are not permitted | VLOS up to 500 m; beyond line of sight only with an approved operations manual and a B-VLOS rating (101.05.11) | VLOS by default; beyond line of sight with NCAA authorisation and within radio line of sight (21.9.6.6) |
| **People** | Over or within 50 m of people only with a specific IACM approval, and only people who are part of the operation | No flight over or within 50 m of any person unless approved, or the people are part of the operation (101.05.13) | At least 30 m from anyone not involved, or 15 m with their consent; not over populated areas under the standard conditions (21.9.6.4, 21.9.6.18) |
| **Structures and lines** | Within 50 m of a structure with the owner's written permission; no closer than 5 m laterally and 20 ft vertically to live high-tension wires | Within 50 m of a structure only with approval, the owner's permission and safety measures (101.05.14) | A special authorisation, requested at least 30 days ahead, for areas of high radio interference such as high-tension wires (21.9.6.21) |
| **Aerodromes** | Within 5 km only with a specific approval and the aerodrome operator's written permission | Not within 10 km of an aerodrome reference point unless authorised (101.05.10) | Not within 4 km of a controlled aerodrome without ATC authorisation (21.9.6.36); ATC clearance for controlled airspace ([AOL 24/29](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/tygotxei/controlled-airspace.pdf)) |
| **Night** | Only with a specific IACM approval | Restricted line of sight only, unless night flying is approved on the operator's specifications (101.05.12) | Only with a special authorisation (21.9.6.15) |
| **Landowner** | Owner's written permission near structures | Landowner permission for every flight, kept in the flight records ([TGM Part 101](https://caasanwebsitestorage.blob.core.windows.net/flight-operations-tgm/TGM%20Part%20101%20RPAS%20Operations%20Manual.pdf)) | An NCAA approval gives no rights against the owner or occupier of the land; written consent is needed (AC-AWS061 §12.7.1) |

For a long corridor, these rules usually mean flying short legs within line of sight, planning around settlements and roads, and using satellite imagery for the stretches a drone cannot reasonably cover.

::::section{id="sensitive-sites" tone="alt" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="Plant, key points and military areas"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Mozambique" icon="alert"}
Flights over National Key Points, military or other government installations, or any place of strategic interest are prohibited, with no approval route (DOS-09-2018, Table 3). Lei n.º 6/2024 restricts aircraft operations within at least 5 km of areas of strategic national security (art. 11(1)). Treat processing plants, compressor stations and LNG sites as places of strategic interest and plan them with the operator's security team.
:::
:::card{title="South Africa" icon="alert"}
Flights adjacent to or above a national key point, strategic installation, prison, police station or court, or in restricted airspace, need the controlling authority's written permission and notice to SACAA on form [CA 101-20](https://caasanwebsitestorage.blob.core.windows.net/flight-operations-forms/CA%20101-20.pdf) before the operation. Some compressor stations are declared national key points; ask the asset owner.
:::
:::card{title="Nigeria" icon="alert"}
Restricted areas are published in the Nigeria AIP; check it and the NOTAMs before each job. Photographing a military protected place is an offence under the [Official Secrets Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=421). Part 21 sets no numeric zone around oil and gas installations, so flights near them are planned through the asset owner's security department.
:::
:::

In every country, security features of an installation stay out of the deliverables, and flights near your own facilities are planned with your security team from the start.

::::section{id="data" eyebrow="The data" title="Who may receive, keep and publish the imagery"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Mozambique** is the strictest. Under [Lei n.º 6/2024](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/mz/2024/mz-government-gazette-series-i-dated-2024-06-04-no-108.pdf):

- the original survey data belong to the State, and processed data must be deposited with the cartography and defence bodies (art. 14);
- supplying the data to the client needs its own authorisation (art. 13(2));
- reproducing or publishing aerial photographs, mosaics or orthophotos without authorisation is an infraction (art. 16(1)(c));
- so is acquiring or using survey products of illegal origin (art. 16(1)(d)), which makes the client's own position part of the plan.
::::
::::col
**South Africa.** Imagery that shows identifiable people or homes can be personal information under POPIA, with the client usually the responsible party; some uses of a register may need the Information Regulator's prior authorisation. See [POPIA and aerial imagery](/za/popia).

**Nigeria.** Part 21 forbids using a drone to observe a person without consent, or private property without the owner's consent, and requires consent before publishing images of individuals (21.18.1.1). The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 adds notice to the Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach likely to put people's rights at risk (s.40) and a recorded basis for transfers abroad (s.41).

::::section{id="satellite" eyebrow="Satellite first" title="Where the drone rules stop"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A satellite-based survey involves no drone flight, no pilot and no site visit, so the aviation rules above do not govern it. That is why satellite screening is available in all three countries while drone permissions are arranged, and why a corridor survey usually starts from satellite and sends a drone only to the stretches that need detail.

Two points stay open. In **Mozambique**, Lei n.º 6/2024 defines aerial surveys by reference to sensors on board aircraft, so satellite-only products appear to fall outside it; that is an interpretation, and counsel should confirm it, together with the cartography authority's role in geospatial production. In **Nigeria**, no rule was found requiring a licence to buy or analyse commercial satellite imagery; confirm it with counsel for your project. Data-protection law applies to satellite imagery as it does to drone imagery.

::::section{id="checklist" tone="alt" eyebrow="For clients" title="Five questions to ask any drone operator, in any of the three countries"}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Which approvals cover this job, and when do they expire?"}
The operator certificate and its specifications, the Air Service Licence in South Africa, the aircraft registrations and the pilots' licences, each checked against the operations this job needs: night, beyond line of sight, near people, roads, structures or aerodromes.
:::
:::step{title="Which separate authorisations does this survey need?"}
In Mozambique, the Defence authorisation for the survey and the authorisation to release the data to you. In Nigeria, the security clearance and any notice to the Director of Federal Surveys. In South Africa, notices and permissions for key points and restricted airspace.
:::
:::step{title="Who obtains the site permissions?"}
Landowner and occupier consent, access to your facilities, your security department's clearance and any aerodrome, police or protected-area permission. Put the split in the contract.
:::
:::step{title="What happens to the data?"}
Where it is stored and processed, who may reproduce the orthophotos, what must be deposited with the State, and how images of people and homes are handled under data-protection law.
:::
:::step{title="What does the plan look like without the drone?"}
Which stretches satellite imagery can cover now, and which truly need a drone, so the schedule does not wait on the slowest permission.

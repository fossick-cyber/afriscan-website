::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem" title="Land changes between visits"}
Pipelines, power lines, concessions and project sites cover more ground than any team can walk. Between patrols and site visits, new structures go up inside protection zones, tracks open into concessions, and ground is dug or cleared near your assets. By the time anyone notices, a structure that could have been discussed early has become a compensation claim, a resettlement cost, or a dispute about what was there first.

AfriScan gives you a dated, reviewed picture of what is on your land and what has changed, so your land, integrity and social-performance teams can act early and on facts.

:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Structures in your protection zones and servitudes" icon="corridor"}
A register of each structure within the distances you choose, with its distance to your line or boundary, its coordinates and the stretch of route it sits in.
:::
:::card{title="New tracks, cleared ground and excavations" icon="excavation"}
Changes between dated surveys, flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views so your field teams know where to look.
:::
:::card{title="A dated record of what was there, and when" icon="calendar"}
Baselines on a known date, such as a resettlement cut-off date, and evidence packs that support community engagement and legal processes.
:::
:::

:::steps
:::step{title="Scope"}
Send your route or boundary as KML, KMZ, GeoJSON, Shapefile, GPX or GeoPackage, or draw it with us. We agree the distances and zones that matter: a statutory protection strip, your company standard, or a hazard zone your engineers have defined.
:::
:::step{title="Screen from satellite"}
We map structures and change across the whole route or site from dated satellite imagery, open building datasets or imagery you already hold.
:::
:::step{title="Check up close"}
Where the detail matters, drone surveys capture orthophotos and elevation models of the flagged stretches, subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires.
:::
:::step{title="Review and deliver"}
A person checks every result before it reaches you. You receive a PDF report in English or Portuguese, GIS layers and an interactive map file. Re-surveys follow on a schedule agreed with you.
:::

::::section{id="principles" tone="dark" eyebrow="Our standard" title="Land and assets, not people"}
AfriScan maps what is on the land: structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks. We do not track people or vehicles, and whether a structure is authorised is for you and the authorities to decide.

:::cards{cols="4"}
:::card{title="Dated, credited sources" icon="file-check"}
Every report names its sources and, where the source provides it, the capture date. Map-service basemaps are never passed off as survey imagery, and open data is credited as its licence requires.
:::
:::card{title="Checked by a person" icon="user-check"}
Automatic detection proposes; a reviewer confirms. Anything the imagery cannot settle is listed for a ground check.
:::
:::card{title="Scheduled, never “real‑time”" icon="clock"}
Satellites and drones capture images on particular dates, so we re-survey on a schedule agreed with you and tell you what changed.
:::
:::card{title="Supports your decisions" icon="scale"}
Our dated records support community engagement, legal processes and IFC Performance Standard 5 cut-off-date records. They do not replace your census, your surveyors or your engineers.

::::section{id="esia" eyebrow="For land, resettlement and ESIA teams" title="A dated record of what stands on the ground"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}

We map structures within set distances of your route or inside your project area, on imagery with a known capture date. That supports your cut-off-date documentation and census planning under IFC Performance Standard 5 and World Bank ESS5, and your comparison of route alternatives. It does not replace the census or the asset inventory: it tells your field teams where the structures are before they arrive, and it records what was there on the date that matters.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Mozambique’s 50 m strip"}
The Land Law (Lei n.º 19/97, de 1 de Outubro, art. 8) makes the land within 50 m on each side of oil, gas, water, electricity and telecommunications lines a partial protection zone, where no land-use right (DUAT) can be acquired, only special licences (art. 9). Our default buffers report structures within 50 m and 100 m, and we add any width your concession or company standard sets.
:::

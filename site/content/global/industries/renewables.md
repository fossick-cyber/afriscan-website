::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="The land question arrives before the first panel"}
Utility-scale solar and wind projects are land projects first. Lenders and their technical advisers want a clean land history, the connection line needs a servitude across other people's land, the ESIA needs a land-cover baseline, and construction has to be evidenced against the programme. Each of those questions is easier to answer from dated imagery than from a site visit.

:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="“What was on this land before we arrived?”" icon="history"}
Development and permitting teams need a dated land history for the site and the connection route, for due diligence and the ESIA.
:::
:::card{title="“The connection line crosses dozens of properties.”" icon="route"}
Grid constraints push projects to build their own connection lines, and each one needs a servitude and a count of what it would affect.
:::
:::card{title="“We need construction evidence for the lenders.”" icon="calendar"}
Owners' engineers and lenders' technical advisers need dated evidence of earthworks and progress between site visits.
:::
:::

::::section{id="site-selection" tone="alt" eyebrow="Site selection and due diligence" title="A dated land history for each candidate site"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
For each candidate site and connection route, the imagery history shows what has been built, cleared or cultivated, and roughly when, from archived satellite imagery and open datasets. A land-use and land-cover baseline maps built-up land, cropland, bare ground, water and tree cover, combined with our structure register, for the ESIA. Terrain and flood screening highlights steep, erosion-prone and historically flooded ground.

We then compare the options side by side: structures affected, land cover and screening results for each site and each connection route. It is an input to your site selection, not a full alternatives assessment, and the land history is not a title, tenure or valuation check.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="connection-lines" eyebrow="Structures on the site and along the connection line" title="What the site and its line would affect"}
We map the structures inside the site boundary and in a ring around it, and along the connection line within the servitude width and any wider band you choose, each measured to the boundary or the line. Where structures or land uses are affected, a dated register of the structures in the footprint, with reviewer categories, supports your census and asset inventory under IFC Performance Standard 5. It supports the census; it does not replace it.

::::section{id="construction" tone="alt" eyebrow="Construction and earthworks" title="Dated progress between site visits"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Dated drone orthophotos and elevation models of the site, compared side by side, show where ground has been cut or filled between visits, and volumes of stockpiles and spoil are calculated from the elevation models. Heavy plant and equipment visible on each survey date is recorded as a snapshot of activity on site. Drone surveys are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires.

These records give owners' engineers and lenders' advisers dated evidence to set against the construction programme. They are not certified surveys or a quantity surveyor's valuation, and we make no promise of turnaround for bid windows before the scope is agreed.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="fire" eyebrow="Fire near the site" title="Fire-hotspot notices and burnt-area maps"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
In the dry season, grass fires threaten site boundaries, substations and connection lines. Fire hotspots detected by satellite near your site and line are filtered to the buffer around them, checked by a reviewer and sent as notices, followed by maps of the burnt area. The hotspot data comes from NASA FIRMS. This is not an emergency or early-warning service, and small, short-lived or cloud-covered fires can be missed.

::::section{id="countries" tone="alt" eyebrow="Mozambique, South Africa, Nigeria" title="Where renewable projects meet land rules" lead="A summary for orientation, as of 26 September 2026, not legal advice."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="South Africa" icon="map" eyebrow="English"}
Grid constraints mean many projects, from public procurement rounds to private wheeling deals, build their own connection lines, and each needs a servitude and a count of the structures it would affect. Lenders apply the Equator Principles and the IFC Performance Standards. Commercial drone work needs a UASOC and an Air Service Licence under [Civil Aviation Regulations Part 101](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202303/48228rg11556gon3170.pdf).
:::
:::card{title="Mozambique" icon="map" eyebrow="Português · English"}
In Mozambique the larger land question for new generation is resettlement on hydropower and associated line projects, covered on our [project finance and ESIA page](key:project-finance-esia#countries). Connection lines fall under the servitude rules of the Electricity Law, described on the [power and utilities page](key:power-utilities#countries).
:::
:::card{title="Nigeria" icon="map" eyebrow="English"}
Solar-hybrid mini-grids are the main renewable build-out, and their land questions are household estimates and site histories for candidate communities, covered under [mini-grid and electrification planning](key:power-utilities#energy-access).
:::

::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="Large areas, few people, and change at the edges"}
Estates, plantations, conservation areas and offsets cover far more ground than any team can patrol. Clearing, new tracks and structures appear at the boundary, fire moves through in the dry season, and buyers, certifiers, lenders and funders increasingly ask for dated evidence of what the land looked like, and when.

:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="“Clearing and new tracks keep appearing at the boundary.”" icon="boundary"}
Estate and reserve managers need to know where and roughly when the edge changed, before it becomes a dispute.
:::
:::card{title="“Our buyers want plot histories.”" icon="history"}
Exporters and their EU customers need dated evidence of what each plot looked like against the deforestation-free cut-off date.
:::
:::card{title="“We have to show the offset is working.”" icon="leaf"}
Offset and restoration programmes need a dated trend of land cover and disturbance that their reviewers and funders can check.
:::
:::

::::section{id="estates" tone="alt" eyebrow="Estates and plantations" title="Boundaries, clearing and fire across the estate"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We map structures inside your estate boundary and in a ring around it, and flag new structures, cleared ground and new tracks at the boundary between dated surveys, with fence lines and gates checked by a reviewer on drone imagery where flown. Vegetation and land-cover change across the estate is compared between dates from Copernicus Sentinel data, and regular forest-loss and ground-disturbance alerts from global systems are checked by our team and sent as notices.

In the fire season, satellite-detected fire hotspots near your blocks are filtered to your boundaries, checked and sent as notices, followed by maps of the burnt area. Revegetation on replanted or restored blocks is followed between dates, and a land-cover baseline and the estate's imagery history show what the land looked like before.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="eudr" eyebrow="Deforestation-free supply chains" title="Plot histories against the EUDR cut-off date"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The EU Deforestation Regulation asks operators placing cocoa, rubber, palm oil and other listed commodities on the EU market to show that the land was not deforested after 31 December 2020 ([European Commission](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/regulation-deforestation-free-products_en)). For each plot you supply, we assemble a dated history from archived satellite imagery and open datasets, check tree-cover loss and disturbance alerts against the cut-off date, and add a land-cover baseline and the structures inside and around the plot.
::::
::::col
The results come as a GIS layer per plot, with the evidence for each, ready for your own due-diligence system. We can also analyse imagery you already hold for your plots.

:::callout{tone="scope" title="What a plot history is, and is not"}
A dated record of what the imagery and open data show for a plot. It is not a due-diligence statement, it does not make a plot compliant, and the plot boundaries are supplied or checked by you.
:::

::::section{id="carbon-restoration" tone="alt" eyebrow="Carbon and landscape restoration" title="Dated land-cover records for restoration programmes"}
REDD+ and restoration programmes need a baseline and a dated trend. We deliver a land-cover baseline, forest-loss and disturbance alerts, vegetation change and revegetation between dates, radar comparisons that show larger changes even under rainy-season cloud, settlement growth trends around the project area and the area's imagery history. Where benefit-sharing plans need them, a dated register of structures and estimated households supports that planning, with the assumptions stated.

These records support your monitoring, reporting and verification. We give no carbon tonnage and no verification opinion.

::::section{id="offsets-protected-areas" eyebrow="Offsets and protected areas" title="Disturbance, tracks and structures inside reserves and offsets"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Biodiversity offsets and protected areas are often remote, and some are insecure, which makes satellite-first records the practical route. We map land-cover change, disturbance alerts, revegetation, fire and burnt areas, and flag new structures, cleared ground and tracks at the boundary and inside it. Radar comparisons show larger changes even under rainy-season cloud. Surface excavations inside a reserve, such as pits and spoil, are mapped between dates.

It shows larger patches of change, not individual trees or species, and it is not an ecological survey.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="Land, never people or animals"}
We map land cover, clearing, tracks, structures and excavations. We do not detect people or livestock, and anti-poaching, guarding and security response are outside what we do.

::::section{id="countries" tone="alt" eyebrow="Mozambique, South Africa, Nigeria" title="Where the rules shape the record" lead="A summary for orientation, as of 26 September 2026, not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Mozambique" icon="map" eyebrow="Português · English"}
Biodiversity offsets for Category A and A+ projects are required under the offsets directive (Diploma Ministerial n.º 55/2022), with BIOFUND implementing the national [biodiversity offsets programme](https://www.biofund.org.mz/en/projects/biodiversity-offsets-program/). Many reserves are remote and some are insecure, so satellite-first records let monitoring start without a site visit. Reports can be delivered in Portuguese.
:::
:::card{title="South Africa" icon="map" eyebrow="English"}
Estates already have strong fire systems through fire protection associations and the CSIR's [AFIS](https://www.afis.co.za/), so our work there leads with boundary, clearing and land-cover change rather than fire. Over special nature reserves and world heritage sites, the [Protected Areas Act](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a57-03.pdf) (s47) restricts flights below 2,500 ft, so work there is planned as satellite-only.
:::
:::card{title="Nigeria" icon="map" eyebrow="English"}
The EU Deforestation Regulation matters most for Nigerian cocoa, rubber and palm oil, where exporters and their EU buyers need plot histories against the cut-off date. Remote, satellite-first records suit plots spread across many smallholdings and states.
:::

::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem, in your words" title="Reserves are long, narrow and easy to build in"}
Road and rail reserves run through towns, markets and farmland, and the land beside them is some of the most valuable in the district. Structures go up inside the reserve and the building restriction area between inspections; informal crossings and tracks open across the line; and when a road is widened or a railway rehabilitated, each structure inside the reserve becomes part of the land-acquisition and resettlement plan.

:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="“We don't know what's in the reserve any more.”" icon="houses"}
The last inventory is years old, and the reserve has filled in since. Land teams need a current, measured list before they can plan.
:::
:::card{title="“The new alignment has to be fixed before land take starts.”" icon="route"}
Every option affects different structures. Counting them, before the route is fixed, keeps resettlement costs and delay visible in the decision.
:::
:::card{title="“We need to see construction progress and borrow pits.”" icon="excavation"}
Supervising engineers and project owners need dated evidence of earthworks and borrow pits between site meetings.
:::
:::

::::section{id="reserves" tone="alt" eyebrow="Road and rail reserves" title="Structures inside the reserve and the restriction area"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We measure each structure the review confirms to the centre line or reserve boundary you supply, in true metres, and place it in the bands you set: the reserve, the building restriction area beyond it and any wider band you want to watch. Each 500 m of road or railway is rated for encroachment density, a count rule that shows where structures cluster, so inspections and engagement can start where they matter most.

Between dated surveys, new and removed structures are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, and a notice after each re-survey says what changed and where. New tracks and informal crossings over the line are mapped between dates. Tracks under tree canopy can be missed, which is why every result is confirmed by a reviewer.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="freight-rail" eyebrow="Freight-rail corridors" title="The reserve, the crossings and what is built beside the line" lead="A freight line runs for long distances through farmland, towns and sidings, and its reserve is some of the easiest land in a district to build on. What imagery shows is land change: new structures, crossings, tracks and works. It cannot see theft, vandalism or people."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Reserve encroachment.** We map the structures inside the rail reserve and the land beside it, measure each one to the track centre line, place it in the bands you set and rate each 500 m of line for encroachment density. On many freight corridors the reserve is wider than the fenced formation, so the register shows where building has moved inside the reserve long before it reaches the track.

**Informal crossings and tracks.** New tracks and informal crossings over the line are mapped between dated surveys where they are wide enough to show at the imagery's resolution, so your safety and reserve teams know where the line is being crossed. We map the crossings themselves, never the people who use them. Narrow paths and crossings under tree canopy can be missed, which is why a reviewer confirms each one.

**New structures along the reserve.** Between dated surveys, new and removed structures are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, and a trend of built-up land shows where settlement is concentrating around stations, sidings, yards and level crossings.

**Rehabilitation and upgrading.** Before a freight line is rehabilitated, doubled or given new sidings and passing loops, a dated baseline of the reserve records what stood inside it. Where structures are affected, a dated register supports the census and asset inventory for the resettlement plan, and where a realignment is considered, the options are compared by the structures they affect.
::::
::::col
### What you receive

:::checklist
- A register of structures inside the rail reserve, by band and chainage
- An encroachment-density rating for each 500 m of line
- New informal crossings and tracks over the line, mapped between dates
- New and removed structures along the reserve between dated surveys
- Settlement growth around stations, sidings and yards
- A dated baseline before rehabilitation or upgrading works
:::

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Land change, not theft"}
Satellites and drones cannot see cable or rail theft, vandalism or who was on the line. They show structures, crossings, tracks, cleared ground and works along the reserve, so your corridor teams know where to look.

::::section{id="works-beside-the-line" tone="alt" eyebrow="Construction next to the line" title="Excavation, earthworks and building beside the track"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Works next to a railway can affect it long before anyone reports them: a borrow pit cut near the embankment, a new building across a drainage line, a trench for another utility through the reserve, spoil tipped at the edge of the formation. Fresh digging, spoil heaps, trenches and earthworks on or near the reserve are flagged between surveys, with their chainage and distance to the line, so track and reserve teams know where to check first. Heavy plant visible at a site is recorded on each survey date as a snapshot of activity on the land, never as tracking.

Where a flagged stretch needs detail, a drone survey captures it, subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. Drainage crossings and erosion-prone stretches can be screened from elevation data alongside, as described under [terrain and flood](key:rail-roads#terrain-flood).

This is screening of what is visible at the surface. It is not an engineering assessment of the track, the formation or the embankments, and it does not measure ground movement.
::::
::::col
### What you receive

:::checklist
- Fresh excavation, trenches and earthworks near the line, flagged for checking
- New structures beside the reserve, confirmed by a reviewer
- Heavy plant visible at works on each survey date
- Before-and-after views of each flagged site
- Drone checks of flagged stretches, subject to approvals

::::section{id="new-alignments" eyebrow="New alignments and widening" title="Count the structures before the land take"}
For a new highway, a bypass, a widening or a new railway, we count the structures along each alignment option within the reserve width and any building restriction area, so the route decision carries its land cost. Once the alignment is fixed, a dated register of the structures in the reserve, with reviewer categories, supports the census and asset inventory for the resettlement action plan. It does not replace the census, and it is not a finding that any structure must be removed.

::::section{id="construction" tone="alt" eyebrow="Construction and borrow pits" title="Dated evidence of earthworks between site meetings"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
During construction, dated drone orthophotos and elevation models compared side by side show where ground has been cut or filled between visits, and volumes of borrow pits, spoil heaps and stockpiles are calculated from the elevation models. Heavy plant visible on each survey date is recorded as a snapshot of activity on site, never as tracking.

These are not certified surveys or a quantity surveyor's valuation. They give the supervising engineer and the owner dated evidence of progress to set against the programme. Drone work is subject to the permits and authorisations each job requires.
::::
::::col

- Side-by-side dated orthophotos and elevation models
- Cut-and-fill maps between visits
- Borrow-pit, spoil and stockpile volumes
- Heavy plant visible on each survey date
- Rehabilitation of closed borrow pits, tracked between dates

::::section{id="terrain-flood" eyebrow="Terrain and flood" title="Where the road meets water and slope"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Steep, erosion-prone and drainage-crossing stretches of an alignment are highlighted from elevation data, with drone elevation models for detail where flown, and stretches in ground seen as water in past satellite records are flagged for planning. After a flood or cyclone, a reviewer's before-and-after check shows visible damage to the road or railway and the structures along it, once post-event imagery is available. It is screening, not a hydraulic model or a structural assessment.

::::section{id="evidence" tone="alt" eyebrow="Evidence and disputed plots" title="What stood beside the road, and when"}
When a plot along the reserve is disputed, the imagery history shows roughly when it was built on or cleared, a location sheet gathers every dated image of that point, and an evidence pack fixes the record with file fingerprints and an independent timestamp. These records support legal and community processes. They do not decide claims or support any eviction: that is for the authorities and the courts.

::::section{id="countries" eyebrow="Mozambique, South Africa, Nigeria" title="Reserve widths your register is measured against" lead="A summary of public rules for orientation, as of 26 September 2026, not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
| Instrument | What it sets |
|---|---|
| **Mozambique**<br>[Lei n.º 19/97, de 1 de Outubro (Lei de Terras)](https://www.pdul.gov.mz/content/download/486/2635/file/Lei%20de%20Terras.pdf), art. 8 | Partial protection zones of 50 m on each side of a railway's axis, 30 m along primary roads and 15 m along secondary and tertiary roads, where no land-use right (DUAT) can be acquired |
| **South Africa**<br>[SANRAL Act 7 of 1998](https://www.sagc.org.za/pdf/legislation/S%20A%20National%20Roads%20Agency%20Act%207%20of%201998.pdf), s.48 | SANRAL's written permission for any structure in the building restriction area: land outside urban areas within 60 m of a national road's boundary, or within 500 m of a point of intersection |
| **Nigeria**<br>Federal and state road and rail authorities | Reserve widths are set per road and railway by the responsible authority, so we measure to the widths you supply |

In Mozambique, reports can be delivered in Portuguese for land services and district government. In South Africa, if a register is to be used as evidence about unlawful conduct, POPIA's prior-authorisation rule (s57) may apply to your organisation: take advice first. Drone surveys in every country are subject to the permits and authorisations each job requires; the rules are compared in [drone survey rules by country](/drone-regulations) and summarised on [where we work](/countries).

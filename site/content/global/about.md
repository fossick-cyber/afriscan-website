::::section{id="what-we-do" eyebrow="What we do" title="A dated record of what stands on your land"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Land beside a pipeline, inside a power-line servitude or around a concession changes between visits. Houses go up inside protection strips, ground is cleared, pits are dug and tracks are cut, and each change becomes a cost, a delay or a dispute if nobody knows about it until years later.

AfriScan gives land, integrity, ESIA and resettlement teams a dated, reviewed record of what stands on the ground: each structure located, measured to your line or boundary and placed in the bands that matter to you, then compared between surveys so you can see what is new. We work from satellite imagery first, add drone detail where it is needed, and use the imagery you already hold wherever you have it.

Results are files your team keeps: a PDF report in English or Portuguese, GIS layers for your own maps, and an interactive map file that opens in a web browser.

::::section{id="where" tone="alt" eyebrow="Where we work" title="Built on real corridors in Mozambique"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The platform behind AfriScan was built and tested on high-pressure gas pipeline routes in Mozambique. Our published example, the [sample register](/results) from one of those pipelines, is shown with the route owner's permission and labelled for what it is: a register built from reviewer marks, drawn as strip views and shown on Google satellite imagery with its attribution.

Satellite-based surveys are available in every country with a site here, in Southern, East, West and Central Africa, with no site visit and no drone flight. Each country has its own section of this site, with the local law, the regulators and the vocabulary our registers are measured against.

::::section{id="standard" eyebrow="How we work" title="The rules we apply to every job"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Land and assets, never people" icon="shield"}
We map structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks. We do not identify people, and we do not follow people or vehicles. Whether a structure is authorised is for you and the authorities to decide.
:::
:::card{title="A person reviews every result" icon="user-check"}
Automatic sources propose; a reviewer confirms, removes and adds on the imagery before delivery, and anything the imagery cannot settle is listed for a ground check. See the [methodology](/methodology).
:::
:::card{title="Dated and credited" icon="history"}
Every report names its imagery and, where the source provides it, the capture date. Open data is credited as its licence requires. See [data sources and credits](/data-sources).
:::
:::card{title="Honest about limits" icon="info"}
We publish no accuracy percentage, promise no capture date and make no claim imagery cannot support. What a register cannot show is written into every report.
:::
:::card{title="Your data stays yours" icon="lock"}
We never publish a client's route, imagery or results without written permission. The only route on this site is shown with its owner's permission.
:::
:::card{title="No permit assumed" icon="drone"}
Drone surveys are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. Every drone proposal sets out what that flight needs and how it fits the schedule.
:::
:::

::::section{id="afridrone" tone="alt" eyebrow="AfriScan and Afridrone" title="Two names, two roles"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
AfriScan is [Afridrone](https://afridr.one/)’s land and corridor monitoring service; Afridrone flies the drone work. AfriScan is the name for the survey, the register and the reports.

Afridrone is working towards the operator approvals each country requires. Until a job's approvals and security clearances are in place, that job is planned on satellite imagery or on the imagery you already hold.

::::section{id="work-with-us" eyebrow="Working together" title="How a project starts"}
Send the route or boundary, the country and province, the distances that matter and what the record is for. We reply with a written proposal: scope, imagery plan, method, deliverables and schedule. We respond to tenders, RFQs and RFPs. Send your supplier or prequalification forms with your request; the proposal states which registrations are in place for your contract and which are being arranged. Many projects start with one stretch, one site or one concession boundary.

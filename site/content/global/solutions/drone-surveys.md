::::section{id="what" eyebrow="What it is" title="Satellite first, drone for the detail"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Most of a corridor or concession never needs a drone. Satellite screening maps it end to end and tells you which stretches have changed, which have the most structures, and where the imagery could not settle a question. Those are the places a drone earns its flight time.

A drone survey gives your team a dated, georeferenced orthophoto of each flagged stretch or site, detailed enough for a reviewer to check roofs, fences, gates and fresh digging, with surface and terrain elevation models, a point cloud and a 3D model processed from the same flight. Dated drone surveys are also usually the most reliable way to fix a record on a particular day, such as a resettlement cut-off date, when satellite capture dates cannot be guaranteed.

Every drone job is planned around the approvals and security clearances that particular flight needs. Satellite-based work carries on while they are obtained.

::::section{id="permits" eyebrow="Permits, country by country" title="What a drone survey needs in each country" lead="The main requirements an operator must meet, as of 26 September 2026. This section summarises public rules for information. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Mozambique" icon="map" eyebrow="IACM · Lei n.º 6/2024"}
- Drone flights are regulated by the civil aviation authority, [IACM](https://www.iacm.gov.mz/aeronaves-nao-tripulados/).
- The Minister of National Defence authorises aerial surveys in national territory ([Lei n.º 6/2024, de 4 de Junho, art. 13(1)](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/mz/2024/mz-government-gazette-series-i-dated-2024-06-04-no-108.pdf)).
- Supplying the survey data to the client needs authorisation from the cartography entity (art. 13(2)).
- Reproducing or publishing aerial photographs or orthophotos without authorisation is an infraction (art. 16(1)\(c)), and so is using products of illegal origin (art. 16(1)(d)).
:::
:::card{title="South Africa" icon="map" eyebrow="SACAA · Part 101"}
- Commercial drone work needs a UASOC from the South African Civil Aviation Authority and an Air Service Licence ([Civil Aviation Regulations Part 101, GN R.3170, 2023](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202303/48228rg11556gon3170.pdf)).
- Flights within 50 m of people, structures or public roads need specific approval, and the landowner's permission is needed for each flight ([SACAA Part 101 operations manual](https://caasanwebsitestorage.blob.core.windows.net/flight-operations-tgm/TGM%20Part%20101%20RPAS%20Operations%20Manual.pdf)).
- Special nature reserves and world heritage sites control their airspace up to 2,500 ft ([Protected Areas Act, s47](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a57-03.pdf)).
- Servitude diagrams are the work of professional land surveyors ([Geomatics Profession Act, s13](https://www.sagc.org.za/pdf/legislation/Geomatics%20Profession%20Act%20-%20Act%2019%20of%202013.pdf)); orthophotos do not replace them.
:::
:::card{title="Nigeria" icon="map" eyebrow="NCAA · Part 21"}
- Commercial drone work needs an RPAS Operator Certificate from the NCAA, with ONSA security clearance built into the process, and each drone must be registered ([Nig.CARs 2023 Part 21](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/wabn2axn/nigcars-part-21-remotely-piloted-aircraft-system.pdf)).
- Standard conditions are daytime, within visual line of sight, at or below 400 ft and not over populated areas; anything else needs authorisation.
- Controlled airspace, including around Lagos and Port Harcourt, needs air-traffic clearance ([NCAA letter, June 2025](https://ncaa.gov.ng/media/tygotxei/controlled-airspace.pdf)).
- Aerial photography for survey purposes needs three months' notice to the Director of Federal Surveys, unless a shorter period is allowed ([Survey Co-ordination Act, s.1](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=501)).
:::
:::

Where a rule's reach is unsettled, such as whether Mozambique's Lei n.º 6/2024 applies to satellite-derived mapping or whether Nigeria's survey notice applies to drone orthophotos, we say so in the proposal and recommend your counsel confirms it.

The full rules, with a checklist for clients and a dated source for each requirement, are in each country guide: [Mozambique](/mz/drone-regulations) ([em português](/mz/pt/lei-de-drones)), [South Africa](/za/drone-regulations) and [Nigeria](/ng/drone-regulations). The three are compared side by side in [drone survey rules by country](/drone-regulations), and the wider country picture is on [where we work](/countries).

::::section{id="limits" eyebrow="Limits" title="What a drone survey is not"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### What it gives you
:::checklist
- Detail on the stretches and sites that need it, on a known date
- Elevation models, volumes and cut-and-fill for engineering and progress reporting
- Imagery that a reviewer can use to check fences, gates and small structures
:::
::::
::::col
### What it is not
:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Not a certified, cadastral or statutory survey: it supports your registered surveyor
- Never a flight without permits: each job is subject to the approvals and security clearances it requires
- No thermal, LiDAR or multispectral payloads, and no long-range flights
- Not imagery of people: flights map land and assets, and plans avoid flying over people
:::

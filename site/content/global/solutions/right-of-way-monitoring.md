::::section{id="what" eyebrow="What it is" title="A dated register of what stands inside your right of way"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A right of way, servitude, wayleave or protection zone only protects a line while it stays clear. Encroachment rarely arrives all at once. A cooking shelter goes up beside a field, then a house, a fence line and a track to reach it. Between patrols and helicopter flights nobody sees it happen, and by the time it is noticed the structure is a compensation question, a safety concern and, later, a dispute about who was there first.

Right-of-way monitoring replaces that uncertainty with a record. We map the structures within the distances that apply to your line, measure each one to the route you supply, and rate every 500 m of route for **encroachment density**, so your land and integrity teams know which stretches to visit first. Then we repeat the survey on an agreed schedule and tell you what changed.

The method is the same whatever your industry calls the strip: a pipeline right of way, a Mozambican *zona de protecção parcial*, a South African servitude or a Nigerian power-line right of way. What stands within X metres of the line, and since when?
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Encroachment density, not risk"}
Each 500 m segment is rated **High** (more than 5 structures inside the widest buffer), **Medium** (1 to 5) or **Low** (none). It is a count rule that helps you prioritise. It is not a safety, hazard or integrity assessment. See the [methodology](/methodology#rating).
:::

::::section{id="widths" tone="alt" eyebrow="Statutory widths" title="The strips we measure against, country by country" lead="We report the distances you choose. These are the widths set in law for the lines we survey most often, as of 26 September 2026. This section summarises public rules for information. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Mozambique" icon="map" eyebrow="Zona de protecção parcial"}
- **[Lei n.º 19/97, de 1 de Outubro (Lei de Terras), arts. 8 and 9](https://www.pdul.gov.mz/content/download/486/2635/file/Lei%20de%20Terras.pdf):** a partial protection zone of 50 m on each side of oil, gas, water, electricity and telecommunications lines, where no land-use right (DUAT) can be acquired, only special licences. Railways: 50 m each side of the axis. Roads: 30 m (primary) and 15 m (secondary and tertiary).
- **[Lei n.º 8/2026, de 3 de Junho (Petroleum Law), art. 75](https://www.inp.gov.mz/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/BR_104_I_SERIE_2.o_SUPLEMENTO_2026.pdf):** a 50 m partial protection zone around petroleum infrastructure; the safety zone is set by regulation.
- **[Lei n.º 12/2022, de 11 de Julho (Electricity Law), art. 43](https://arene.org.mz/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Lei-de-Electricidade-2022.pdf):** an administrative servitude of up to 50 m from the line's axis, registered against the concession. Compensation is not owed to holders who acquired their rights after the line was built (art. 43(10)).
:::
:::card{title="South Africa" icon="map" eyebrow="Servitudes and road reserves"}
- **The registered servitude:** pipeline, power-line and bulk-water widths are set in each servitude agreement and diagram.
- **[SANRAL Act 7 of 1998, s48](https://www.sagc.org.za/pdf/legislation/S%20A%20National%20Roads%20Agency%20Act%207%20of%201998.pdf):** on national roads, structures in the building restriction area (outside urban areas, within 60 m of the road boundary) need SANRAL's written permission.
:::
:::card{title="Nigeria" icon="map" eyebrow="Rights of way"}
- **[Oil Pipelines Act, s.12](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=425):** on the licence holder's application, buildings and cultivation can be restricted within a specified distance, of up to 100 feet, from the land in the pipeline licence.
- **[NESIS Regulations 2015, Table 3.1](https://nemsa.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/NESIS-Regulations-2015-1-1.pdf):** power-line rights of way of 50 m (330 kV), 30 m (132 kV) and 11 m (33 kV and 11 kV), divided equally either side of the centre line, with no structures under the line.
:::
:::

Two of these rules show why the date of a record matters. Mozambique's Electricity Law ties compensation to whether rights were acquired before or after the line was built, and NESIS limits a licensee's liability for structures built after the line. A dated register supports those processes: it records what stood where on the imagery date. It does not decide them.

::::section{id="how" eyebrow="How it works" title="Baseline first, then scheduled re-surveys"}
:::steps
:::step{title="Route and widths" icon="route"}
You send the route as KML, KMZ, GeoJSON, Shapefile, GPX or GeoPackage, or we draw it with you and you confirm it. We agree the distances: the statutory strip, your company standard, and a wider band to watch.
:::
:::step{title="Baseline register" icon="corridor"}
We map the structures along the whole line from dated satellite imagery, open building datasets or imagery you already hold. A reviewer confirms, removes and adds structures by hand, and anything the imagery cannot settle goes to a "verify on the ground" list.
:::
:::step{title="Scheduled re-surveys" icon="calendar"}
On the cadence we agree, we survey the line again on new dated imagery. New and removed structures are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views of each change.
:::
:::step{title="Change notice and close checks" icon="send"}
After each survey your team receives a notice of what changed and where. Where a flagged stretch needs more detail, a drone survey captures it, subject to the permits and authorisations each job requires.
:::
:::

Alongside the register, a survey can add the signals that tend to come before new structures: [new tracks and fresh digging](key:excavation-mapping) on or near the right of way, how [built-up land around the line](key:change-detection) has grown year by year, and notices of satellite-detected fire hotspots near above-ground installations.

:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### What it shows
:::checklist
- The structures visible from above within the widths you set, and their distance to the line
- What changed between two dated surveys, confirmed by a reviewer
- Which stretches carry the most structures, and where to send people first
- Works, tracks and cleared ground visible at the surface
:::
::::
::::col
### What it does not show
:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Anything between surveys: it is scheduled, never continuous
- The condition of the pipe or line, leaks, taps or any integrity measure
- Who lives in a structure, who owns it, or whether it is authorised
- Structures hidden under dense canopy, and a cadastral or statutory survey of the strip
:::

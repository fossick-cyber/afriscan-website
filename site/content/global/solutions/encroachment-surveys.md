::::section{id="what" eyebrow="What it is" title="What stands inside and around your boundary"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Boundaries are long and rarely walked. A mining right, an exploration licence, a plantation, a solar site or a special economic zone can run for tens of kilometres of edge, most of it out of sight of the gate. Settlement pressure builds from outside: new homesteads at the edge, fields cleared across the line, a track opened to reach them. Inside, structures can appear on land you need for an expansion or a buffer.

An encroachment survey records the position of each structure against your boundary, inside, outside or both, so your tenure, land and social-performance teams can see the pattern and engage early. It uses the same method as our [right-of-way surveys](key:right-of-way-monitoring), applied to a polygon instead of a line.

The same register works for industrial estates, ports, free zones and land banks, where the question is what has been built on land held for a purpose.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="Words we use, and don't"}
We describe structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks, never the people who built them, and we never label a structure illegal. What the imagery shows is a fact. Its status is your decision and the authorities'.
:::

::::section{id="modes" tone="alt" eyebrow="How it works" title="Three ways to survey a boundary"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Inside the boundary" icon="boundary" eyebrow="Your land"}
The structures inside the concession, site or estate, with an optional setback so structures close to the edge are listed separately. Useful before an expansion, a resettlement or a handover.
:::
:::card{title="In a ring around it" icon="target" eyebrow="The approach"}
The structures within a ring of the width you choose outside the boundary, where settlement pressure usually builds first. Each structure is listed with its distance to the edge.
:::
:::card{title="Both, compared over time" icon="compare" eyebrow="Change at the edge"}
Inside and outside together, re-surveyed on a schedule agreed with you. New structures, cleared ground and new tracks at the edge are flagged and confirmed by a reviewer.
:::
:::

:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Boundary and bands"}
You send the polygon and we agree the ring width, any inside setback, and the zones inside the boundary that matter most, such as a planned pit, a buffer or a conservation set-aside.
:::
:::step{title="Structures and categories"}
We map structures from dated imagery and open building datasets; reviewers confirm them, mark by hand where automatic detection is not enough, and, where scoped, tag each with what the imagery shows: main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure, under construction.
:::
:::step{title="Edges, tracks and fences"}
New tracks and access roads into the area are mapped between dates. Fence lines and gates are checked by a reviewer on drone orthophotos, subject to the approvals and security clearances each flight requires.
:::
:::step{title="Review and delivery"}
A person reviews every result. You receive the register, maps and GIS layers, and a notice after each re-survey.
:::
:::

::::section{id="deliverables" eyebrow="What you receive" title="A boundary register and the layers behind it"}
:::checklist
- **A structure register** with ID, coordinates, inside or outside, distance to the boundary and, where scoped, a reviewer category
- **Area figures:** the area surveyed and the structures inside and within each band of the boundary
- **An area-wide structure layer** for large concessions and districts, ready for your own GIS
- **Boundary change** between dated surveys: new structures, cleared ground and new tracks at the edge
- **A PDF report** in English or Portuguese, **GIS layers** (GeoPackage, GeoJSON, KMZ, Shapefile) and an interactive map file
:::

::::section{id="limits" tone="alt" eyebrow="Limits" title="What an encroachment survey is not"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### What it shows
:::checklist
- Where structures stand relative to your boundary, and how that changes between dated surveys
- Where land is being cleared or opened up with new tracks
- What the imagery shows about each structure, where categories are scoped
:::
::::
::::col
### What it does not show
:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Whether a structure is authorised, or who built or uses it
- Title, tenure or the legal line of your boundary
- Structures under dense canopy, and fences on satellite imagery
- Anything about people: we map land and assets, never who is on them
:::

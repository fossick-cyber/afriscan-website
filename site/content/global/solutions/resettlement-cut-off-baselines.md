faq:
  - q: Is the baseline a substitute for the census and asset inventory?
    a: No. It is a dated record of the structures in the footprint, with imagery, coordinates and IDs, that supports your census and asset inventory under IFC Performance Standard 5. The census itself, of households, people and assets, stays with your field teams, and so does the question of who is eligible.
  - q: Does the imagery set the cut-off date?
    a: No. The cut-off date comes from your resettlement process and the host government's procedures, and IFC guidance ties it to the completion of the census and asset inventory. Our record documents what was on the ground on the imagery dates around it.
  - q: Can you give us imagery for the exact cut-off date?
    a: Not with certainty from satellites. Archive coverage and dates vary by place, and a new capture depends on satellite availability and weather, so its date is never guaranteed. Where one date matters, a drone survey on the day (subject to the permits and authorisations the job requires) or the dated archive scenes either side of it are usually the better plan. We set this out in the proposal.
  - q: How do you show structures built after the cut-off date?
    a: We re-survey the footprint on a schedule agreed with you, flag new structures between dated surveys, have a reviewer confirm each one, and add it to the register with its first-seen date. The results can be packaged as an evidence pack with file fingerprints and an independent timestamp.
  - q: Can the register tell us whether a structure is lived in, or who owns it?
    a: No. Reviewer categories describe what the imagery shows, such as main building, outbuilding or livestock enclosure. Use, occupancy, ownership and value are for your census and asset inventory, and compensation needs verification on the ground.

::::section{id="what" eyebrow="What it is" title="A dated record of the footprint, before the questions start"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Resettlement goes wrong in the gaps: between the ESIA baseline and the census, between the census and the start of works, and between one site visit and the next. New structures appear between the baseline, the census and the start of works, and without a dated record it is one account against another.

IFC Performance Standard 5 recognises the problem. It expects a census to "discourage ineligible persons, such as opportunistic settlers, from claiming benefits" (para. 12), and it does not require a client "to compensate or assist those who encroach on the project area after the cut-off date for eligibility, provided the cut-off date has been clearly established and made public" (para. 23). That protection is only as good as your record of what was there.

We build that record from imagery with a known capture date: a drone survey, a dated satellite scene or your own georeferenced imagery. Never from map-service basemaps, which carry no date.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="What the standard says"}
IFC Performance Standard 5 (2012), paras. 12 and 23, quoted as published. IFC Guidance Note 5 ties the cut-off date to the completion of the census and asset inventory. Imagery supports that record; it does not set the date.

[IFC Performance Standard 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf) · [Guidance Note 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-ps-guidance-note-5-en.pdf)

::::section{id="how" tone="alt" eyebrow="How it works" title="Four moments in a resettlement programme"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Before the census" icon="search" eyebrow="Plan the fieldwork"}
A structure layer across the footprint and its buffer, with estimated households from stated assumptions, so census teams know how many structures to expect, where they cluster and how to split the work. Reviewers mark structures by hand where automatic detection is not enough, in dense villages or under mixed tree cover.
:::
:::card{title="At the cut-off date" icon="calendar" eyebrow="Fix the record"}
A register of the structures in the footprint on imagery captured as close to the cut-off date as the imagery plan allows, each with an ID, coordinates, a photo crop and, where scoped, a reviewer category. Packaged as an evidence pack with file fingerprints and an independent timestamp.
:::
:::card{title="After the cut-off" icon="compare" eyebrow="Record what arrives"}
Re-surveys of the footprint on a schedule agreed with you. New structures are flagged automatically, confirmed by a reviewer and added to the register with the date they first appear, with before-and-after views of each.
:::
:::card{title="When a grievance arrives" icon="file-check" eyebrow="Check one location"}
For a single claim, a location sheet of every dated image covering that point, compared with the cut-off register, so your grievance officer can see what was there on each date.
:::
:::

The register uses the same IDs from start to finish, so the census team, the RAP author, the lender's monitor and the grievance officer are all looking at the same structure.

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Dated imagery only"}
A cut-off record is only useful if its date can be shown. Every register names its imagery and, where the source provides it, the capture date; a cut-off record is built only on imagery whose date is known. We never build a cut-off record on map-service basemaps, and we tell you in the proposal which dates the imagery plan can realistically reach.
:::

::::section{id="limits" tone="alt" eyebrow="Limits" title="What a cut-off baseline is not"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### What it supports
:::checklist
- Your census and asset inventory, by showing where the structures are before teams arrive
- The cut-off-date record, by documenting what stood in the footprint on dated imagery
- Your grievance mechanism, by showing what was at one location on each date
- Your lender's monitoring, with dated evidence between site visits
:::
::::
::::col
### What it does not do
:::checklist{tone="no"}
- It does not replace the census, the asset inventory or the socio-economic survey
- It does not set the cut-off date, or decide who is eligible for compensation
- It does not establish the use, occupancy, ownership or value of a structure
- It does not make a project conform to a standard: conformity comes from your whole process
:::
::::
:::::

We map structures, not people. Registers and maps of settlements go only to the contacts you name, and we never publish them without your written permission.

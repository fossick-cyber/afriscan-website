::::section{id="what" eyebrow="What it is" title="The land history nobody wrote down"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Land rarely comes with a history. A lender financing a solar plant, a company acquiring onshore assets or a plantation, or a developer choosing between candidate sites often inherits whatever happened on the land before: a village that grew into the site, clearing that began after a permit was issued, a road that brought settlement to the concession edge.

An imagery history reconstructs what can be seen. We gather the archived satellite imagery and open datasets that cover your area, date each observation to the images either side of it, and set out what was built, cleared or changed, and roughly when. A current register of the structures on the land today, and a land-use and land-cover baseline, complete the picture at the decision date.

Where the history may be relied on later, it can be packaged as an [evidence pack](key:evidence-packs) with file fingerprints and an independent timestamp, fixing what was known at completion.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Why we write “roughly when”"}
An image shows the land on the day it was captured. A change between two images happened somewhere in between, so every date in the history is a window, not a day. The report shows the window and the sources either side of it.
:::

::::section{id="how" tone="alt" eyebrow="How it works" title="From archive search to a dated history"}
:::steps
:::step{title="Area and question" icon="map"}
You send the parcel, route or site and what the history must answer: when settlement began, when clearing happened, what stood there at a given date.
:::
:::step{title="Archive search" icon="search"}
We check which dated very-high-resolution scenes exist in commercial archives, and which open datasets cover the area, before scoping the work.
:::
:::step{title="Reconstruct" icon="history"}
Reviewers work through the dated imagery in order, recording what appears, disappears or changes, alongside year-by-year trends in built-up land and land cover from open datasets.
:::
:::step{title="Now, and the record" icon="file-check"}
We add the current state: a register of structures on the land and within any corridor or hazard zone. The history and register can be packaged as an evidence pack.
:::
:::

Open datasets used are credited as their licences require, including Google Open Buildings (CC BY 4.0), ESA WorldCover (CC BY 4.0) and Copernicus Sentinel data. See [imagery and data sources](/imagery#open-data).
::::

::::section{id="deliverables" eyebrow="What you receive" title="A history your due-diligence team can cite"}
:::checklist
- **A dated timeline** of what was built, cleared or changed, each entry with its date window and sources
- **Before-and-after views** for the key changes, from the dated imagery named in the report
- **A land-use and land-cover baseline** at the decision date: built-up land, cropland, bare ground, water and tree cover
- **Settlement growth trends** around the site or route, as an area-level picture
- **A current structure register** for the land, any right of way or boundary, and any hazard zone
- **Archive scenes** used in the analysis, delivered as imagery where the supplier's terms allow
- **An evidence pack**, where scoped, and a PDF report in English or Portuguese with GIS layers
:::

::::section{id="limits" tone="alt" eyebrow="Limits" title="What an imagery history is not"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### What it shows
:::checklist
- What dated imagery shows on the land over time, and roughly when it changed
- Where settlement and clearing have grown around the site
- What stands on the land at the decision date
:::
::::
::::col
### What it does not show
:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Title, ownership, tenure, valuation or liability
- A compliance determination under any regulation
- Anything in periods or places the archive does not cover
- Who built, cleared or used the land
:::

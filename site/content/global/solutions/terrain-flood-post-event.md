::::section{id="what" eyebrow="What it is" title="Where the ground works against your asset"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Linear assets cross everything: hills, gullies, wetlands and rivers that flood in the rainy season. The stretches where the ground works against you are where erosion undermines towers and pipe cover, where access tracks wash out, and where settlement on low ground is most exposed. In southern Africa, cyclones and floods add another question every few years: what has been damaged along the route, and where should crews go first?

Terrain and flood screening answers the first question before you build or before the rainy season. From elevation data and historical surface-water records we highlight the steep, erosion-prone, drainage-crossing and historically flooded stretches of your route or site, and list the structures and route segments inside them.

Post-event mapping answers the second. After a flood or cyclone, radar shows the flooded area even under cloud, and a reviewer compares imagery captured before and after the event to check structures and assets along your route.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="What “historically flooded” means"}
Seen as water in past satellite records. It is a screening signal for planning and preparedness, not a flood line, a return period or a hydraulic result.
:::

::::section{id="how" tone="alt" eyebrow="How it works" title="Before you build, and after an event"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Terrain and flood screening" icon="layers" eyebrow="Planning and preparedness"}
- **Slope and erosion:** steep and erosion-prone stretches from elevation data, with drone elevation models for detail where flown.
- **Drainage crossings:** where the route crosses concentrated flow paths and river channels.
- **Flood exposure:** structures, route segments and sites in historically flooded, low-lying or river-crossing ground.
- **Output:** the stretches to look at first, by chainage, in the same register as your structures.
:::
:::card{title="Post-event mapping" icon="water" eyebrow="After a cyclone or flood"}
- **Flood extent:** from radar imagery captured after the event, which shows larger flooded areas even under cloud.
- **Before and after:** a reviewer compares dated optical or drone imagery from before and after the event for structures and assets along your route or site.
- **Output:** a map of the flooded area and a list of visible changes to check on the ground, by chainage.
- **Timing:** depends on when usable imagery captured after the event exists.
:::
:::

Radar also supports screening between optical surveys in the rainy season, showing larger ground changes, such as clearing and earthworks, even under cloud.
::::

::::section{id="deliverables" eyebrow="What you receive" title="Screening your engineers and crews can act on"}
:::checklist
- **A terrain and water table** by route segment or site zone: slope class, erosion-prone ground, drainage crossings and flood exposure
- **Structures and segments in exposed ground**, listed from the same register as your encroachment survey
- **After an event:** a flooded-area map and a reviewer's before-and-after list of visible changes, by chainage
- **A PDF report** in English or Portuguese and **GIS layers** in GeoPackage, GeoJSON, KMZ and Shapefile

Every proposal names the services it includes. These are the ones this solution draws on.

### What it gives you
:::checklist
- The stretches of a route or site to look at first for slope, erosion, drainage and flood exposure
- The flooded area after an event, and visible changes along your route
- A shared register with your structure survey
:::

### What it is not
:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Not a hydraulic model, an official flood line or an insurance rating
- Not a geotechnical study or engineering design
- Not a structural assessment or a loss adjustment: visible damage only
- Not an emergency or early-warning service
:::

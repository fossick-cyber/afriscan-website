faq:
  - q: Is drone imagery always better than satellite imagery?
    a: It is more detailed, and its date is easier to control. But a drone covers far less ground per day, needs permits and site access, and cannot show what was there last year. For a long route, satellite imagery gives the whole picture and the history; the drone adds detail where the satellite picture raises a question.
  - q: Can satellite imagery show individual houses?
    a: Very-high-resolution satellite imagery shows individual houses, outbuildings and cleared plots in most rural settings, and our open segmentation models are built for imagery of about 50 cm per pixel or finer. Small structures, dense villages and homesteads under trees are harder, which is where a reviewer marks by hand or a drone flight adds detail.
  - q: What happens in the rainy season?
    a: Optical satellite and drone imagery cannot see through cloud. Satellite archives have fewer usable scenes in cloudy months, and drones cannot fly in rain or strong wind. Radar satellites can show larger ground changes, such as clearing and earthworks, even under rainy-season cloud, and the flagged areas are checked on optical imagery or by drone when conditions allow.
  - q: We already fly our own drones. Do we need anything else?
    a: Possibly not. Send your georeferenced orthophotos and we run the same structure, change and corridor analysis on them. Satellite imagery is then useful for the stretches and dates your flights do not cover, and for the history before your programme started.

## The short answer {#short-answer}

| Question | Satellite scenes | Drone orthophotos | Your own imagery | Web-map basemaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Whole routes and networks, concessions, districts | Stretches and sites, flown in line-of-sight legs | Whatever you fly or buy | Wide, but for screening only |
| **Detail** | Houses, outbuildings, cleared plots, larger excavations | Adds small structures, fresh digging, materials and the layout of a compound | Depends on its resolution | Varies; not survey imagery |
| **Date** | The archive's dates, or a requested window | The day you fly | As captured | Unknown |
| **The past** | **Yes**: archive scenes can show last year, or five years ago | No | Only from when you started | No usable date |
| **Permits and access** | No drone permit and no site visit | Aviation, site and, in some countries, survey and data authorisations | Your own | None, and no rights to deliver it |
| **Weather** | Needs cloud-free days | Cannot fly in rain or strong wind; cannot see through cloud | As captured | Unknown |

The row most people overlook is the past. Only a satellite archive can show what stood beside your line before anyone thought to look, which is often the most valuable single picture in a dispute or a resettlement programme.

## Six questions that decide the plan {#questions}

:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="How long is the route, or how big the area?" icon="route"}
A drone covers a pipeline in short legs within the pilot's line of sight, with time on site for each. A satellite scene covers the whole route at once. For a network of hundreds of kilometres, satellite imagery is the only realistic baseline; the drone is for the stretches the baseline flags. Very long networks are split into sections so each gets imagery fine enough to see individual buildings.
:::
:::step{title="How small is the thing you need to see?" icon="search"}
Houses, outbuildings, cleared plots, new tracks and larger excavations show on very-high-resolution satellite imagery. Small sheds, fresh trenches, stacked materials, a fence line or the layout of one compound usually need a drone orthophoto, at a few centimetres to a few tens of centimetres per pixel. Coarser open satellite data, such as Copernicus Sentinel, shows larger patterns only: vegetation, land-cover change, burnt ground.
:::
:::step{title="Does one date matter?" icon="calendar"}
For a resettlement cut-off date, a handover or an incident, the date is the point. A drone flown on the day gives the most control, subject to the permits and authorisations each job requires. Satellite scenes fall on the dates satellites captured cloud-free images; a new capture falls somewhere in a requested window and is never guaranteed. Bracketing the date with a scene before and one after is often the strongest plan. See [dated imagery for cut-off dates](/insights/dated-imagery-cut-off-dates).
:::
:::step{title="What season will it be?" icon="sun"}
Rainy seasons thin out usable satellite scenes and ground drones for days at a time. Tall crops and full leaf cover hide low structures and bare ground. Where timing is flexible, a dry-season capture gives the clearest picture; where it is not, radar screening can show larger changes under cloud until optical imagery or a drone can confirm them.
:::
:::step{title="What do permits and access allow?" icon="shield"}
Drone surveys need the operator's aviation approvals and permissions for each site, and in Mozambique a separate Defence authorisation for the survey and another to hand over its data. Around plant, key points and military areas, flights are restricted or prohibited. In insecure areas, a team on site may not be sensible at all. Satellite imagery needs no site visit. The rules in each country are compared in [drone rules by country](/drone-regulations).
:::
:::step{title="What imagery do you already hold?" icon="layers"}
Many utilities and mines fly their own drones, and many projects bought imagery for their ESIA. If it is georeferenced, it can be analysed the same way as ours, and the plan only has to fill the gaps in area and date.
:::
:::

## Plans that work {#plans}

:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="An existing pipeline or line network" icon="pipeline" eyebrow="Satellite, then drone checks"}
A satellite baseline of the whole network, rated per 500 m for encroachment density, then scheduled re-surveys that flag new structures. Drone checks go to the stretches where the register shows the most change, or where the imagery cannot settle what is there.
:::
:::card{title="A new route or loop" icon="route" eyebrow="Satellite for options, drone for the chosen line"}
Structure counts along each route option from satellite imagery, so the choice is made on the same numbers. Once the alignment is fixed, a drone orthophoto and elevation survey of the corridor, subject to permits, supports design and the land process.
:::
:::card{title="A resettlement footprint" icon="calendar" eyebrow="Archive history, then a dated record"}
Dated archive scenes to show the footprint before the census, a record as close to the cut-off date as the imagery plan allows, and re-surveys afterwards. A drone flight on the cut-off date where the permits allow.
:::
:::card{title="A concession boundary" icon="boundary" eyebrow="Satellite ring, drone on hot spots"}
Structures and ground disturbance inside the boundary and in a ring around it from satellite imagery, with drone detail where excavations or new settlement cluster.
:::
:::

## What neither can do {#limits}

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Neither optical satellite nor drone imagery sees through cloud, and neither sees at night
- Neither sees under dense canopy, inside buildings or underground
- Neither shows who lives in a structure, who owns it or whether it is authorised
- No date for a new satellite capture can be promised in advance
- Neither replaces a cadastral survey: drone positions without ground control are good to a few metres, not to a boundary surveyor's standard
:::

Whatever the imagery, a person reviews every result before delivery, the report names the imagery and its capture date, and anything the imagery cannot settle goes on a ground-check list. Web-map basemaps are used only to screen and plan; they are never delivered and never the basis of a dated record. The detail is in the [methodology](/methodology#imagery).

## In each country {#countries}

- **Mozambique.** Drone surveys need IACM approval for the flight, the Minister of National Defence's authorisation for the survey and a separate authorisation to release the data, and publishing orthophotos without authorisation is an infraction under Lei n.º 6/2024. Satellite-first plans are the norm, with drones where the detail justifies the permissions. See the [Mozambique drone guide](/mz/drone-regulations).
- **South Africa.** Commercial drone work needs a UASOC and an Air Service Licence, with landowner permission for every flight. Many utilities and mines already fly, so analysing their own imagery, with satellite between flights, is often the first step. See the [South Africa drone guide](/za/drone-regulations).
- **Nigeria.** Commercial drone work needs an NCAA operator certificate with ONSA security clearance built in, and flights over populated areas need specific approval. Satellite imagery covers the long rights of way; drones go where access and permissions allow. See the [Nigeria drone guide](/ng/drone-regulations).

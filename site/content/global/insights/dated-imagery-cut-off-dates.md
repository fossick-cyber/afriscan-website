## What the standard asks for {#standard}

[IFC Performance Standard 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf) (2012) expects a census where involuntary resettlement is unavoidable, to "identify the persons who will be displaced by the project, determine who will be eligible for compensation and assistance, and discourage ineligible persons, such as opportunistic settlers, from claiming benefits" (para. 12). "In the absence of host government procedures, the client will establish a cut-off date for eligibility", and information about it "will be well documented and disseminated throughout the project area".

Paragraph 23 gives the date its practical force: "The client is not required to compensate or assist those who encroach on the project area after the cut-off date for eligibility, provided the cut-off date has been clearly established and made public."

[Guidance Note 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-ps-guidance-note-5-en.pdf) fills in the detail (GN32):

- "The date of completion of the census and assets inventory represents a cut-off date."
- The loss of fixed assets "(such as built structures, crops, fruit trees, and woodlots) established after the cut-off date should not be compensated".
- "Historic" cut-off dates, set when a project was ready and then left to age through delays, are a known complication: new households formed by natural growth from eligible households "are to be considered eligible".
- Where the government process was not adequately documented and disseminated, or the census is outdated or inadequate, "additional study is required".

GN33 adds that restrictions on building and farming after a cut-off date can be a hardship, and that the time between the cut-off date and compensation "should be limited".

:::callout{tone="legal" title="What the standard does not say"}
Neither Performance Standard 5 nor its Guidance Note mentions satellite or drone imagery. A dated imagery record is a method a project can choose to support its census, its cut-off-date documentation and its grievance process. It is not an IFC requirement, and it never replaces the census or the asset inventory. National procedures may set the date and the process, for example Mozambique's resettlement regulation for economic activities ([Decreto n.º 31/2012](/mz/resettlement-cut-off-date#regulation)).
:::

## Where imagery helps, and where it does not {#where-it-helps}

Read against GN32, the census and the cut-off date raise four questions a project will be asked later. Imagery answers two of them well.

| The question | Can dated imagery answer it? |
|---|---|
| What structures stood in the footprint around the cut-off date? | **Yes**, for structures visible from above, on the dates the imagery was captured |
| What has been built since, and where? | **Yes**, by comparing later dated surveys with the baseline, confirmed by a reviewer |
| Who lived in or used each structure, and are they eligible? | **No.** That is the census, the inventory and the eligibility process |
| Was the cut-off date well documented and made public? | **No.** That is the project's consultation and disclosure record |

The first two are exactly the questions that turn into disputes years later, when the people who did the census have moved on and the only record is a spreadsheet. A register tied to dated imagery lets anyone reopen the picture.

## What "dated" should mean {#what-dated-means}

An image's date has to be the date the pixels were captured, not the date a file was made or published. Before imagery is used for a cut-off record, check which kind of date you have:

:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Satellite scenes" icon="satellite" eyebrow="Acquisition date in the metadata"}
A purchased very-high-resolution scene comes with its acquisition date and time, cloud cover and resolution. Keep the metadata file with the imagery; it is the evidence of the date.
:::
:::card{title="Drone orthophotos" icon="drone" eyebrow="Capture time in each photo"}
Each photo carries its capture time, and the processing report shows which photos made the mosaic. Flown on a chosen day, subject to the approvals and security clearances the survey requires.
:::
:::card{title="Web basemaps and footprints" icon="x-circle" eyebrow="No usable date"}
Web-map layers have no stated date and are often stitched from several. Open building-footprint datasets come from imagery that may be years old. Useful for planning; never for a dated record.
:::
:::

**Record, for every image in the baseline:** the platform or sensor, the acquisition date, the resolution, the cloud visible over the footprint, how the image was georeferenced, and a fingerprint of each delivered file so later copies can be shown to match. A register that cannot say which image each structure was seen on is much weaker than one that can.

## Choosing imagery around the date {#choosing}

The census takes weeks; imagery is captured on single days. So the practical question is which dates the imagery plan can reach, and how the gap between those dates and the census is described.

:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Start with the archive" icon="history"}
Check which dated scenes already exist over the footprint, and when. In many places there will be scenes from before the census window, sometimes from inside it. Archive coverage, dates and cloud vary a great deal from place to place, especially through a rainy season.
:::
:::step{title="Bracket the date" icon="compare"}
A scene before the cut-off date and one after it is often more useful than one scene near it. A structure visible on both was there across the date. A structure visible only on the later scene appeared in the interval, and goes on the list for the census team to settle in the field.
:::
:::step{title="Fix a date where one matters" icon="calendar"}
Where a single day matters, a drone survey gives the most control over the date, subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. A new satellite capture can be requested for a window, but its date depends on satellite availability and weather and is never guaranteed.
:::
:::step{title="Write the tolerance into the method" icon="file-text"}
State in the RAP methodology or census protocol how the imagery dates relate to the cut-off date, for example "structures visible on imagery captured 12 days before the census closed". A stated, reproducible gap is defensible; an unstated one invites argument.
:::
:::

## What the register holds, and how census teams use it {#register}

A structure register built for a cut-off date lists structures, not households. Each one has an ID, coordinates, its position in the footprint, a photo crop and the imagery it was seen on, and, where scoped, a category the imagery can show, such as main building, outbuilding or livestock enclosure. A person reviews every entry; anything the imagery cannot settle is listed for a ground check rather than guessed.

That makes it a working tool for the census rather than a rival to it:

- **Before fieldwork**, it tells the census team how many structures to expect, where they cluster and how to split the work.
- **During fieldwork**, enumerators can match each structure they visit to a register ID, so the census and the imagery record point at the same building.
- **After the cut-off**, the same IDs carry through each re-survey, so a new structure is recorded with the date it was first seen and the image it was first seen on.

## The limits a register should state {#limits}

:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Occupancy, use, ownership, tenure or value
- Seasonal users who leave no structure, such as the herders GN32 asks projects to consider
- Structures under dense canopy or hidden by larger roofs
- Very recent work, such as foundations or a cleared plot, which can be hard to read from above
- The exact day a structure was built: only that it appeared between two image dates
- Anything on a day with no imagery, or under cloud
:::
::::
::::col
**Historic cut-off dates.** Where a project has stalled, a series of dated images can show how the footprint has changed since the original census: new structures within existing compounds, and new clusters. It cannot say which new structures belong to households formed by natural growth, which GN32 treats as eligible, and which do not. That is for an updated census.

**Alignment.** Two images of the same place are never perfectly aligned. An offset of a few metres can make one structure look like a removed one and a new one, which is why every change is confirmed by a reviewer on both images before it enters the record.
::::
:::::

## After the cut-off date {#after}

GN33's point about hardship cuts both ways: the longer the gap between the cut-off date and compensation, the more the footprint changes, and the harder the original record is to apply. Scheduled re-surveys of the footprint during that gap show what has been built, where and roughly when, and let the project act early and consistently rather than all at once when works begin.

Where a record may be relied on later in a grievance, a negotiation or a legal process, the imagery, register and maps can be packaged as an [evidence pack](key:evidence-packs) with a fingerprint of each file and an independent timestamp. That supports legal and community processes; it does not decide them.

## A checklist for resettlement teams {#checklist}

:::checklist
- The cut-off date, how it was set, and the census window around it
- The imagery plan: which dated scenes exist, what a drone survey or a new capture could add, and the realistic dates each can reach
- The tolerance between imagery dates and the cut-off date, written into the method
- Imagery metadata and file fingerprints kept with the register
- One set of structure IDs shared by the register, the census and every re-survey
- A ground-check list for anything the imagery cannot settle
- A re-survey schedule for the period between the cut-off date and compensation
- The national rules that apply where the project is, from its country site: [DR Congo](/cd/resettlement-cut-off-date), [Ghana](/gh/resettlement-compensation-baselines), [Kenya](/ke/resettlement-cut-off-date), [Mozambique](/mz/resettlement-cut-off-date), [Nigeria](/ng/esia-support), [South Africa](/za/esia-baselines), [Tanzania](/tz/valuation-cut-off-baselines), [Uganda](/ug/rap-cut-off-date-baselines), [Zambia](/zm/resettlement-baselines), or the [country site](/countries) for the others
:::

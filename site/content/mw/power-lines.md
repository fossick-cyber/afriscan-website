::::section{id="the-law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The Electricity Act" title="What the law says about building in a wayleave"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part VIII of the Electricity Act (Cap. 73:01), "Rights over land", gives a licensee rights over land for its lines.

- **Section 39, wayleaves over land.** A licensee may place lines over land. Where consent is unreasonably withheld, the energy regulator, MERA, may consent instead and set compensation "by way of payment of a lump sum or an annual rental".
- **Section 41, trees and structures.** Trees or undergrowth that obstruct a line may be cut or trimmed after "not less than fourteen days' notice". "Except with the permission of the licensee, no person shall erect any building or structure in the wayleave." For a structure put up after the line was built, "the licensee may request such a person to remove or adjust" it and, if the person does not, "may apply to the Authority for an order for the removal or adjustment".

Two facts therefore matter for each structure: where it stands against the wayleave, and whether it was there before the line. A register measured to the wayleave edge answers the first. For the second, the register's imagery dates, compared with scenes from before construction where the archive has them, give your wayleave officers and, where it comes to that, MERA a dated record to work from.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Check the amendment"}
An Electricity (Amendment) Act was published on 31 May 2024. Its commencement and its effect on sections 39 and 41 were not confirmed for this page, so check them with your counsel. This page summarises public rules for information, as read on 27 September 2026. It is not legal advice.

::::section{id="existing-lines" eyebrow="Existing lines" title="A register of the wayleave, segment by segment, then what changed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We buffer the line you supply at the wayleave width for each section and list each structure the review confirms, with its distance to the line, its distance to the wayleave edge, its chainage and its coordinates. Each 500 m segment is rated high, medium or low for **encroachment density** by a count rule, so officers know which stretches to visit first. The rating is not a safety or clearance rating.

Engagement campaigns work best when they come with a map of where the new structures are. Because the register is sorted by segment and chainage, it can be read against the Traditional Authority and Group Village Head areas you work with, so each meeting starts from that area's own list.

After the baseline, the line is re-surveyed on a schedule agreed with you. New and removed structures are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views, and your team receives a notice after each survey. New tracks, cleared ground and fresh digging near poles and towers are flagged the same way, for a check on the ground. Around the substations you name, the line becomes a boundary: we list what stands inside the site and in a ring around it.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="new-lines" tone="alt" eyebrow="New lines and interconnectors" title="Count the homes on each route, then fix the census baseline"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A 400 kV interconnector from Mozambique was energised in August 2026, and interconnectors to Zambia and to Tanzania are at feasibility stage while the grid inside the country is reinforced. Each new line needs a route, a wayleave and a resettlement plan.

Resettlement plans for new lines census the households, structures and trees in the wayleave. We compare the structures along each route option within the same widths, so the choice can weigh the land it affects. Once the route is fixed, we produce the structure layer as a reviewed, dated GIS file that the census team can start from and that later surveys are compared with. It supports the census and asset inventory; it does not replace them.

Between the census and energisation, years can pass. For the recent 400 kV line the census ran in April and May 2019 and the line was energised in August 2026; a comparison against the cut-off-date layer shows what has appeared in the wayleave since.

Lines that cross the border continue into Mozambique, where the servitude rules differ: see [power lines in Mozambique](/mz/power-lines).
::::
::::col

::::section{id="how" tone="alt" eyebrow="How it works" title="From line file to reviewed wayleave register"}
:::steps
:::step{title="Agree the widths"}
You send the line, pole or tower positions if you have them, the voltage, the wayleave width for each section and what the register is for. The written proposal fixes the widths, the imagery and the deliverables.
:::
:::step{title="Screen the whole line"}
Structures are mapped along the full length from dated satellite imagery, open building datasets or imagery you already hold, with no flight over the line.
:::
:::step{title="Add detail where it is needed"}
Spans that need a closer look are flown by drone, subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires, with the Surveyor General's 30-day notice built into the plan.
:::
:::step{title="Review, measure and deliver"}
A person checks every result. Each structure is measured to the line and the wayleave edge, each segment is rated, and the files go to the contacts you name.
:::

:::checklist

### What we don't do

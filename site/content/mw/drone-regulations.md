The Civil Aviation Act 2017, in force since 1 September 2020, still forbids operating any aircraft in Malawi without a certificate, licence or other authorisation from the Malawi Civil Aviation Authority (MCAA), so each commercial drone operation needs MCAA's written authorisation, decided case by case. Survey flights also bring in the Land Survey Act, with 30 days' notice to the Surveyor General before an aerial survey and a deposit of the photographs and data within 30 days after it, and the Land Survey Regulations 2020, which register drones used in survey and mapping work. This guide sets out what to check before a drone team works on your wayleave, reserve or site.

faq:
  - q: Does satellite screening need MCAA authorisation or the Surveyor General's notice?
    a: >-
      Satellite screening involves no flights over your site, so no aircraft is operated in Malawi and the
      Civil Aviation Act authorisation does not come into it. The Land Survey Act is less clear-cut: it
      defines "survey" as "every type of land survey, including cadastral surveys and aerial surveys" (s.2)
      but does not define "aerial survey", so whether the s.42 notice and deposit reach the analysis of
      satellite imagery is a question for your counsel. Every AfriScan proposal for Malawian work flags it.
  - q: Is a small drone exempt?
    a: >-
      No published rule exempts it. The Act's definition of "aircraft" covers "any machine that can derive
      support in the atmosphere from the reactions of the air", which takes in multirotor and fixed-wing
      drones, and s.43(2) applies to operating "an aircraft". Exemptions under s.79 can be granted only on
      an application by an operator, a licensee, a certificate holder or the holder of an authorisation,
      for at most six months, so they are not a route for a first-time drone applicant.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly drone surveys in Malawi?
    a: >-
      In principle, yes, with MCAA's authorisation for each operation. A foreign-registered aircraft may be
      operated in Malawi (s.43(1)(a)), and MCAA may validate a certificate, licence or authorisation issued
      by another State (s.43(5)), but it may also refuse on the basis of public interest (s.43(7)).
      Registering an aircraft in Malawi is limited to Malawian citizens, permanent residents, companies
      organised under Malawian law whose aircraft are based and mainly used in Malawi, and government (2013
      registration regulations, reg. 4).
  - q: How far ahead does a drone survey need to be planned?
    a: >-
      At least 30 days for the Surveyor General's notice (Land Survey Act, s.42(1)). MCAA publishes no form
      or timeline for drone authorisations, so ask it early, in writing. After the flights, the deposit of
      photographs, plans and data is due within 30 days of completion (s.42(2)).
  - q: Who receives the survey photographs and data?
    a: >-
      The Surveyor General, as well as you. Within 30 days of completing an aerial survey, the surveyor
      deposits one printed copy of every photograph taken for it, with any plan made from it, and copies of
      all data and subsequent maps and plans (s.42(2)). Agree in the contract who makes the deposit and how
      personal data in the imagery is handled.
  - q: Can drone imagery of our site appear on your website?
    a: >-
      Only with your written permission. We publish no imagery of State, military or police sites, and our
      public sample is from a high-pressure gas pipeline in Mozambique.

::::section{id="no-rules" eyebrow="The regulatory gap" title="No drone regulations, so authorisation is case by case"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
MCAA's [RPAS and Drones](https://www.caa.gov.mw/rpas-and-drones/) page is empty, its Legislations and Exemptions pages say "No documents found", and its [Regulations](https://www.caa.gov.mw/regulations/) page lists only the 2013 Aviation Regulations and the Civil Aviation (Security) Regulations 2024 (read 27 September 2026). The Ministry of Transport and Public Works' civil aviation plan put it plainly: "There are no regulations covering the operation of remotely piloted aircraft systems (commonly known as drones), and these should be prepared urgently" ([NTMP Civil Aviation Sub-Sectoral Plan](https://www.transport.gov.mw/index.php/resource-centre/downloads/strategic-plan?download=18:ntmp-aviation-sub-sectoral-plan), §5.5). That plan predates MCAA, and MCAA's regulations list shows the gap is still open.

MCAA told Parliament's Transport and Public Infrastructure Committee in July 2024 that it had drafted regulations. Whether they cover drones is not public, and none covering drones had been published by 27 September 2026. The Act says the Authority "shall publish" the rules, orders and directives it makes (s.89(3)), so any drone rules it makes must be published.

The Aviation Regulations 2013, saved by s.96(3) of the 2017 Act "until replaced", do not fill the gap. The Aerial Work Regulations cover agricultural operations, rotorcraft external loads, glider and banner towing, film and television work, fish spotting and news and traffic reporting; the word "unmanned" appears in the 2013 set only for free balloons.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="warn" title="What that means in practice"}
- No published drone application form or timeline
- No published limits on height, line of sight or night flying for drones
- A written MCAA authorisation for each operation, applied for directly
- MCAA's Service Charter (December 2025) lists aircraft registration and ad-hoc flight clearances, with no entry for drones

::::section{id="caa" tone="alt" eyebrow="The Civil Aviation Act 2017" title="What the Act itself says"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Scope {#scope-of-the-act}

The Act defines "aircraft" as "any machine that can derive support in the atmosphere from the reactions of the air other than the reactions of the air against the earth's surface" (s.2). That covers multirotor and fixed-wing drones, and the Act's definition of "accident" mentions "the case of an unmanned aircraft". "Aerial work" is an operation using an aircraft "for specialized services such as agriculture, construction, photography, surveying, observation and patrol".

### Authorisation {#authorisation}

- **s.43(1):** no person may operate a civil aircraft in Malawi unless it is registered in Malawi or under the laws of another State, or carry out civil aviation activities that need a certificate, licence or other authorisation without one.
- **s.43(2):** "A person shall not operate an aircraft in the territory of Malawi for any other purpose save for military, police, customs or immigration operations, except under the authority of, and in accordance with, a certificate, licence or other authorization issued by the Authority."
- **s.43(3)–(4):** applications are made "in the prescribed manner".
- **s.43(5):** the Authority may validate a certificate, licence or authorisation issued by another State.
- **s.43(7):** it may refuse for non-compliance "or on the basis of public interest".
::::
::::col
### Exemptions are for existing holders {#exemptions}

MCAA may grant a written exemption "on an application by an operator, a licencee or a certificate holder, or a holder of any authorization", for at most six months, and none may "compromise aviation safety and security standards" (s.79). The Minister may also exempt by Gazette notice on MCAA's recommendation (s.79(3)). A first-time drone applicant therefore starts with an authorisation, not an exemption.

### Aircraft registration {#registration}

The Aviation (Aircraft Registration and Marking) Regulations 2013 forbid operating a civil aircraft "eligible for registration" unless it is registered (reg. 3), and limit registration to Malawian citizens, permanent residents, corporations organised and doing business under Malawian law whose aircraft are based and primarily used in Malawi, and government bodies (reg. 4). How MCAA applies this to small drones is not published: ask it in writing.

### Liability {#liability}

Damage caused "by an aircraft in flight, during take-off or landing" is recoverable from the owner "without proof of negligence or intention" (s.80(2)). Third-party liability insurance naming the project belongs in every drone contract.

::::section{id="survey-law" eyebrow="Survey law" title="The Surveyor General: notice, deposit and a registered drone"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Land Survey Act (Cap. 59:03), s.42, "Aerial and topographic survey and topographic mapping":**

1. Notice in writing to the Surveyor General "at least thirty days before the survey is carried out", for "an aerial survey of any land in Malaŵi" (s.42(1)).
2. Within 30 days of completion, "one printed copy of every photograph taken for the purpose of such aerial survey together with one copy of any plan made as a result of the survey; and … copies of all data and subsequent maps and plans" (s.42(2)).
3. A surveyor's topographical mapping data go to the Surveyor General "for quality control and archiving" (s.42(3)).
4. Failing to give the notice or make the deposit is an offence (s.42(6)).

"Survey" means "every type of land survey, including cadastral surveys and aerial surveys" (s.2).

**Land Survey Regulations 2020 (GN 49 of 2020):**

- **reg. 100(1):** "Every GNSS receiver, including Drones, shall be registered with the Surveyor General before it may be used for measurement in any survey and mapping work."
- **reg. 100(2) and 101:** the Surveyor General checks the receiver, software and data collection, downloading and processing methods, specifies the survey types allowed and tests the equipment "before granting a registration number".
- **Schedule note:** it repeats the 30-day notice and says "The surveyor general shall check any aerial image for quality control before use."
- **regs 92–93:** in customary-estate demarcation, "Orthophoto or aerial, satellite and large scale topographic maps approved by the Surveyor General" are used to identify features, and parcel boundaries are marked on them.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Two open points"}
- The Land Survey (Amendment) Act 2022 came into force on 1 July 2022 (GN 25 of 2022). Its text was not reviewed for this guide, so whether it changed s.42 is for your counsel to confirm.
- The Act does not define "aerial survey". Whether s.42 reaches the analysis of purchased satellite imagery is also for your counsel.
:::

:::callout{tone="scope" title="What our registers are not"}
A structure register built from imagery is a monitoring and planning record, not a survey plan, and it is not Surveyor General-approved imagery for demarcation. Boundary and cadastral work is for a land surveyor.

::::section{id="limits" tone="alt" eyebrow="Operating limits" title="Line of sight, height and night: no published Malawian rule"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
No published Malawian rule sets visual-line-of-sight, beyond-visual-line-of-sight, night or height limits for drones.

The only precedent is the humanitarian drone-testing corridor at Kasungu, launched in June 2017 under a specific permission from the then Department of Civil Aviation, with a "maximum distance of 80km, altitude limit at 400 metres above ground" from Kasungu airfield. It was a controlled test corridor, not a general rule, and its current status is not confirmed.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="How we plan flights until rules exist"}
Daytime flights within the pilot's visual line of sight, at or below 120 m (400 ft) above ground, away from aerodromes and protected places, unless MCAA authorises otherwise in writing. That is an assumption from international (ICAO) practice, not Malawian law. Beyond-visual-line-of-sight work needs a specific MCAA authorisation.

::::section{id="zones" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="Where not to fly without extra clearance"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Aerodromes" icon="flag"}
Coordinate with MCAA air traffic services near Kamuzu International (Lilongwe), Chileka (Blantyre), Mzuzu and Kasungu, and near the tourist aerodromes at Majete, Liwonde and Tongole. MCAA controls airspace use by "rules, orders, directives, circulars or notices" (s.33). No statutory drone distance from an aerodrome is published.
:::
:::card{title="State residences and security sites" icon="shield"}
The Minister may declare a restricted area by Gazette notice where "necessary in the interests of defence, public safety, public health or public order" and restrict or prohibit "the navigation of any aircraft" there (s.82); premises can also be declared protected places (Protected Places and Areas Act, s.4). After an April 2026 drone flight over the Vice-President's residence in Lilongwe, police said the area "is a restricted security zone" and charged the operator with criminal trespass. Keep away from State residences, barracks and police installations, and publish no imagery of them.
:::
:::card{title="Substations, power stations and lines" icon="power"}
No statutory drone buffer exists around substations, hydro power stations or energised lines. Work under the site owner's permit to work and keep well clear of energised conductors.
:::
:::card{title="Mines, pits and borders" icon="mine"}
Mining licence areas need the holder's consent. Artisanal pits are unsafe and socially sensitive ground: we do not fly over them. Avoid flights near border posts; the Lake Malawi boundary is disputed and out of scope. Malawi has no onshore oil or gas pipelines; treat fuel depots as restricted industrial sites.
:::

::::section{id="data" tone="alt" eyebrow="Privacy and personal data" title="The Data Protection Act 2024 and survey imagery"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The [Data Protection Act 2024](https://www.dpa.mw/download/data-protection-act-2024/) (Act No. 3 of 2024) was commenced by GN 40 of 2024, and MACRA acts "in its capacity as the designated Data Protection Authority". Processing includes "collection, recording, storage and publication", so orthophotos of homes and census or RAP lists can be personal data. The sections that matter for a survey:

- **s.41, registration:** anyone processing the personal information of more than 10,000 data subjects resident in Malawi, or information "of significance to the economy, society, or security of Malawi", must register; the Authority decides within 14 days (s.41(3)).
- **s.36 and s.37, breaches:** notify the Authority within 72 hours, and the people affected within 72 hours where the risk to them is high.
- **ss.38 to 40, transfers:** personal data may leave Malawi only where the recipient is bound by a law, binding corporate rules, a contractual clause, a code of conduct or a certification that gives adequate protection, or where a s.39(4) condition applies, such as informed consent. The basis is recorded (s.38(2)). Part VII sets no data-localisation requirement.
- **s.30:** data protection impact assessments.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What to agree with any mapping supplier"}
Who is the controller and who the processor; whether either party must register under s.41; where imagery and results are processed and stored, and the s.38 basis for any transfer out of Malawi; how the supplier will tell you of a breach in time for the 72-hour notice; how long files are kept; who may publish the orthophotos; and who makes the Surveyor General's deposit.
:::

This is one reason AfriScan maps land and assets, never people: a register lists structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks with their distance to the line, and carries no names.

::::section{id="offences" eyebrow="Enforcement" title="Offences"}
- **Civil Aviation Act 2017, s.64:** operating an aircraft or offering civil aviation services without a certificate, licence or authorisation, giving false information and using forged documents.
- **s.76:** dangerous flying, for the pilot and the owner, with possible suspension or cancellation of privileges.
- **s.82(4):** breaking a restricted-area order or instruction.
- **s.78:** any other breach without a set penalty, with directors also liable.
- **Land Survey Act, s.42(6):** failing to give the aerial-survey notice or make the deposit.
- **Data Protection Act 2024:** administrative penalties for unlawful processing, and the s.49 offence of publishing information known to have been obtained in breach of the Act.

Each carries penalties set in the Acts; this page quotes no amounts.

::::section{id="imports" tone="alt" eyebrow="Equipment" title="Radios and State buyers"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Radio equipment falls under MACRA's type approval and spectrum rules (Communications Act 2016, s.96). Whether a drone's controller needs its own type approval is not settled in the published material; ask MACRA.
::::
::::col
The Public Procurement Regulations 2020 list "Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS)" in their Fourth Schedule, "List of national security or defence related items". They are saved by the Public Procurement and Disposal of Public Assets Act 2025 so far as they are consistent with it, so State buyers treat drones as security items.

::::section{id="checklist" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Malawi" lead="Ask any operator, including us, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="MCAA written authorisation"}
Covering this operation: the area, dates, altitudes, aircraft and pilots, and any MCAA registration of the aircraft.
:::
:::step{title="The Surveyor General's registration and notice"}
The drone's registration number under reg. 100 of the Land Survey Regulations 2020, and a copy of the 30-day notice under s.42(1). Agree who deposits the photographs, plans and data within 30 days of completion (s.42(2)).
:::
:::step{title="Pilot credentials MCAA accepts"}
For example a foreign remote pilot licence MCAA has validated, with logbooks and training records.
:::
:::step{title="An airspace plan"}
Evidence of coordination with MCAA air traffic services near aerodromes, and no flights near protected places or State residences.
:::
:::step{title="Site permissions"}
The landowner or occupier; the Traditional Authority and Group Village Head on customary land; the utility's permit to work; the mining-licence holder.
:::
:::step{title="Third-party liability insurance"}
Naming the project, since the owner of the aircraft is strictly liable for damage on the ground (s.80(2)).
:::
:::step{title="A data clause"}
Registration status, lawful basis, storage location, the s.38 transfer basis, the 72-hour breach notice, retention and who may publish the orthophotos.
:::
:::step{title="Procedures and a risk assessment"}
Visual line of sight, clearance from energised conductors, emergency procedures and how communities along the route are told about the flights.
:::
:::step{title="A Malawian entity where one is needed"}
Registration with the Registrar General, an MRA TPIN and tax clearance, and, for public clients, the procurement registrations their tender asks for.

::::section{id="afriscan" tone="alt" eyebrow="How we work" title="AfriScan and drone work in Malawi"}

We start from satellite. Satellite screening involves no flights over your site, so it can cover a whole line, road or project area before anyone decides whether a drone is needed. Whether the Land Survey Act's notice reaches satellite-derived mapping is an open point that each Malawian proposal flags for your counsel.

Drone surveys are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. In Malawi that means written authorisation from the Malawi Civil Aviation Authority for the operation, 30 days' notice of the aerial survey to the Surveyor General, a drone registered with the Surveyor General, and the deposit of photographs and data afterwards. Every proposal that includes drone work names who will fly, lists the approvals its flights need and who applies for each, and schedules no flight before they are in place.

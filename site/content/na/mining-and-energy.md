  - q: Can you see boreholes, wells and springs?

  - q: Can you work inside a diamond restricted area or a national park?
    a: From satellite imagery, yes, because nobody enters the area. ::::section{id="consent-distances" tone="alt" eyebrow="The 100 m and 300 m rules" title="A register of what stands near your planned workings" lead="A summary of the public rules as last reviewed on 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The [Minerals (Prospecting and Mining) Act 33 of 1992](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Minerals%20(Prospecting%20and%20Mining)%20Act%2033%20of%201992.pdf) bars a licence holder from exercising its rights on cultivated land, or "within a horizontal distance of 100 metres of any spring, well, borehole, reservoir, dam, dipping-tank, waterworks, perennial stream or pan, artificially constructed watercourse, kraal, building or any structure of whatever nature", or within 300 m of a surveyed erf, "without the prior permission in writing of the owner" (s.52(1)(d)). The [Petroleum (Exploration and Production) Act 2 of 1991](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Petroleum%20(Exploration%20and%20Production)%20Act%202%20of%201991.pdf) sets the same 100 m and 300 m distances for petroleum rights (s.16(1)(b)).

Both Acts also protect public land. Under the Minerals Act, work in towns and villages, on "a proclaimed road, including such parts adjoining such road as may… be regarded as the road reserve", on aerodromes, harbours, railways and cemeteries, or on land reserved for public purposes needs the Minister's prior permission (s.52(1)(b)); under the Petroleum Act it needs the Commissioner's approval (s.16(1)(a)). On private land, the Minerals Act asks for a written compensation agreement with the owner, lodged with the Mining Commissioner, or an ancillary right (s.52(1)(a)).

**What we deliver.** We buffer the footprint of your planned workings, drill pads or plant by 100 m and 300 m, or by your own distances, and list each structure the review confirms inside each band: its ID, its distance to the workings, its coordinates and what the imagery shows, such as a dwelling, an outbuilding, a kraal or a dam. Your land team starts the permission conversation with a map instead of a guess.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What the imagery can show"}
Buildings, kraals, dams, reservoirs and cleared or cultivated ground usually show in the imagery. A borehole, a well, a spring or a dipping-tank is often too small to see from above. The register says which features it could confirm, and your field team's points can be added to the same layers.
:::

::::section{id="licence-areas" eyebrow="Licence edges and excavations" title="What changes inside your licence, and in a ring around it"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
For a mining licence, an exclusive prospecting licence or a petroleum licence, we map the structures, tracks and cleared ground inside the boundary and in a ring around it, and compare dated surveys to show what is new. Excavations, pits, spoil heaps and water-filled workings are mapped the same way, each with the first date it appears in the imagery. New tracks and access roads into the licence area are traced between dates.

Claims, the small-scale mining title, may be pegged only by Namibian citizens who hold a non-exclusive prospecting licence, or by companies whose shares only Namibian citizens may own ([Minerals Act, s.25](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Minerals%20(Prospecting%20and%20Mining)%20Act%2033%20of%201992.pdf)). A pit near your boundary may sit on a lawful claim. We map the ground and its changes; whether a working is authorised is for you and the Mining Commissioner to establish, and we never identify the people working there.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="rehabilitation" tone="alt" eyebrow="Uranium and open-pit mines" title="Construction, stockpiles and rehabilitation, dated"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Uranium mining in the Erongo region, and quarries and mines elsewhere in the country, move a great deal of ground. We track it between dates:

- **Rehabilitation and revegetation** on rehabilitated land and closed borrow pits, compared between dated scenes;
- **Construction and earthworks progress** from dated drone orthophotos and elevation models, with maps of where ground has been cut or filled between visits;
- **Stockpile and earthworks volumes** from drone elevation models, subject to the approvals and security clearances each flight requires;
- **Structures in the zones your engineers define**, such as blast and exclusion zones, each listed with its distance to the zone edge.

If your survey team already flies the mine, send the orthophotos and elevation models and we run the same analysis on them.
::::

::::section{id="green-hydrogen" eyebrow="Energy and green hydrogen" title="Dated baselines for onshore sites, routes and concessions"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Namibia's energy projects, from offshore discoveries with onshore supply bases to solar plants and green hydrogen in the south, all start with land: routes to agree, concessions to manage and a record of what stood there before construction. We provide:

- **A land-use and land-cover baseline** of built-up land, cropland, bare ground, water and vegetation around the site, combined with the structure register;
- **An imagery history** of the site and its routes from archived satellite scenes: what was built, cleared or changed, and roughly when;
- **Route option comparisons** for the pipelines, water lines and transmission lines a new plant needs, counting the structures each alignment would affect;
- **Boundary, track and construction progress** between dated surveys, with a notice after each one;
- **Host-town growth** in towns such as Lüderitz, Walvis Bay and Oranjemund, from year-by-year settlement growth trends.

The southern green-hydrogen concessions lie inside Tsau ǁKhaeb National Park, where a drone flight needs approval and filming needs MEFT approval, so the baseline starts from satellite imagery.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Environmental clearance"}
The Environmental Management Act 7 of 2007 lists activities that need an Environmental Clearance Certificate from the Environmental Commissioner, among them "the transmission and supply of electricity", mining and quarrying, "oil, water, gas and petrochemical and other bulk supply pipelines", public roads, railways and harbours, and communication networks ([GN 29 of 2012](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/2012/4878.pdf)). We supply dated layers your ESIA consultant can use in the assessment and in monitoring.

For the Southern Corridor, a Strategic Environmental and Social Assessment for green energy production and industrialisation was put out for public comment until 4 September 2026 ([Namibia Green Hydrogen Programme](https://gh2namibia.com/sesa-findings-ready-for-public-review/)).
:::

We found no statutory protection strip for pipelines in Namibia: send the widths your servitude agreements or standards use. A draft Downstream Gas Bill published by the Electricity Control Board in August 2026 is not law.

::::section{id="land" tone="alt" eyebrow="Land rights and project resettlement" title="A dated record of what stood in the footprint"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
In Namibia "resettlement" usually means the land-reform programme. Here we mean project land acquisition. We found no national law on project-induced resettlement: displacement runs through the sector Acts, the Expropriation Ordinance 13 of 1978 and the lender's standard, such as the World Bank's ESS5 or, on IFC- and Equator-financed projects, IFC Performance Standard 5.

On communal land, nobody may occupy or use land "other than under a right acquired in accordance with the provisions of this Act" (s.43), land may be withdrawn from a communal area only after the rights in it "have first been acquired by the State and just compensation for the acquisition of such rights is paid" (s.16), and a land board may grant occupational land rights for government and State-owned-enterprise projects (s.36A) ([Communal Land Reform Act 5 of 2002](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Communal%20Land%20Reform%20Act%205%20of%202002.pdf)). A [Land Bill](https://laws.parliament.na/Public/Bills/Details/c99ac196-dc91-4b0d-bf06-371fe7f75d88) that would replace the communal and commercial land reform Acts is before Parliament; the current Acts still apply.

A [cut-off-date baseline](key:resettlement-cut-off-baselines) records the structures in your footprint on that date, with IDs, coordinates and imagery, and shows what has appeared since. It supports, and never replaces, your census, asset inventory and valuation. For a single disputed point, a location check puts the dated images of it on one sheet.

::::section{id="restricted-areas" eyebrow="Diamond areas, parks and drones" title="Satellite first where access is restricted"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Diamond restricted areas include onshore diamond mining licence areas, bulk-sampling areas, processing places and areas the Minister declares, and anyone who is not a listed official needs the Minister's permit "to enter, reside, travel, work or be in, or to visit a restricted area" ([Diamond Act 13 of 1999](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Diamond%20Act%2013%20of%201999.pdf), ss.27(k), 52). In national parks, NCAA's checklist allows no drone flight unless approval is granted.

In diamond restricted areas and national parks we work from satellite imagery unless your permits and consents cover a partner's crew. Elsewhere, drone surveys are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires. In Namibia that includes an NCAA Letter of Approval for each operation, the landowner's permission, and the airfield rules near mine and lodge airstrips. Afridrone is working towards the approvals Namibia requires.

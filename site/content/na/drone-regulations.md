faq:
  - q: Does a satellite survey need NCAA approval?
    a: No aircraft is flown, so the Part 101 approvals do not apply to the satellite work itself, and no landowner letter is needed to analyse satellite imagery. Counsel should confirm that for your project. Drone stretches, where you want them, are planned separately and are subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly commercial drone surveys in Namibia?
    a: "Partly. NCAA's directive says an RPAS Letter of Approval \"will be issued to a Namibian or foreign operator\", so a foreign operator can apply for flights within visual line of sight, allowing time for international payments, maps of each operation and insurance valid in Namibia. The RPAS Operator Certificate needed for flights beyond visual line of sight is \"available to a Namibian natural or juristic person only\". Foreign pilots also sit NCAA's theory exam and skills test before their certificates are validated."
  - q: How far ahead should a drone survey be planned?
    a: Part 101 asks for a Category III application at least 30 days before the flight. NCAA's instructions add that payment must reach it at least 10 days before the approval date, that international transfers take about three weeks, and that a complete application is processed in about a week. Park, airspace and landowner letters are gathered alongside, so start them at the same time.
  - q: Is there a height limit?
    a: Two figures are in force. Part 101 limits a drone under 25 kg to 50 m above the ground or water unless the Executive Director approves more, and the AIP and NCAA's checklist use 150 ft above the surface. Flying above 150 ft needs a CAUA approval from NCAA's ANSSO department. Ask for the approved height to be written into the approval.
  - q: Can a drone fly over our own servitude where other people's homes stand?
    a: Part 101 requires direct control of the drone near any person, vehicle or structure, and bars using a drone to observe another person or their property without consent. Whose consent covers third-party structures inside a servitude is a question for counsel before the flight plan is fixed. Satellite imagery is the usual first step along settled stretches.
  - q: Can a drone fly over a diamond restricted area or a national park?
    a: Only with the permissions for that area. Entering or being in a diamond restricted area needs a permit from the Minister under the Diamond Act, and whether an overflight counts is unresolved, so the licence holder's consent and counsel's view come first. NCAA's checklist says no flight within a national park unless approval is granted, and filming in national parks needs MEFT approval. The AIP closes three coastal nature reserves to all aircraft.
  - q: Does Namibia have a data-protection law that covers drone imagery?
    a: We found no general data-protection Act. Part 101's consent rules, the constitutional right to privacy (Article 13) and NCAA's rule against filming private property without the owner's permission apply to drone work, so contracts should set out how imagery is processed, masked, stored and kept.

Namibia's drone rules were substituted into the Namibian Civil Aviation Regulations by [GN 89 of 2020](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/legislations/Gazettes/Government%20Gazette%207157.pdf) (Government Gazette 7157, 27 March 2020). We found no later amendment of Part 101. NCAA wrote in July 2024 that "RPAS regulations were drafted and in the interim a directive was issued" ([NCAA](https://www.ncaa.com.na/fullarticle.php?for=83)), so the directives carry much of the practical detail, and it is worth asking NCAA whether anything new has been published before a flight plan is fixed.

::::section{id="categories" tone="alt" eyebrow="Categories" title="Why a survey for a client is Category III"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 101 covers remotely piloted aircraft (RPA), unmanned free balloons, kites and rockets, in addition to the general flight rules in Part 91 (101.01.2(1)). It sorts operations into three categories (101.02.1):

- **Category I:** recreational flying "within the confines of the private property of the operator", with no reward.
- **Category II:** sport, recreation and research, including tourism, with no commercial reward.
- **Category III:** a "commercial operation… for purposes of business, and for remuneration, hire, reward or gain". Mapping a servitude or a licence area for a client falls here.

A drone counts as a recreational toy only if it weighs under 250 g, has no fuel system, cannot carry a payload, is "not fitted with a camera", stays within 120 m above ground and 50 m of the operator, and flies at 10 knots or less (101.02.1(2)). No survey drone meets that test.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="What a Category III operator must show"}
Adequate organisation and accountability, control of flight operations, training, ground handling and maintenance arrangements, and safety management under Part 140 (101.02.2(1)). Owning, registering or seeking approval for an RPA built to military or intelligence specifications needs the Executive Director's written authorisation (101.02.2(2)).
:::
::::
:::::

Part 101 also lists what it does not apply to (101.01.2(2)), including "RPA operations that constitute an air service within the meaning of the Air Services Act, 1949" and "operations utilising a RPA or RPA system separately from commercial air transport services". Read literally, those exclusions sit awkwardly beside Category III. In practice NCAA applies Part 101 and its directives to commercial drone operators, and the [open questions](#open-questions) below include what the Air Services Act means for survey work.
::::

::::section{id="letter-of-approval" eyebrow="The operation" title="The RPAS Letter of Approval"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**When it is needed.** No Category II or III operation may take place "outside the property of the owner or operator" except under an approval (101.03.1).

**When to apply.** At least 30 days before a Category III operation, and 15 days before a Category II one (101.03.2(3)); the Executive Director may accept other periods "on good cause shown" (101.03.2(4)).

**How long it lasts.** The approval is "specific to a particular RPA or RPA system operation and is valid for the period of the specific operation only unless the Executive Director extends its validity up to a maximum of 12 months" (101.03.2(5)).

**What it covers.** An RLA covers "all drones and pilots who the operator intends to use, as per the serial numbers and names listed on the application form" (AD 1/2/3-6, 5.5). Changing a drone or a pilot is an amendment (5.6).

**What goes in** (101.03.3(1)): operator and owner details and the drone's technical data; the registration or airworthiness certificate where relevant; the radiotelephony identification; a foreign RPA operator certificate "where applicable"; each remote pilot's certificate; any aircraft radio station licence; a description of the operation (line of sight or beyond, dates, route, levels and duration); control-link and communications-failure procedures; the payload; "proof of adequate insurance liability, including third party insurance coverage"; and "proof of registration as a surveyor, for purposes of commercial aerial and survey mapping, where applicable". Documents in another language need a sworn English translation (101.03.3(2)).

**Issue and display.** NCAA issues the approval when the requirements are met, the personnel are competent and it is "not contrary to the interests of aviation safety" (101.03.4). The holder displays it at its principal place of business and produces the original on request (101.03.6).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="What NCAA asks for in practice"}
From [NCAA's RPAS instructions](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/forms-applications/flight-operations/0.%20NCAA%20RPAS%20Instructions%20June%202021.pdf), the [application form](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/forms-applications/flight-operations/2.%20FSS-GEN-FORM%20081%20RPA%20Application%20Form%20Rev%202.pdf) and the [submission checklist](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/forms-applications/flight-operations/5.%20FSS-OPS-FORM%20177%20RPAS%20Submission%20Checklist.pdf):

- drone-specific liability insurance, valid for the applicant, the flight dates, Namibia and the drones, with proof in English;
- registration of every Namibia-based drone;
- a copy of each pilot's identity document;
- a letter of intent or cover letter for Category III;
- the co-ordinates of the flight area in Word or PDF, preferably with a KMZ, outside NCAA's restricted areas unless a CAUA or MEFT approval is also sought (maps are not required for local operators applying for 12-month approvals);
- MEFT, Namibian Film Commission and CAUA letters where relevant;
- a certificate of registration as a surveyor, if applicable.

The directive names form FSS-OPS-FORM-612-01, while the form NCAA publishes is FSS-GEN-FORM 081 Rev 2: ask NCAA which it wants.
:::
::::
:::::

**Lead times, in NCAA's words.** "Please apply 30 days prior to your flight." Payment must reach NCAA "a minimum of 10 days before the required approval date", "international payment transfers can take on average three weeks", and "Providing we have received all the correct documentation and payment the processing time is one week". Applications go to rpas@ncaa.na. NCAA's current charges are set in [Part 187, replaced in April 2026](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/legislations/NAMCARS/GRN%20Gazette%208890%20Part%20187%20%2016%20April%202026.pdf).

::::section{id="operator-certificate" tone="alt" eyebrow="Flights beyond sight" title="The RPAS Operator Certificate, for Namibian operators only"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
"No RPAS operator shall operate Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) or cargo operations except under the authority of and in accordance with the conditions of a certificate issued in terms of this directive" (AD 1/2/3-6, 5.4). Part 101 sets the same line from the other side: no flight beyond direct unaided visual line of sight, and none further than 300 m from the point of operation, unless the Executive Director specifically approves BVLOS operations (101.05.2(5)(e)).

The certificate follows a five-phase certification process and an operations manual, set out in NCAA advisory pamphlets. The operator names an Accountable Manager, a Quality Manager, a Safety Manager and a Flight Operations Manager (the safety and quality posts may be combined), runs quality assurance and a safety management system, has office accommodation, holds pilot licences, certificates of competency or letters of authorisation, accepts NCAA inspections and audits, including of its sub-contractors, and keeps documents for two years.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Who can hold one"}
"A RPAS Operator Certificate shall be available to a Namibian natural or juristic person only" (AD 1/2/3-6, 5.5). By contrast, "An RPAS Letter of Approval will be issued to a Namibian or foreign operator, that is a natural or juristic person".

So a long corridor is flown in blocks within the pilot's sight, unless an operator with a Namibian ROC flies it beyond sight.
:::

**Advertising and records.** "Any advertisement made by the holder of an ROC shall reflect the certificate number of the ROC" (5.4). NCAA keeps a register of the RLAs and ROCs it issues, amends or reviews (5.8), but does not publish it, so ask for the numbers and check them with NCAA. An RLA or ROC "may be varied, suspended, or revoked" (5.7).

::::section{id="pilots" eyebrow="Each pilot" title="The NCAA remote pilot certificate"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
"Unless undergoing a skills test or receiving instruction, no person shall act either as a remote pilot of a Remotely Piloted Aircraft (RPA) unless that person is the holder of a valid remote pilot certificate, containing the ratings suitable for the purpose of executing the operation" ([AD 1/2/4-1 Rev 1](https://www.ncaa.com.na/directives.php?pagetitle=Directives&dir=Personnel+Licensing+Directives&download=AVIATION+DRECTIVE+RPAS+CERTIFICATES+%28New%29.pdf), 8.1.1). The certificate is issued for aeroplanes, multi-rotors or hybrid VTOL aircraft, with ratings for visual line of sight (VLOS), extended visual line of sight (E-VLOS), beyond visual line of sight (B-VLOS) and night.

An applicant (8.2.1):

- is 16 or over;
- holds a valid class 3 medical certificate for B-VLOS work or Category III operations;
- holds a restricted certificate of proficiency in radiotelephony (aeronautical) for Category III operations;
- has Level 4 English language proficiency;
- has completed flight training at an institution the Executive Director approves;
- passed the online theory exam within 90 days before the skills test, at an NCAA-accredited test centre or training organisation;
- passed the skills test with an accredited examiner within 30 days of finishing training.
::::
::::col
**Validity.** Two years (7.4, 8.6.1), revalidated in the last 90 days. A certificate lapsed for more than 36 months means meeting the initial requirements again (8.6.5). Logbooks are kept for five years after expiry (8.7.3).

**Foreign pilots.** A foreign certificate can be validated, but "any applicant for the validation of a foreign RPAS certificate must write and pass the theoretical knowledge examination at an NCAA-approved Test Centre, and successfully pass the final practical skills test with an NCAA approved Examiner" (9.1–9.4).

**Heavier drones.** Pilots of drones of 4 kg or more need training "acceptable to the Executive Director" (101.05.2(7)).

**Registration.** The directive asks for every drone "permanently based in Namibia" to be registered "in terms of NAMCAR Part 47" (AD 1/2/3-6, 5.1), but [Part 47](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/legislations/NAMCARS/NAMCAR%20Part%2047.pdf) states that it does not apply to remotely piloted aircraft. How drones are registered, and whether a foreign-owned drone can be, is one of the [open questions](#open-questions).

::::section{id="rules" tone="alt" eyebrow="The flight" title="Operating rules that shape a corridor or licence-area survey" lead="Part 101 and the AIP set different figures for height and airfields. Plan to the stricter one, or have the approval say which applies."}
| Rule | What the texts say | What it means for a survey |
|---|---|---|
| **Height** | A drone under 25 kg may not fly "at a height of more than 50 metres above the ground or water, unless the flight is approved by the Executive Director" (101.05.2(5)(f)). The AIP (ENR 1.1 §12.1(a)) and NCAA's checklist use 150 ft above the surface | Get the height written into the approval; a CAUA approval above 150 ft |
| **Airfields** | Not within 3 km of an aerodrome during aircraft operations without the aerodrome operator's permission (101.05.2(5)(b)), nor near its reference point, approach and take-off paths, navigation aids or traffic zone without permission (101.05.9). The AIP and the checklist use 5 NM from an aerodrome boundary | Keep 5 NM from farm, lodge and mine airstrips, or apply for permission |
| **Controlled and restricted airspace** | Not in a prohibited area, a restricted area or controlled airspace "without specific approval from the Executive Director" (101.05.2(5)(a)); not in a published control zone or traffic zone (AIP §12.1(b)) | A CAUA approval from ANSSO; some prohibited areas are closed outright (below) |
| **Beyond sight** | Not beyond direct unaided visual line of sight, and not further than 300 m from the point of operation, without BVLOS approval (101.05.2(5)(e)) | Corridors flown in blocks, or by an operator with a Namibian ROC |
| **People and structures** | Not at any distance from a person, vessel, vehicle or structure unless the drone is under the operator's direct control; gatherings of people need direct control, "an approved fail safe system" and approval (101.05.2(5)(c)–(d)) | Direct control along settled stretches; no flights over gatherings |
| **Roads** | Not flown so as to endanger or obstruct traffic on a public road (101.05.2(1)(c)) | A traffic plan at road crossings |
| **Flight plans** | Needed for BVLOS flights and flights above 120 m; in uncontrolled airspace above 120 m, flights within five kilometres of the launch area notify the nearest air traffic control, and flights beyond it file a flight plan (101.05.5) | Filed with the operation plan |
| **Other traffic** | Give way to manned aircraft, and be able to halt, re-route and change level in flight (101.05.2(2)); detect-and-avoid capability in mixed-use airspace (101.05.6) | Part of the operator's procedures |
| **Goods** | Carrying goods needs the Executive Director's approval (101.05.2(8)) | No payload drops |
| **Across a border** | Authorisation from the destination State, and the Executive Director's approval for inbound flights or overflight (101.05.1) | Orange and Kunene river stretches |
| **Inspection** | Inspection "without prior notification" (101.05.4(2)); the Executive Director may direct that a flight not take place and "seize and detain that RPA" (101.05.4(7)) | Documents on site |
| **Incidents** | Accidents and incidents causing damage, injury or death are reported (101.05.7(1)); Category II and III operators keep emergency, contingency and threat-response procedures (101.05.7(2)–(4)) | In the operations manual |

**Airspace approvals.** NCAA's instructions say: "Filming in restricted airspace or above 150ft requires CAUA approval from NCAA ANSSO department, please submit forms 7 and 8 with land owners permission." CAUA stands for civil activity use airspace, the approval under Part 71.

**Insurance.** Part 101 requires "adequate liability insurance in respect of third party risks", with a copy of the policy available for inspection (101.06.2); the Executive Director may dispense with it depending on the class and category.

**Equipment.** Importing or exporting a drone or its components must comply with customs requirements "as specified in Document NAM-CATS-RPA" (101.04.1(1)), a document NCAA has not published, so ask NCAA and customs before shipping equipment. The Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia (CRAN) may prescribe type approval for categories of radio equipment and may seize equipment that does not comply ([Communications Act, s.80](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Communications%20Act%208%20of%202009.pdf)).

::::section{id="restricted-areas" eyebrow="Where the answer is no, or not yet" title="Prohibited, restricted and military areas, diamond areas and parks"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Closed to all aircraft" icon="x-circle" eyebrow="AIP ENR 5.1"}
Three coastal nature reserves, the Wolfs Bay seal colony (FYP 93), Bird Island at Walvis Bay (FYP 94) and the Cape Cross seal colony and bird platforms (FYP 95), from the ground to 1,000 ft: "No person shall, in any aircraft whatsoever, fly into these areas". Over State House (FYP 96), no aircraft may fly below 8,500 ft AMSL ([AIP Namibia, Enroute](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/aeronautical-information/aeronautical-information-publication/aips/Enroute.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="Walvis Bay harbour" icon="water" eyebrow="AIP ENR 5.1 · FYR 97"}
Over the harbour area, within 3.5 NM and up to 500 ft, no aircraft may fly below 500 ft "without the authorisation of the port authority", except while taking off or landing.
:::
:::card{title="Military and training areas" icon="shield" eyebrow="AIP ENR 5.1 and 5.2"}
Danger areas FYD 130 (the Windhoek and Eros flying training area) and FYD 135 (Keetmanshoop), and military areas activated by NOTAM: FYR 131 Grootfontein, FYR 132 Bum Bum, FYR 133 Oserikari weapons range, FYR 134 Swart Koppies and FYM 401 Etiro, where civilian use is co-ordinated through the military liaison officer when the area is active.
:::
:::card{title="Prohibited places" icon="lock" eyebrow="Protection of Information Act 84 of 1982"}
Prohibited places include government defence works, among them "any telegraph, telephone, radio or signal station or office" (s.1), and approaching, passing over or entering one "for any purpose prejudicial to the security or interests of the Republic" is an offence punishable by up to 20 years' imprisonment (s.2). The UK's travel advice notes that police have detained people for photographing the President's official residence. Our deliverables never map or show defence sites or signal stations.
:::
:::card{title="Diamond restricted areas" icon="mine" eyebrow="Diamond Act 13 of 1999"}
Onshore diamond mining licence areas, bulk-sampling areas, processing places and areas the Minister declares are restricted areas, marked by signs (s.52). Anyone who is not a listed official needs the Minister's permit "to enter, reside, travel, work or be in, or to visit a restricted area" (s.27(k)). Whether a drone overflight counts is unresolved; the licence holder's consent is needed in any case ([Diamond Act](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Diamond%20Act%2013%20of%201999.pdf)).
:::
:::card{title="National parks" icon="tree" eyebrow="MEFT · Nature Conservation Ordinance"}
NCAA's checklist says "Not within a national park unless approval is granted", and its instructions say filming in national parks requires MEFT approval. The [Nature Conservation Ordinance](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Nature%20Conservation%20Ordinance%204%20of%201975.pdf) bans driving game with an aircraft "for any purpose whatsoever, including that of filming or photography" (s.43). The southern green-hydrogen concessions lie inside Tsau ǁKhaeb National Park.
:::
:::

**Key points.** Part 101 also excludes recreational flying "in or around any key points as described in Document NAMCATS-RPA", a document NCAA has not published, and we found no Namibian key-points statute. NCAA supplies a map of restricted areas to applicants. At oil and gas, power and mining sites we found no site-specific drone ban: the landowner rule, the consent rules and the airfield rules near mine and lodge strips apply.

::::section{id="privacy" tone="alt" eyebrow="People and property" title="Consent, private property and privacy"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 101 bars using a drone (101.05.4(4)):

- to observe another person, unless that person has consented;
- to observe anyone's movable or immovable property, unless the owner has consented;
- to photograph or film a person for publication or other public dissemination, unless that person has consented.

NCAA's instructions add: "No filming may be conducted on private property without the express permission of the property owner." Article 13(1) of the Constitution protects the privacy of homes, correspondence and communications. We found no general data-protection Act in Namibia, so the protection of imagery and registers rests on these rules and on the contract.

For a drone survey this means a written scope limited to the client's own land, servitude or licence area, the written consent of the owners the flight needs, and people masked in anything published. These consent rules govern drones; they do not govern satellite imagery.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="How our registers are written"}
Our registers describe structures, cleared ground, excavations and tracks, never people, and carry no names. Results go only to the contacts you name, and the contract states how imagery is processed, masked, stored and kept.

::::section{id="vetting-penalties" eyebrow="Vetting and enforcement" title="Security background checks and penalties"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Vetting.** People engaged in RPA operations are "subject to security background checks and, where necessary, vetting, in accordance with… section 134 of the Act and Part 114" (101.05.4(3)). Someone who refuses consent "may not be granted any authorisation required under this Act" ([Civil Aviation Act 6 of 2016](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/legislations/Acts/Civil%20Aviation%20Act,%202016.pdf), s.134(2)). Part 114, inserted by GN 293 of 2018 (Government Gazette 6763, listed in [NCAA's regulations index](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/SYSDocs/NAMCARs-NAMCATS%20Index%2020241001.xlsx)), applies to pilots and to anyone the Executive Director requires, and where a person has lived outside Namibia it asks for criminal record certificates from each country where that person has lived for six months or more in the five years before the enquiry (114.02.15). Ask NCAA which crew members it will vet, and allow time for foreign certificates.
::::
::::col
**Offences.** Part 185, substituted by the same notice, makes it an offence to make false statements or entries, use falsified documents, act contrary to the regulations or to a direction, condition or directive made under them, use an approval one does not hold, or operate an aircraft without a valid certificate of registration or airworthiness (185.01.2(1)). The penalty is a fine or imprisonment of up to two years, or both (185.01.2(2), with s.54(2)(c)(ii) of the Act), and administrative fines per violation, plus daily fines, can be imposed subject to notice and review (185.02.2).

**Other action.** NCAA can refuse or prohibit an operation, inspect without notice, direct that a flight not take place and seize the drone (101.05.4), and vary, suspend or revoke an RLA or ROC. The Diamond Act and the Protection of Information Act carry their own penalties, including imprisonment.

::::section{id="open-questions" tone="alt" eyebrow="Still open" title="Questions to settle with NCAA and counsel" lead="The public texts leave these points unresolved. We state them as open rather than guess."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Does drone survey work also need an air service licence?"}
The [Air Services Act 51 of 1949](https://www.ncaa.com.na/publications/legislations/Acts/Air%20Services%20Act%2051%20of%201949.pdf) is not among the laws the Civil Aviation Act 2016 repealed. It requires a licence from the Transportation Commission for any "air service", defined as "any service performed by means of an aircraft for reward" (ss.1–2), and applications are published for objections and heard. Part 101 excludes operations that constitute an air service. Whether a drone survey operator needs this licence is unresolved.
:::
:::step{title="How are drones registered?"}
The directive points to Part 47, which excludes remotely piloted aircraft, while Part 185 makes it an offence to operate an aircraft without a valid certificate of registration. Whether, and how, a foreign-owned drone can be registered follows from the answer.
:::
:::step{title="Whose consent covers third-party structures in a servitude?"}
Part 101's consent rules speak of the owner of the property observed. Along a servitude that crosses farms and communal land, counsel should say whether the servitude holder's rights, the landowner's consent or the consent of each occupant is needed.
:::
:::step{title="Does an overflight need a Diamond Act permit?"}
The permit covers entering or being in a restricted area. Whether a drone flying over one needs it, in addition to the licence holder's consent, is unresolved.
:::
:::step{title="Can the coastal nature reserves ever be flown?"}
The AIP closes FYP 93–95 to "any aircraft whatsoever", while Part 101 allows flights in prohibited areas with specific approval. The AIP's wording is the stricter; counsel should say whether any approval route exists.
:::
:::

Orthophotos and registers are monitoring and planning records, not cadastral surveys. General plans and diagrams need approval and must be signed by a land surveyor ([Land Survey Act 33 of 1993](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Land%20Survey%20Act%2033%20of%201993.pdf), ss.35–36), and the [Professional Land Surveyors', Technical Surveyors' and Survey Technicians' Act 32 of 1993](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Professional%20Land%20Surveyors'%20Technical%20Surveyors'%20and%20Survey%20Technicians'%20Act%2032%20of%201993.pdf) reserves some work to registered practitioners. Part 101's "proof of registration as a surveyor… where applicable" points the same way: ask counsel which mapping work on your job needs a registered surveyor.

Landowner, land board or traditional authority letters as the land requires, MEFT approval in parks, Diamond Act permits and the licence holder's consent in restricted areas, and your own site rules.

Security background checks under the Act and Part 114, with foreign criminal record certificates where they apply.

faq:
  - q: Does a small drone, under 25 kg, need an authorisation too?
    a: Yes. Article 16.º-A of the Civil Aviation Law makes no exception for weight or purpose. In its homologation block for remotely piloted aircraft (RPA), ANAC's 2023 fee schedule has lines for two weight classes, up to 25 kg and from 25 to 150 kg, each with ownership registration, a registration (matrícula) certificate and an airworthiness certificate. The technical rules behind those lines are not published on ANAC's regulations page, so confirm with ANAC what each operation needs.
  - q: Does a company flying drones over its own concession need an authorisation?
    a: Yes. The special authorisation in article 16.º-A applies to every flight. Aerial work for the operator's own benefit is a private aerial service (articles 56.º and 57.º of the Civil Aviation Law), and ANAC sets its requirements too. ANAC's 2026 draft sport-aviation regulation would also require a specific ANAC authorisation for any use that is not sport or leisure.
  - q: Can a foreign company carry out the survey?
    a: "The texts reviewed do not bar it outright, but three points need checking. A foreign air company needs an operating authorisation from ANAC, filing its shareholders and their nationalities and naming a legal representative in Angola (article 52.º of the Civil Aviation Law); whether this reaches aerial work with drones is a question for counsel. INACOM's import authorisation for telecommunications material is given to companies registered in Angola with an Angolan tax number (NIF). And in the petroleum sector, the services on ANPG's exclusivity list are reserved to Angolan commercial companies (Decreto Presidencial n.º 271/20, articles 3.º and 11.º)."
  - q: Are flights beyond visual line of sight, at night or over people allowed?
    a: ANAC publishes no drone-specific technical rules that deal with them. Because every flight needs the special authorisation in article 16.º-A, any such operation happens only within what ANAC authorises for that case.
  - q: Does a satellite-only survey need these authorisations?
    a: The Civil Aviation Law's authorisations concern aircraft, and no aircraft flies in satellite work. That is a reading of the text, not an opinion. If the deliverables are meant as official cartography or geographic information, ask IGCA whether an alvará or homologation is needed (Decreto Presidencial n.º 115/21, article 6.º).
  - q: Can we publish the survey imagery?
    a: It depends on the site and the contract. We never publish imagery of military sites, refineries, terminals or diamond plants, and each survey contract should say who may reproduce the orthophotos and on what terms. Where the imagery shows identifiable people, Lei n.º 22/11 on personal data applies.

::::section{id="who-authorises" eyebrow="1. Who authorises" title="One authorisation for every flight, and several bodies around it" lead="ANAC authorises the flights and licenses aerial work. Around that authorisation sit IGCA for cartography and geographic information, INACOM for radio equipment, the data-protection agency (APD) for personal data, and whoever holds each site."}
| Body | What it authorises or controls | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| **ANAC**, Autoridade Nacional da Aviação Civil, an "entidade administrativa independente" | The special authorisation for each flight; licensing of civil unmanned-aircraft operation and of aerial work; aircraft registration | [Lei n.º 28/21, art. 37(2)(f) and (5)(f)](https://doc.anac.ao/share/s/_O1uG0TFSm2P4qc6D-tKCg); [Civil Aviation Law, arts. 9, 11, 16.º-A, 54, 55 and 156](https://doc.anac.ao/share/s/neWfA1YVT4-WGO9cBmuGsg) |
| **ANAC and the State** | Banning or restricting air traffic over given areas, temporarily or permanently, "com efeito imediato" | Civil Aviation Law, arts. 16 and 16.º-B |
| **IGCA**, Instituto Geográfico e Cadastral de Angola | Alvarás (professional licences) for cadastre, cartography, geodesy, topography and GIS work; homologation of geographic information; supervision of private cartography and photogrammetry | [Decreto Presidencial n.º 115/21, arts. 6 and 14](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ang203438.pdf) |
| **INACOM** | Import authorisation for telecommunications material, for companies registered in Angola with a valid Angolan NIF, after registering with INACOM | [INACOM's equipment-import page](https://inacom.gov.ao/importacao-equipamentos/) |
| **APD**, Agência de Protecção de Dados | Notification or authorisation of personal-data processing; transfers abroad | [Lei n.º 22/11, arts. 33–35](https://apd.ao/fotos/frontend_1/editor2/110617_lei_22-11_de_17_junho-proteccao_dados_pessoais.pdf) |
| **Mining-right holders, concessionaires, aerodrome operators** | Access to each site and consent to the flight | [Código Mineiro, arts. 200–203](https://c2a.portais.gov.ao/uploads/Codigo_Mineiro_de_Angola_2011_1dfcae949b.pdf); Land Law, art. 27 |

Whether a drone's radio equipment falls under INACOM's import authorisation is a question for INACOM itself: its page speaks of telecommunications material in general.

:::callout{tone="note" title="Old references to INAVIC"}
INAVIC no longer exists. Lei n.º 28/21 folded INAVIC's powers and acts into ANAC, which succeeds to its rights and obligations (article 68.º), and any reference to INAVIC in any instrument is read as a reference to ANAC (article 72.º). Guides and contracts that still say "apply to INAVIC" are out of date.
:::

::::section{id="civil-aviation-law" tone="alt" eyebrow="2. The Civil Aviation Law" title="The articles a drone survey has to meet" lead="Lei n.º 14/19 was altered and republished by Lei n.º 31/21 of 20 December 2021, in the Diário da República, I Série, n.º 238. Lei n.º 31/21 replaced the term “VANT” with “Sistemas de Aeronaves Pilotadas Remotamente” throughout (article 4.º(2)) and added, among others, article 16.º-A."}
- **Article 16.º-A(1):** "Nenhuma aeronave capaz de navegar sem piloto pode sobrevoar o território angolano sem autorização especial da Autoridade Nacional da Aviação Civil" (no aircraft able to fly without a pilot may fly over Angolan territory without ANAC's special authorisation). Paragraph 2 gives ANAC the approval of the provisions for their safe navigation. No open category is published: every flight needs the authorisation.
- **Article 11(1)(ii):** ANAC licenses and controls the use of unmanned aircraft systems, "acautelando o seu uso indevido" (guarding against their misuse).
- **Articles 54 and 55:** specialised aerial services include aerial photography, aerial photogrammetry, aerial cinematography, aerial topography, and prospecting and detection of natural features. Their requirements are set "por acto próprio" of ANAC.
- **Articles 56 and 57:** aerial work for the operator's own benefit is a private aerial service, whose requirements ANAC also sets.
- **Article 122:** insurance is compulsory for "qualquer transportador ou operador de aeronave". The insurance regime is set in its own legislation.
- **Article 155(1)(d):** using an aircraft with aerophotogrammetric equipment without the authorisation of "a autoridade competente" is grounds for suspension or a fine. The law does not name that authority, so ask ANAC before each survey.
- **Article 156(2):** fines apply, among others, to aerial work without the required licence or authorisation (b), to "violação de espaço aéreo por RPA ou UA não autorizados" (k) and to using an RPA or UA not registered with ANAC (l). This site quotes no fine amounts.
- **Article 160:** in the interest of national sovereignty, the defence authorities may order any aircraft to be seized.

The law defines unmanned aircraft (UA), remotely piloted aircraft (RPA) and their systems (UAS and RPAS) in article 7(3). Under [Lei n.º 28/21](https://doc.anac.ao/share/s/_O1uG0TFSm2P4qc6D-tKCg), civil operation of unmanned aircraft is subject to ANAC licensing (article 37(2)(f)), and ANAC authorises aerial work by operators (article 37(5)(f)).

::::section{id="fee-schedule" eyebrow="3. What the fee schedule shows" title="A registration and licensing regime exists, but its technical rules are not published"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
[ANAC's fee regulation](https://doc.anac.ao/share/s/uYJ4VC35S66DfDW8i5WtYw), in the Diário da República, II Série, n.º 7, of 11 January 2023, shows which acts ANAC performs for remotely piloted aircraft. Its block 9.3, "taxas por homologação", has:

- two weight classes, up to 25 kg and from 25 to 150 kg, each with ownership registration, a registration (matrícula) certificate and an airworthiness certificate;
- RPA inspections;
- RPA pilot licences and RPA pilot-training certificates;
- approval of RPA construction and authorisation of experimental flights.

It also has generic aerial-work lines that apply to any aircraft: an aerial-work certificate for each type of service, the opening of an aerial-work licensing file, and a per-day authorisation for photography.

The schedule says which acts exist, not what each one requires. On 27 September 2026 ANAC's regulations page listed five regulations, none of them on remotely piloted aircraft, and ANAC's public list of aerial-work operators, dated 2 August 2022, included no drone operator.

::::section{id="draft-2026" tone="alt" eyebrow="4. The 2026 draft" title="ANAC's draft sport-aviation regulation" lead="From 29 May to 13 June 2026, ANAC consulted on a draft sport-aviation regulation (Consulta Pública n.º 003/2026). The consultation is closed; adoption was not confirmed on 27 September 2026. It is a draft, not a rule in force."}
- **Model aircraft (article 2(1)(a)):** up to 30 kg, flying no more than 50 m above the take-off point, and used "exclusivamente em actividades desportivas e de lazer" (only for sport and leisure).
- **Where model flying is barred (article 106):** within 5,000 m of the outer limits of any airport or of controlled or restricted airspace; inside urban perimeters; within 5,000 m of, or over, military installations and "órgãos de soberania" (the seats of state power). ANAC may exceptionally authorise flights in the first two.
- **Everything else (article 107):** unmanned aircraft beyond those limits, "bem como as que se destinem a utilização não desportiva", need a specific authorisation from the regulator "para qualquer tipo de operação".
- **Transition (article 108):** 90 days for those covered to conform.

If it is adopted as drafted, a commercial survey will still always need a specific ANAC authorisation, and the 5 km distances are a sensible planning buffer for surveys near airports and military sites. [Consultation page](https://www.anac.ao/ao/consulta-publica/consultas-em-curso/consulta-publica-n-003-2026-projecto-de-regulacao-de-aviacao-desportiva/) · [Draft text](https://doc.anac.ao/share/s/Zd4jOOKmT2KLtI5PmSa8uw)

::::section{id="where-not-to-fly" eyebrow="5. Where not to fly without specific authorisation" title="Airports, military sites, the border and mining zones"}
:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="Airports and aerodromes" icon="alert"}
The Land Law reserves a 100 m strip around airports and aerodromes (article 27(7)(j)), and ANAC gives a binding opinion on buildings, power lines and antennas near airports (Civil Aviation Law, article 11(1)(jj)). The 2026 draft keeps model aircraft 5 km from airports and controlled or restricted airspace.
:::
:::card{title="Military sites and seats of state power" icon="shield"}
The Land Law reserves 100 m around military and other defence and security installations (article 27(7)(k)). ANAC may ban or restrict air traffic over given areas (articles 16 and 16.º-B), and the 2026 draft keeps model aircraft 5 km from these sites.
:::
:::card{title="The land border" icon="boundary"}
The Land Law reserves a 2 km strip along the land border (article 27(7)(i)). A flight plan should keep clear of it.
:::
:::card{title="Mining restricted and protection zones" icon="mine"}
The restricted zone reaches up to 1 km around mining areas and is signposted "Zona Restrita. Acesso Proibido" (Código Mineiro, article 200). Anyone who does not work there enters only by invitation or with the right holder's authorisation, after "prévia comunicação formal ao titular do direito e às autoridades policiais locais" (article 201(2)). The protection zone reaches up to 5 km from the deposits' outer limits and is signposted "Zona de Protecção Mineira – Permanência Proibida" (articles 202 and 203).
:::

- **Diamond concessions:** no flight over or near a diamond concession without the right holder's written consent and ANAC's authorisation.
- **Power lines:** safety distances from live lines are agreed with the line operator before the flight.
- **Refineries, terminals and oil installations:** the flight needs the installation operator's consent as well as ANAC's authorisation.
- **Scope:** we do no field or drone work in Cabinda province outside Cabinda city, or in Lunda Norte and Lunda Sul provinces, in line with current travel advice ([FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/angola), [Travel Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/angola)).

::::section{id="data-and-imagery" tone="alt" eyebrow="6. Data and imagery" title="Personal data, image capture and publication"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Personal data ([Lei n.º 22/11 of 17 June 2011](https://apd.ao/fotos/frontend_1/editor2/110617_lei_22-11_de_17_junho-proteccao_dados_pessoais.pdf)).** Processing personal data needs "notificação prévia à Agência de Protecção de Dados ou à autorização desta" (article 35(1)). The APD answers a notification within 30 days, after which it is deemed made (paragraph 2), and it may simplify or waive notification for some categories (paragraph 3). Security of processing is in articles 30 and 31.

**Transfers abroad.** To a country with adequate protection, notifying the APD is enough (article 33). To any other country, an APD authorisation is always needed, and it "só pode ser concedida" on listed grounds such as unambiguous written consent, a contract with or in the interest of the data subject, an important public interest or contractual safeguards (article 34(1)).

**Breaches.** [APD Circular n.º 02/APD/2024](https://apd.ao/fotos/frontend_1/editor2/circular_002.pdf) of 25 October 2024 requires the APD to be notified of a personal-data breach as soon as it is known.
::::
::::col
**Image capture for public-security reasons ([Lei n.º 2/20 of 22 January 2020](https://apd.ao/fotos/frontend_1/editor2/200222_lei_2-20_de_22_janeiro-videovigilancia.pdf)).** This law applies to image-capture systems used "por razões de segurança pública" in public places, private places of common use and restricted places (article 2(1)), with fixed or mobile cameras, including vehicles carrying image-capture equipment (article 3(1)(a) and (c)). In strictly private places, capture may not take in "imagens de propriedades limítrofes" (article 2(4)). AfriScan maps structures and ground; it runs no patrols and follows no one. A project that wants drones for security purposes should review this law with its counsel.

**Geographic information.** IGCA homologates the geographic information produced (Decreto Presidencial n.º 115/21, article 6(1)(u)).

**Publication.** We never publish imagery of military sites, refineries, terminals or diamond plants, and each survey contract says who may reproduce the orthophotos.

::::section{id="foreign-operators" eyebrow="7. Foreign operators" title="What a company from outside Angola should check"}
None of the texts reviewed bars a foreign company outright from obtaining the authorisations. Three rules weigh on the choice of who flies, though:

- **Operating authorisation (Civil Aviation Law, article 52):** a foreign air company needs an ANAC operating authorisation, filing its shareholders and their nationalities and naming a legal representative in Angola. The article was written for air transport companies; whether it reaches aerial work with drones is a question for counsel.
- **Importing the equipment:** INACOM's authorisation to import telecommunications material is given to companies registered in Angola, with a valid Angolan NIF.
- **Petroleum:** goods and services on ANPG's exclusivity list may be supplied only by Angolan commercial companies, meaning companies seated in Angola whose share capital is held entirely by Angolan citizens or companies (Decreto Presidencial n.º 271/20, articles 3(q) and 11(1)). Our [local-content notes (in Portuguese)](/ao/pt/conteudo-local) set out the regimes.

The published texts we reviewed do not say whether a Defence or security clearance is needed for aerial survey on top of ANAC's special authorisation. Confirm it with ANAC and your counsel for each job.

::::section{id="checklist" tone="alt" eyebrow="8. Checklist" title="What to check before you commission a drone survey in Angola" lead="Eleven points for the team commissioning the work, in the order they usually come up."}
1. **ANAC's special authorisation** for the flights concerned (Civil Aviation Law, article 16.º-A), and the aerial-work authorisations ANAC requires for aerial photography.
2. The operator's **aerial-work licence or certificate** from ANAC for that type of service.
3. **Authorisation to use aerophotogrammetric equipment** from the competent authority (article 155(1)(d)).
4. **Each drone's registration** with ANAC (article 156(2)(l)), and the matrícula and airworthiness certificates ANAC requires for its class.
5. The **RPA pilot licences** ANAC requires for the operation.
6. The operator's **insurance**, naming the project (article 122).
7. If the deliverables are cartography or topography, whether an **IGCA alvará** is needed (Decreto Presidencial n.º 115/21, article 6(1)(s)).
8. **Site consents:** from the concessionaire or mining-right holder; formal notice to the right holder and the local police before entering a restricted zone (Código Mineiro, article 201(2)); from nearby aerodrome operators (the 2026 draft uses 5 km as a reference); from the utility, for lines.
9. A **flight plan** that keeps clear of military sites, the border strip and areas ANAC has restricted, with no work in Cabinda outside the city or in the Lundas.
10. **Data:** APD notification or authorisation, APD authorisation for transfers to countries without adequate protection, who owns the imagery, and a rule against publishing sensitive sites.
11. In **petroleum**, whether the service is on ANPG's exclusivity list and, if it is, a supplier that is an Angolan commercial company.

::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem" title="A railway draws building to both sides" lead="A line under rehabilitation or a new branch brings work, trade and stations. It also brings houses, stalls and fields close to the track, inside the strip that works and operations need to keep clear."}
On rail and road corridors, land changes between one phase of works and the next. A reopened embankment, a new halt or a level crossing draws building; a flood carries soil away and changes the ground by the bridges. When the works or resettlement team arrives, it needs to know what was there and since when, and there is rarely a dated record to hand it.

AfriScan builds that record from imagery: the structures inside each band, with their distance to the track axis and the date of the image, and the change between surveys. For lenders and independent monitors, it is dated evidence between site visits.

::::section{id="reserves" tone="alt" eyebrow="The strips in the law" title="What Angola's Land Law reserves along transport routes" lead="A summary of published rules as of 27 September 2026, with sources. It is not legal advice."}
Article 27 of [Lei n.º 9/04 of 9 November 2004 (the Land Law)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ang49570.pdf) includes several strips along transport routes among its partial reserves:

| Route | Strip in the Land Law | Article 27(7) |
|---|---|---|
| Public-interest railways and stations | "uma faixa de protecção confinante em cada eixo da via", with no width set in the article | (f) |
| Motorways and four-lane roads | 30 m on each side | (g) |
| Provincial roads | 30 m | (h) |
| Secondary and municipal roads | 15 m | (h) |

Uses that do not collide with the reserve's purpose are allowed (paragraph 6), and reserves are constituted by the Government (paragraph 2). Land the State does not own enters a reserve only by expropriation or administrative servitude, and fair compensation is always due (paragraphs 9–10). Because the law sets no rail width, the register measures the width set by the concessionaire, the project owner or the impact study.

::::section{id="works" eyebrow="Rehabilitation and new branches" title="A baseline before the works, and tracking during them"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
**Before the works.** A register of the structures inside each band on a known date, before works start or public utility is declared. In expropriation by zones or alignments, compensation for property outside the first zone counts the necessary improvements made between the declaration of public utility and the taking of possession ([Lei n.º 1/21, article 33](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ang201252.pdf)), so dated registers on both dates help separate what existed from what came later.

**Branches and reroutes.** For a new branch or a realignment, we count the structures within each width along the alternatives, to show which one touches fewer homes and fields.
::::
::::col
**During the works.** Scheduled re-surveys show earthworks progress, camps and new access roads, and new structures by the line, for the works team and for the lender's independent monitor.

**After the works.** A final survey records the corridor as it was left, as a reference for operations.

::::section{id="resettlement" tone="alt" eyebrow="Resettlement" title="The cut-off date, on dated imagery"}
On projects financed by development lenders, the lender applies standards such as IFC Performance Standard 5 or the World Bank's Environmental and Social Standard 5, and the census cut-off date decides who is eligible for compensation. Angola's expropriation law, Lei n.º 1/21, leaves forced vacating and "realojamento" (resettlement) operations to a separate instrument (article 2(2)–(3)).

A register of the structures on the cut-off date, built from dated imagery, supports IFC Performance Standard 5 cut-off-date records and shows what appeared afterwards. It does not take the place of the census, the asset inventory or the consultation with households, which stay with the project. The environmental impact assessment procedure is in [Decreto Presidencial n.º 117/20](https://anrm.gov.ao/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/DP-117-20-de-22-de-Abril-Novo-Regulamento-Geral-de-Avaliacao-de-Impacte-Ambiental-e-do-Procedimento-de-Licenciamento-Ambiental.pdf), and the register comes as layers the ESIA team can use in its own maps.

::::section{id="floods" eyebrow="Floods and erosion" title="After the rains, before the field team"}
When a flood reaches the line, post-event imagery shows where the water went, where soil was washed out and where the ground changed around bridges and embankments. The field team gets a map of the worst-hit stretches and starts there.

Optical imagery cannot see through cloud, and the rainy season delays it. Radar shows larger changes even under rainy-season cloud, and a terrain model helps pick out stretches on steep ground or in flood-prone areas before works begin.

faq:
  - q: Does the 30 m strip apply to every pipeline?
    a: The Land Law lists land occupied by oil and gas installations and conductors, "com uma faixa confinante de 30m de cada lado" (with a 30 m adjoining strip on each side), among its partial reserves (Lei n.º 9/04, article 27(7)(g)). Because reserves are constituted by the Government (paragraph 2), whether the strip binds on a given stretch is a question for the project's counsel. The register measures each structure against that strip and any other width you give us.
  - q: Do you detect leaks, unauthorised taps or fuel theft?
    a: No. We map what is visible at the surface, such as structures, fresh excavations, spoil heaps, tracks and cleared ground, and flag it so your field teams know where to check. The pipe itself, its condition and anything underground are outside what the imagery shows.
  - q: Does the register replace the Decree 120/08 assessment?
    a: No. The assessment belongs to the Ministry and the national concessionaire, and it covers what imagery cannot show, such as who lives in each structure and what rights they hold over the land. The register gives a dated, located count of structures, fields and tracks, to size the fieldwork and to check the inventories against.
  - q: Do your reports come in Portuguese as well as English?
    a: Yes. PDF reports are delivered in Portuguese or English, so the same register can go to the Angolan land team and to an international lender, and the GIS layers open in QGIS, ArcGIS and Google Earth.

    a: No. Stations, terminals, refineries and the operator's other installations are part of the asset and are left out of the encroachment register.
  - q: Do you need access to the route or to communities?

::::section{id="problem" eyebrow="The problem" title="What changes along a pipeline between patrols" lead="Most of Angola's oil and gas is produced offshore, but the pipelines that come ashore, the terminals, the refineries and the onshore blocks sit on land where people live, farm and build."}
A right of way is long, narrow and rarely watched. Between two patrols a house goes up a few metres from the line, a new track crosses it, someone opens a field or digs sand near a valve. None of it shows at head office until someone reports it, and each week that passes makes it dearer to resolve: a compensation claim, a round of talks with the traditional authority, a delayed permit.

AfriScan gives your land and integrity teams a dated, reviewed picture of what stands inside each band, and of what has changed since the last survey.

::::section{id="the-law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The law" title="The 30 m strip and Angola's land-access regime for petroleum" lead="A summary of published rules as of 27 September 2026, with sources. It is not legal advice."}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
- **The partial reserve ([Lei n.º 9/04, article 27](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ang49570.pdf)):** partial reserves include land occupied by oil and gas installations and conductors, with a 30 m adjoining strip on each side (paragraph 7(g)). Uses that do not collide with the purpose of the reserve's founding instrument are allowed (paragraph 6). Land the State does not own enters a reserve only by expropriation or administrative servitude, and "é sempre devida indemnização justa" (fair compensation is always due; paragraphs 9–10).
- **Who constitutes the reserve (paragraph 2):** the Government. Whether the strip applies to a given stretch without its own instrument is a question for the project's counsel.
- **Pipelines ([Decree 120/08](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ang119457.pdf), article 28):** the land-access regime for petroleum operations applies to companies that build and operate oil and gas pipelines.
- **When talks fail (articles 26–27):** mediation first, then expropriation for public utility or an administrative servitude, with "uma justa, adequada e pronta indemnização".
- **Notice to landholders (article 29(2)):** at least 10 days before operations, landholders are told about the machinery, the duration, the roads and tracks, explosives, vehicles and, for seismic work, the lines.

::::section{id="decree-120-08" eyebrow="The prior assessment" title="Decree 120/08 asks for a picture of the land before, during and after" lead="Before a prospecting licence is granted or a bid round is launched, the Ministry and the national concessionaire identify and assess the burdens on the land, then update that assessment twice."}
Article 11 of Decree 120/08 requires an assessment that covers, among other points, reserved areas, "a estrutura social e populacional da área" (the area's social and population structure) and "a identificação de titulares de direitos fundiários" (who holds land rights). The social, population and land-rights results are updated before exploration starts and again before development and production (paragraph 4). The assessment covers the licence or concession area and also the areas for pipelines, gas lines and "servidões legais de passagem", adjoining land and unitisation areas (paragraph 5).

That is the kind of baseline AfriScan produces. It does not replace fieldwork, but it shows where fieldwork is needed:

::::section{id="new-routes" eyebrow="New pipelines and reroutes" title="Weigh the land before you fix the route"}
Before a route is fixed, counting the structures within 30 m and 100 m of each alternative shows which option touches fewer homes, fields and neighbourhoods, and where servitude talks will take longest. The same register, made on a known date, becomes the baseline for the negotiation and for expropriation by zones or alignments, where compensation for property outside the first zone takes account of the "benfeitorias necessárias" (necessary improvements) made between the declaration of public utility and the taking of possession ([Lei n.º 1/21, article 33](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ang201252.pdf)).

Angola's [Gas Master Plan](https://c2a.portais.gov.ao/uploads/Plano_Director_do_Gas_DP_72_25_Aprovado_d3725c0829.pdf) (Decreto Presidencial n.º 72/25) sets out a development model for an inland gas transmission pipeline, with branches and connection points to industry and power plants. It is a model, not a route; once candidate routes exist, comparing them is the first useful survey. See [route and site selection](/solutions/route-site-selection).

::::section{id="facilities" tone="alt" eyebrow="Terminals, refineries and stations" title="What grows around the fence line"}
Around terminals, refineries, pump stations and tank farms, land changes quickly: neighbourhoods edging towards the fence, new access roads, trading at the gates. We map the structures inside the zones your engineers define around each facility, with their distance to the source, and compare them between dates.

The operator's own installations are part of the asset and never count as encroachment. We never publish imagery of refineries, terminals or other oil installations: results go only to the contacts you name. We propose a drone flight near a facility only with the operator's consent, ANAC's authorisation and the other approvals and security clearances the job requires.

::::section{id="onshore-blocks" eyebrow="Onshore blocks" title="A baseline before seismic and drilling"}
In the onshore basins, such as the Kwanza and the Lower Congo, seismic lines and well sites cross farmland, villages and tracks. A dated register along the planned lines and around the pads shows where the structures and fields that the article 29 notice concerns lie, and stands as a reference for ground-damage compensation.

After the operations, a re-survey of the same places shows what changed: tracks opened, ground cleared and vegetation recovering.
::::

::::section{id="local-content" tone="alt" eyebrow="Local content" title="Before you contract a service for the petroleum sector"}
Decreto Presidencial n.º 271/20 sets exclusivity, preference and competition regimes for suppliers to Angola's petroleum sector, and goods and services on the exclusivity list may be supplied only by Angolan commercial companies. ANPG defines the lists each year and publishes them on its website. If the service is on the exclusivity list, the proposal sets out how the work can be contracted under those rules. Our [local-content notes (in Portuguese)](/ao/pt/conteudo-local) set out the rules.

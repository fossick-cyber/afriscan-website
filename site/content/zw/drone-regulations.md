lead: Drone operations in Zimbabwe are regulated by the Civil Aviation Authority of Zimbabwe (CAAZ) under the Civil Aviation (Remotely Piloted Aircraft) Regulations, 2018, S.I. 271 of 2018. Every drone needs a CAAZ letter of approval and registration, and every pilot a remote pilot licence. Companies flying for commercial, corporate or non-profit purposes need an RPAS Operator Certificate with operations specifications, and commercial operators also need an Air Services Permit. CAAZ's application adds security clearances and written approvals from landowners, local authorities and, where relevant, national parks. This page sets out the public rules and what to check before a drone team works on your wayleave, reserve or mining location.

faq:
  - q: Does a satellite corridor survey need CAAZ approval?
    a: "No aircraft flies, so S.I. 271 of 2018 does not apply to the satellite work itself, and no permit procedure for satellite analysis was found when this page was reviewed. One point is open: the Land Survey Act gives the Surveyor-General supervision and control of \"all matters pertaining to aerial and space surveys conducted and geoinformation obtained in relation to Zimbabwe\" (s.7(1)(b)). No registration, deposit or permit rule under it was found; how it applies to satellite mapping is a question for Zimbabwean counsel. Either way, imagery of prohibited or protected places is never published."
  - q: Can a foreign company register and fly a drone in Zimbabwe?
    a: "Registration is open only to Zimbabwean citizens, permanent residents and bodies whose principal place of business is in Zimbabwe, and a drone registered outside Zimbabwe cannot be registered (s.6). A drone can be imported temporarily for commercial use (s.7), but an RPAS Operator Certificate application includes the certificate of registration of each drone it covers (s.16(2)(a)). CAAZ's fee schedule also lists a \"Foreign Operator's Permit\" that no section of the regulations describes, so ask CAAZ in writing what it allows. Read together, the rules point to two routes: a Zimbabwe-registered company with its own approvals, or a Zimbabwean operator whose certificate covers the work."
  - q: How long does a remote pilot licence last?
    a: Two years. The revalidation check falls in the 90 days before expiry, a licence cannot be renewed once three years have passed since it expired, and pilots keep their logbooks for 5 years (S.I. 271, ss.8–14).
  - q: Can a drone fly beyond the pilot's sight along a power line or pipeline?
    a: Only for an operator whose RPAS Operator Certificate and operations specifications cover beyond-visual-line-of-sight flight, with CAAZ approval, in visual meteorological conditions and below 400 ft unless approved otherwise (s.40). Otherwise a corridor is flown in legs within the pilot's sight up to 500 m away, or with a trained observer keeping visual contact up to 1,000 m from the pilot (s.2).
  - q: Do we need permission to fly near our own substation, pump station or depot?
    a: "A drone may not fly within 30 m of a structure or building unless it is an approved operation under the operator's certificate and the owner has given permission (s.43), so your written permission is part of the file for your own assets. Operations \"adjacent to or above\" a national key point or strategic installation need CAAZ approval (s.39(3)); no list of key points is public. Protected-place orders are served on the owner or occupier rather than published, so your security team is the one that knows whether a site has that status."
  - q: How long does CAAZ take to decide?
    a: The regulations give CAAZ 30 days to decide a complete application for a letter of approval and for registration (ss.4–5), and CAAZ's FAQ says most complete registration applications are reviewed within one working week. The security clearances CAAZ's form refers to have no published procedure or timetable, so plan for them separately.
  - q: Can the drone orthophoto be used as a land survey?
    a: Not on its own. Surveys for Deeds Registry diagrams and general plans, and boundary and beacon surveys, are reserved to practising land surveyors (Land Survey Act s.16). Where a deliverable must be relied on as a survey, it needs a registered land surveyor's certification. Our registers and change reports are mapping records built from imagery; they show your surveyor and your wayleave officers where to look.

A commercial drone job also needs the security clearances and written approvals CAAZ's application refers to."}
:::cards{cols="4"}
:::card{title="Each aircraft" icon="drone" eyebrow="CAAZ"}
A **letter of approval** (s.4) and **registration** by CAAZ (s.5): "No remotely piloted aircraft shall be operated within Zimbabwe, unless it has been registered by the Authority."
:::
:::card{title="Each pilot" icon="user-check" eyebrow="CAAZ"}
A **remote pilot licence**, valid for 2 years, with English at level 4 and a restricted radiotelephony certificate (ss.8–14).
:::
:::card{title="The operator" icon="building" eyebrow="CAAZ"}
An **RPAS Operator Certificate (ROC)** with **operations specifications**, valid for 1 year, for commercial, corporate and non-profit operations (ss.15–21).
:::
:::card{title="The air service" icon="file-check" eyebrow="Ministry responsible for Transport"}
An **Air Services Permit** for commercial operations (s.15(b)), applied for to the Ministry responsible for Transport ([S.I. 69 of 2018](https://www.caaz.co.zw/download/aerial-work-si-69-of-2018/), s.3(1)).
:::
:::

Around those approvals sits the site. CAAZ's online application for organisations makes the applicant declare that it will obtain "all security clearances from Ministry of Defense, Ministry of Information Communication Technology and any other Agencies or Line Ministries as maybe appropriate", and that it will fly only "after written Approval letters from land owners concerned, local authorities, national parks, state etc. in charge of the property/area of operation" ([CAAZ online application](https://caaz.co.zw/online-payments/apply/drone)). Along a pipeline or a line that crosses many farms, communal land and council areas, those letters and clearances are usually the longest part of the plan.

S.I. 271 of 2018 is the current instrument: no later drone regulation appears on [CAAZ's legislation page](https://www.caaz.co.zw/legislation/) or in the Veritas index of statutory instruments, as checked on 27 September 2026.

::::section{id="aircraft" eyebrow="The aircraft" title="Letter of approval, registration, and who may register"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Letter of approval (s.4).** It comes before registration. CAAZ decides within 30 days, the letter is valid for 12 months at first and then for 3 years, and applications must be in English.

**Registration (s.5).** CAAZ inspects the aircraft and will "vet" the owner or operator. It decides within 30 days; its FAQ says "most complete applications will be reviewed within one working week". A change of ownership must be applied for within 30 days.

**Who may register (s.6).** A Zimbabwean citizen or permanent resident, or a body "whose principal place of business is in Zimbabwe". A drone registered outside Zimbabwe, or owned by someone who is not eligible, cannot be registered.

**Classes (First Schedule).** Class 1 is below 2 kg and receives an approval letter; classes 2 to 7 run from 2 kg to larger than 150 kg and receive a certificate of registration. CAAZ's FAQ says "all flying RPAs/drones should be registered", and its [2025 annual report](https://www.caaz.co.zw/download/caaz-2025-annual-report-2/) describes drones under 2 kg receiving letters of approval for custody.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="What an organisation files"}
CAAZ's online form asks a company for:

- its representative's Zimbabwe national ID or valid passport
- the Certificate of Incorporation
- the registered office notice
- the list of directors and secretaries
- a Tax Clearance Certificate
- proof of drone ownership
- a "Letter of resolution from the majority of the board (two thirds)"

"All documents must be provided in English." The purposes offered include aerial survey.

::::section{id="pilot" tone="alt" eyebrow="The pilot" title="The remote pilot licence"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Categories and ratings" icon="clipboard"}
Aeroplane, multi-rotor or hybrid VTOL, rated for VLOS, E-VLOS or B-VLOS flight. Pilots are 18 or over.
:::
:::card{title="Medical, radio and English" icon="user-check"}
A class 4 medical certificate for beyond-sight flight or aircraft of class 3 and above, a self-declared medical assessment otherwise; a restricted Certificate of Proficiency in Radiotelephony (Aeronautical); English at level 4.
:::
:::card{title="Exam, training and test" icon="file-check"}
A theory exam at an accredited centre within 90 days before the skills test, CAAZ-approved flight training, the skills test within 30 days of training, and the licence application within 30 days of the test.
:::
:::

The licence is **valid for 2 years**, with a revalidation check in the 90 days before it expires. It cannot be renewed once three years have passed since it expired, and logbooks are kept for 5 years (S.I. 271, ss.8–14).
::::

::::section{id="operator" eyebrow="The operator" title="The RPAS Operator Certificate and the Air Services Permit"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**The ROC (ss.15–21).** Commercial, corporate and non-profit operations need an RPAS Operator Certificate with operations specifications. The application includes the certificate of registration and the letter of approval of **each** drone the certificate will cover, and the original operations manual for a first issue. A drone is registered under one ROC only; one ROC can list several drones, and it cannot be transferred. It is **valid for 1 year**, and renewal is applied for at least 60 days ahead. Extended line-of-sight work needs at least one trained observer aged 18 or over (s.21(2)).

**The Air Services Permit (s.15(b)).** Commercial operators also need an Air Services Permit, applied for to the Ministry responsible for Transport under S.I. 69 of 2018, s.3(1). Whether the aerial-work operator certificate in S.I. 69 of 2018 also applies to drones on top of the ROC is a question to put to CAAZ in writing.

**Frequencies (s.45(5)–(6)).** CAAZ allocates the frequencies the drone uses, on application.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="What the operator must keep up"}
- An approved operations manual (s.22) and records kept for 2 years (s.23)
- A safety management system (s.24)
- Security (s.25): background checks, criminal record checks every two years, security awareness training, secure storage and a security coordinator
- CAAZ audits, which can extend to "partners, suppliers or subcontractors" (s.26)
- Third-party insurance "at all times" (s.28)
- An approved C2 datalink (s.36) and maintenance programme (s.56(2))
- CAAZ approval for accessory equipment "not covered in the original design" (s.68), such as a new sensor payload

::::section{id="clearances" tone="alt" eyebrow="Security, land and restricted places" title="The clearances and letters around every flight"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Security clearances and land approvals

CAAZ's organisation form commits the applicant to obtain the security clearances of the Ministry of Defence, the Ministry of ICT and any other agency or line ministry that is appropriate, and to fly only with written approval letters from the landowners, local authorities, national parks and state bodies in charge of the area. No public procedure or timetable for the clearances was found. CAAZ's FAQ says a "ZIMRA or Defense clearance" for an imported drone "may be obtained at the port of entry".

For a corridor, that means letters from the owners and occupiers along the route, the rural district or urban councils, the parks authority where the route crosses a park, and the asset owner within 30 m of towers, pump stations and substations (s.43). Take-off or landing on a public road also needs the local authority (s.30).
::::
::::col
### Prohibited and protected places

- **Official Secrets Act [Chapter 11:09], s.3.** For "any purpose prejudicial to the safety or interests of Zimbabwe", it is an offence to approach, inspect, pass over or enter a prohibited place, or to obtain, record or publish information useful to an enemy, with up to 25 years' imprisonment. Prohibited places include defence works, military and air force establishments and places the President declares by order (ss.2 and 13) ([Veritas](https://www.veritaszim.net/node/230)).
- **Protected Places and Areas Act [Chapter 11:12].** The Minister of Home Affairs may declare a protected place or area (ss.4–6). The order is served on the owner or occupier by a police officer (s.7) and is not published, and its directions may restrict "surveying or making sketches or taking photographs". Whether those directions reach overflight or remote imaging is a question for counsel.
- **Key points (s.39(3)).** Operations "adjacent to or above" a national key point or strategic installation need CAAZ approval. No list is public; the asset owner is the one to ask.

::::section{id="flying" eyebrow="Operating rules" title="The Part V rules that shape a corridor flight"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Only with CAAZ approval (s.39(3))" icon="alert"}
Above 400 ft; within 3 NM of an aerodrome; in restricted or prohibited airspace; adjacent to or above a nuclear power plant, prison, police station, crime scene, court, national key point or strategic installation. This is an approval route for an operator whose certificate covers it, not an outright ban.
:::
:::card{title="Line of sight (ss.2, 40)" icon="eye"}
VLOS: below 400 ft and within 500 m. E-VLOS: below 400 ft, with an observer up to 1,000 m from the pilot. BVLOS only with CAAZ approval under the operator's certificate, in visual conditions and below 400 ft unless approved.
:::
:::card{title="People, structures, roads (ss.42–44)" icon="houses"}
Not over or within 30 m of people, unless they are part of an approved operation; not within 30 m of a structure without an approved operation **and** the owner's permission; not over, along or within 30 m of a public road without approval and safety measures.
:::
:::card{title="Night (s.41)" icon="clock"}
Only reduced line of sight, or an operator whose certificate approves each drone for night flight. Night flight in controlled airspace needs a separate approval.
:::
:::card{title="Aerodromes and radio (ss.31, 44, 45)" icon="flag"}
Never over an aerodrome or approach path without approval. Within 3 NM of an aerodrome the pilot carries an air-band radio unless flying reduced line of sight, and uses the registration as the call-sign.
:::
:::card{title="Reports and defects (s.69)" icon="file-text"}
Defects are notified to CAAZ within 24 hours for drones of 20 kg or more, and for lighter drones after serious incidents or accidents.
:::
:::

**What this means for a pipeline or line.** Plan legs of up to 500 m within sight, or up to 1,000 m with an observer; expect to need the asset owner's letter near every tower, pump station and substation; and check the 3 NM aerodrome rule and the 30 m road rule at every crossing. Other rules: no towing, aerobatics or swarm flights without approval (s.39(2)), nothing dropped (s.32), no alcohol for 8 hours before duty (s.35), a first-aid kit and a fire extinguisher at the ground station (ss.53–54), and way given to manned aircraft (s.49).

::::section{id="import" tone="alt" eyebrow="Importing a drone" title="Temporary import, pre-clearance and the port of entry"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
S.I. 271 allows temporary import for "private, corporate, non-profit operations or commercial use" (s.7). It needs:

- CAAZ **pre-clearance at least 30 days before** entry, giving the owner, country of registration, model, serial number, weight, specifications, purpose, period and place of operation; the pre-clearance is valid for 3 months;
- a **ZIMRA temporary import permit** on entry;
- at the border, the clearance CAAZ's FAQ describes: ZIMRA or Defence clearance "may be obtained at the port of entry into the country and upon declaration that you would like to clear your RPA/drone".

Sellers must tell buyers about CAAZ's requirements (s.64), and maintenance technicians must be citizens, permanent residents or work-permit holders (s.58).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Temporary import is not an operator certificate"}
A drone imported under s.7 is still not registered in Zimbabwe, and an ROC covers only registered drones (s.16). Ask the operator which Zimbabwean registration and certificate each drone on your job flies under.

::::section{id="survey-data" eyebrow="Survey and data" title="What the orthophoto is, and who holds the data"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Survey law

The [Land Survey Act [Chapter 20:12]](https://www.veritaszim.net/node/207) reserves surveys for Deeds Registry diagrams and general plans, and boundary and beacon surveys, to practising land surveyors, and forbids anyone else to "hold himself out in any manner whatever as a land surveyor" (s.16). Section 7(1)(b) gives the Surveyor-General supervision and control of "all matters pertaining to aerial and space surveys conducted and geoinformation obtained in relation to Zimbabwe"; no registration, deposit or permit rule under it was found.

A drone orthophoto, and a structure register built from it, is a mapping record. Where your deliverable must be relied on as a survey, add certification by a registered land surveyor to the scope.
::::
::::col
### Personal data

- **The authority.** Under the Cyber and Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07], POTRAZ is the Data Protection Authority (s.5). Structure registers linked to occupants, as in resettlement work, are personal information.
- **Licensing.** S.I. 155 of 2024 requires a **data controller licence**, in tiers from 50 data subjects upwards, valid for 12 months, and a data protection officer.
- **Breaches.** POTRAZ must be notified within 24 hours on form DP3, and data subjects within 72 hours when the risk is high.
- **Transfers abroad.** POTRAZ is notified first, with a DPIA, express consent, adequacy and a data-sharing agreement (CDPG 5 of 2024). A controller not established in Zimbabwe that uses means located there designates a local representative (s.4(3)).
- **Processors.** A processor works under a written contract with the controller (s.18(5)). Veritas reports POTRAZ inspections from 1 September 2026.

::::section{id="penalties" tone="alt" eyebrow="Penalties" title="Why the paperwork matters"}
:::facts{cols="3"}
- Flying an unregistered drone: up to level 14 or 15 years (S.I. 271 s.5(11))
- Breaking a Part V operating rule: up to level 14 or 2 years (s.55)
- Commercial aerial work without the certificate: up to level 12 or 1 year (S.I. 69 of 2018 s.3(10))
- Official Secrets Act s.3: up to 25 years
- Protected Places and Areas Act: up to level 7 or 2 years
- Holding out as a land surveyor: up to level 6 or 6 months (Land Survey Act s.16)
:::

The Civil Aviation Amendment Act, 2024 also adds a civil-penalty order regime to the Civil Aviation Act. Processing personal information without a data controller licence carries up to level 11 or 7 years (Cyber and Data Protection Act; S.I. 155 of 2024).

::::section{id="setbacks" tone="alt" eyebrow="The distances in the Act" title="What the Mines and Minerals Act keeps mining away from"}
The Mines and Minerals Act [Chapter 21:05] closes some ground to prospecting without consent (s.31) and keeps mining itself away from infrastructure (s.34) ([Mines and Minerals Act](https://web.archive.org/web/20251006131822/https://zimlii.org/akn/zw/act/1961/38/eng@2016-12-31)). Each distance is a band we can report, around your boundary or around the working areas you define.

| Near | Distance | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A principal homestead, built or registered as an intended site | 450 m | Not open to prospecting without the owner's, or on communal land the occupier's, written consent (s.31) |
| Farm-worker housing, dip tanks, and buildings and permanent improvements above a value threshold | 90 m | The same (s.31) |
| Land under cultivation | On it or within 15 m | The same (s.31) |
| Other farm buildings | 9 m | The same, unless compensated (s.31) |
| The middle of a road | 15 m | No mining (s.34) |
| A railway track | 45 m | No mining, including mining buildings (s.34) |
| A pipeline | 5 m, or 25 m for asbestos pipelines over 30 cm | No prospecting or mining without the owner's consent (s.34(7)) |
| A power line | 10, 25 or 40 m from the centre line, by voltage | No prospecting or mining (s.34(8)) |

Section 31 also closes other mining locations, town limits plus a 50 m belt, aerodromes, State rifle ranges, any railway reserve, cemeteries, holdings of 100 ha or less without consent, and "Communal Land occupied as a village without the written consent of the rural district council". Around pipelines the 2026 Pipelines Act adds a wider rule: no mining or blasting within 125 m of either side of the centre ([Act No. 5 of 2025](https://www.veritaszim.net/node/7892)).

:::callout{tone="legal" title="Checked 27 September 2026"}
Read from ZimLII's consolidated text of the Act. Parliament is considering a new Mines and Minerals Bill (H.B. 1, 2025), which was still before the National Assembly on 22 September 2026 ([Veritas](https://www.veritaszim.net/node/8112)). This is a summary, not legal advice.
:::

::::section{id="excavation" eyebrow="Pits, spoil and cleared ground" title="A dated map of the ground disturbance on and around your location"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
New pits, spoil heaps, water-filled excavations, cleared ground and new tracks inside your location and in a ring around it are mapped from dated imagery, with new sites flagged between surveys and confirmed by a reviewer. Each record carries its coordinates, its distance to your boundary and to the working areas you define, and the imagery date on which it first appears. In the rainy season, radar comparisons show larger ground changes even under cloud, and flagged areas are followed up with optical imagery or your own drone flights.

What we map is the ground. We never identify, count or follow people, and underground workings and covered shafts are not visible from above. Artisanal mining is one of the sectors reserved exclusively for Zimbabweans ([S.I. 215 of 2025](https://www.veritaszim.net/node/7808)); whether a mapped pit is on your ground, and who holds rights to it, is for your tenure team and the authorities. A flagged site is an indication for your teams to check, not proof of anything.

::::section{id="expansions" tone="alt" eyebrow="Plants, dumps and new footprints" title="Record the footprint before it changes"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Mineral prospecting and mining are First Schedule projects under the Environmental Management Act and need an EMA certificate before they are implemented (s.97). Beneficiation policy is adding plant: Cabinet announced on 10 June 2025 that lithium sulphate value-addition facilities are being established and that "with effect from January 2027 the export of lithium concentrate will no longer be allowed", and linked new chrome titles above 100 ha to furnace capacity ([Post-Cabinet briefing](https://www.veritaszim.net/node/7547)).

When a plant, pit, dump or tailings facility is planned, we produce a dated record of the structures in the footprint, with reviewer categories such as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction, and on request estimated households with the assumptions stated. It supports the census, asset inventory and cut-off-date records; categories describe what the imagery shows, not use, ownership or value. For blast radii and tailings zones, your engineers supply the zones and we list the structures inside them.

::::section{id="your-imagery" tone="alt" eyebrow="Already flying drones?" title="We analyse your own imagery"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
CAAZ's 2025 annual report records 54 drones over 2 kg registered that year. If your team or a contractor already flies the location, the flying is done; turning each set of orthophotos into a list of what is new, where and how close it is to your boundary is the part we add. Send the orthophotos or GeoTIFFs and we run the excavation, structure and change analysis on them, with a person reviewing every result and dated satellite scenes between flights.

New drone surveys, where you want them, are subject to the CAAZ approvals and security clearances each operation requires, including written approvals from landowners and local authorities and the owner's permission to fly within 30 m of any structure. Afridrone is working towards the approvals Zimbabwe requires.

:::callout{tone="legal" title="Personal data on a mine"}
A register of structures linked to occupants is personal information under the Cyber and Data Protection Act. POTRAZ is the Data Protection Authority, and Veritas reports POTRAZ inspections from 1 September 2026, with "mining enterprises" fifth of the nine categories listed ([Veritas](https://www.veritaszim.net/node/8052)). Settle the controller and processor roles, and whether a data controller licence is needed, before a register is built.

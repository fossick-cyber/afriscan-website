::::section{id="protected-strips" tone="alt" eyebrow="The protected strips" title="What the transmission-line protection regulations set" lead="A summary of the public rules as of 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
:::::columns{split="1-1" align="center"}
::::col
The Volta River Authority (Transmission Line Protection) Regulations 1967 ([L.I. 542, Gazette of 5 May 1967](https://ghalii.org/akn/gh/officialGazette/government-gazette/1967-05-05/34/eng@1967-05-05)), made under the Volta River Development Act 1961 (Act 46) and amended by the (Amendment) Regulations 2004 ([L.I. 1737, Gazette of 20 February 2004](https://ghalii.org/akn/gh/officialGazette/government-gazette/2004-02-20/8/eng@2004-02-20)), protect the land along high-voltage lines.

As GRIDCo, the national transmission company, described them in September 2026, they prohibit "drilling, excavation, lorry parks, shops, garages, barns and real estate development within a distance of 15 metres on either side of the centre of 161-kilovolt (kV) transmission lines and 20 metres for 330kV lines", and larger 330 kV tower bases need wider protected areas ([GNA, 4 September 2026](https://gna.org.gh/2026/09/gridco-intensifies-campaign-against-encroachment-on-transmission-corridors/)).

We report those strips by default for 161 kV and 330 kV lines, and measure to the widths you give us for other voltages and for distribution lines.

::::section{id="register" eyebrow="What we map along the line" title="A register of what stands in the strip, then what changed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The first survey lists each structure the review confirms inside the protected strip and in the wider bands you choose, with an ID, its distance to the centre line, its band, its chainage and its coordinates. Results are grouped by 500 m segment, or by span where you send the tower positions, and each segment is rated high, medium or low by the number of structures near the line, so land-management teams can plan their visits.

Re-surveys then flag what changed: new and removed structures, fresh excavation near towers, cleared ground and new tracks across the corridor, each confirmed by a reviewer, with before-and-after views. A change notice after each scheduled survey says what changed and where. Reviewers can also tag each structure with what the imagery shows, such as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction.

::::section{id="new-lines" tone="alt" eyebrow="New lines and upgrades" title="Count the structures before the route is fixed"}
For a new line, an interconnector or an upgrade, we compare route options side by side: the structures within set distances of each alignment, how built-up land has grown near each one, and the stretches that cross steep or flood-prone ground. Once the route is chosen, a dated register along it is the baseline for acquisition and compensation, and later re-surveys show what appeared after that date.

Customary land matters here. Stool, skin and family land is administered with chiefs, and the Office of the Administrator of Stool Lands collects ground rent, so the engagement plan for a new line starts with traditional authorities as well as the District Assembly. A shared, dated map helps both.

::::section{id="drones" eyebrow="Drones near high-tension lines" title="Why transmission work in Ghana starts from satellite"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Drone flights near high-tension wires need a special GCAA authorisation, because Part 28 lists areas of high radio interference among the operations that need one. Flights within 30 m of buildings, vehicles or groups of people need explicit permission, and flights over congested areas need GCAA permission unless the drone can land safely in an emergency. Power stations are also on the GCAA's list of no-fly facilities.

So the whole line is screened from satellite first, and drone checks are planned only for the spans that need more detail, subject to the GCAA authorisations and security clearances each job requires. Where your own line teams already fly drones, we run the analysis on their imagery.

::::section{id="summary" eyebrow="In short" title="Five questions every supplier file in Ghana should answer"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Who holds the contract?" icon="user-check"}
On a mining lease, a Ghanaian firm that meets the ownership test wherever the local procurement list reserves the service. We treat our own mapping work as reserved under item 35 until counsel settles it. On upstream petroleum work, a company with a Petroleum Commission permit.
:::
:::card{title="Which permits apply?" icon="file-check"}
The Petroleum Commission permit for upstream work, and an environmental permit where the project itself is an undertaking listed in L.I. 2504.
:::
:::card{title="Where is the supplier registered?" icon="clipboard"}
GHANEPS for government procurement, the PPA Supplier Portal, and company registration with GIPA, renewed every year.
:::
:::card{title="Who flies the drone?" icon="drone"}
If the scope includes drone work, a company registered in Ghana whose GCAA ROC and Operations Specifications cover the job.
:::
:::card{title="Who handles personal data?" icon="lock"}
Who is controller and who is processor, whether each is registered with the Data Protection Commission, and where imagery and results are processed and stored.

::::section{id="mining" tone="alt" eyebrow="Mining local content" title="The Minerals Commission's local procurement list"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Mining procurement in Ghana follows L.I. 2431 and the Minerals Commission's [6th-edition Local Procurement List](https://www.mincom.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Local-Content-Sixth-Edition-2025.pdf), in force from 1 January 2025 and still the latest on the Commission's [local-content page](https://www.mincom.gov.gh/local-content/) on 27 September 2026. Among its items:

| Item | Service | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Security services | Exclusive to Ghanaian firms |
| 34 | Civil works | Exclusive to Ghanaian firms |
| 35 | "Geophysical Ground-based Survey; Mapping, Pitting and Trenching" | "Strictly Incorporated in Ghana with Exclusive Ghanaian Directors and Shareholders" |
| 36 | "Geophysical Airborne-based Survey" | Incorporated in Ghana, with at least 30 per cent Ghanaian directors and shareholders |
| 43 | Tailings | Exclusive to Ghanaian firms |

The Commission's chief executive said in February 2026 that local content is to be "embedded in every mining agreement, procurement decision and employment policy" ([Minerals Commission](https://www.mincom.gov.gh/minerals-commission-chief-unveils-reforms-to-boost-ghanaian-ownership/)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="How we work on a mining lease"}
- A qualifying Ghanaian firm holds the contract with the lease holder.
- AfriScan is its technology supplier: imagery analysis, reviewed registers, GIS layers and reports.
- The work is land and environmental mapping. It is never offered or described as security.
- Any drone flight is flown by a company registered in Ghana whose ROC covers it.
:::

::::section{id="petroleum" eyebrow="Petroleum" title="The Petroleum Commission permit and local participation"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Service companies working in upstream petroleum need a permit from the Petroleum Commission, set up under Act 821, "before commencement of operations", and the permit is renewed every year ([Petroleum Commission](https://petrocom.gov.gh/initial-registration-permit/)). The application asks for the certificate of incorporation and certificate to commence business, company regulations, tax, SSNIT and VAT certificates, a signed HSE policy, a business plan and company profile, financial statements, and certificates from professional bodies such as an "EPA Permit, Air Operator Certificate, NPA Licence, Aviation Licence".
::::
::::col
The Commission lists the petroleum local-content regulations, L.I. 2204 of 2013 as amended by L.I. 2435 of 2021, among its [laws and regulations](https://www.petrocom.gov.gh/laws-regulations/), with guidance that names joint ventures, channel partnerships and strategic alliances as routes for local participation. For an upstream contract, the proposal states which company holds the permit and how local participation is arranged.

Downstream petroleum is regulated by the National Petroleum Authority (Act 691), and electricity and natural-gas licensing by the Energy Commission, which also has local-content regulations for the power sector (L.I. 2354).

::::section{id="public-procurement" tone="alt" eyebrow="Public procurement" title="GHANEPS and the PPA Supplier Portal"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Public bodies buy under the Public Procurement Act 2003 (Act 663), as amended by Act 914. The Ghana Electronic Procurement System, [GHANEPS](https://www.ghaneps.gov.gh/), is mandatory "for all government procurement" under a directive of 18 September 2023 and the notice of 23 November 2023, and suppliers register on it. The Public Procurement Authority also runs a [Supplier Portal](https://suppliers.ppa.gov.gh).
::::
::::col
State utilities and agencies publish their own adverts and tenders as well. Tell us the portal, the tender reference and the prequalification category your process uses, and send any supplier forms with your request.

We respond to tenders, RFQs and requests for information; the proposal names the company that holds each registration your contract needs.

::::section{id="company" eyebrow="Company registration and drones" title="GIPA registration, and who may fly"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The Ghana Investment Promotion Authority Act, 2026 (Act 1173), assented to on 15 July 2026, replaced the GIPC Act 2013. According to the [Authority's notice](https://gipa.gov.gh/ghana-investment-promotion-centre-gipc-now-an-authority/), it removes the blanket minimum-capital requirements for wholly foreign-owned enterprises and joint ventures, keeps a reduced threshold for trading enterprises, and requires registered enterprises to renew their registration every year.
::::
::::col
Commercial drone work needs a GCAA RPAS Operator Certificate, and only a company registered under the laws of Ghana can complete certification. For evaluation, split any scope into its satellite and desk-based part, which needs no aviation approval, and its drone part, which does. Afridrone is working towards the authorisations Ghana requires; every proposal with a drone element names the company that will fly it.

::::section{id="data" tone="alt" eyebrow="Data protection" title="The Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843)"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The Data Protection Commission says that "all entities involved in processing personal data are required to register" under s.27(1) of the Data Protection Act 2012, naming data controllers, data processors and "Foreign Companies – International businesses processing personal data within Ghana" ([DPC registration](https://dpc.gov.gh/registration/)). Processing while unregistered is barred (s.53) and is an offence (s.56). The application asks, among other things, for the countries to which personal data will be transferred and for the applicant's security measures; renewal needs a compliance report, and the Commission's guidance calls for a Data Protection Supervisor.

Aerial and satellite imagery of homes and compounds can contain personal data. The contract should say who is controller and who is processor, where imagery and results are processed and stored, which countries data goes to, and how long it is kept.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="How we keep personal data down"}
- Registers record structures and coordinates, not names.
- We map land and assets, never people.
- Results go only to the contacts you name.
- The data terms for each project state where data is processed and stored, and for how long.

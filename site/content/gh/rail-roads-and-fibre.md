:::cards{cols="2"}
:::card{title="“Someone has been digging along our cable route.”" icon="excavation"}
Road works, building foundations, borrow pits and, in mining districts, galamsey pits can open up along a buried cable. Each shows at the surface as fresh digging, spoil and cleared ground, and each is easier to deal with when it is found early.
:::
:::card{title="“Structures have gone up in the railway reserve.”" icon="rail"}
Where a line has been quiet for years, sheds, stalls, walls and houses can appear inside the reserve. Before rehabilitation or new services, the operator needs a dated picture of what stands there.
:::
:::card{title="“The widening needs its land counted.”" icon="route"}
A road widening or a new alignment starts with how many structures stand within the land it will take, and how that number is changing as towns grow along the road.
:::
:::card{title="“We have to show progress on site.”" icon="layers"}
Contractors, lenders and supervising engineers need dated evidence of clearing, earthworks and construction between site visits.

::::section{id="fibre" tone="alt" eyebrow="Fibre and cable wayleaves" title="New digging along buried routes, flagged between surveys"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A buried fibre route leaves little to see from above, but the ground around it does. We follow the route you send and a band either side of it, and flag fresh excavations, trenches, pits and spoil heaps, cleared ground, new tracks and new structures between dated surveys. A reviewer confirms each flag, and your field teams receive a ranked list of stretches where the ground has changed, with before-and-after views of each.

In gold-mining districts, the same method shows galamsey pits and washing sites opening near the route, as land and water change. Along roads, it shows where other works are digging close to your cable.

Laying a new route is itself regulated: "Cable or Fibre Optic Cable Laying" is one of the undertakings that need an environmental permit under the [Environmental Assessment Regulations 2025 (L.I. 2504)](https://www.epa.gov.gh/new/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Environmental-Protection-Environmental-Assessment-Regulations-2025-2.pdf). A dated baseline along the planned route supports that application, and the first survey after laying becomes the reference for later change.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What satellites can and cannot show"}
- Fresh digging, trenches, pits and spoil at the surface
- Cleared ground, new tracks and new structures
- Change between two dated surveys

They cannot see a cable, a cut, theft or people. A flag is a reason to check the stretch, not proof of damage.

::::section{id="rail" eyebrow="Railway reserves" title="What stands inside the reserve, and what crosses it"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Railways in Ghana are governed by the Railways Act 2008 (Act 779), under which the Ghana Railway Development Authority works. We list the structures the review confirms inside the reserve width you give us, with their distance to the track centreline or the reserve boundary, their chainage and coordinates, and rate each 500 m segment by the number of structures near the track.

Between surveys, we flag new and removed structures, cleared ground, and new tracks, roads and trenches that cross the reserve. The Act provides for licences for a road or utility line across a railway line, so a list of new crossings, with the date each first appears, helps the railway authority check them.

Before a line is rehabilitated, extended or reopened, a dated register of the reserve is the baseline for engagement and compensation planning, and later surveys record construction progress against it.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="roads" tone="alt" eyebrow="Road corridors" title="Count the structures before the land take"}
For a road widening, a new bypass or a new alignment, we count the structures within the distances you set from each option, show how built-up land near each one has grown over recent years, and flag stretches that cross steep, erosion-prone, low-lying or river-crossing ground. Once the alignment is fixed, a dated register of the land it takes becomes the baseline for acquisition and compensation, and re-surveys show what appeared after that date.

Road construction and railway construction both need an environmental permit under L.I. 2504, whose baseline includes a location map of communities, water bodies, forest reserves and sacred groves. We supply those layers to the consultants who prepare the report.

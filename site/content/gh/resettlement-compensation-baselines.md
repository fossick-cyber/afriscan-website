faq:
  - q: Does the imagery set the cut-off date?
    a: No. Under L.I. 2175 the cut-off date is the date agreed by the holder of the mineral right and the Minerals Commission "which is published in the relevant district" (reg. 16). On lender-financed projects, IFC Performance Standard 5 asks for it to be clearly established and made public. Our register records what stood in the footprint on the imagery dates closest to it; it supports the cut-off-date record rather than defining it.
  - q: Is the register a substitute for the census, the housing inventory or the cadastral maps?
    a: No. L.I. 2175 asks the resettlement plan for a demographic survey, "a housing inventory and a survey of conditions of buildings", tenure identification and cadastral maps at 1:2500. The census and inventories stay with your field teams, and the cadastral maps with a professional land surveyor. The register tells them where the structures are, how many to expect and which ones appeared after the cut-off date.
  - q: Can you show what was built after the cut-off date?
    a: Yes. Re-surveys of the footprint flag new structures between dated surveys, a reviewer confirms each one, and it enters the register with the first date on which it appears. Whether a structure counts as "speculative development" under the regulations is for the process to decide, not for the imagery.
  - q: Can the register be presented at the public forum?
    a: That is your decision. L.I. 2175 has the draft baseline report presented at a public forum with the District Assembly, traditional authorities, civil society, the EPA officer and the inhabitants (reg. 9). A plain map and register, with the imagery dates on every page, is easy to show there.
  - q: Do you identify the households?
    a: No. We map structures, not people, and the register holds no names. Where you need a planning figure before the census, we estimate households from structure counts, with the assumptions stated, for your team to replace with census data.
  - q: Does this apply outside mining?
    a: Yes. Pipelines, power lines, roads and other projects that acquire land need the same dated record, for compulsory acquisition under Ghana's land law, for wayleave processes and, where lenders are involved, for IFC Performance Standard 5 or World Bank ESS5.

:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### The Constitution and the land law {#constitution}

Article 20 of the [1992 Constitution](https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Ghana_1996) allows compulsory acquisition only for public purposes it lists, with "the prompt payment of fair and adequate compensation" and access to the High Court, and requires displaced inhabitants to be resettled "on suitable alternative land with due regard for their economic well-being and social and cultural values" (art. 20(3)). Compulsory acquisition now runs under the Land Act 2020 (Act 1036).

### Mining: Act 703 and L.I. 2175 {#mining-rules}

The [Minerals and Mining Act 2006 (Act 703)](https://www.mincom.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Minerals-and-Mining-Act-2006-Act-703.pdf) sets the compensation principles for land in a mining area (ss.72–74), including the crop survey and crop identification map (s.72(5)). The [Compensation and Resettlement Regulations 2012 (L.I. 2175)](https://www.mincom.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Minerals-and-Mining-Compensation-Resettlement-Regulations-2012-L.I.-2175.pdf) then set the procedure:

- **reg. 1:** notices posted in public places "including markets, churches, mosques and schools", a period for claims, and no compensation for "speculative development on land subject to a mineral right after the cut-off date" (1(8));
- **reg. 3:** crops at annual rates, and immovable property at "full replacement cost";
- **regs 4 and 5:** payment within a set period after determination, interest while it stays unpaid, and a claimant's right to apply to the High Court;
- **regs 6–12:** the resettlement plan, with a land-use study, a demographic survey, "a housing inventory and a survey of conditions of buildings", tenure identification and cadastral maps at 1:2500 (reg. 8), and a draft baseline report presented at a public forum (reg. 9);
- **reg. 16:** "speculative development" is any improvement "after the cut-off date that is intended to merit or enhance compensation to a person who is otherwise not entitled to that compensation".
::::
::::col
### Lenders: IFC Performance Standard 5 {#ps5}

Lender-financed projects add [IFC Performance Standard 5](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2012-ifc-performance-standard-5-en.pdf), or the World Bank's ESS5 on Bank-financed ones. PS5 asks for a census, in part to discourage ineligible persons from claiming benefits (para. 12), and does not require compensation for those who move into the project area after a cut-off date that has been clearly established and made public (para. 23).

:::callout{tone="legal" title="What a dated register supports"}
A dated, reproducible record of the structures in the footprint serves both frameworks: it shows what stood where on the date that matters. It supports your census, your housing inventory and your PS5 cut-off-date records. Setting the cut-off date, deciding who is entitled, and the census, inventories, cadastral maps and consultations all stay with your team and the authorities.

::::section{id="dated-imagery" eyebrow="The imagery" title="Why the date on the imagery decides the value of the record"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A cut-off record is only as strong as the date on the imagery behind it. We build it from sources with a known capture date: very-high-resolution satellite scenes from commercial archives, a new satellite capture where the archive is too old, your own georeferenced drone imagery, or a drone survey flown subject to the GCAA authorisations and security clearances each job requires. Map-service basemaps, which carry no reliable date, are never used as the record.

Archive coverage varies from place to place, and a new capture depends on satellite availability and weather, so its date is never guaranteed. Before you commit, we tell you which dated scenes exist closest to your cut-off date, and the proposal sets out the plan. Where the footprint has changed in the past, the imagery history shows what was built or cleared, and roughly when.

### The crop survey {#crop-survey}

Act 703 s.72(5) has the lease holder carry out "a survey of the crops and produce a crop identification map", in the presence of the occupier and the land-valuation agency. That survey stays on the ground. What we add is a dated land-cover layer, showing cropland, tree cover, bare ground, water and built-up land across the footprint, so the survey teams know where the farms are before they set out.
::::
::::col
### The environmental permit baseline {#environmental-permit}

Mining, pipelines, roads, railways and other undertakings in the First Schedule of the [Environmental Assessment Regulations 2025 (L.I. 2504)](https://www.epa.gov.gh/new/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Environmental-Protection-Environmental-Assessment-Regulations-2025-2.pdf) need an environmental permit. The baseline includes a location map showing communities, water bodies, forest reserves and sacred groves, and a concession map for mining. The same structure register and land-cover layers feed that map; your environmental consultant prepares the report.

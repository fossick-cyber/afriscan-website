::::section{id="law" tone="alt" eyebrow="The law in brief" title="What Act 703 says about land in a mining area" lead="A summary of the public rules as of 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The [Minerals and Mining Act 2006 (Act 703)](https://www.mincom.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Minerals-and-Mining-Act-2006-Act-703.pdf) balances the lease holder's rights against those of the people who own or occupy the land:

- **s.72(3):** a lawful occupier keeps grazing and cultivation rights where they do not interfere with mining.
- **s.72(4):** in a mining area, the owner or lawful occupier "shall not erect a building or a structure without the consent of the holder of the mining lease", or of the Minister where consent is unreasonably withheld.
- **s.72(5):** the lease holder carries out "a survey of the crops and produce a crop identification map", in the presence of the occupier and the land-valuation agency.
- **s.72(6):** no upgrade "to a higher value crop" without the holder's written consent.
- **s.73:** a compensation claim is copied to the Minister and the valuation agency, the Minister determines it if the parties do not agree, and resettlement is on suitable alternative land at the holder's cost.

Each of these turns on what stood on the land, and when.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Compensation and the cut-off date"}
The Minerals and Mining (Compensation and Resettlement) Regulations 2012 (L.I. 2175) fix compensation to a cut-off date published in the district, and the holder "is not liable to pay compensation to any person who undertakes speculative development on land subject to a mineral right after the cut-off date" (reg. 1(8)).

[Cut-off-date baselines in Ghana](/gh/resettlement-compensation-baselines)
:::

:::callout{tone="note" title="The law is changing"}
The Ministry of Lands and Natural Resources reported on 15 July 2026 that Cabinet had endorsed a revised Minerals and Mining Bill, with leases capped at 20 years, district mining committees and mandatory Community Development Agreements ([MLNR](https://mlnr.gov.gh/government-endorses-overhaul-of-20-year-old-mining-law-reports-gains-across-lands-and-forestry-sectors/)). L.I. 2462, on mining in forest reserves, has been revoked.
:::

::::section{id="lease-edge" eyebrow="Lease-edge records" title="What stands inside your lease, and in a ring around it"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
We list the structures the review confirms inside your lease boundary and in the ring you choose around it, each with an ID, coordinates, its distance to the boundary and the imagery date. Between surveys, new and removed structures, cleared ground and new tracks at the lease edge are flagged automatically and confirmed by a reviewer, so the land team can see where the edge is under pressure. Where you fly drones, a reviewer also checks fence lines and gates, which are too thin to find on satellite imagery.

A reviewer can tag each structure with what the imagery shows, such as main building, outbuilding, livestock enclosure or under construction, which is the starting point for a land-access or resettlement register. Across a whole concession, the same work produces a structure layer your GIS team can load straight into its own system.

We work from the boundary you give us, or from the lease polygon on the Minerals Commission's [public cadastre map](https://miningrepository.mincom.gov.gh/map), and the report says which one it used.

::::section{id="galamsey" tone="alt" eyebrow="Galamsey and surface excavation" title="Pits, washing sites and disturbed water on and around your lease"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Galamsey leaves marks that imagery can show: pits and trenches, spoil heaps, water-filled excavations and washing sites, cleared ground, new footpaths and tracks, and discoloured water in the streams and rivers downstream. We map them inside your lease and around it, flag new sites between dated surveys, and give your land, environment and community teams a ranked list of places to check.

Detection of pits and spoil is tuned and checked on part of your area before it runs on the rest, and every flagged site is confirmed by a reviewer. Global ground-disturbance alerts add a regular screen between surveys, and when cloud hides the ground, radar comparisons show larger changes, such as clearing and earthworks, even under rainy-season cloud. Drone checks of flagged areas capture more detail, subject to the GCAA authorisations and security clearances each job requires.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="Land and water, never people"}
We describe the ground: where it has been dug, cleared or flooded, when it changed and how large the disturbed area is. We never count, identify or describe the people involved. Underground workings are not visible from above, and a flag is an indication to check, not proof of anything. What you do with the record, and whom you share it with, is your decision.
:::

::::section{id="forest-and-water" eyebrow="Forest reserves and river buffers" title="The edges that environmental permits care about"}
Many leases border forest reserves or are crossed by rivers. We map where tree cover has been lost or has regrown along a reserve edge, where the ground beside a river has been cleared or dug, and how land cover inside your buffer zones changes between dates, from Copernicus Sentinel data and, where they exist, very-high-resolution scenes. Forest-loss alerts from global satellite systems are checked by our team before they reach you.

The Environmental Assessment Regulations 2025 (L.I. 2504) list forest reserves, water bodies and sacred groves among the features a baseline location map must show, so the same layers feed your environmental permit work.

::::section{id="cut-off" tone="alt" eyebrow="Expansions and resettlement" title="Record the footprint before the census"}
When a pit, dump or haul road expands, we produce a dated register of the structures inside the new footprint on the imagery closest to the cut-off date, with reviewer categories and, on request, estimated households with the assumptions stated. After the cut-off date, re-surveys flag new structures in the footprint for your team to check against the census.

The register supports the census and asset inventory that L.I. 2175 and, where lenders are involved, IFC Performance Standard 5 require; it does not replace them. The crop survey and crop identification map under Act 703 s.72(5) stay with you, the occupier and the land-valuation agency, and a dated land-cover layer helps plan them.

::::section{id="environmental-permit" tone="alt" eyebrow="Environmental permits" title="Baseline maps for the Preliminary Environmental Report"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Under the [Environmental Assessment Regulations 2025 (L.I. 2504)](https://www.epa.gov.gh/new/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Environmental-Protection-Environmental-Assessment-Regulations-2025-2.pdf), in force since 25 July 2025 under the Environmental Protection Act 2025 (Act 1124), mineral exploration, mining and land reclamation are among the undertakings that need an environmental permit. The Preliminary Environmental Report's baseline includes "the location map and topographical sheet showing communities, water bodies, forest reserves, sacred groves and other environmentally sensitive areas", adjacent land use in each direction, and "a concession map is required for a mining undertaking".

We supply the dated inputs: a land-use and land-cover baseline, the structure register for the concession and the ring around it, and change since earlier imagery. Your environmental consultant prepares and signs the report.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="Inputs, not the report"}
- Land use and land cover around the concession
- Structures and settlements, with their distance to the boundary
- Water bodies and tree cover, and change between dates
- The imagery named and dated in every layer

::::section{id="local-content" eyebrow="Local content" title="Working through a Ghanaian firm on mine leases"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
The Minerals Commission's [6th-edition Local Procurement List](https://www.mincom.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Local-Content-Sixth-Edition-2025.pdf), current on 27 September 2026, reserves "Geophysical Ground-based Survey; Mapping, Pitting and Trenching" (item 35) for firms "Strictly Incorporated in Ghana with Exclusive Ghanaian Directors and Shareholders", requires at least 30 per cent Ghanaian directors and shareholders for airborne survey (item 36), and reserves security services to Ghanaians (item 20).

So on a mining lease, AfriScan works as the technology supplier to a qualifying Ghanaian firm, which holds the contract with the lease holder, and our work is described as land and environmental mapping, never as security. Whether remote satellite analysis falls under item 35 is a question for counsel; the proposal sets out the arrangement for your contract.

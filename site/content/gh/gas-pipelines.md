Settlement grows up to the fences of processing plants, booster stations and depots. The zones your engineers define around them need a current list of what stands inside.

::::section{id="corridor-widths" tone="alt" eyebrow="The widths you measure against" title="Corridors acquired project by project" lead="A summary of the public record as of 27 September 2026, for orientation. It is not legal advice; the sources are linked."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The width that matters is the width of your own corridor, which comes from the way its land was acquired. One onshore gas corridor, acquired under Ghana's statutory wayleave and compulsory-acquisition regime, is described in a [Supreme Court judgment of 15 December 2021](https://ghalii.org/akn/gh-hr-accra/judgment/ghasc/2021/130/eng@2021-12-15) as "the right of way covering a distance of 111 Kilometres on a 35 metre corridor".

So we measure to the width your corridor actually has: half the corridor either side of the centreline you send, or the boundary of the corridor polygon, with wider bands for planning and engagement. Tell us the instrument, wayleave or company standard the register should follow, and it is named in the report.

Downstream, petroleum products are regulated by the National Petroleum Authority under the NPA Act 2005 (Act 691); a new NPA Bill was before Parliament in September 2026.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="A typical band set"}
- The corridor itself, for example 17.5 m either side of a 35 m corridor
- 50 m from the line
- 100 m from the line
- 200 m from the line, for planning

Several widths can be reported in one survey. Each structure carries its exact distance too, so any other width can be read from the register later.
:::

::::section{id="operating-lines" eyebrow="Operating pipelines" title="A register of what stands on the corridor, then what changed"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
For an operating line, the first survey lists each structure the review confirms within the widths you set, with an ID, its distance to the centreline, its band, its chainage and its coordinates in WGS84 and the route's UTM zone. Each 500 m segment is rated high, medium or low by the number of structures near the line, so the right-of-way team can plan its visits by segment rather than by kilometre post.

Re-surveys then flag what changed: new and removed structures, fresh excavations, trenches and spoil, cleared ground and new tracks across the corridor, each confirmed by a reviewer and shown before and after. A change notice after each survey says what changed and where, and settlement-growth trends show which stretches are filling up year by year.
::::
::::col

::::section{id="mining-country" tone="alt" eyebrow="Pipelines in mining country" title="New pits and washing sites near a buried line"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Where a pipeline crosses gold-mining districts, galamsey pits, washing sites, spoil and cleared ground can appear within metres of the line, and the streams it crosses can change colour downstream. We map those changes along the corridor and in a band beside it, flag new sites between surveys, and rank the segments where the ground has changed most.

Detection of pits and spoil is tuned and checked on part of your route before it runs on the rest, and each flagged site is confirmed by a reviewer.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="The ground, not the people"}
The register describes land: a pit, a spoil heap, a washing site, cleared ground, and the date it first appears. It never identifies or counts people. What the pipeline owner does with the record, and whom it is shared with, is its decision.
:::

::::section{id="new-pipelines" eyebrow="New pipelines" title="Route options and a baseline before the investment decision"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
For a pipeline that is still on the drawing board, we compare alignments side by side: the structures within set distances of each option, where built-up land has grown over the past years, and the stretches that cross steep, erosion-prone, low-lying or river-crossing ground. That lets land impact be weighed alongside engineering before the route is fixed.

Once a route is chosen, a dated structure register along it becomes the baseline for acquisition and compensation, and later re-surveys show what appeared after the baseline date.

Under the [Environmental Assessment Regulations 2025 (L.I. 2504)](https://www.epa.gov.gh/new/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Environmental-Protection-Environmental-Assessment-Regulations-2025-2.pdf), onshore petroleum surveys, the construction and operation of onshore pipelines, their maintenance and rehabilitation, and oil and gas pipelines generally are among the undertakings that need an environmental permit. The baseline includes a location map showing communities, water bodies, forest reserves and sacred groves, and we supply those layers to the consultants who prepare the report.

::::section{id="upstream" eyebrow="Upstream and downstream work" title="Permits and contracts on petroleum projects"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
Service companies working for upstream operators need a permit from the Petroleum Commission before operations start ([Petroleum Commission](https://petrocom.gov.gh/initial-registration-permit/)). The application asks for company, tax and social-security documents and certificates from professional bodies, and the Commission lists the petroleum local-content regulations, L.I. 2204 as amended by L.I. 2435, among the rules it applies. For upstream work, the proposal states how the permit requirement is met and which company contracts with you.
::::
::::col
Drone flights along a pipeline follow the GCAA's rules: no flight within 30 m of buildings, vehicles or groups of people without explicit permission, no flight near listed facilities without prior approval and security clearance, and an operator whose certificate covers the work. That is why corridor work starts from satellite.

### What we do

:::checklist

### What we don't do

- Replace patrols, field checks or the census

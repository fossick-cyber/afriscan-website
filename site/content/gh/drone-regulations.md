faq:
  - q: Does satellite-based screening need a drone permit?
    a: No. Nothing flies in satellite-based work, so the Part 28 approvals do not come into it. Whether any other permission applies to buying or analysing commercial satellite imagery for your project is a question for your counsel, as is the question of whether aerial mapping needs any permission under Ghana's survey law.
  - q: Can a foreign company fly drone surveys in Ghana?
    a: A foreign operator can be approved by the GCAA for a particular operation under Part 28.16, and the application goes in well before the flights. The documents include its operator certificate, registration and airworthiness papers, pilot licences showing English proficiency to at least ICAO Level 4, insurance, an operator security programme and the proposed flight plan. A foreign firm that wants its own RPAS Operator Certificate needs a company registered in Ghana, because only such a company can complete certification (AC 28-007).
  - q: Our own team flies drones over our own pipeline. Is that commercial?
    a: Part 28 defines commercial operation as "all RPAS operation for remuneration or hire" and private operation as any operation that is neither commercial nor recreational. The GCAA's draft circular on categories, still a draft, gives "an oil distribution company using the RPAS to monitor their pipelines" as a private example, and says the same work becomes commercial "if other companies contract them for the same service". Private operations stay within visual line of sight unless the Authority permits otherwise, and may not go above 400 ft or beyond line of sight without meeting the commercial requirements. Confirm the category for your programme with the GCAA or your counsel.
  - q: Can a drone fly along a line that passes homes and markets?
    a: Only within the populous-area and 30 m rules. Except for take-off and landing, or with GCAA permission, a drone may not fly over congested areas or open-air assemblies unless it is high enough to land safely in an emergency, and it may not fly within 30 m of buildings, vehicles or groups of people not under the pilot's control "without explicit permission from relevant persons or owners" (Part 28.10.7). On settled stretches, satellite imagery is usually the better starting point.
  - q: Can a drone survey a 161 kV or 330 kV line?
    a: Only with a special authorisation. Part 28.4 lists operations in "areas of high RF transmission/interference (e.g. radar sites, high tension wires)" among those that need one, so plan for it on any flight along a transmission line, or start from satellite imagery.
  - q: How do we check an operator's certificate?
    a: The GCAA publishes a list of the RPAS operators it has approved, with the kinds of work each is approved for. The list shows no certificate numbers or expiry dates, so ask the operator for the ROC and its Operations Specifications themselves, and check that they cover surveying and mapping, the area, and any beyond-line-of-sight or populous-area work the job needs.
  - q: What happens after an accident?
    a: The operator must notify the GCAA in writing, and late notice of an accident is one of the listed RPAS sanctions. Part 28's accident provisions still refer to the Civil Aviation Act 2004 (Act 678), which Act 1120 has repealed while saving the directives issued under it, so ask your counsel how they now apply.

::::section{id="at-a-glance" eyebrow="At a glance" title="Six things a commercial survey flight needs" lead="A drone survey in Ghana is lawful only when every one of these is in place for that job. They belong in the plan from the first day, not the week before the flight."}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="An operator certificate" icon="file-check" eyebrow="GCAA"}
An RPAS Operator Certificate (ROC) with Operations Specifications that list the operations allowed. Commercial flying, "for remuneration or hire", needs one (Part 28.1(8)), and only a company registered in Ghana can complete certification.
:::
:::card{title="Registered drones with permits" icon="drone" eyebrow="GCAA"}
Every drone is registered with the GCAA (28.6), carries a 9GR- registration mark and holds its own permit (28.7). The permit and evidence of registration travel with the aircraft.
:::
:::card{title="Qualified pilots" icon="user-check" eyebrow="GCAA"}
A remote pilot licence for each commercial pilot (28.9(2)), aged 18 or over, with a Class III medical, plus a radiotelephony licence and English proficiency where the flight needs radio contact with air traffic services.
:::
:::card{title="Special authorisations" icon="power" eyebrow="GCAA"}
Night flying, hazardous operations, aerodrome restricted areas and areas of high radio interference such as high-tension wires need a special authorisation (28.4). Flights above 400 ft or beyond line of sight need their own approval.
:::
:::card{title="Security approvals" icon="shield" eyebrow="GCAA · Ministry of National Security"}
No-fly zones around security facilities, airports and listed sites were set with the Ministry of National Security, and flights into them "would require security clearances and special approvals from State Entities" (AC 28-009).
:::
:::card{title="Permissions and data" icon="scale" eyebrow="Owners · DPC"}
Explicit permission for flights within 30 m of buildings, vehicles or groups of people, the asset or landowner's written consent, and personal data in the imagery handled under the Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843).
:::

::::section{id="framework" eyebrow="The legal framework" title="Act 1120, and the directives it carried forward"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The **Ghana Civil Aviation Act, 2024 (Act 1120)**, assented to on 31 July 2024, is the primary law. It repealed the Civil Aviation Act 2004 (Act 678) and two later aviation Acts (Acts 906 and 985), and it deems the directives, instructions and circulars issued under Act 678 to be issued under Act 1120 (s.75). Part 28 and the RPAS circulars therefore still apply. The Act defines "air transport" to include "aerial work and all forms of remotely piloted operations" (s.74), lets the Authority set administrative penalties in its directives within a statutory cap (s.37), provides for prohibited areas declared by Executive Instrument (s.40), and makes the GCAA the prosecutor of offences under the Act and its directives (s.67).

**GCAD Part 28, the RPAS Directives of November 2018,** with their Implementing Standards, is the drone rulebook and the only RPAS directive on the GCAA's directive page. **GCAD Part 11, Aerial Work,** says that no one may conduct aerial work in Ghana without certification by the Authority, and that aerial work by remotely piloted aircraft complies with Part 28 (11.1.1(2) and (4)).

**Aviation security.** Part 28.10.16 requires operators to comply with the Civil Aviation (Security) Directives. Ghana Gazette No. 21 of 1 February 2024 gave notice that new Ghana Civil Aviation (Aviation Security) Directives took effect on 31 January 2024 and revoked the 2018 security directives.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Read Part 28 through Act 1120"}
Part 28's enforcement clause (28.17) still cites Act 678 and Act 906, and its accident clause (28.10.15) refers to Act 678. Both Acts are repealed, with savings for the directives, so ask your counsel how those clauses now operate.
:::

::::section{id="categories" tone="alt" eyebrow="The categories" title="Commercial, private or recreational, and where a survey drone fits"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Three categories (Part 28.3) {#three-categories}

- **Commercial:** "All RPAS operation for remuneration or hire". A survey flown for a client is commercial.
- **Private:** "All RPAS operation other than commercial or recreational". The importation circular limits it to registered sole proprietorships, partnerships and companies (AC 28-12, §3.2B).
- **Recreational:** flying for its own sake.

Aerial work, in Part 28's definitions, includes "surveying, observation and patrol". Private and recreational operations stay within visual line of sight "unless otherwise permitted by the Authority" and may not go above 400 ft or beyond line of sight "without compliance with commercial RPAS requirements" (28.1(13)–(14)).

The GCAA's [draft circular on categories](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/wp-content/uploads/2024/DRAFTS/AC%2028-10%20Determination%20of%20Category%20of%20RPAS%20Operations%201.pdf), still filed as a draft, gives worked examples: a land surveying company offering its services to third parties is commercial, while "an oil distribution company using the RPAS to monitor their pipelines" is private, and becomes commercial "if other companies contract them for the same service".
::::
::::col
### Mass classes (Part 28.6(4)) {#mass-classes}

| Class | Maximum take-off mass | Flying |
|---|---|---|
| Small | up to 1.5 kg | Visual line of sight only |
| Light | over 1.5 kg, up to 7 kg | Visual line of sight only |
| Large | over 7 kg | Line of sight, or beyond it with prior authorisation |

A large RPAS also needs a special certificate of airworthiness (restricted category) or an experimental certificate (28.5(3)), and its pilot a remote pilot licence (28.9(3)).

::::section{id="approvals" eyebrow="The approvals" title="What a commercial survey operator needs, one by one"}
### The RPAS Operator Certificate {#roc}

The ROC comes with Operations Specifications (28.1(8)). Only "a registered company under the laws of Ghana" can complete certification ([AC 28-007](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/wp-content/uploads/2023/RPAS%20AOC/AC%2028-007%20-%20RPAS%20Certification%20Process.pdf), §2.2B), evidence of third-party liability insurance is a condition of issue (28.10.1(3)), and a certificate that is revoked, varied or suspended must be returned to the Authority (28.10.1(8)). The operator also needs an approved safety management system with hazard identification and an emergency response plan (28.8), an approved operations manual (28.10.3), an accountable manager (28.9(12)) and records that include security management records (28.9(13)).

### Each drone {#aircraft}

- **Registration** of every drone (28.6(1)), with marks in the 9GR- pattern (AC 28-008). The GCAA's FAQs say drones are presented at the GCAA office for registration; small and light drones can renew their registration remotely ([AC 28-13](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/RPAS/AC%2028-13%20RPAS%20Remote%20Registration%20Renewal%20-%20Clean.pdf)).
- **A permit** for each RPAS (28.7), carried with the evidence of registration (28.9(8)).
- **Import approval** for a drone brought into Ghana (28.1(17)), on form R28-AF-001. The GCAA sends Ghana Customs a Clearance Authorization Letter, and "all required documentation for the RPAS operation shall be obtained before written approval is given to import" (AC 28-12, §4.1).

### Each pilot {#pilots}

A remote pilot licence for commercial flying (28.9(2)), held by someone aged 18 or over with a Class III medical certificate (28.9(6)). Where the flight needs VHF contact with air traffic services, the pilot also needs a radiotelephony licence and an English Language Proficiency Certificate (28.9(9)).

### Each flight {#flights}

- **Special authorisation** (28.4) for the carriage of goods or dangerous goods, night flying, banner towing, cross-border flights, hazardous operations, dropping, acrobatic flights, aerodrome restricted areas, and "areas of high RF transmission/interference (e.g. radar sites, high tension wires)". Plan one for any power-line job.
- **Above 400 ft** above ground level: "prior notification and approval" (28.1(12)).
- **Beyond visual line of sight:** every such flight needs a means to "Detect And Avoid (DAA) traffic and all other hazards" (28.10.6(2)(a)). Beyond-line-of-sight flight under visual flight rules also needs approval, visual meteorological conditions throughout and one of: detect-and-avoid or other mitigation, an area void of traffic, or segregated airspace (28.10.6(2)(e)). In controlled airspace the flight is coordinated with air traffic control in advance, and the drone needs VHF voice or datalink (28.10.6(2)(b); 28.13(3)). The Implementing Standards accept direct, autopilot and waypoint beyond-line-of-sight operations (IS 28.14(4)).
- **Flight planning:** the flight-planning rules apply to beyond-line-of-sight and above-400 ft operations (28.10.2(1)).
- **Radio:** NCA approval where the drone uses non-standard control frequencies.

::::section{id="certification" tone="alt" eyebrow="The certification path" title="Five phases to an operator certificate" lead="AC 28-007 sets out how a company obtains a commercial operator certificate. A client does not go through these phases, but knowing them explains what an operator's Operations Specifications do and do not cover."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="Pre-application"}
The applicant submits a pre-application form and a concept of operations (CONOPS, [AC 28-003](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/RPAS/AC%2028-003%20-%20Guidelines%20for%20Presentation%20of%20RPAS%20Category%20of%20Operations%20(CONOPS)%20Clean.pdf)), which the GCAA reviews against its risk methodology, ARRO ([AC 28-004](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/wp-content/uploads/2023/RPAS%20AOC/AC%2028-004%20-%20Assessment%20of%20Risk%20for%20RPAS%20Operators%20%28ARRO%29%20Methodology.pdf)). The GCAA says ARRO adapts the JARUS SORA method.
:::
:::step{title="Formal application"}
A schedule of events and statement of compliance, the operations manual and proof of third-party liability insurance, draft ROC and Operations Specifications, the safety management, training, maintenance control and dangerous goods manuals, and the areas of operation and method of control.
:::
:::step{title="Document evaluation"}
The GCAA evaluates the documents.
:::
:::step{title="Demonstration and inspection"}
"A minimum of two Inspectors" observe demonstrations, including a "simulation of revenue operations".
:::
:::step{title="Certification"}
The GCAA issues the ROC, the Operations Specifications and the ROC complexity tables. A beyond-line-of-sight rating that was not demonstrated is left out of the Operations Specifications.
:::
:::

::::section{id="corridor-rules" eyebrow="Flying a right of way" title="The rules that shape a corridor survey" lead="Pipelines, transmission lines and railways run past towns, markets, airports and energy installations. These are the Part 28 rules a flight plan along them has to fit."}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Default limits {#limits}

- **Height:** 400 ft above ground level, unless approved.
- **Line of sight:** visual line of sight by default, and one aircraft per remote pilot station (28.1(7)).
- **Night:** no night flying without authorisation (28.1(16)).
- **Airports:** no flight within 10 km of an airport or helipad with a published instrument flight procedure, or 5 km of one without, "unless approved by the Authority" (28.1(15)).
::::
::::col
### Towns, people and property {#populous-areas}

- **Populous areas:** except for take-off and landing, or with GCAA permission, no flight over congested areas of cities, towns or settlements or over open-air assemblies "unless at such a height as will permit, in the event of an emergency arising, a landing to be made without undue hazard to persons or property" (28.10.7(1)).
- **The 30 m rule:** no flight "within a radius of 30 meters from buildings and vehicles not under the person's control or group of people without explicit permission from relevant persons or owners" (28.10.7(2)).
- **Other laws still apply:** operators comply with "noise control, emissions and privacy regulations" (28.1(19)), and a GCAA approval does not relieve anyone of other legislation (28.1(20)).
::::
:::::

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Why corridor work starts from satellite"}
Rights of way lined with homes, kiosks and farms run into the 30 m and populous-area rules wherever they pass settlement, and transmission lines add the special authorisation for high-tension wires. Satellite imagery covers the whole corridor first, and drone flights are then planned only for the stretches that need them, with the permissions those stretches require.
:::

::::section{id="zones" tone="alt" eyebrow="Sensitive sites" title="No-fly zones set with the Ministry of National Security"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
[AC 28-009](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/wp-content/uploads/2023/RPAS%20AOC/AC%2028-009%20-%20RPAS%20NO%20Fly%20Zones.pdf) records no-fly zones determined "by the GCAA and the Ministry of National Security". Each needs "prior approval from the appropriate authorities" (§3.1):

- **10 km** "from the boundaries of military, police, prison, or national security facilities";
- **the airport distances** above, around the airports and airstrips the circular lists;
- **1 km** "or within line of sight of these facilities, whichever is greater" for the other listed facilities: the seat of government in Accra, power stations, ports, the oil refinery, a gas processing plant and other industrial sites, and heritage sites such as forts, castles and museums;
- a facility inside a larger zone follows that zone's rule.

The circular's lists include every regional police headquarters and every prison, so regional capitals and parts of the Western Region's gas and port areas are heavily constrained. Flights into these zones "would require security clearances and special approvals from State Entities". Restricted, prohibited, danger and special-use areas in the Aeronautical Information Publication need "prior authorisation from the Authority and any other relevant Governmental agencies" (28.1(11)).
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="note" title="What we publish, and what we don't"}
This page describes the zones by category. It publishes no map or coordinates of sensitive sites. For an asset that the circular does not list, a flight is planned with the asset owner's written consent and a check of the AIP, and the operator asks the GCAA in writing which security approvals the job needs.
:::

:::callout{tone="scope" title="Security and storage duties"}
Operators follow the aviation security directives, store drones in a way that prevents and detects tampering, and pilots working in aerodrome restricted areas are background-checked (IS 28.10.16).

::::section{id="foreign" eyebrow="Foreign operators and crews" title="Approval per operation, or a Ghanaian company"}
:::::columns{split="1-1"}
::::col
### Approval for a foreign operator (Part 28.16) {#foreign-approval}

A foreign operator can be approved for a particular operation, and applies well before it. The application, on the form in IS 28.16.1, includes:

- the operator's certificate, its registration and the certificate of airworthiness;
- pilot licences "showing English Language Proficiency to at least ICAO Level 4", and medicals;
- the radio station licence and insurance;
- a noise certificate (ICAO Annex 16) and an operator security programme;
- "Proof of Economic Authority, if applicable";
- the proposed flight plan.

The form covers aerial mapping and aerial surveying, and asks for the camera's "transmission destination" and for emergency set-down sites. Once approved, the operator files a flight plan before each operation (28.16(5)), tells the GCAA and air traffic control at once of cancellations, and submits changes.
::::
::::col
### A Ghanaian company of its own {#ghanaian-company}

Only a company registered under the laws of Ghana can complete ROC certification. For foreign investors, the Ghana Investment Promotion Authority Act, 2026 (Act 1173), assented to on 15 July 2026, replaced the GIPC Act 2013 (Act 865). The [Authority's notice](https://gipa.gov.gh/ghana-investment-promotion-centre-gipc-now-an-authority/) says it removes the blanket minimum-capital requirements for wholly foreign-owned enterprises and joint ventures, keeping a reduced threshold for trading enterprises, and that registered enterprises now renew their registration every year. Ask your counsel about the Act's own text.

AC 28-008 also provides "temporal use" approvals, for recreational operation only (§4.2); they do not cover survey work.

::::section{id="privacy" tone="alt" eyebrow="Privacy and personal data" title="Part 28's privacy clause and the Data Protection Act 2012"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
Part 28 requires operators to comply with privacy regulations (28.1(19)), and invasion of privacy is one of the listed RPAS sanctions.

The **Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843)** is enforced by the Data Protection Commission. Its [registration page](https://dpc.gov.gh/registration/) says that "all entities involved in processing personal data are required to register" (s.27(1)), naming data controllers, data processors and "Foreign Companies – International businesses processing personal data within Ghana". Processing while unregistered is barred (s.53) and is an offence (s.56). The application asks, among other things, for the countries to which the applicant intends to transfer personal data and for its security measures, and renewal needs a compliance report. The Commission's guidance calls for a Data Protection Supervisor.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="scope" title="What to agree with any mapping supplier"}
- Map land and assets, not people.
- Keep names and personal details out of the register unless your process needs them.
- Say who is controller and who is processor, where imagery and results are processed and stored, and for how long.
- Name the countries any data is transferred to, as the DPC application asks.

::::section{id="overlays" eyebrow="Beyond aviation" title="Survey law, mining local content and petroleum permits"}
:::cards{cols="3"}
:::card{title="Survey law" icon="ruler"}
Cadastral and boundary surveys, such as the cadastral maps of a resettlement area at 1:2500 that the mining compensation regulations ask for, are work for a professional land surveyor. Whether aerial mapping itself needs any survey permission has not been settled: ask your counsel.
:::
:::card{title="Mining local content" icon="mine"}
The Minerals Commission's 6th-edition list reserves "Geophysical Ground-based Survey; Mapping, Pitting and Trenching" (item 35) for firms incorporated in Ghana with exclusively Ghanaian directors and shareholders, and requires at least 30 per cent Ghanaian directors and shareholders for "Geophysical Airborne-based Survey" (item 36). Whether either item catches drone mapping for a mine is a question for counsel.
:::
:::card{title="Petroleum permits" icon="pipeline"}
Service companies working in upstream petroleum need a Petroleum Commission permit before operations start. The application asks for certificates from professional bodies, and gives "EPA Permit, Air Operator Certificate, NPA Licence, Aviation Licence" as examples ([Petroleum Commission](https://petrocom.gov.gh/initial-registration-permit/)).

::::section{id="penalties" tone="alt" eyebrow="Enforcement" title="Penalties"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The GCAA's [RPAS Sanctions](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/RPAS/RPAS%20Sanctions%20-%20Clean.pdf) set civil penalties, in penalty units, at minimum, moderate and maximum levels for operators, operator personnel and individuals, together with certificate actions from suspension to revocation. Act 1120 caps the administrative penalties the directives may impose (s.37(9)) and lets the Authority impose administrative penalties for offences under the Act (s.72). This site quotes no amounts: see the sources.

The listed sanctions include commercial operation without GCAA approval, flying in restricted, prohibited, danger or special-use areas, flying above 400 ft or beyond line of sight without approval, flying over populous areas or without a required special authorisation (both at the maximum level, with suspension or revocation), breaching the airport distances, invasion of privacy, and late written notice of an accident. Unlawful use leads to mandatory revocation and "Criminal prosecution".
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="warn" title="Clients carry risk too"}
The sanctions schedule lists "Using the services of an uncertificated RPAS entity", at the moderate to maximum level, under the heading "RPAS Vendors & Individuals". Whether it reaches a corporate client is a question for counsel; checking the operator's certificate before the first flight avoids the question.

::::section{id="checklist" eyebrow="For clients" title="Checklist: before you commission a drone survey in Ghana" lead="Ask any operator, including one we propose, for each of these before the first flight."}
:::steps{style="list"}
:::step{title="The operator"}
A company registered in Ghana with a current GCAA ROC and Operations Specifications covering surveying and mapping, the area, and any beyond-line-of-sight or populous-area work. Cross-check the [GCAA's published list of RPAS operators](https://www.gcaa.com.gh/web/certified-rpas-operators/), then ask for the ROC itself.
:::
:::step{title="The drones"}
Each drone registered (9GR-) with a current permit, and GCAA import clearance for imported equipment.
:::
:::step{title="The pilots"}
A remote pilot licence and Class III medical for each pilot, and a radiotelephony licence and English proficiency where air traffic control contact is needed.
:::
:::step{title="Insurance"}
Third-party liability cover, which is also a condition of the ROC.
:::
:::step{title="Special approvals"}
Special authorisations for night work or flights near high-tension wires, and approvals for flights above 400 ft or beyond line of sight, requested well ahead.
:::
:::step{title="A no-fly check"}
The route checked against AC 28-009 and the AIP (security facilities, airports, listed sites), with the security approvals each zone needs.
:::
:::step{title="Permissions along the route"}
A plan for the 30 m rule (owners and "relevant persons"), populous-area approval where the height exception does not apply, and the written consent of the land or asset holder.
:::
:::step{title="Community engagement"}
A plan that involves chiefs and traditional authorities, the District Assembly and the people living along the route before flights begin.
:::
:::step{title="Foreign crews"}
The Part 28.16 approval for any operator or crew from abroad.
:::
:::step{title="Personal data"}
Data Protection Commission registration under Act 843, and a flight plan that maps land and assets rather than people.
:::
:::step{title="Surveys and sector rules"}
A professional land surveyor for anything cadastral, a local-content route for work on a mine lease, and a Petroleum Commission permit for upstream petroleum work.
:::
:::step{title="An accident procedure"}
Written notice to the GCAA after any accident, within the time the Authority sets.
:::

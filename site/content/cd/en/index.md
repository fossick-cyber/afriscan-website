::::section{id="rules" eyebrow="What the texts say" title="Distances set in Congolese law, measured structure by structure" lead="Three sets of rules give a survey in the DRC its widths: the servitudes, the electricity law and the Mining Code. Checked against the published texts on 27 September 2026. A summary of the public rules for orientation; it is not legal advice."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
**Servitudes.** [Arrêté interministériel n° 0021 of 29 October 1993](https://www.leganet.be/Legislation/Droit%20civil/AM.021.29.10.1993.Servitude.htm) lists among the servitudes "les emprises des lignes de haute tension sur une distance de 25 mètres de part et d'autre" (the emprises of high-voltage lines, 25 metres on each side) and railway emprises "de 5 à 50 mètres suivant catégories" (5 to 50 metres, depending on category). It prohibits "toute occupation, toute construction et tout lotissement" there: any occupation, construction or subdivision (art. 2).

**Electricity.** [Loi n° 14/011 of 17 June 2014 on the electricity sector](https://www.leganet.be/Legislation/Droit%20economique/Energie/Loi.14.011.17.06.2014.htm) penalises anyone who builds on or under power lines or occupies the emprises of the State's public electrical installations (art. 125). It also says that overhead conductors crossing a land concession do not stop the holder fencing or building there: a servitude of passage for maintenance remains, and the holder gives three months' notice before building (arts 108 and 109).

**Mining.** Under the [Mining Code](https://www.leganet.be/Legislation/Droit%20economique/Code%20Minier/Loi.18.001.09.03.2018.html), a mining right holder may not occupy, without the consent of the owner or lawful occupant, land within 1,000 metres of houses or buildings or within 800 metres of cultivated fields (art. 279, as amended in 2018). These distances protect the people who live and farm there; they do not make a house near a mine unlawful.

AfriScan measures each structure it records against these distances, or against the ones you set.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Who decides"}
Whether a structure is permitted is for you and the competent authorities to decide. How the 1993 arrêté fits with art. 108 of the electricity law, which does not stop a land holder building under a line that crosses the concession, is a point for counsel.

**For lenders and E&S teams.** Dated structure registers support your census and asset inventory and the cut-off-date record that IFC Performance Standard 5 and the World Bank's ESS5 expect. They do not replace the census. Congolese ESIA rules also ask for "l'inventaire précis et détaillé de l'état initial du site", a precise and detailed inventory of the site's initial state (Décret n° 14/019, art. 19), and a dated register feeds straight into it.

::::section{id="satellite" tone="alt" eyebrow="Satellite first" title="Documented remotely, with no site access and no overflight"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A satellite survey needs neither access to the site nor a flight over it. It starts from your route or boundary file and satellite imagery, open building datasets or the imagery you already hold, and documents how an emprise or a perimeter changes across several dates. All of the analysis is done remotely.

Where teams can no longer reach part of a site, satellite imagery still shows what has changed since the last visit. Re-surveys follow a schedule agreed with you, with a notice after each one; how often depends on the imagery available and on cloud cover.

Where satellite imagery is not enough, a drone survey can be carried out by an eligible Congolese operator, subject to the approvals and security clearances each job requires, starting with the prior authorisation of the Autorité de l'Aviation Civile (AAC) under art. 58 bis of the civil aviation law.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Drones in the DRC"}
No drone may fly over the country or be operated there without the AAC's prior authorisation, whatever its weight. The texts, the prohibited zones and a checklist for clients:

::::section{id="subcontracting" eyebrow="Working with us in the DRC" title="A technology supplier, alongside an eligible Congolese company"}
Subcontracting in the DRC is reserved to Congolese-owned companies with their registered office in the country (Loi n° 17/001 of 8 February 2017, amended by Loi n° 26/017 of 30 June 2026), unless the subcontracting regulator, ARSP, grants a derogation. AfriScan therefore works in the DRC as a technology supplier to an eligible Congolese company that holds the contract: the prime contractor carries the contract, and AfriScan supplies the image analysis and the registers.

Each proposal sets out the arrangement for your contract and the documents available. [Subcontracting in the DRC, for procurement teams](/cd/subcontracting)

::::section{id="context" eyebrow="The context" title="Corridors rehabilitated through emprises occupied for years"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
The Katanga export corridors, the railways and roads to the ports and to neighbouring countries, are the subject of concession contracts and rehabilitation programmes, including a thirty-year rail concession signed in August 2026. These projects run through emprises that have been occupied for a long time; where a lender finances them, its resettlement standards call for a census closed at a cut-off date.

The same need arises on roads, where a project can widen its emprise partway through and redefine its cut-off date, and on fibre-optic cables laid in road emprises.

::::section{id="texts" tone="alt" eyebrow="What the texts say" title="Railway and road emprises" lead="Checked against the published texts on 27 September 2026. A summary of the public rules for orientation; it is not legal advice."}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
[Arrêté interministériel n° 0021 of 29 October 1993](https://www.leganet.be/Legislation/Droit%20civil/AM.021.29.10.1993.Servitude.htm) lists among the servitudes:
- "les emprises des chemins de fer de 5 à 50 mètres suivant catégories", railway emprises of 5 to 50 metres by category;
- road emprises, "conformément aux plans d'urbanisme et plans cadastraux", in line with town-planning and cadastral plans;
- riverbanks, "jusqu'au moins 10 mètres", to at least 10 metres.

It prohibits "toute occupation, toute construction et tout lotissement" there, any occupation, construction or subdivision (art. 2). We found no published railway law that sets these widths more precisely.

Near mines, the Mining Code also bars a mining right holder from occupying, without the consent of the competent authorities, land "réservé au projet de chemin de fer", reserved for a railway project, or a road (art. 279). That rule protects the line against mining occupation; it is not a servitude of the line.
::::
::::col
:::callout{tone="legal" title="Who decides"}
Whether a structure is permitted is for you and the competent authorities to decide. A line's category, and so the width of its emprise, should be confirmed with the line's manager or with counsel.
:::

::::section{id="cut-off-date" eyebrow="When the emprise or the timetable changes" title="A cut-off date can be redefined; the imagery records each date"}
:::::columns{split="2-1"}
::::col
A resettlement plan published in January 2025 for a 150 km road financed by the [World Bank](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099012225111023710/pdf/P1618771d5d78d0311a4241565cbe8c3429.pdf) shows how a project evolves:
- the cut-off date was set at 13 November 2021, then 6 March 2023, then 15 August 2024;
- the 2021 and 2023 plans used a 9 m emprise;
- the August 2024 update widened the emprise to 15 m over the first ten kilometres, and the remaining 140 km are to be updated to 15 m, or 12 m in constrained areas.

The same document defines the cut-off date as "la date qui marque le début de l'opération de recensement des personnes et de leurs biens", the date that marks the start of the census of people and their assets, after which assets put in place "ne sont pas indemnisés", are not compensated. A dated image survey keeps a record of what existed at each date; if the width changes, the register is measured again against the new emprise.

### What we do

:::checklist
- Record the structures, tracks and earthworks visible in and around the emprise

- Compare route options
- Work remotely, with no access to the works site

### What we don't do

:::checklist{tone="no"}
- Stand in for a land surveyor's topographic survey or a measurement of quantities
- See underground or detect theft
- Decide whether a structure is permitted
- Stand in for the census or the asset inventory
